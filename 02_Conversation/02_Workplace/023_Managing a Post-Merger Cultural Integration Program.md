# Topic: Managing a Post-Merger Cultural Integration Program

**Scenario:** You are the Head of People & Culture at a global financial services firm. Your company has just acquired a smaller, more agile fintech company with a completely different culture—flat hierarchy, rapid decision-making, and a casual work environment. The acquisition is complete, but cultural friction is already emerging: the acquired team feels "stifled" by your bureaucracy, while your legacy team worries that the new culture will "lower standards." The CEO has asked you to lead a cultural integration program to: **assess** both cultures, **identify** areas of alignment and friction, **design** a new, shared culture, **communicate** the vision, **support** the transition, and **measure** progress. You are now presenting the cultural integration plan to the joint leadership team.

---

## Dialogue

| **Speaker** | **Dialogue / Text** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **You** | *(opening the meeting)* Thank you all for joining. I know the word "culture integration" can feel abstract—but it's the most important work we'll do. A successful merger doesn't just combine balance sheets; it combines people, values, and ways of working. Today, I'm presenting our plan to build a new, shared culture that leverages the best of both worlds. |
| **Acquired Company CEO** | I appreciate that. But my team is nervous. They feel like their way of doing things—which has worked for 15 years—is being dismissed. |
| **You** | I hear that—and I want to be clear: we're not here to erase your culture. We're here to create something new. We'll conduct a **cultural assessment** that includes surveys, focus groups, and interviews to understand what's working on both sides. Then we'll co‑create a new set of values and behaviors. |
| **Legacy Team Member** | What about the power dynamics? We're the bigger company. How do we ensure their voice is heard? |
| **You** | We'll have a joint steering committee with equal representation from both sides. Every decision about culture will be made collaboratively. I also propose we create **cross‑functional culture squads**—small teams with members from both companies—to work on specific culture initiatives. |
| **Acquired Team Member** | What about the practical differences—like how we run meetings, how we communicate, how we give feedback? |
| **You** | We'll create a **culture playbook** that defines our shared norms: meeting etiquette, communication channels, decision‑making processes, and feedback practices. It will be a living document—we'll adapt it as we learn. |
| **Legacy Team Member** | What's the timeline? |
| **You** | 12 months, broken into 4 phases: **Phase 1**—Discovery and assessment (3 months). **Phase 2**—Co‑creation of the new culture (3 months). **Phase 3**—Pilot implementation (3 months). **Phase 4**—Full rollout and evaluation (3 months). |
| **Acquired Company CEO** | And how will we know if it's working? |
| **You** | We'll track three metrics: **#1**—Employee engagement and belonging scores. **#2**—Retention rates, especially during the integration. **#3**—Productivity and collaboration indicators. We'll report progress quarterly to the board. |
| **Legacy Team Member** | I'm willing to give this a try—as long as it's genuinely collaborative. |
| **You** | It will be. We're not just implementing a plan; we're building a new culture together. |
| **You** | *(closing)* This is an opportunity to create something better than either company had individually. I'm asking for your patience, your honesty, and your participation. Let's build a culture that makes us proud. |
| *(6 months later – progress update)* | |
| **Acquired Company CEO** | *(to the task force)* I have to admit, I was skeptical at first. But the culture squads have made a real difference. I'm seeing more cross‑team collaboration and fewer tensions. |
| **Legacy Team Member** | And the culture playbook has given us clarity on how to work together. It's not perfect, but it's a huge improvement. |
| **You** | That's exactly what we aimed for. We'll continue to refine and adapt. This is a journey, not a destination. |

| **Cultural assessment** | Noun Phrase | A systematic review of values, behaviors, and norms. |
| **Co‑create** | Verb | To develop something collaboratively. |
| **Joint steering committee** | Noun Phrase | A group with equal representation from both sides. |
| **Cross‑functional culture squads** | Noun Phrase | Small teams from both sides working on specific culture initiatives. |
