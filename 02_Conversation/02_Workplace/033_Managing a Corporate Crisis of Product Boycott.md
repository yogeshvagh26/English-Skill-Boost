# Topic: Managing a Corporate Crisis of Product Boycott / Consumer Activism

**Scenario:** You are the Chief Communications Officer at a global consumer goods company. A social media campaign has gone viral, calling for a boycott of your products due to concerns about your company's environmental practices and labor standards. The campaign has gained traction with influencers, activists, and media outlets. Sales have dropped 15% in key markets, and the board is demanding action. You need to convene an emergency crisis team (CEO, Head of CSR, Head of Supply Chain, General Counsel, Head of Marketing, and Head of Customer Service) to: **understand** the root causes of the boycott, **engage** with activists and stakeholders, **address** legitimate concerns, **correct** misinformation, **communicate** the company's commitment to improvement, **develop** a concrete action plan, **rebuild** trust with consumers, and **monitor** the impact of the response.

---

## Dialogue

| **Speaker** | **Dialogue / Text** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **CEO** | *(opening the emergency meeting)* We have a crisis. A boycott campaign against our company is going viral. Our sales are down, our brand is being damaged, and our employees are concerned. We need to respond quickly and effectively. |
| **You** | I've been monitoring the situation. The campaign is being driven by a coalition of environmental and labor activists. Their concerns include our carbon footprint, use of single‑use plastics, and labor practices in our supply chain. Some allegations are exaggerated, but others have merit. |
| **Head of CSR** | We've already made progress on sustainability and ethics, but we haven't communicated it effectively. This is an opportunity to tell our story and make new commitments. |
| **General Counsel** | We need to be careful about legal exposure. We should avoid making statements that could be used against us in court. But we also need to be transparent and credible. |
| **You** | I recommend we engage directly with the activists. We'll invite them to a meeting to understand their concerns and explore solutions. We'll also publish a comprehensive sustainability and ethics report and commit to new, ambitious targets. |
| **Head of Marketing** | What about our messaging? We need to counter the misinformation while demonstrating that we're listening and acting. |
| **You** | We'll launch a campaign called *"Listen, Act, Lead"* —showing that we're hearing the concerns, taking action, and leading on sustainability. We'll also showcase the progress we've already made. |
| **CEO** | What about the specific demands? Some are unrealistic. |
| **You** | We'll focus on the ones that are reasonable and impactful. For example, we can commit to reducing single‑use plastics by 30% by 2028, achieve carbon neutrality in our operations by 2030, and publish an annual ethics report. |
| **Head of Customer Service** | What about our customers? They're calling with questions. |
| **You** | We'll provide a script: *"We take these concerns seriously. We are engaging with stakeholders and taking action to improve our environmental and social performance. You can learn more on our website."* |
| **CEO** | Let's launch a CEO blog post and video addressing the concerns directly. I'll be transparent and committed. |
| **You** | *(closing)* This is a challenge, but also an opportunity to demonstrate leadership and accelerate our sustainability journey. Let's engage authentically, act decisively, and rebuild trust. |
| *(6 months later – follow‑up)* | |
| **You** | *(to the leadership team)* The boycott has subsided. Our engagement with activists has been productive—they've acknowledged our progress. Sales are recovering, and our brand perception is improving. The new sustainability commitments are being implemented. |
| **CEO** | We've turned a crisis into a catalyst for positive change. |
| **You** | This experience has made us a stronger, more transparent, and more responsible company. We'll continue to lead on these issues. |
