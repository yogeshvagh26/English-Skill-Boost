# Phase 9: Prepositions

## 1. Types

Welcome to Phase 9! You've mastered **verbs** (types, forms, agreement, irregulars, gerunds, infinitives, participles), **nouns** (all types), **pronouns** (all types), **adjectives** (types, order, comparisons), **adverbs** (time, place, frequency, degree, manner, sentence adverbs, position), and **articles & determiners**. Now it's time to explore **prepositions** — the words that show relationships between nouns/pronouns and other words in a sentence.

Think of it this way: *Prepositions are the "relationship words." They show how things relate in TIME (before, after, during), PLACE (in, on, at), DIRECTION (to, from, toward), and more. Without prepositions, your sentences would be a disconnected list of words.*

## 1. Prepositions of Time

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Prepositions of time** tell us when something happens. They answer the question "When?"

**Key Points:**
- Show relationships in time
- Answer "When?" questions
- Include: at, on, in, before, after, during, since, for, until, by, from...to

### 📐 Study: Prepositions of Time Chart

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|-------------|-----|---------|
| **at** | Specific times, holidays | at 5 PM, at Christmas |
| **on** | Days, dates | on Monday, on July 4th |
| **in** | Months, years, seasons, parts of day | in June, in 2024, in summer |
| **before** | Earlier than | before 5 PM |
| **after** | Later than | after 5 PM |
| **during** | Throughout | during the meeting |
| **since** | From a point in the past | since 2020 |
| **for** | Duration | for 5 years |
| **until** | Up to a point | until 5 PM |
| **by** | Not later than | by 5 PM |
| **from...to** | Start and end | from 9 AM to 5 PM |

### 👀 See: Examples

**At:**
- "I will meet you **at** 5 PM."
- "We celebrate **at** Christmas."
- "She wakes up **at** 7 AM."
- "I work **at** night." (night)

**On:**
- "I will see you **on** Monday."
- "She was born **on** July 4th."
- "We have a meeting **on** Friday."
- "I work **on** weekends."

**In:**
- "I was born **in** June."
- "She graduated **in** 2020."
- "I like to swim **in** summer."
- "I work **in** the morning."

**Before/After:**
- "I will call you **before** 5 PM."
- "She arrived **after** 5 PM."
- "I need to finish **before** the deadline."
- "We can meet **after** lunch."

**During:**
- "I fell asleep **during** the movie."
- "She works **during** the day."
- "We met **during** the conference."

**Since/For:**
- "I have worked here **since** 2020."
- "I have worked here **for** 5 years."
- "She has lived here **since** 2018."
- "She has lived here **for** 6 years."

**Until/By:**
- "I will stay **until** 5 PM."
- "Please finish **by** 5 PM."
- "She waited **until** midnight."

**From...to:**
- "I work **from** 9 AM **to** 5 PM."
- "She studies **from** morning **to** night."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I will meet you in 5 PM. | I will meet you **at** 5 PM. |
| I will see you at Monday. | I will see you **on** Monday. |
| She was born in July 4th. | She was born **on** July 4th. |
| I work since 5 years. | I work **for** 5 years. |
| I work for 2020. | I work **since** 2020. |
| I will finish until 5 PM. | I will finish **by** 5 PM. |

## 2. Prepositions of Place

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Prepositions of place** tell us where something is located. They answer the question "Where?"

**Key Points:**
- Show location or position
- Answer "Where?" questions
- Include: in, on, at, under, over, above, below, between, among, next to, beside, behind, in front of, opposite

### 📐 Study: Prepositions of Place Chart

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|-------------|-----|---------|
| **in** | Enclosed space | in the room, in the box |
| **on** | Surface | on the table, on the wall |
| **at** | Specific point | at the bus stop, at home |
| **under** | Below | under the table |
| **over** | Above (covering) | over the bridge |
| **above** | Higher than | above the clouds |
| **below** | Lower than | below sea level |
| **between** | In the middle (two) | between the chairs |
| **among** | In the middle (more than two) | among the students |
| **next to/beside** | Adjacent | next to the bank |
| **behind** | At the back of | behind the door |
| **in front of** | Before | in front of the house |
| **opposite** | Facing | opposite the park |

### 👀 See: Examples

**In:**
- "The book is **in** the bag."
- "She lives **in** London."
- "I was **in** the car."
- "The keys are **in** my pocket."

**On:**
- "The book is **on** the table."
- "She lives **on** this street."
- "The picture is **on** the wall."
- "I sat **on** the chair."

**At:**
- "I am **at** home."
- "She is **at** the bus stop."
- "We met **at** the restaurant."
- "He works **at** the bank."

**Under/Over:**
- "The cat is **under** the bed."
- "The plane flew **over** the city."
- "She hid **under** the blanket."
- "He jumped **over** the fence."

**Above/Below:**
- "The birds are flying **above** us."
- "The submarine is **below** the surface."
- "The temperature is **above** zero."
- "The treasure is **below** the ground."

**Between/Among:**
- "The book is **between** the two chairs."
- "She is **among** her friends."
- "The store is **between** the bank and the hotel."
- "He is popular **among** his colleagues."

**Next to/Beside/Behind/In front of:**
- "The bank is **next to** the school."
- "I sat **beside** her."
- "He is **behind** me."
- "The car is **in front of** the house."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I am in home. | I am **at** home. |
| She lives at London. | She lives **in** London. |
| The book is in the table. | The book is **on** the table. |
| He is in the bus stop. | He is **at** the bus stop. |
| The cat is in the bed. | The cat is **under** the bed. |
| The store is among the bank and the hotel. | The store is **between** the bank and the hotel. |

## 3. Prepositions of Direction

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Prepositions of direction** tell us where something is going. They answer the question "Where to?"

**Key Points:**
- Show movement toward something
- Answer "Where to?" questions
- Include: to, from, into, out of, toward, onto, off

### 📐 Study: Prepositions of Direction Chart

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|-------------|-----|---------|
| **to** | Toward a destination | go to school, walk to the store |
| **from** | Origin/starting point | come from London, from here to there |
| **into** | Entering | walk into the room, get into the car |
| **out of** | Exiting | walk out of the room, get out of the car |
| **toward** | In the direction of | walk toward the park, go toward the station |
| **onto** | Moving to a surface | jump onto the table, get onto the bus |
| **off** | Moving away from | get off the bus, take the book off the shelf |

### 👀 See: Examples

**To:**
- "I am going **to** school."
- "She walked **to** the store."
- "We flew **to** Paris."
- "I need to go **to** the bank."

**From:**
- "I came **from** London."
- "She received a gift **from** her friend."
- "The train goes **from** London **to** Paris."
- "I borrowed this book **from** the library."

**Into:**
- "He walked **into** the room."
- "She got **into** the car."
- "I put the money **into** my pocket."
- "The cat jumped **into** the box."

**Out of:**
- "He walked **out of** the room."
- "She got **out of** the car."
- "I took the money **out of** my pocket."
- "The cat jumped **out of** the box."

**Toward:**
- "She walked **toward** the park."
- "He ran **toward** the exit."
- "The car was driving **toward** the city."
- "They moved **toward** the door."

**Onto/Off:**
- "She jumped **onto** the table."
- "He got **onto** the bus."
- "Take the book **off** the shelf."
- "She got **off** the bus."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I go to home. | I go **home**. |
| She went to outside. | She went **outside**. |
| He walked to into the room. | He walked **into** the room. |
| She got out of the car. (✓) | She got **out of** the car. |

## 4. Prepositions of Movement

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Prepositions of movement** tell us how something moves or the path it takes. They answer the question "How?" or "Which way?"

**Key Points:**
- Show the path of movement
- Include: across, along, through, past, around, over, under, up, down

### 📐 Study: Prepositions of Movement Chart

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|-------------|-----|---------|
| **across** | From one side to another | walk across the road |
| **along** | Following a line | walk along the river |
| **through** | Going inside/pass through | drive through the tunnel |
| **past** | Going beside/ beyond | walk past the building |
| **around** | Circular movement | walk around the block |
| **over** | Going above | jump over the fence |
| **under** | Going below | crawl under the bed |
| **up** | Moving upward | climb up the stairs |
| **down** | Moving downward | walk down the hill |

### 👀 See: Examples

**Across:**
- "She walked **across** the road."
- "He swam **across** the river."
- "They drove **across** the bridge."
- "I ran **across** the field."

**Along:**
- "We walked **along** the beach."
- "She drove **along** the highway."
- "He ran **along** the river."
- "I walked **along** the street."

**Through:**
- "She walked **through** the forest."
- "He drove **through** the tunnel."
- "I walked **through** the crowd."
- "The train goes **through** the mountains."

**Past:**
- "She walked **past** the school."
- "He ran **past** the store."
- "I drove **past** the park."
- "She walked **past** me."

**Around:**
- "We walked **around** the block."
- "She drove **around** the city."
- "I went **around** the building."
- "He ran **around** the track."

**Over/Under:**
- "He jumped **over** the fence."
- "She crawled **under** the table."
- "The plane flew **over** the city."
- "The cat hid **under** the bed."

**Up/Down:**
- "He climbed **up** the stairs."
- "She walked **down** the hill."
- "I ran **up** the stairs."
- "The ball rolled **down** the hill."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| We walked through the road. | We walked **across** the road. |
| She walked through the beach. | She walked **along** the beach. |
| He walked over the city. | He walked **around** the city. |
| She crawled over the table. | She crawled **under** the table. |

## 5. Prepositions of Manner

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Prepositions of manner** tell us how something is done. They answer the question "How?"

**Key Points:**
- Show how an action is performed
- Include: by, with, like, as, without

### 📐 Study: Prepositions of Manner Chart

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|-------------|-----|---------|
| **by** | Method/means | by car, by bus, by hand |
| **with** | Using a tool/instrument | with a knife, with a pen |
| **like** | Similar to | like a child, like a bird |
| **as** | Role/function | as a teacher, as a friend |
| **without** | Not having | without help, without money |

### 👀 See: Examples

**By:**
- "I travel **by** train."
- "She wrote the letter **by** hand."
- "He paid **by** credit card."
- "I learned it **by** experience."

**With:**
- "She cut the bread **with** a knife."
- "I wrote the letter **with** a pen."
- "He opened the door **with** a key."
- "She ate the soup **with** a spoon."

**Like:**
- "She sings **like** a professional."
- "He runs **like** a cheetah."
- "She dances **like** a dancer."
- "He looks **like** his father."

**As:**
- "She works **as** a doctor."
- "He behaves **as** a leader."
- "I speak **as** your friend."
- "She was hired **as** a manager."

**Without:**
- "He left **without** saying goodbye."
- "She finished **without** any help."
- "He came **without** his wallet."
- "She survived **without** water."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I travel by a train. | I travel **by** train. |
| She wrote with hand. | She wrote **by** hand. |
| He works like a doctor. | He works **as** a doctor. |
| She looks as her mother. | She looks **like** her mother. |

## 6. Prepositions of Agent

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Prepositions of agent** tell us who or what performs an action. They are most commonly used in passive voice.

**Key Points:**
- Show who/what performs an action
- Used in passive voice
- Include: by, with

### 📐 Study: Prepositions of Agent Chart

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|-------------|-----|---------|
| **by** | Person/animal performing action | by the teacher, by the dog |
| **with** | Instrument/tool used | with a knife, with a hammer |

### 👀 See: Examples

**By (Agent):**
- "The book was written **by** a famous author."
- "The cake was made **by** my mother."
- "The project was completed **by** the team."
- "The letter was sent **by** Sarah."
- "The house was built **by** the construction company."

**With (Instrument):**
- "The cake was cut **with** a knife."
- "The door was opened **with** a key."
- "The painting was done **with** a brush."
- "The hole was dug **with** a shovel."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| The book was written with the author. | The book was written **by** the author. |
| The cake was cut by a knife. | The cake was cut **with** a knife. |

## 7. Prepositional Phrases

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **prepositional phrase** is a group of words that begins with a preposition and ends with a noun or pronoun (the object of the preposition).

**Key Points:**
- Begin with a preposition
- End with a noun or pronoun
- Function as adjectives or adverbs

### 📐 Study: Prepositional Phrase Structures

**Structure 1: Prepositional Phrase**
```
[PREPOSITION] + [OBJECT]
in + the room
on + the table
```

**Structure 2: Prepositional Phrase as Adjective**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [PREPOSITIONAL PHRASE]
The book + is + on the table.
```

**Structure 3: Prepositional Phrase as Adverb**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [PREPOSITIONAL PHRASE]
She + walked + into the room.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Prepositional Phrases as Adjectives:**
- "The book **on the table** is mine." (Which book? The one on the table)
- "The woman **in the red dress** is my sister." (Which woman?)
- "The house **at the end of the street** is empty." (Which house?)
- "The man **with the blue eyes** is my uncle." (Which man?)

**Prepositional Phrases as Adverbs:**
- "She walked **into the room**." (Where did she walk?)
- "He arrived **after the meeting**." (When did he arrive?)
- "I spoke **with confidence**." (How did I speak?)
- "We met **at the restaurant**." (Where did we meet?)

**Common Prepositional Phrases:**
- "**In the morning**" → I work **in the morning**.
- "**At the end**" → She is **at the end**.
- "**On the table**" → The book is **on the table**.
- "**For example**" → **For example**, I like pizza.
- "**In addition**" → **In addition**, I need more time.

## 💬 Practice: Conversations

### Dialogue 1: Using Prepositions of Time

- A: "What time do you usually wake up?"
- B: "I wake up **at** 7 AM **in** the morning."
- A: "I work **from** 9 AM **to** 5 PM."
- B: "Do you work **on** weekends?"
- A: "No, I work **during** the week."
- B: "I will call you **after** work."

### Dialogue 2: Using Prepositions of Place

- A: "Where is the bank?"
- B: "It's **on** Main Street, **next to** the supermarket."
- A: "Is it **between** the supermarket and the hotel?"
- B: "Yes, it's **between** them."
- A: "Where do you live?"
- B: "I live **in** London, **near** the park."

### Dialogue 3: Using Prepositions of Direction

- A: "Where are you going?"
- B: "I'm going **to** the store."
- A: "What are you going **to** buy?"
- B: "I need to go **to** the bank first."
- A: "Walk **toward** the station and you'll see it."
- B: "Thanks! I'll walk **across** the bridge."

### Dialogue 4: Using Prepositions in Everyday Life

- A: "Who wrote this book?"
- B: "It was written **by** a famous author."
- A: "I'm interested **in** reading it."
- B: "You should read it. I paid **for** it."
- A: "I'll borrow it **from** the library."
- B: "You can borrow it **from** me."
