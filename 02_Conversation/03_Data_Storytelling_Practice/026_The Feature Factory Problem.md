## 1. Situation : Product Roadmap Prioritization – The "Feature Factory" Problem

**Context:** You are a Senior Data Analyst at a project management SaaS. The Head of Product (Maya) is facing pressure from the VP of Sales (Robert) to build a specific feature requested by a large Enterprise prospect. The Head of Engineering (Mark) is pushing back because it would take 3 months of engineering effort. The CEO (Sarah) wants a data-driven decision. You've been asked to analyze feature requests, user feedback, and potential revenue impact to help prioritize the roadmap. You need to present a framework that helps the team make a rational, data-backed decision—rather than just building features for the loudest voice.

## 3. Vocabulary and Expressions

| Word/Phrase                     | Part of Speech | Meaning                                                                                | Example Sentence                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------- | -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Feature prioritization**      | Noun Phrase    | The process of deciding which features to build and in what order                      | *Feature prioritization should be data-driven, not opinion-driven.*                               |
| **Weighted scoring model**      | Noun Phrase    | A framework that assigns different weights to different criteria for prioritization    | *Our weighted scoring model gives revenue 40% weight and user pain 30% weight.*                  |
| **Effort / Complexity**         | Noun           | The amount of engineering time and resources required to build a feature               | *The Enterprise feature has high effort (3 months), so we prioritize lower-effort items first.*  |
| **User pain**                   | Noun Phrase    | How much a feature addresses a customer's problem or frustration                       | *Mobile complaints are the highest user pain—80% of support tickets are about mobile.*           |
| **Revenue impact**              | Noun Phrase    | The potential revenue increase (or churn reduction) from a feature                     | *AI reporting has a $3M revenue impact from reduced churn.*                                      |
| **Strategic alignment**         | Noun Phrase    | How well a feature fits with the company's long-term vision and goals                  | *Mobile improvements align with our strategy of being the best mobile-first tool.*               |
| **Roadmap sequencing**          | Noun Phrase    | The order in which features are built and released                                     | *Roadmap sequencing allows us to deliver value sooner, not later.*                               |
| **Opportunity cost**            | Noun Phrase    | The value of the best alternative you give up when choosing one option                 | *Building the Enterprise feature first has an opportunity cost of delaying mobile improvements.* |
| **ROI (Return on Investment)**  | Acronym        | The financial return you get from an investment (e.g., building a feature)             | *The ROI of mobile improvements is high—low effort with high user impact.*                      |
| **Cohort analysis**             | Noun Phrase    | Grouping users by a shared characteristic to track behavior over time                  | *Cohort analysis showed that mobile users have 20% higher churn than desktop users.*             |
| **Voice of the Customer (VOC)** | Noun Phrase    | Feedback, complaints, and requests collected from customers                            | *We use support tickets, surveys, and reviews as our VOC data.*                                  |
| **MVP (Minimum Viable Product)** | Acronym        | The simplest version of a product or feature that still delivers value                 | *We can start with an MVP of the Enterprise feature to validate the deal earlier.*               |
| **Time-to-value**               | Noun Phrase    | The time it takes for a user to realize the benefit of a feature                       | *Mobile improvements have a short time-to-value—users will see improvements immediately.*        |

## 4. Key Takeaways

### Common Sentence Patterns (for Product Roadmap Prioritization)

| Pattern                                                              | Use                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| *"I've built a feature prioritization framework across [X] dimensions."* | To introduce a structured approach.                                                    |
| *"Let me walk you through a scorecard I built for each option."*     | To present data in a clear, comparable format.                                         |
| *"If we use a weighted scoring model, [Option A] scores highest—because it has [reason]."* | To present the recommendation objectively, backed by the framework.                    |
| *"That's a fair point—let me add a [adjustment factor] to account for that."* | To acknowledge counterarguments and refine the model.                                  |
| *"We can actually achieve [all options] by [timeframe]—if we sequence correctly."* | To turn a "trade-off" into a "sequencing" problem.                                     |
| *"The story isn't [conflict narrative]. The story is [sequencing narrative]."* | To reframe the narrative (anchor pattern).                                             |
| *"Let's use this framework for all future prioritization decisions."* | To institutionalize the process and prevent future debates.                            |

### Useful Phrases to Remember
- **Introducing the framework:** *"We need a decision framework—otherwise we'll build features based on whoever speaks loudest."*
- **Balancing short-term revenue vs. long-term strategy:** *"The Enterprise feature is a short-term revenue win. Mobile improvements are a long-term retention play. We need both—just in the right order."*
- **When someone says "but this customer is important":** *"Every customer is important—that's why we need to prioritize based on data, not relationships."*
- **Handling "we've already promised it":** *"We can set expectations with the customer by offering a phased approach—a simple version first, enhancements later."*
- **Introducing "opportunity cost":** *"If we build Feature X, we can't build Feature Y. The opportunity cost is [quantified impact]. Are we okay with that?"*
- **When proposing an MVP:** *"Let's test the Enterprise feature as an MVP first—then we can iterate based on real customer feedback."*

### Practical Speaking Tips (for Product Roadmap Storytelling)
1.  **Create a "Feature Scorecard"**—a table with each feature and its scores across dimensions (Revenue, User Pain, Effort, Strategic Fit). This makes the decision transparent and objective.
2.  **Use a 2x2 "Impact vs. Effort" Matrix.** Place features in four quadrants: High Impact/Low Effort (do first), High Impact/High Effort (do second), Low Impact/Low Effort (do third), Low Impact/High Effort (don't do). This is intuitive for stakeholders.
3.  **Quantify "User Pain" with support ticket data.** Say: *"80% of our support tickets are about mobile—that's a clear signal of user pain."*
4.  **Always include an "Effort" estimate from Engineering.** Without it, you can't compare initiatives. Work with Engineering to get a rough estimate (e.g., "small," "medium," "large").
5.  **Use "Cohort Analysis" to identify high-churn segments.** If you see that mobile users have 20% higher churn, improving the mobile app has a clear retention benefit.
6.  **"Phased Approach" is your friend.** Don't frame it as "Feature A vs. Feature B." Frame it as "Feature A in Month 1, Feature B in Month 2, Feature C in Month 3." This turns conflict into sequencing.
7.  **Involve stakeholders early.** When you build the framework, share it with stakeholders and ask: *"Are these the right criteria? What weights would you assign?"* This builds buy-in before the decision is made.
8.  **Celebrate the "small wins."** If a low-effort, high-impact feature is chosen, celebrate the quick win. Say: *"We can deliver mobile improvements in 1 month—users will see the benefits immediately."*
9.  **Track "Feature Usage" after launch.** When you build a feature, track how many users adopt it. This validates your prioritization decisions and informs future planning.
10. **Use the "RICE" Framework (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort).** It's a popular prioritization framework in product management. You can adapt it to your context.
