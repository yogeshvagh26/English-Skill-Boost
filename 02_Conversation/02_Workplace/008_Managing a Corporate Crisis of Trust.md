# Topic: Managing a Corporate Crisis of Trust (CEO Misconduct Allegations)

**Scenario:** You are the Head of Corporate Communications at a global financial services firm. A major news outlet has published a detailed investigation alleging that your CEO engaged in financial impropriety and personal misconduct over the past several years. The stock is down 12% in pre‑market trading, employees are in shock, and clients are calling for reassurance. The board has convened an emergency meeting. You are now leading the crisis response team (Chair of the Board, General Counsel, Head of HR, and the Interim CEO) to: **manage** the internal and external communications, **coordinate** with the board on an independent investigation, **protect** employee morale and client confidence, **maintain** business continuity, and **begin** the process of rebuilding trust—all while navigating immense media scrutiny and legal exposure.

---

## Dialogue

| **Speaker** | **Dialogue / Text** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Board Chair** | *(opening the emergency call)* The situation is critical. The allegations are serious, and we need to act decisively. I’ve accepted the CEO’s resignation effective immediately. We need to communicate this and demonstrate that we are taking this with the utmost seriousness. |
| **You** | I've drafted a holding statement for the press: *"We have been made aware of serious allegations. The board has accepted the CEO's resignation and has appointed an interim CEO. We have launched a comprehensive, independent investigation. We remain committed to transparency, integrity, and the long-term interests of our clients and shareholders."* |
| **General Counsel** | We need to be very careful about the wording—we cannot admit liability, but we also cannot appear to be covering anything up. I’ll work with you to finalize the legal language. We are engaging a top external law firm to conduct an independent investigation. |
| **Interim CEO** | What about our employees? They are panicking. |
| **You** | I recommend we hold an internal town hall today. You should address them directly—acknowledge the news, reassure them that the organization is resilient, and emphasize our commitment to a fair and transparent process. We’ll also send an internal email inviting questions. |
| **Head of HR** | We also need to prepare managers to handle team questions. I’ll distribute a manager toolkit with talking points and FAQs. We should also reinforce our employee assistance program (EAP). |
| **Board Chair** | And what about our clients and regulators? |
| **You** | We will proactively reach out to our top 20 clients today to personally assure them of continuity. We’ll also file the necessary regulatory notifications immediately. We need to control the narrative with facts, not speculation. |
| **Interim CEO** | We also need a succession plan to stabilize the leadership. I’ll prepare a message for the board on my vision for the next 90 days. |
| **You** | We'll communicate the leadership transition clearly and affirm that the business strategy remains unchanged. I’ll also prepare a script for investor calls and a media statement for the evening news cycles. |
| **You** | *(closing)* We are in the crucible of a crisis, but our response will define our future. Let’s lead with transparency, accountability, and compassion. We’ll have another update call in 4 hours. |
| *(48 hours later – follow-up)* | |
| **You** | *(to the crisis team)* The independent investigation is underway. Our statement has been well-received by the market—the stock is recovering. The town hall was effective; employee sentiment is holding. |
| **General Counsel** | We've received positive feedback from regulators for our proactive notification. |
| **You** | This is a long road to rebuilding trust, but we have made the right moves to restore stability and confidence. Now we focus on the investigation and long-term governance reforms. |

## Vocabulary

| **Word / Phrase** | **Part of Speech** | **Meaning** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Crisis of trust** | Noun Phrase | A situation where confidence in leadership is severely damaged. |
| **Misconduct allegations** | Noun Phrase | Claims of improper or unethical behavior. |
| **Pre‑market trading** | Noun Phrase | Trading activity that occurs before the stock market officially opens. |
| **Media scrutiny** | Noun Phrase | Intense examination and criticism by the media. |
| **Legal exposure** | Noun Phrase | The risk of lawsuits or legal penalties. |
| **Independent investigation** | Noun Phrase | An impartial inquiry conducted by an external party. |
| **Holding statement** | Noun Phrase | A preliminary public communication before full details are available. |
| **Interim CEO** | Noun Phrase | A temporary leader appointed to fill a vacancy. |
| **Employee assistance program (EAP)** | Noun | A confidential counseling and support service for employees. |
| **Continuity** | Noun | The maintenance of consistent operations. |
| **Manager toolkit** | Noun Phrase | A set of resources to help managers handle discussions. |
| **Succession plan** | Noun Phrase | A strategy for transitioning leadership roles. |
| **Regulatory notifications** | Noun Phrase | Formal submissions required by government agencies. |
| **Crucible** | Noun | *(figurative)* A severe test or trial. |
| **Transparency** | Noun | Openness and honesty about actions. |
| **Accountability** | Noun | The willingness to take responsibility for outcomes. |
| **Compassion** | Noun | Sympathetic concern for others' suffering. |
| **Governance reforms** | Noun Phrase | Changes to the system of rules and practices for leadership. |
| **Erosion of trust** | Noun Phrase | The gradual weakening of confidence in an entity. |
| **Resilient** | Adjective | Able to withstand or recover quickly from difficulties. |
