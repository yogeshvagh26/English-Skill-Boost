## 1. Situation : Customer Health Score – The "Silent Churn" Warning

**Context:** You are a Senior Data Analyst at a B2B SaaS company. The Head of Customer Success (Maya) is concerned—her team's "customer health score" has dropped from 75 to 62 over the last quarter. She's not sure why, but she's worried about churn. The Head of Sales (Robert) dismisses it—he says the health score is "just a number" and that revenue is still growing. The CEO (Sarah) wants to understand if this is a leading indicator of churn. You've analyzed the health score components and found that the drop is driven by declining engagement among a specific segment. You need to present your findings and recommend a proactive retention strategy.

## 4. Key Takeaways

### Common Sentence Patterns (for Health Score Storytelling)

| Pattern                                                              | Use                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| *"I've analyzed the health score drop across three dimensions."*     | To introduce a structured analysis.                                                    |
| *"First, the health score components—it's driven by [component]."*   | To explain what the score measures and which part is failing.                          |
| *"Second, which segments are driving the drop—[Segment] is dropping."* | To localize the problem.                                                               |
| *"Third, the correlation with churn—it's a leading indicator."*      | To prove the metric's predictive power.                                                |
| *"The health score is a leading indicator—with [X]% accuracy."*      | To quantify its reliability.                                                           |
| *"We can see the warning signs [X] days before churn."*              | To emphasize the actionability of the metric.                                          |
| *"My recommendation is a [X]-part proactive retention strategy."*    | To provide a clear, actionable plan.                                                   |
| *"The story isn't [dismissive claim]. The story is [actionable insight]."* | To reframe the narrative (anchor pattern).                                             |

### Useful Phrases to Remember
- **Introducing the health score:** *"The health score is a composite metric that tells us, in real-time, which customers are thriving and which are at risk."*
- **When someone dismisses the score:** *"It's not just a number—it's a prediction. And it's 90% accurate."*
- **Introducing the "threshold alert" concept:** *"When a customer's health score drops below 50, we automatically trigger a "save plan." This isn't optional—it's a process."*
- **When proposing segment-specific retention:** *"The drop is in SMB—so our retention strategy needs to be tailored to SMB behavior."*
- **When asking about the CSM role:** *"CSMs should own the "save plan"—they know the customer and can take immediate action."*
- **When the strategy works:** *"We saved 200 SMB customers last quarter—$200k in retained revenue. The health score system is paying for itself."*

### Practical Speaking Tips (for Health Score Narratives)
1.  **Explain what the health score actually measures.** Don't assume everyone knows the components—walk them through it.
2.  **Show the correlation with churn.** Use data to prove that the health score predicts churn—this builds buy-in.
3.  **Segment the score.** Is the drop universal or segment-specific? Segmenting reveals where to focus.
4.  **Set clear threshold alerts.** Define what score triggers an intervention—and what the intervention process is.
5.  **Automate the alerts.** Manual monitoring doesn't work—automated alerts ensure no at-risk customer slips through.
6.  **Involve CSMs in the process.** CSMs are your front line—give them the tools and authority to act.
7.  **Track recovery.** When a health score improves, track it—it's a sign that the intervention worked.
8.  **Model the ROI of retention.** Show how much revenue you save by preventing churn—this justifies the investment.
9.  **Celebrate saves.** When you save a customer, celebrate it—it reinforces the value of the health score system.
10. **Iterate and improve.** Review the health score model quarterly—adjust components and thresholds based on real-world performance.
