# Topic: Managing a Corporate Crisis of Confidence in Financial Reporting

**Scenario:** You are the Chief Financial Officer (CFO) at a publicly traded company. An external auditor has raised concerns about irregularities in the company's revenue recognition practices, suggesting that some revenue was booked prematurely. The audit committee has been notified, and the stock has dropped 18% in after‑hours trading. The board has asked you to lead the crisis response. You need to convene an emergency meeting with the CEO, General Counsel, Head of Investor Relations, and the Audit Committee Chair to: **investigate** the facts, **communicate** with the board and regulators, **prepare** a public statement, **manage** investor and media questions, **address** employee concerns, and **initiate** corrective actions—all while maintaining confidence in the company's financial integrity.

---

## Dialogue

| **Speaker** | **Dialogue / Text** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Audit Committee Chair** | *(opening the emergency call)* We have a serious issue. The auditor has flagged irregularities in how we've recognized revenue on three large contracts. They believe revenue was booked prematurely. We need to understand the full extent of this and act quickly. |
| **You** | I've already initiated an internal review. The irregularities appear to be isolated to a small number of contracts, but we need to confirm that. I'm working with the internal audit team to conduct a thorough investigation. |
| **General Counsel** | We need to notify the SEC and the stock exchange. We'll also need to prepare for potential shareholder lawsuits. We should engage external legal counsel immediately. |
| **CEO** | What about our public statement? We need to address this before the market opens tomorrow. |
| **You** | I suggest a holding statement: *"We have been made aware of concerns regarding revenue recognition on certain contracts. We are conducting a thorough investigation and will provide an update when we have more information. We are committed to transparency and accuracy in our financial reporting."* |
| **Head of Investor Relations** | What about our investors? We're going to get calls at 5 AM. |
| **You** | We'll prepare a Q&A document for investor calls. Key messages: **#1**—We're taking this seriously. **#2**—We're conducting a thorough investigation. **#3**—We're committed to full transparency and will provide a timely update. |
| **CEO** | And what about our employees? They're going to be worried. |
| **You** | We'll send an internal email to all employees: *"We have been made aware of a concern related to financial reporting. We are investigating and will keep you informed. Our commitment to integrity remains unwavering."* |
| **Audit Committee Chair** | What about the restatement? Will we need to restate earnings? |
| **You** | It's too early to say. We'll know more once the investigation is complete. I'll update you within 48 hours. |
| **You** | *(assigning tasks)* **Legal**—engage external counsel and prepare for regulatory notifications. **Investor Relations**—prepare Q&A and schedule investor calls. **Internal Audit**—accelerate the investigation. **CEO**—approve the public statement. I'll coordinate the overall response. |
| **You** | *(closing)* This is a serious moment, but we've faced challenges before. Our response must be transparent, decisive, and focused on integrity. We'll reconvene in 6 hours. |
| *(48 hours later – follow-up)* | |
| **You** | *(to the leadership team)* The investigation is complete. We've identified the errors—they were the result of miscommunication between sales and finance, not fraud. We're preparing a restatement of earnings for the affected periods. |
| **General Counsel** | We've filed the necessary notifications with the SEC. |
| **Head of Investor Relations** | Investor calls have gone well—our transparency is being appreciated. |
| **CEO** | What about the corrective actions? |
| **You** | We've already implemented new controls: **#1**—Enhanced review of revenue contracts. **#2**—Additional training for sales and finance teams. **#3**—A new escalation process for revenue recognition issues. |
| **Audit Committee Chair** | I'm satisfied with the response. Let's ensure we emerge from this stronger. |
| **You** | We will. This has been a wake‑up call, but we've addressed it with integrity and transparency. |

## Vocabulary

| **Word / Phrase** | **Part of Speech** | **Meaning** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Revenue recognition** | Noun Phrase | The process of recording revenue in financial statements. |
| **Premature revenue booking** | Noun Phrase | Recording revenue before it is earned. |
| **External auditor** | Noun Phrase | An independent firm that reviews financial statements. |
| **Restatement** | Noun | Correcting previously issued financial statements. |
| **Internal review** | Noun Phrase | An investigation conducted by the company's own team. |
| **SEC** | Noun | Securities and Exchange Commission (US regulator). |
| **Shareholder lawsuits** | Noun Phrase | Legal action taken by investors. |
| **Holding statement** | Noun Phrase | A preliminary public communication. |
| **Q&A document** | Noun Phrase | A prepared list of questions and answers. |
| **Investor calls** | Noun Phrase | Calls with shareholders to discuss company performance. |
| **Transparency** | Noun | Openness and honesty about actions. |
| **Integrity** | Noun | The quality of being honest and having strong moral principles. |
| **Escalation process** | Noun Phrase | A procedure for raising issues to a higher authority. |
| **Enhanced controls** | Noun Phrase | Strengthened procedures to prevent errors. |
| **Investigative findings** | Noun Phrase | The results of a formal inquiry. |
| **Corrective actions** | Noun Phrase | Steps taken to fix a problem and prevent recurrence. |
| **Stakeholders** | Noun | All parties with an interest in the organization. |
| **Regulatory notification** | Noun Phrase | Informing government agencies of a significant issue. |
| **Fraud** | Noun | Intentional deception for financial gain. |
| **Miscommunication** | Noun | A failure to convey information clearly. |
