# Phase 12: Voice

Welcome to Phase 12, the final phase of your grammar journey! You've mastered **verbs**, **nouns**, **pronouns**, **adjectives**, **adverbs**, **articles & determiners**, **prepositions**, **conjunctions**, and **modals**. Now it's time to explore **voice** — the relationship between the subject and the action in a sentence.

Think of it this way: *Voice tells you who is doing the action and who is receiving it. In **active voice**, the subject DOES the action. In **passive voice**, the subject RECEIVES the action. "The cat ate the fish" (active) vs. "The fish was eaten by the cat" (passive). Understanding voice gives you more flexibility and precision in your writing.*

## 1. Active Voice

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** In **active voice**, the subject of the sentence performs the action expressed by the verb. The subject is the doer of the action.

**Key Points:**
- The subject performs the action
- The object receives the action
- Usually more direct and stronger
- More common in everyday English

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure: Active Voice**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [OBJECT]
The cat + ate + the fish.
She + wrote + a letter.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- "The dog **chased** the ball." (The dog did the chasing)
- "She **wrote** a letter." (She did the writing)
- "He **built** a house." (He did the building)
- "They **ate** the pizza." (They did the eating)
- "The teacher **explained** the lesson." (The teacher did the explaining)

**Active Voice in Different Tenses:**

| Tense | Example |
|-------|---------|
| Simple Present | She **writes** a letter. |
| Present Continuous | She **is writing** a letter. |
| Present Perfect | She **has written** a letter. |
| Simple Past | She **wrote** a letter. |
| Past Continuous | She **was writing** a letter. |
| Past Perfect | She **had written** a letter. |
| Simple Future | She **will write** a letter. |
| Future Perfect | She **will have written** a letter. |
| Modals | She **can write** a letter. |

**Advantages of Active Voice:**
- Clearer and more direct
- More concise
- More engaging and dynamic
- Often shorter than passive

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Overusing passive voice | Use active voice when possible |
| Using active when passive is needed | Use passive when the agent is unknown |

## 2. Passive Voice

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** In **passive voice**, the subject receives the action expressed by the verb. The object of the active sentence becomes the subject of the passive sentence.

**Key Points:**
- The subject receives the action
- The doer may be unknown or unimportant
- Formed with "be" + past participle (V3)
- Usually less direct than active

### 📐 Study: How to Form Passive Voice

**Step 1:** Identify the object of the active sentence.
**Step 2:** Move the object to the subject position.
**Step 3:** Add the appropriate form of "be" (according to the tense).
**Step 4:** Use the past participle (V3) of the main verb.
**Step 5:** Add "by" + the original subject (optional).

**Formula:**
```
Active:  [SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [OBJECT]
Passive: [OBJECT] + [BE + V3] + [BY + SUBJECT]

Active:   The cat   + ate     + the fish.
Passive:  The fish  + was eaten + by the cat.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Active to Passive Transformation:**

| Active | Passive |
|--------|---------|
| She writes a letter. | A letter **is written** (by her). |
| She wrote a letter. | A letter **was written** (by her). |
| She has written a letter. | A letter **has been written** (by her). |
| She is writing a letter. | A letter **is being written** (by her). |
| She will write a letter. | A letter **will be written** (by her). |
| She can write a letter. | A letter **can be written** (by her). |

**When to Use Passive Voice:**

| Situation | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| **The agent is unknown** | My car **was stolen**. (I don't know who) |
| **The agent is unimportant** | The house **was built** in 2020. (Who built it doesn't matter) |
| **The object is more important** | The president **was elected** yesterday. (The president is the focus) |
| **Formal/Scientific writing** | The experiment **was conducted** in a lab. |
| **To avoid responsibility** | Mistakes **were made**. (Not: I made mistakes) |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| The fish was ate. | The fish **was eaten**. |
| The letter was wrote. | The letter **was written**. |
| The car is steal. | The car **is stolen**. |
| The cake was make. | The cake **was made**. |
| The book is being read. (✓) | The book **is being read**. |

## 3. Passive of Every Tense

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** Every tense has a passive form. The "be" verb changes according to the tense, and the main verb is always in the past participle (V3).

### 📐 Study: Passive Voice Tense Chart

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|-------|--------|---------|
| **Simple Present** | She **writes** a letter. | A letter **is written** (by her). |
| **Present Continuous** | She **is writing** a letter. | A letter **is being written** (by her). |
| **Present Perfect** | She **has written** a letter. | A letter **has been written** (by her). |
| **Present Perfect Continuous** | She **has been writing** a letter. | (Not common in passive) |
| **Simple Past** | She **wrote** a letter. | A letter **was written** (by her). |
| **Past Continuous** | She **was writing** a letter. | A letter **was being written** (by her). |
| **Past Perfect** | She **had written** a letter. | A letter **had been written** (by her). |
| **Past Perfect Continuous** | She **had been writing** a letter. | (Not common in passive) |
| **Simple Future** | She **will write** a letter. | A letter **will be written** (by her). |
| **Future Continuous** | She **will be writing** a letter. | A letter **will be being written** (by her). (Rare) |
| **Future Perfect** | She **will have written** a letter. | A letter **will have been written** (by her). |
| **Modal** | She **can write** a letter. | A letter **can be written** (by her). |

### 👀 See: Examples for Each Tense

**Simple Present:**
- Active: "She **writes** a letter."
- Passive: "A letter **is written** (by her)."
- Active: "They **eat** the cake."
- Passive: "The cake **is eaten** (by them)."

**Present Continuous:**
- Active: "She **is writing** a letter."
- Passive: "A letter **is being written** (by her)."
- Active: "They **are eating** the cake."
- Passive: "The cake **is being eaten** (by them)."

**Present Perfect:**
- Active: "She **has written** a letter."
- Passive: "A letter **has been written** (by her)."
- Active: "They **have eaten** the cake."
- Passive: "The cake **has been eaten** (by them)."

**Simple Past:**
- Active: "She **wrote** a letter."
- Passive: "A letter **was written** (by her)."
- Active: "They **ate** the cake."
- Passive: "The cake **was eaten** (by them)."

**Past Continuous:**
- Active: "She **was writing** a letter."
- Passive: "A letter **was being written** (by her)."
- Active: "They **were eating** the cake."
- Passive: "The cake **was being eaten** (by them)."

**Past Perfect:**
- Active: "She **had written** a letter."
- Passive: "A letter **had been written** (by her)."
- Active: "They **had eaten** the cake."
- Passive: "The cake **had been eaten** (by them)."

**Simple Future:**
- Active: "She **will write** a letter."
- Passive: "A letter **will be written** (by her)."
- Active: "They **will eat** the cake."
- Passive: "The cake **will be eaten** (by them)."

**Future Perfect:**
- Active: "She **will have written** a letter."
- Passive: "A letter **will have been written** (by her)."
- Active: "They **will have eaten** the cake."
- Passive: "The cake **will have been eaten** (by them)."

**Modal Verbs:**
- Active: "She **can write** a letter."
- Passive: "A letter **can be written** (by her)."
- Active: "She **must write** a letter."
- Passive: "A letter **must be written** (by her)."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| The letter is wrote. | The letter **is written**. |
| The letter was being wrote. | The letter **was being written**. |
| The letter has been wrote. | The letter **has been written**. |
| The letter will be wrote. | The letter **will be written**. |
| The letter can be wrote. | The letter **can be written**. |

## 4. Special Passive Structures

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** Some special structures are used in passive voice for different purposes, including the passive with "get," the passive with "have/get something done," and the passive with reporting verbs.

### 📐 Study: Special Passive Structures

**1. Passive with "Get"**

**Definition:** "Get" can replace "be" in informal passive voice. It often implies that something happened to the subject, sometimes unexpectedly.

**Structure:**
```
[SUBJECT] + [GET] + [PAST PARTICIPLE]
I + got + injured.
She + got + promoted.
```

**Examples:**
- "I **got** injured in the accident."
- "She **got** promoted to manager."
- "He **got** arrested by the police."
- "They **got** married last year."
- "My phone **got** stolen."

**Get vs. Be (Passive):**
- "I **was** promoted." (formal)
- "I **got** promoted." (informal, more natural)

---

**2. Have/Get Something Done**

**Definition:** This structure is used when someone arranges for someone else to do something, or when something happens to someone.

**Structure:**
```
[SUBJECT] + [HAVE/GET] + [OBJECT] + [PAST PARTICIPLE]
I + had + my hair + cut.
She + got + her car + repaired.
```

**Examples:**
- "I **had** my hair **cut**." (Someone else cut it)
- "She **got** her car **repaired**." (Someone else repaired it)
- "We **had** our house **painted**." (Someone else painted it)
- "He **got** his teeth **checked**." (Someone else checked them)
- "I **need to have** my computer **fixed**."

**Have vs. Get (Something Done):**
- "I **had** my hair cut." (formal)
- "I **got** my hair cut." (informal)

---

**3. Passive with Reporting Verbs**

**Definition:** With reporting verbs (say, think, believe, know, etc.), there are two passive forms.

**Structure 1: It is said that...**
```
[IT IS] + [PAST PARTICIPLE] + [THAT] + [CLAUSE]
It is said + that + she is a doctor.
```

**Structure 2: Subject + is said to + Infinitive**
```
[SUBJECT] + [IS SAID TO] + [INFINITIVE]
She + is said to + be a doctor.
```

**Examples:**

**With "Say":**
- Active: "People **say** she is a doctor."
- Passive 1: "**It is said** that she is a doctor."
- Passive 2: "She **is said to be** a doctor."

**With "Believe":**
- Active: "People **believe** he is honest."
- Passive 1: "**It is believed** that he is honest."
- Passive 2: "He **is believed to be** honest."

**With "Know":**
- Active: "People **know** he is a genius."
- Passive 1: "**It is known** that he is a genius."
- Passive 2: "He **is known to be** a genius."

**Other Reporting Verbs:**
- think, consider, report, claim, expect, suppose, understand, assume

**Tense Variations:**
- Present: "She **is said to be** a doctor." (active meaning)
- Past: "She **is said to have been** a doctor." (past meaning)
- Future: "She **is said to be going to** be a doctor." (future meaning)

**Examples:**
- "He **is said to have** left the country." (past)
- "They **are expected to arrive** soon." (future)
- "She **is believed to have been** a spy." (past)
- "The project **is supposed to be** finished." (expectation)

## 5. When to Use Active vs. Passive Voice

### 📚 Quick Reference

| Situation | Use | Example |
|-----------|-----|---------|
| **Most writing/speaking** | Active | She wrote the letter. |
| **The agent is unknown** | Passive | The letter was stolen. |
| **The agent is obvious** | Passive | The criminal was arrested. (by police) |
| **The object is more important** | Passive | The letter was written yesterday. |
| **Formal/academic writing** | Passive | The experiment was conducted. |
| **To avoid responsibility** | Passive | Mistakes were made. |
| **Instructions/recipes** | Passive | The mixture is heated. |

## 💬 Practice: Conversations

### Dialogue 1: Active Voice

- A: "What **did** you **do** yesterday?"
- B: "I **wrote** a letter to my friend."
- A: "**Did** you **send** it?"
- B: "Yes, I **sent** it this morning."
- A: "Who **helped** you?"
- B: "My sister **helped** me."

### Dialogue 2: Passive Voice

- A: "What happened to your car?"
- B: "It **was stolen** last night."
- A: "**Was** it **parked** on the street?"
- B: "Yes, it **was parked** on the street."
- A: "**Has** it **been found** yet?"
- B: "No, it **hasn't been found** yet."

### Dialogue 3: Passive with Get

- A: "What happened to you?"
- B: "I **got injured** in the accident."
- A: "**Were** you **taken** to the hospital?"
- B: "Yes, I **was taken** to the hospital."
- A: "**Did** you **get treated**?"
- B: "Yes, I **got treated** for my injuries."

### Dialogue 4: Have Something Done

- A: "You look different! Did you do something?"
- B: "I **had** my hair **cut**."
- A: "It looks great! Where **did** you **get** it **done**?"
- B: "I **got** it **done** at the new salon."
- A: "I need to **get** my hair **done** too."

### Dialogue 5: Reporting Verbs

- A: "**Is it true** that she is a doctor?"
- B: "Yes, she **is said to be** a doctor."
- A: "**Is it known** that he is a genius?"
- B: "Yes, he **is known to be** a genius."
- A: "**Is it believed** that they are rich?"
- B: "They **are believed to be** very wealthy."
