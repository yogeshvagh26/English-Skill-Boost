# Phase 5: Pronouns

Welcome to Phase 5! You've mastered **verbs** (types, forms, agreement, irregulars, gerunds, infinitives, participles) and **nouns** (countable, uncountable, proper, common, collective, material, abstract, compound, possessive, plurals). Now it's time to explore **pronouns** — the words that replace nouns to avoid repetition and make language flow more naturally.

Think of it this way: *Nouns name things. Pronouns replace nouns. Without pronouns, you'd have to repeat names constantly. "Sarah went to Sarah's car because Sarah forgot Sarah's keys." With pronouns: "Sarah went to **her** car because **she** forgot **her** keys."*

1. **Learn** the concept and rules

## Topic Overview: Types of Pronouns

| Type | Definition | Examples |
|------|------------|----------|
| **Personal** | Replace specific people or things | I, you, he, she, it, we, they |
| **Reflexive** | Reflect back to the subject | myself, yourself, himself, herself |
| **Reciprocal** | Show mutual action | each other, one another |
| **Relative** | Connect clauses | who, whom, which, that |
| **Demonstrative** | Point to specific things | this, that, these, those |
| **Possessive** | Show ownership | mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs |
| **Interrogative** | Ask questions | who, whom, which, what, whose |
| **Indefinite** | Refer to non-specific things | everyone, somebody, anything, all |
| **Distributive** | Refer to members of a group | each, either, neither |
| **Emphatic** | Emphasize a noun/pronoun | myself, yourself, himself (as emphasis) |

## 1. Personal Pronouns

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Personal pronouns** replace specific people or things. They change form based on person (first, second, third), number (singular, plural), and case (subject, object).

**Key Points:**
- Replace specific nouns (people or things)
- Change form based on person, number, and case
- Most common type of pronoun
- Essential for natural English

### 📐 Study: Personal Pronoun Chart

| Person | Subject | Object | Example (Subject) | Example (Object) |
|--------|---------|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| 1st Singular | I | me | **I** like coffee. | She likes **me**. |
| 2nd Singular | you | you | **You** are nice. | I like **you**. |
| 3rd Singular | he | him | **He** is tall. | I saw **him**. |
| 3rd Singular | she | her | **She** is kind. | I saw **her**. |
| 3rd Singular | it | it | **It** is raining. | I see **it**. |
| 1st Plural | we | us | **We** are happy. | They saw **us**. |
| 2nd Plural | you | you | **You** are all welcome. | I thank **you**. |
| 3rd Plural | they | them | **They** are coming. | I saw **them**. |

### 👀 See: Examples

**Subject Pronouns (Do the Action):**
- "**I** am a teacher."
- "**You** are my friend."
- "**He** works at a bank."
- "**She** sings beautifully."
- "**It** is raining."
- "**We** are going home."
- "**They** are playing outside."

**Object Pronouns (Receive the Action):**
- "She called **me**."
- "I told **you**."
- "I saw **him**."
- "I spoke to **her**."
- "I broke **it**."
- "They helped **us**."
- "I invited **them**."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Me and John are friends. | **John and I** are friends. |
| Her and I are going. | **She and I** are going. |
| I gave it to she. | I gave it to **her**. |
| Him is a doctor. | **He** is a doctor. |
| Between you and I | Between you and **me** |

**Subject vs. Object Pronoun Rules:**
- Use **subject pronouns** (I, you, he, she, it, we, they) as the SUBJECT of a sentence
- Use **object pronouns** (me, you, him, her, it, us, them) as the OBJECT of a sentence

**Personal Pronoun Order (Politeness):**
- When listing multiple people, put yourself last: "John, Sarah, and **I**" (NOT: I, John, and Sarah)
- When listing in object position: "The gift is for Sarah, John, and **me**"

## 2. Reflexive Pronouns

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Reflexive pronouns** refer back to the subject of the sentence. They are used when the subject and object are the same.

**Key Points:**
- Reflect back to the subject
- End in -self or -selves
- Used when subject and object are the same

### 📐 Study: Reflexive Pronoun Chart

| Person | Reflexive | Example |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| 1st Singular | myself | I hurt **myself**. |
| 2nd Singular | yourself | You hurt **yourself**. |
| 3rd Singular | himself | He hurt **himself**. |
| 3rd Singular | herself | She hurt **herself**. |
| 3rd Singular | itself | It hurt **itself**. |
| 1st Plural | ourselves | We hurt **ourselves**. |
| 2nd Plural | yourselves | You hurt **yourselves**. |
| 3rd Plural | themselves | They hurt **themselves**. |

### 👀 See: Examples

**Reflexive Pronouns in Action:**
- "I taught **myself** to play guitar."
- "She hurt **herself**."
- "We enjoyed **ourselves** at the party."
- "They blamed **themselves** for the mistake."
- "He cut **himself** while cooking."
- "The cat cleaned **itself**."

**Reflexive Pronouns with Common Verbs:**

| Verb | Example |
|------|---------|
| hurt | She hurt **herself**. |
| teach | I taught **myself**. |
| enjoy | We enjoyed **ourselves**. |
| blame | They blamed **themselves**. |
| cut | He cut **himself**. |
| make | She made **herself** a sandwich. |
| buy | I bought **myself** a gift. |
| take care of | He takes care of **himself**. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| He hurt hisself. | He hurt **himself**. |
| They hurt theirselves. | They hurt **themselves**. |
| I hurt me. | I hurt **myself**. |
| She made herself a cake. (✓) | She made **herself** a cake. |
| I did it myself. (✓) | I did it **myself**. |

## 3. Reciprocal Pronouns

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Reciprocal pronouns** show mutual actions or relationships between two or more people. They express that the action is shared.

**Key Points:**
- Show mutual action
- Only two: "each other" and "one another"
- "Each other" for two people; "one another" for more than two

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Each Other (Two People)**
```
[SUBJECT 1] + [VERB] + [EACH OTHER]
John and Mary + love + each other.
They + help + each other.
```

**Structure 2: One Another (More Than Two)**
```
[SUBJECT 1] + [VERB] + [ONE ANOTHER]
The students + help + one another.
The team + supports + one another.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Each Other Examples:**
- "John and Mary love **each other**."
- "The two friends helped **each other**."
- "They looked at **each other**."
- "My brother and I trust **each other**."
- "The couple supported **each other**."

**One Another Examples:**
- "The students help **one another**."
- "The team members support **one another**."
- "The colleagues respect **one another**."
- "The neighbors look after **one another**."

**Each Other vs. One Another:**

| Pronoun | Number | Example |
|---------|--------|---------|
| each other | Two people | John and Mary love **each other**. |
| one another | More than two | The students help **one another**. |

**Note:** In modern English, "each other" and "one another" are often used interchangeably.

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| They love each others. | They love **each other**. |
| John and Mary love one another. (OK) | John and Mary love **each other**. |
| The students helped each other. (OK) | The students helped **one another**. |

## 4. Relative Pronouns

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Relative pronouns** introduce relative clauses and connect them to the main clause. They refer to a noun mentioned earlier (the antecedent).

**Key Points:**
- Introduce relative clauses
- Refer to an antecedent
- Connect clauses together

### 📐 Study: Relative Pronoun Chart

| Pronoun | Use | Example |
|---------|-----|---------|
| **who** | People (subject) | The man **who** called is my uncle. |
| **whom** | People (object) | The woman **whom** I saw is my aunt. |
| **which** | Things/animals | The book **which** I read is great. |
| **that** | People or things | The book **that** I read is great. |
| **whose** | Possession | The man **whose** car was stolen is angry. |

### 👀 See: Examples

**Who (Subject for People):**
- "The man **who** lives next door is a doctor."
- "She is the woman **who** won the award."
- "The teacher **who** taught me was kind."

**Whom (Object for People):**
- "The woman **whom** I met is a lawyer."
- "The man **whom** you called is here."
- "The student **whom** the teacher praised passed the exam."

**Which (Things/Animals):**
- "The book **which** I read is excellent."
- "The car **which** he bought is red."
- "The movie **which** we watched was exciting."

**That (People or Things):**
- "The book **that** I read is excellent."
- "The man **that** I met is a doctor."
- "The car **that** he bought is red."

**Whose (Possession):**
- "The man **whose** car was stolen is angry."
- "The woman **whose** son won the award is proud."
- "The company **whose** product was recalled is facing problems."

**Relative Pronoun Usage:**

| Pronoun | Antecedent | Function |
|---------|------------|----------|
| who | people | subject |
| whom | people | object |
| which | things/animals | subject/object |
| that | people or things | subject/object |
| whose | people or things | possession |

**Who vs. Whom:**
- Use **who** as the subject: "Who called you?"
- Use **whom** as the object: "Whom did you call?"
- In informal English, "who" is often used for both.

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| The man which lives here. | The man **who** lives here. |
| The book who I read. | The book **which/that** I read. |
| The man that I saw. (✓) | The man **who(m)** I saw. |
| The car which is red. (✓) | The car **that/which** is red. |
| I don't know who you are. (✓) | I don't know **who** you are. |

## 5. Demonstrative Pronouns

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Demonstrative pronouns** point to specific things or people. They indicate distance (near or far) and number (singular or plural).

**Key Points:**
- Point to specific things
- Show distance (near/far)
- Show number (singular/plural)

### 📐 Study: Demonstrative Pronoun Chart

| Pronoun | Number | Distance | Example |
|---------|--------|----------|---------|
| **this** | Singular | Near | **This** is my book. |
| **these** | Plural | Near | **These** are my books. |
| **that** | Singular | Far | **That** is your book. |
| **those** | Plural | Far | **Those** are your books. |

### 👀 See: Examples

**This (Singular, Near):**
- "**This** is delicious!"
- "**This** is what I wanted."
- "**This** is my favorite movie."

**These (Plural, Near):**
- "**These** are the best cookies."
- "**These** are the ones I want."
- "**These** are my friends."

**That (Singular, Far):**
- "**That** is a beautiful house."
- "**That** is the man I told you about."
- "**That** was a great idea."

**Those (Plural, Far):**
- "**Those** are my shoes."
- "**Those** are the books I need."
- "**Those** are the people I saw."

**Demonstrative Pronouns vs. Demonstrative Adjectives:**

| Pronoun (Replaces Noun) | Adjective (Modifies Noun) |
|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| **This** is my book. | **This** book is mine. |
| **That** is his car. | **That** car is his. |
| **These** are my keys. | **These** keys are mine. |
| **Those** are his shoes. | **Those** shoes are his. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| These is my book. | **This** is my book. |
| This are my books. | **These** are my books. |
| That are my shoes. | **Those** are my shoes. |
| Those is my car. | **That** is my car. |

## 6. Possessive Pronouns

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Possessive pronouns** show ownership or possession. They replace a possessive adjective + noun.

**Key Points:**
- Show ownership
- Stand alone (replace noun)
- Different from possessive adjectives (which modify nouns)

### 📐 Study: Possessive Pronoun Chart

| Subject | Possessive Adjective | Possessive Pronoun |
|---------|----------------------|-------------------|
| I | my | **mine** |
| you | your | **yours** |
| he | his | **his** |
| she | her | **hers** |
| it | its | **its** (rare) |
| we | our | **ours** |
| they | their | **theirs** |

### 👀 See: Examples

**Possessive Pronouns in Action:**
- "This book is **mine**." (NOT: This book is my.)
- "Is this pen **yours**?"
- "The decision was **his**."
- "The car is **hers**."
- "This house is **ours**."
- "The responsibility is **theirs**."

**Possessive Adjectives vs. Possessive Pronouns:**

| Possessive Adjective + Noun | Possessive Pronoun |
|------------------------------|-------------------|
| This is **my** book. | This book is **mine**. |
| Is this **your** pen? | Is this pen **yours**? |
| That is **his** car. | That car is **his**. |
| This is **her** house. | This house is **hers**. |
| That is **our** project. | That project is **ours**. |
| Those are **their** shoes. | Those shoes are **theirs**. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| This book is my. | This book is **mine**. |
| Is this yours? (✓) | Is this **yours**? |
| That car is her. | That car is **hers**. |
| This house is our. | This house is **ours**. |
| Those shoes are their. | Those shoes are **theirs**. |

**Important:** "Its" as a possessive pronoun is very rare. We usually use "its" as a possessive adjective: "The cat licked **its** paw."

## 7. Interrogative Pronouns

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Interrogative pronouns** are used to ask questions. They replace the noun being asked about.

**Key Points:**
- Used in questions
- Replace the unknown noun
- Help form questions

### 📐 Study: Interrogative Pronoun Chart

| Pronoun | Use | Example |
|---------|-----|---------|
| **who** | People (subject) | **Who** called you? |
| **whom** | People (object) | **Whom** did you call? |
| **which** | Choice | **Which** is yours? |
| **what** | Things/ideas | **What** is that? |
| **whose** | Possession | **Whose** is this? |

### 👀 See: Examples

**Who (Subject for People):**
- "**Who** called you?"
- "**Who** is that man?"
- "**Who** wants to go?"
- "**Who** did it?"

**Whom (Object for People):**
- "**Whom** did you call?"
- "**Whom** are you waiting for?"
- "To **whom** should I speak?"

**Which (Choice):**
- "**Which** is yours?"
- "**Which** do you prefer?"
- "**Which** of these is correct?"

**What (Things/General):**
- "**What** is that?"
- "**What** do you want?"
- "**What** happened?"

**Whose (Possession):**
- "**Whose** is this?"
- "**Whose** are these shoes?"
- "**Whose** is the car?"

**Interrogative Pronouns vs. Interrogative Adjectives:**

| Pronoun (Replaces Noun) | Adjective (Modifies Noun) |
|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| **Who** is that? | **Whose** book is this? |
| **What** is that? | **What** time is it? |
| **Which** is yours? | **Which** book is yours? |
| **Whose** is this? | **Whose** car is this? |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Whom called you? | **Who** called you? |
| Who did you see? (informal) | **Whom** did you see? (formal) |
| Which you want? | **What** do you want? |
| Whose book is this? (✓) | **Whose** book is this? |

## 8. Indefinite Pronouns

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Indefinite pronouns** refer to non-specific people, things, or amounts. They don't point to a particular person or thing.

**Key Points:**
- Refer to general, non-specific things
- Many are always singular
- Some can be singular or plural

### 📐 Study: Indefinite Pronoun Chart

**Always Singular:**

| Pronoun | Example |
|---------|---------|
| everyone | **Everyone** is here. |
| everybody | **Everybody** enjoys the party. |
| everything | **Everything** is ready. |
| someone | **Someone** is at the door. |
| somebody | **Somebody** called. |
| something | **Something** smells good. |
| anyone | **Anyone** is welcome. |
| anybody | **Anybody** can do it. |
| anything | **Anything** is possible. |
| no one | **No one** knows. |
| nobody | **Nobody** likes waiting. |
| nothing | **Nothing** matters more. |
| each | **Each** has a different opinion. |
| either | **Either** is acceptable. |
| neither | **Neither** works correctly. |

**Always Plural:**

| Pronoun | Example |
|---------|---------|
| both | **Both** are correct. |
| few | **Few** know the truth. |
| many | **Many** have tried. |
| several | **Several** are available. |

**Singular or Plural:**

| Pronoun | Singular | Plural |
|---------|----------|--------|
| all | **All** is lost. | **All** are coming. |
| some | **Some** is left. | **Some** are here. |
| none | **None** is perfect. | **None** are perfect. |
| any | **Any** is fine. | **Any** are welcome. |

### 👀 See: Examples

**Singular Indefinite Pronouns:**
- "**Everyone** is here."
- "**Somebody** called you."
- "**Everything** is ready."
- "**No one** knows the answer."
- "**Each** has a different opinion."

**Plural Indefinite Pronouns:**
- "**Both** are correct."
- "**Many** have tried."
- "**Several** are available."
- "**Few** know the truth."

**Singular or Plural:**
- "**All** is lost." (one thing)
- "**All** are coming." (many people)
- "**Some** is left." (uncountable)
- "**Some** are here." (countable)

**Indefinite Pronoun Agreement:**
- Singular pronouns → singular verb: "Everyone **is** here."
- Plural pronouns → plural verb: "Both **are** correct."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Everyone are here. | Everyone **is** here. |
| Somebody are calling. | Somebody **is** calling. |
| Everybody have a book. | Everybody **has** a book. |
| Both is correct. | Both **are** correct. |
| No one are coming. | No one **is** coming. |

**A Common Confusion: "Everyone" vs. "Every one"**

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| everyone | all people | **Everyone** is here. |
| every one | each one | **Every one** of the apples is fresh. |

## 9. Distributive Pronouns

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Distributive pronouns** refer to members of a group individually, rather than as a whole. They distribute meaning among group members.

**Key Points:**
- Consider group members individually
- Always singular
- Take singular verbs

### 📐 Study: Distributive Pronoun Chart

| Pronoun | Use | Example |
|---------|-----|---------|
| **each** | Every individual | **Each** has a book. |
| **either** | One or the other | **Either** is acceptable. |
| **neither** | Not one nor the other | **Neither** works. |

### 👀 See: Examples

**Each:**
- "**Each** of the students has a book."
- "**Each** is responsible for their work."
- "**Each** of the options is acceptable."
- "**Each** has a different opinion."
- "The teacher gave **each** a test."

**Either:**
- "**Either** of the options is acceptable."
- "**Either** is fine with me."
- "**Either** of the two will work."
- "You can choose **either**."

**Neither:**
- "**Neither** of the answers is correct."
- "**Neither** works in this situation."
- "**Neither** of the candidates is qualified."
- "I like **neither**."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Each of the students have a book. | Each of the students **has** a book. |
| Either of the options are fine. | Either of the options **is** fine. |
| Neither of the answers are correct. | Neither of the answers **is** correct. |
| Each are responsible. | Each **is** responsible. |

**Distributive vs. Indefinite Pronouns:**

| Distributive (Individual) | Indefinite (General) |
|---------------------------|---------------------|
| **Each** has a book. | **Everyone** has a book. |
| **Either** is acceptable. | **Someone** is here. |
| **Neither** is correct. | **Nobody** knows. |

## 10. Emphatic Pronouns

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Emphatic pronouns** are used to emphasize a noun or pronoun. They have the same form as reflexive pronouns but serve a different function.

**Key Points:**
- Same forms as reflexive pronouns
- Used for emphasis, not reflection
- Can be omitted without changing meaning

### 📐 Study: Emphatic Pronoun Chart

| Pronoun | Example |
|---------|---------|
| myself | I **myself** saw it. |
| yourself | You **yourself** said it. |
| himself | He **himself** did it. |
| herself | She **herself** called. |
| itself | The cat **itself** opened the door. |
| ourselves | We **ourselves** built it. |
| yourselves | You **yourselves** saw it. |
| themselves | They **themselves** organized it. |

### 👀 See: Examples

**Emphatic Pronouns in Action:**
- "I **myself** saw the accident."
- "She **herself** told me the news."
- "The president **himself** attended the meeting."
- "We **ourselves** built this house."
- "They **themselves** organized the event."
- "The cat **itself** opened the door."

**Emphatic Pronouns with "By" (Meaning "Alone"):**
- "I did it by **myself**." (alone)
- "She lives by **herself**." (alone)
- "They built it by **themselves**." (without help)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I myself saw it. (✓) | I **myself** saw it. |
| She herself said it. (✓) | She **herself** said it. |
| They did it theirselves. | They did it **themselves**. |

**Reflexive vs. Emphatic Pronouns:**

| Reflexive (Necessary) | Emphatic (Optional) |
|-----------------------|---------------------|
| She hurt **herself**. | She **herself** did it. |
| I taught **myself**. | I **myself** saw it. |
| He cut **himself**. | He **himself** called. |

**Test:** If you can remove the pronoun and the sentence still makes sense, it's emphatic. If the sentence becomes confusing, it's reflexive.

## 📚 Quick Reference Card

| Pronoun Type | Function | Examples |
|--------------|----------|----------|
| **Personal** | Replace specific people/things | I, you, he, she, it, we, they |
| **Reflexive** | Reflect back to subject | myself, yourself, himself, herself |
| **Reciprocal** | Show mutual action | each other, one another |
| **Relative** | Connect clauses | who, whom, which, that |
| **Demonstrative** | Point to specific things | this, that, these, those |
| **Possessive** | Show ownership | mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs |
| **Interrogative** | Ask questions | who, whom, which, what, whose |
| **Indefinite** | Refer to non-specific things | everyone, somebody, anything, all |
| **Distributive** | Refer to group members individually | each, either, neither |
| **Emphatic** | Emphasize a noun/pronoun | myself, yourself, himself (as emphasis) |
