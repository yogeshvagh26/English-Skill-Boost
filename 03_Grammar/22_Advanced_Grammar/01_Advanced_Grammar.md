## 1. Mood

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Mood** in grammar refers to the attitude of the verb toward the action or state expressed. There are three main moods: indicative, imperative, and subjunctive.

**Key Points:**
- **Indicative**: States facts or asks questions
- **Imperative**: Gives commands or requests
- **Subjunctive**: Expresses wishes, hypotheses, or conditions contrary to fact

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Indicative Mood**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [REST]
She + is + a doctor.
I + went + to the store.
```

**Structure 2: Imperative Mood**
```
[VERB] + [REST]
Sit + down.
Be + quiet.
```

**Structure 3: Subjunctive Mood (Present)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [BASE VERB]
I suggest that she + leave + now.
It is important that he + be + here.
```

**Structure 4: Subjunctive Mood (Past)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [WERE]
I wish I + were + a doctor.
If I + were + you, I would go.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Indicative Mood:**
- "She **is** a doctor." (fact)
- "I **went** to the store." (fact)
- "**Did** you **see** the movie?" (question)
- "They **work** hard." (fact)

**Imperative Mood:**
- "**Sit** down."
- "**Be** quiet."
- "**Please help** me."
- "**Don't** go."
- "**Let's** go."

**Subjunctive Mood:**
- "I suggest that she **leave** now."
- "It is important that he **be** here."
- "I wish I **were** a doctor."
- "If I **were** you, I would go."
- "She acts as if she **were** the boss."
- "The doctor recommended that he **rest**."
- "It is essential that she **be** prepared."
- "I demand that he **apologize**."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I suggest that she leaves. | I suggest that she **leave**. |
| It is important that he is here. | It is important that he **be** here. |
| I wish I was a doctor. | I wish I **were** a doctor. |
| If I was you, I would go. | If I **were** you, I would go. |
| I demand that he apologizes. | I demand that he **apologize**. |

## 2. Subjunctive Mood

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** The **subjunctive mood** is used to express wishes, hypotheses, conditions contrary to fact, and suggestions. It is most common in formal English.

**Key Points:**
- Expresses wishes, hypotheses, suggestions
- Uses base form of the verb (present subjunctive)
- Uses "were" for all subjects (past subjunctive)
- Common in formal writing

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Present Subjunctive (Suggestions/Requirements)**
```
[VERB OF RECOMMENDATION/REQUEST] + [THAT] + [SUBJECT] + [BASE VERB]
I suggest + that + she + leave.
The doctor recommended + that + he + rest.
```

**Structure 2: Present Subjunctive (Importance)**
```
[IT IS] + [ADJECTIVE] + [THAT] + [SUBJECT] + [BASE VERB]
It is important + that + he + be + here.
It is essential + that + she + arrive + on time.
```

**Structure 3: Past Subjunctive (Wishes/Hypotheticals)**
```
[I WISH] + [SUBJECT] + [WERE]
I wish + I + were + a doctor.
I wish + she + were + here.
```

**Structure 4: Past Subjunctive (Conditionals)**
```
[IF] + [SUBJECT] + [WERE]
If + I + were + rich, I would travel.
If + she + were + here, she would help.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Present Subjunctive (Recommendations):**
- "I suggest that she **leave** now." (Not: leaves)
- "The doctor recommended that he **rest**." (Not: rests)
- "I demand that he **apologize**." (Not: apologizes)
- "She proposed that we **meet** tomorrow." (Not: meet)
- "The teacher insisted that we **study** harder."

**Present Subjunctive (Importance):**
- "It is important that he **be** here." (Not: is)
- "It is essential that she **arrive** on time." (Not: arrives)
- "It is necessary that they **attend** the meeting." (Not: attend)
- "It is vital that everyone **understand** the rules."

**Past Subjunctive (Wishes):**
- "I wish I **were** a doctor." (Not: was)
- "She wishes she **were** taller." (Not: was)
- "I wish it **were** summer." (Not: was)
- "I wish I **had** more time." (different structure)

**Past Subjunctive (Conditionals):**
- "If I **were** you, I would go." (Not: was)
- "If she **were** here, she would help." (Not: was)
- "If I **were** rich, I would travel." (Not: was)
- "As if she **were** the boss." (Not: was)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I suggest that she leaves. | I suggest that she **leave**. |
| It is important that he is here. | It is important that he **be** here. |
| I wish I was a doctor. | I wish I **were** a doctor. |
| If I was you, I would go. | If I **were** you, I would go. |
| I demand that he apologizes. | I demand that he **apologize**. |

## 3. Nominalization

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Nominalization** is the process of turning verbs or adjectives into nouns. It is common in formal and academic writing.

**Key Points:**
- Turns verbs/adjectives into nouns
- Common in formal writing
- Can make writing more dense
- Often used in academic text

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Verb → Noun**
```
[VERB] → [NOUN FORM]
discuss → discussion
decide → decision
arrive → arrival
```

**Structure 2: Adjective → Noun**
```
[ADJECTIVE] → [NOUN FORM]
happy → happiness
honest → honesty
free → freedom
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Verb to Noun Nominalization:**

| Verb | Noun | Example |
|------|------|---------|
| discuss | discussion | We had a **discussion** about the project. |
| decide | decision | We made a **decision** to go. |
| arrive | arrival | Her **arrival** was unexpected. |
| analyze | analysis | The **analysis** was thorough. |
| investigate | investigation | The **investigation** is ongoing. |
| improve | improvement | We saw great **improvement**. |

**Adjective to Noun Nominalization:**

| Adjective | Noun | Example |
|-----------|------|---------|
| happy | happiness | **Happiness** is important. |
| honest | honesty | **Honesty** is the best policy. |
| free | freedom | We value **freedom**. |
| kind | kindness | Her **kindness** is appreciated. |
| brave | bravery | His **bravery** was rewarded. |

**Nominalization in Academic Writing:**

| Informal | Formal (Nominalized) |
|----------|---------------------|
| We decided to go. | We made a **decision** to go. |
| They analyzed the data. | They conducted an **analysis** of the data. |
| She arrived early. | Her **arrival** was early. |
| We improved the system. | We achieved an **improvement** of the system. |
| He investigated the case. | He conducted an **investigation** of the case. |

**Advantages and Disadvantages:**

| Advantage | Disadvantage |
|-----------|--------------|
| More formal | Can be wordier |
| More academic | Can be harder to read |
| More abstract | Less direct |
| Concise (sometimes) | Overuse can be cumbersome |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Overusing nominalization | Balance with active verbs |
| Choosing wrong noun form | Use correct nominalized form |
| Making text too dense | Keep it readable |

## 4. Reduced Relative Clauses

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Reduced relative clauses** are shortened versions of relative clauses. They make sentences more concise and elegant.

**Key Points:**
- Shortens relative clauses
- Removes "who," "which," "that"
- Removes "be" verb
- Common in formal and informal writing

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Reduced Relative Clause (Active)**
```
[FULL] + [RELATIVE PRONOUN] + [BE] + [VING] + [REST]
The man + who + is + sitting + over there is my uncle.

[REDUCED] + [VING] + [REST]
The man + sitting + over there is my uncle.
```

**Structure 2: Reduced Relative Clause (Passive)**
```
[FULL] + [RELATIVE PRONOUN] + [BE] + [V3]
The book + that + was + written + by her is famous.

[REDUCED] + [V3]
The book + written + by her is famous.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Active Reduced Relative Clauses:**

| Full | Reduced |
|------|---------|
| The man **who is sitting** over there is my uncle. | The man **sitting** over there is my uncle. |
| The woman **who is wearing** a red dress is my sister. | The woman **wearing** a red dress is my sister. |
| The girl **who is singing** is my cousin. | The girl **singing** is my cousin. |
| The students **who are studying** are in the library. | The students **studying** are in the library. |

**Passive Reduced Relative Clauses:**

| Full | Reduced |
|------|---------|
| The book **that was written** by her is famous. | The book **written** by her is famous. |
| The car **that was stolen** has been found. | The car **stolen** has been found. |
| The house **that was built** in 1950 is beautiful. | The house **built** in 1950 is beautiful. |
| The cake **that was made** by my mother is delicious. | The cake **made** by my mother is delicious. |

**When Reduction is NOT Possible:**
- "The man **who lives** next door." → Cannot reduce (no "be" verb)
  - The man **living** next door. (Not possible)
  - Instead: The man **who lives** next door.

**Reduction with "Having":**
- "The students **who have finished** the exam can leave." → "The students **having finished** the exam can leave."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| The man sitting there is my uncle. (✓) | The man **sitting** there is my uncle. |
| The man lives there is my uncle. | The man **who lives** there is my uncle. |
| The book written by her is famous. (✓) | The book **written** by her is famous. |
| The woman wearing a red dress is my sister. (✓) | The woman **wearing** a red dress is my sister. |

## 5. Causative Verbs

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Causative verbs** are used to indicate that someone causes something to happen. The most common causative verbs are "have," "get," "make," and "let."

**Key Points:**
- **Have**: Arrange for someone to do something
- **Get**: Persuade/arrange for someone to do something
- **Make**: Force someone to do something
- **Let**: Allow someone to do something

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Have + Object + Past Participle**
```
[SUBJECT] + [HAVE] + [OBJECT] + [V3]
I + had + my hair + cut.
She + had + her car + repaired.
```

**Structure 2: Get + Object + Past Participle**
```
[SUBJECT] + [GET] + [OBJECT] + [V3]
I + got + my hair + cut.
She + got + her car + repaired.
```

**Structure 3: Make + Object + Base Verb**
```
[SUBJECT] + [MAKE] + [OBJECT] + [V1]
She + made + me + cry.
He + made + her + go.
```

**Structure 4: Let + Object + Base Verb**
```
[SUBJECT] + [LET] + [OBJECT] + [V1]
She + let + me + go.
He + let + her + stay.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Have + Object + Past Participle (Arrange for someone to do something):**
- "I **had** my hair **cut**." (Someone else cut my hair)
- "She **had** her car **repaired**." (Someone else repaired it)
- "We **had** our house **painted**." (Someone else painted it)
- "He **had** his teeth **checked**." (Someone else checked them)

**Get + Object + Past Participle (Arrange for someone to do something):**
- "I **got** my hair **cut**." (More informal than "had")
- "She **got** her car **repaired**."
- "We **got** our house **painted**."
- "He **got** his teeth **checked**."

**Make + Object + Base Verb (Force someone to do something):**
- "She **made** me **cry**."
- "He **made** her **go**."
- "They **made** us **wait**."
- "The teacher **made** the students **study**."

**Let + Object + Base Verb (Allow someone to do something):**
- "She **let** me **go**."
- "He **let** her **stay**."
- "They **let** us **leave** early."
- "My mother **let** me **play** outside."

**Have/Get + Person + Infinitive:**
- "I **had** him **fix** my car." (I arranged for him to fix it)
- "She **got** him **to help** her." (She persuaded him)
- "I **had** my brother **carry** the bags."
- "She **got** her friend **to drive** her."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I had my hair cut. (✓) | I **had** my hair **cut**. |
| I made him to go. | I **made** him **go**. |
| She let me to go. | She **let** me **go**. |
| I got my hair cut. (✓) | I **got** my hair **cut**. |
| I had him to fix my car. | I **had** him **fix** my car. |

## 6. Existential "There"

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Existential "there"** is a dummy subject used to indicate that something exists or happens. It is used when the real subject comes after the verb.

**Key Points:**
- Indicates existence
- Dummy subject (no real meaning)
- Verb agrees with the real subject
- Common in all types of English

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: There + Be + Noun**
```
[THERE] + [BE] + [NOUN]
There + is + a book on the table.
There + are + books on the table.
```

**Structure 2: There + Be + Noun + Prepositional Phrase**
```
[THERE] + [BE] + [NOUN] + [PREPOSITIONAL PHRASE]
There + is + a book + on the table.
There + are + many students + in the classroom.
```

**Structure 3: There + Modal/Verb + Be + Noun**
```
[THERE] + [MODAL] + [BE] + [NOUN]
There + must + be + a solution.
There + might + be + a problem.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Existential "There" with "Be":**
- "**There is** a book on the table."
- "**There are** many students in the classroom."
- "**There was** a problem with the computer."
- "**There were** people waiting outside."
- "**There will be** a meeting tomorrow."
- "**There has been** a change in plans."
- "**There have been** many developments."

**Existential "There" with Modals:**
- "**There must be** a solution."
- "**There might be** a problem."
- "**There could be** a mistake."
- "**There should be** more time."
- "**There ought to be** a better way."

**Existential "There" with Verbs:**
- "**There seems to be** a misunderstanding."
- "**There appears to be** no hope."
- "**There remains** one question."
- "**There exists** a possibility."

**Negative Existential "There":**
- "**There isn't** any milk."
- "**There aren't** any students."
- "**There hasn't been** any news."
- "**There won't be** any problems."

**Questions with Existential "There":**
- "**Is there** a book on the table?"
- "**Are there** any students in the classroom?"
- "**Was there** a problem?"
- "**Will there be** a meeting?"

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| There is many students. | There **are** many students. |
| There are a book on the table. | There **is** a book on the table. |
| There is no any milk. | There isn't any milk. |
| Is there any milk? (✓) | **Is there** any milk? |

## 7. Dummy Subject "It"

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Dummy subject "it"** is a placeholder subject used when the real subject is unknown, implied, or comes later in the sentence. It has no real meaning.

**Key Points:**
- Placeholder subject
- No real meaning
- Used for weather, time, distance, and cleft sentences

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: "It" for Weather**
```
[IT] + [VERB]
It + is + raining.
It + is + cold.
```

**Structure 2: "It" for Time**
```
[IT] + [BE] + [TIME]
It + is + 5 PM.
It + is + time to go.
```

**Structure 3: "It" for Distance**
```
[IT] + [BE] + [DISTANCE]
It + is + 10 miles to the city.
It + is + far to the store.
```

**Structure 4: "It" in Cleft Sentences**
```
[IT] + [BE] + [FOCUS] + [THAT]
It + was + John + who + called.
It + is + coffee + that + I want.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**"It" for Weather:**
- "**It** is raining."
- "**It** is cold."
- "**It** was sunny yesterday."
- "**It** will snow tomorrow."
- "**It** is hot today."

**"It" for Time:**
- "**It** is 5 PM."
- "**It** is time to go."
- "**It** was midnight."
- "**It** is already late."
- "**It** takes 10 minutes."

**"It" for Distance:**
- "**It** is 10 miles to the city."
- "**It** is far to the store."
- "**It** is a long way home."

**"It" for Cleft Sentences:**
- "**It** was John who called."
- "**It** is coffee that I want."
- "**It** was yesterday that I saw her."
- "**It** is you that I love."

**"It" + Adjective + Infinitive:**
- "**It** is important to study."
- "**It** is easy to learn."
- "**It** is difficult to understand."
- "**It** is good to see you."

**"It" + Adjective + That Clause:**
- "**It** is important that you study."
- "**It** is clear that she is right."
- "**It** is obvious that he made a mistake."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Is raining. | **It** is raining. |
| Is 5 PM. | **It** is 5 PM. |
| Is 10 miles to the city. | **It** is 10 miles to the city. |
| It is important to study. (✓) | **It** is important to study. |

## 8. Inversion Rules (Review)

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Inversion** means reversing the normal word order (subject + verb becomes verb + subject). It is used for emphasis, drama, and in certain grammatical structures.

**Key Points:**
- Reverses normal word order
- Used for emphasis
- Used with negative adverbials
- Used in conditionals and comparisons

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Inversion with Negative Adverbials**
```
[NEGATIVE ADVERBIAL] + [AUXILIARY] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB]
Never + have + I + seen + such beauty.
Rarely + does + she + complain.
```

**Structure 2: Inversion in Conditionals**
```
[SHOULD/WERE/HAD] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB]
Were + I + richer, I would travel.
Had + I + known, I would have helped.
```

**Structure 3: Inversion with Comparisons**
```
[COMPARATIVE] + [AUXILIARY] + [SUBJECT]
So beautiful + was + she + that everyone stared.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Inversion with Negative Adverbials:**
- "**Never** have I seen such beauty."
- "**Rarely** does she complain."
- "**Hardly** had I arrived when it started raining."
- "**No sooner** had I left than he arrived."
- "**Not only** does she sing, but she also dances."

**Inversion in Conditionals:**
- "**Were** I richer, I would travel."
- "**Had** I known, I would have helped."
- "**Should** you need help, call me."

**Inversion with Comparisons:**
- "**So beautiful** was she that everyone stared."
- "**So fast** did he run that he won the race."
- "**Such** was her talent that she became famous."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Never I have seen such beauty. | **Never have I** seen such beauty. |
| Rarely she complains. | **Rarely does she** complain. |
| If I were richer, I would travel. | **Were I** richer, I would travel. |

## 9. Hedging

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Hedging** is the use of language to express uncertainty, politeness, or caution. It softens statements and makes them less direct.

**Key Points:**
- Expresses uncertainty
- Shows politeness
- Softens statements
- Common in academic writing

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Hedging with Modals**
```
[MODAL] + [MAIN VERB]
It + may + be + true.
She + might + be + right.
```

**Structure 2: Hedging with Adverbs**
```
[ADVERB] + [MAIN CLAUSE]
Perhaps + it is true.
Probably + she is right.
```

**Structure 3: Hedging with Introductory Phrases**
```
[INTRODUCTORY PHRASE] + [MAIN CLAUSE]
It seems that + she is right.
I think that + it is true.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Hedging with Modals:**
- "It **may** be true."
- "She **might** be right."
- "They **could** be late."
- "It **would** seem so."
- "I **would** say that."

**Hedging with Adverbs:**
- "**Perhaps** it is true."
- "**Probably** she is right."
- "**Possibly** they are late."
- "**Apparently** there is a problem."
- "**Presumably** he will come."

**Hedging with Introductory Phrases:**
- "**It seems that** she is right."
- "**I think that** it is true."
- "**It appears that** they are late."
- "**I believe that** he is honest."
- "**It is possible that** she is correct."
- "**I would say that** he is wrong."

**Hedging in Academic Writing:**
- "The results **suggest** that..."
- "The data **indicate** that..."
- "It **may be** that..."
- "This **could** be explained by..."
- "It **appears** that..."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Overusing hedging | Use when appropriate |
| Underusing hedging | Be polite and cautious |
| It is true. (too direct) | **It may be** true. |
| She is wrong. (too direct) | **I think** she is wrong. |

## 10. Formal vs Informal Grammar

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Formal and informal grammar** refers to the different registers of English. Formal English is used in academic, professional, and official contexts. Informal English is used in casual conversation, texting, and social media.

**Key Points:**
- Formal: Academic, professional, official
- Informal: Casual, conversational, social media
- Different rules for different contexts

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Formal Contractions**
```
[FORMAL] + [INFORMAL]
I am → I'm (informal)
Cannot → Can't (informal)
```

**Structure 2: Formal Vocabulary**
```
[FORMAL] + [INFORMAL]
As a result → So (informal)
Furthermore → Also (informal)
```

**Structure 3: Formal Sentence Structure**
```
[FORMAL] + [INFORMAL]
Never have I seen → I've never seen (informal)
It is important that he be... → He should... (informal)
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Contractions:**

| Formal | Informal |
|--------|----------|
| I am | I'm |
| Cannot | Can't |
| Do not | Don't |
| Will not | Won't |
| Should not | Shouldn't |
| It is | It's |

**Vocabulary:**

| Formal | Informal |
|--------|----------|
| As a result | So |
| Furthermore | Also |
| Nevertheless | Still |
| Therefore | So |
| In conclusion | To sum up |

**Sentence Structure:**

| Formal | Informal |
|--------|----------|
| Never have I seen such beauty. | I've never seen such beauty. |
| It is important that he be present. | He should be present. |
| The experiment was conducted. | We did the experiment. |
| We arrived at the conclusion that... | We decided that... |
| It is recommended that she leave. | She should leave. |

**Common Informal Features:**

| Feature | Example |

## 💬 Practice: Conversations

### Dialogue 1: Formal vs Informal

**Formal:**
- A: "I am pleased to meet you."
- B: "The pleasure is mine."
- A: "I would like to discuss the project."
- B: "I would be happy to assist you."
- A: "I have completed the report."
- B: "That is excellent news."

**Informal:**
- A: "Nice to meet you!"
- B: "Likewise!"
- A: "I wanna talk about the project."
- B: "Sure, happy to help."
- A: "I finished the report."
- B: "That's great!"

### Dialogue 2: Hedging

- A: "**I think** she might be right."
- B: "**It seems that** he is honest."
- A: "**Perhaps** they will come."
- B: "**I believe** it's a good idea."
- A: "**It is possible that** she is correct."
- B: "**I would say** he is wrong."
