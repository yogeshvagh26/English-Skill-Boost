# Phase 3: Verbs
## Participles

Welcome to Participles! You've learned about **verb types**, **verb forms**, **subject-verb agreement**, **irregular verbs**, **gerunds**, and **infinitives**. Now it's time to master **participles** — verb forms that function as adjectives.

Think of it this way: *Gerunds (V4) act like nouns. Infinitives (to + V1) act like nouns, adjectives, or adverbs. Participles (V3 and V4) act like adjectives. Understanding participles will make your English more descriptive and expressive.*

## 1. What Is a Participle?

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **participle** is a verb form that functions as an adjective. It describes or modifies a noun or pronoun.

**Key Points:**
- Participles are verb forms used as adjectives
- They can be part of verb phrases (continuous or perfect tenses)
- They can stand alone as adjectives
- They can form participial phrases

**The Three Types of Participles:**

| Type | Form | Example | Use |
|------|------|---------|-----|
| **Present Participle** | V4 (verb + -ing) | **running** water | Active meaning |
| **Past Participle** | V3 (verb + -ed or irregular) | **broken** glass | Passive meaning |
| **Perfect Participle** | having + V3 | **having finished** | Completed action |

**Participles vs. Gerunds:**

| Feature | Participle | Gerund |
|---------|------------|--------|
| **Function** | Adjective | Noun |
| **Example** | **The crying** baby | **Crying** is natural. |
| **Modifies** | Noun | Acts as noun |
| **Test** | Describes a noun | Can be replaced by noun |

## 2. Present Participle

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** The **present participle** is the -ing form of the verb (V4). It functions as an adjective with an active meaning — the noun is performing the action.

**Key Points:**
- Formed by adding -ing to the base verb
- Has an active meaning
- Can be used alone or in a participial phrase
- Can also be part of continuous tenses

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Present Participle as Adjective**
```
[PRESENT PARTICIPLE] + [NOUN]
running + water
crying + baby
```

**Structure 2: Present Participle in Participial Phrase**
```
[PARTICIPIAL PHRASE] + [,] + [MAIN CLAUSE]
Running quickly, + she caught the bus.
Feeling tired, + I went to bed.
```

**Structure 3: Present Participle in Continuous Tense**
```
[BE] + [PRESENT PARTICIPLE]
She + is + running.
They + were + playing.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Present Participle as Adjective:**
- "**Running** water is clean."
- "She held a **crying** baby."
- "The **shining** sun warmed us."
- "I saw a **flying** bird."
- "The **sleeping** dog didn't wake up."

**Present Participle in Participial Phrase:**
- "**Feeling tired**, I went to bed."
- "**Running quickly**, she caught the bus."
- "**Looking out the window**, she saw a beautiful sunset."
- "**Feeling nervous**, she took a deep breath."
- "**Hearing the news**, I immediately called her."

**Present Participle in Continuous Tenses:**
- "She **is working** on a new project."
- "They **were playing** football."
- "I **have been waiting** for hours."

**Common Present Participle Adjectives:**

| Adjective | Meaning | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| interesting | causing interest | an **interesting** book |
| exciting | causing excitement | an **exciting** movie |
| boring | causing boredom | a **boring** lecture |
| surprising | causing surprise | a **surprising** result |
| frightening | causing fear | a **frightening** experience |
| amazing | causing amazement | an **amazing** view |
| disappointing | causing disappointment | a **disappointing** outcome |
| exhausting | causing exhaustion | an **exhausting** day |

## 3. Past Participle

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** The **past participle** is the V3 form of the verb. It functions as an adjective with a passive meaning — the noun receives the action.

**Key Points:**
- Formed by adding -ed (regular) or irregular forms
- Has a passive meaning
- Can be used alone or in a participial phrase
- Can also be part of perfect tenses and passive voice

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Past Participle as Adjective**
```
[PAST PARTICIPLE] + [NOUN]
broken + glass
written + report
```

**Structure 2: Past Participle in Participial Phrase**
```
[PARTICIPIAL PHRASE] + [,] + [MAIN CLAUSE]
Broken by the storm, + the tree fell.
Written in 1950, + the book is still popular.
```

**Structure 3: Past Participle in Perfect Tense**
```
[HAVE/HAS/HAD] + [PAST PARTICIPLE]
She + has + finished.
They + had + left.
```

**Structure 4: Past Participle in Passive Voice**
```
[BE] + [PAST PARTICIPLE]
The book + was + written.
The car + was + stolen.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Past Participle as Adjective:**
- "**Broken** glass cut my foot."
- "I need to fix the **broken** window."
- "The **written** report was excellent."
- "I found a **lost** puppy."
- "He has a **broken** leg."

**Past Participle in Participial Phrase:**
- "**Broken by the storm**, the tree fell."
- "**Written in 1950**, the book is still popular."
- "**Confused by the question**, he remained silent."
- "**Exhausted from the journey**, she went straight to bed."
- "**Inspired by her mentor**, she decided to become a teacher."

**Past Participle in Perfect Tenses:**
- "She **has finished** her work."
- "They **had left** before I arrived."
- "I **have seen** that movie before."

**Past Participle in Passive Voice:**
- "The house **was built** in 2020."
- "The letter **was written** by a famous author."
- "The car **was stolen** last night."

**Common Past Participle Adjectives:**

| Adjective | Meaning | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| interested | feeling interest | an **interested** audience |
| excited | feeling excitement | **excited** children |
| bored | feeling boredom | a **bored** student |
| surprised | feeling surprise | a **surprised** look |
| frightened | feeling fear | a **frightened** child |
| amazed | feeling amazement | an **amazed** audience |
| disappointed | feeling disappointment | a **disappointed** parent |
| exhausted | feeling exhaustion | an **exhausted** worker |
| tired | feeling tired | a **tired** traveler |
| confused | feeling confusion | a **confused** student |

**Important:** Many adjectives come in pairs — present participle (causing feeling) and past participle (feeling).

| Present Participle (Causing) | Past Participle (Feeling) |
|------------------------------|---------------------------|
| **boring** lecture | **bored** student |
| **exciting** movie | **excited** audience |
| **interesting** book | **interested** reader |
| **surprising** news | **surprised** person |
| **frightening** experience | **frightened** child |
| **amazing** view | **amazed** visitor |
| **disappointing** result | **disappointed** team |

## 4. Perfect Participle

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** The **perfect participle** is formed with "having" + past participle (V3). It shows that one action was completed before another action.

**Key Points:**
- Formed by "having + V3"
- Shows completed action before another action
- Has an active or passive meaning
- Used mainly in written English

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Perfect Participle (Active)**
```
[HAVING] + [V3] + [MAIN CLAUSE]
Having + finished + his homework, he went out.
Having + eaten + dinner, she watched TV.
```

**Structure 2: Perfect Participle (Passive)**
```
[HAVING BEEN] + [V3] + [MAIN CLAUSE]
Having been + told + to wait, she stayed.
Having been + warned, he was careful.
```

**Structure 3: Perfect Participle with Negative**
```
[NOT] + [HAVING] + [V3] + [MAIN CLAUSE]
Not having + finished, he stayed up late.
Not having + seen + the movie, I couldn't discuss it.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Perfect Participle (Active):**
- "**Having finished** his homework, he went out to play."
- "**Having eaten** breakfast, she went to school."
- "**Having studied** all night, she was exhausted."
- "**Having lived** in Paris for years, she knew the city well."
- "**Having worked** hard, he deserved the promotion."

**Perfect Participle (Passive):**
- "**Having been told** to wait, she stayed in the lobby."
- "**Having been warned** about the danger, he was careful."
- "**Having been abandoned** as a child, he grew up in an orphanage."
- "**Having been injured**, he couldn't play in the final."

**Perfect Participle (Negative):**
- "**Not having finished** his work, he stayed up late."
- "**Not having seen** the movie, I couldn't discuss it."
- "**Not having been** invited, she didn't go to the party."
- "**Not having studied** enough, he failed the exam."

**Perfect Participle vs. Present Participle:**

| Present Participle | Perfect Participle |
|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Feeling** tired, I went to bed. | **Having felt** tired for weeks, I saw a doctor. |
| **Finishing** the work, she left. | **Having finished** the work, she left. |

## 5. Participial Phrases

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **participial phrase** is a participle with its modifiers and/or objects. It functions as a single adjective unit in the sentence.

**Structure:**
```
[PARTICIPLE] + [OBJECT/COMPLEMENT] + [MODIFIERS]
Running + to the store + quickly
Written + by a famous author + in 2020
Having + finished + her homework + already
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Present Participial Phrases:**
- "**Running to catch the bus**, she tripped and fell."
- "**Feeling happy about the news**, she called her family."
- "**Looking out the window**, I saw the storm approaching."
- "**Walking through the park**, we saw beautiful flowers."
- "**Singing loudly**, he walked down the street."

**Past Participial Phrases:**
- "**Broken by the storm**, the roof collapsed."
- "**Written by a famous author**, the book became a bestseller."
- "**Confused by the instructions**, he asked for help."
- "**Exhausted from the journey**, I went straight to bed."
- "**Inspired by her mentor**, she pursued her dream."

**Perfect Participial Phrases:**
- "**Having finished his homework**, he went out to play."
- "**Having been told to wait**, she stayed in her seat."
- "**Not having seen the movie**, I couldn't join the discussion."
- "**Having lived in Paris**, she spoke French fluently."
- "**Having been warned**, he was more careful."

## 6. Dangling Participles

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **dangling participle** occurs when a participial phrase does not clearly modify any noun in the sentence. This is a common grammatical error.

**Key Points:**
- The subject of the main clause must be the same as the implied subject of the participial phrase
- If it's not, the participle "dangles"
- Dangling participles create confusion

**Example:**
- "**Walking to the store**, the rain started." (Who was walking? The rain? ❌)
- "**Walking to the store**, I felt the rain start." (I was walking. ✓)

### 📐 Study: How to Fix Dangling Participles

**Method 1: Add a Subject**
- ❌ "**Driving to work**, the car broke down."
- ✓ "**Driving to work**, I had a breakdown."

**Method 2: Change the Phrase to a Clause**
- ❌ "**Walking through the park**, the birds were singing."
- ✓ "**While I was walking** through the park, the birds were singing."

**Method 3: Put the Subject First**
- ❌ "**Running late**, my phone was ringing."
- ✓ "**While I was running** late, my phone was ringing."

### 👀 See: More Examples

**Dangling Participles (Incorrect):**
- "**Reading the book**, the characters seemed real." (Who was reading?)
- "**Looking out the window**, the trees were swaying." (Who was looking?)
- "**Writing the report**, a mistake was made." (Who was writing?)
- "**Having studied all night**, the exam was easy." (Who studied?)

**Corrected Sentences:**
- "**Reading the book**, I felt the characters seemed real." (Added subject)
- "**Looking out the window**, I saw the trees swaying." (Added subject)
- "**While writing** the report, I made a mistake." (Changed to clause)
- "**Having studied** all night, I found the exam easy." (Added subject)

## 7. Common Mistakes with Participles

### ⚠️ Learn: The Top Errors

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I am boring. (when I mean bored) | I am **bored**. |
| The movie is bored. | The movie is **boring**. |
| She looked confuse. | She looked **confused**. |
| The news were surprising. | The news was **surprising**. |
| I was exciting about the trip. | I was **excited** about the trip. |
| Running to the store, the rain started. | **While I was running** to the store, the rain started. |
| Having eat dinner, she left. | Having **eaten** dinner, she left. |
| She has went home. | She has **gone** home. |

**Present vs. Past Participle Adjectives:**

| Present Participle (Active - causing) | Past Participle (Passive - feeling) |
|--------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| The movie is **interesting**. | I am **interested**. |
| The story is **exciting**. | I am **excited**. |
| The lecture is **boring**. | I am **bored**. |
| The news is **surprising**. | I am **surprised**. |
| The game is **frightening**. | I am **frightened**. |
| The view is **amazing**. | I am **amazed**. |
| The result is **disappointing**. | I am **disappointed**. |

**Rule:**
- Use **present participle** (-ing) when the noun CAUSES the feeling
- Use **past participle** (-ed/irregular) when the noun FEELS the feeling

## 8. Participles in Reduced Relative Clauses

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A participle can replace a relative clause (who/which/that) to make sentences shorter and more elegant.

**Examples:**

**Present Participle (Active):**
- Full: "The man **who is standing** over there is my father."
- Reduced: "The man **standing** over there is my father."

- Full: "The girl **who is singing** is my sister."
- Reduced: "The girl **singing** is my sister."

**Past Participle (Passive):**
- Full: "The car **that was stolen** has been found."
- Reduced: "The car **stolen** has been found."

- Full: "The book **that was written** by her is famous."
- Reduced: "The book **written** by her is famous."

---

## 9. Participles in Absolute Phrases

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** An **absolute phrase** contains a participle but has its own subject (not the subject of the main clause). It adds extra information to the sentence.

**Structure:**
```
[NOUN/PRONOUN] + [PARTICIPLE] + [REST] + [,] + [MAIN CLAUSE]
The weather + being + beautiful, we decided to go out.
His work + finished, he went home.
```

**Examples:**
- "**The weather being beautiful**, we went to the beach."
- "**His work finished**, he went home."
- "**Her heart beating fast**, she opened the door."
- "**The decision made**, they moved forward."
- "**All things considered**, we made the right choice."

## 💬 Practice: Conversations

### Dialogue 1: Describing Feelings

- A: "How was the movie?"
- B: "It was **boring**. I was completely **bored**."
- A: "Really? I found it **exciting**."
- B: "I guess we have different tastes. I was **surprised** it got such good reviews."
- A: "I was **amazed** by the special effects."
- B: "I was **disappointed** with the ending."

### Dialogue 2: Describing Experiences

- A: "**Having lived** in many countries, she speaks several languages."
- B: "That's impressive! **Feeling inspired**, I want to learn a new language too."
- A: "**Having studied** Chinese for years, I can help you."
- B: "That would be great! I was **excited** about learning."

### Dialogue 3: Reacting to News

- A: "Did you hear the news? The company is closing."
- B: "I was completely **shocked**! I'm **disappointed**."
- A: "**Having worked** there for 10 years, I'm also **worried**."
- B: "**Feeling anxious**, I need to update my resume."
- A: "That's a good idea. **Being** proactive is important."
