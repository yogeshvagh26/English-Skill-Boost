### 2. Conversation

### 4. Key Takeaways

- **Common Sentence Patterns:**
  - *"I have a check-up and cleaning scheduled for [time]."* – Stating your appointment.
  - *"Is this your first time with us?"* – Asking if the patient is new.
  - *"Have you been having any dental issues or discomfort?"* – Asking about problems.
  - *"Open wide, please."* – Instructing the patient to open their mouth.
  - *"I'll apply some numbing gel if needed."* – Offering pain relief.
  - *"You'll feel some pressure, but it shouldn't be painful."* – Warning about sensation.
  - *"I'll need to take X-rays."* – Informing about necessary imaging.
  - *"I see a small cavity."* – Giving a diagnosis.
  - *"Would you like to do the filling today, or schedule it for another time?"* – Offering timing choices.
  - *"Here are the care instructions."* – Giving post-procedure guidance.

- **Useful Phrases to Remember:**
  - *"I just moved to the area."* – Explaining why you're a new patient.
  - *"I'll make a note of that."* – Recording information for later.
  - *"That explains it."* – Showing understanding of a cause.
  - *"Not at all."* – Reassuring someone something won't hurt.
  - *"Good decision."* – Approving a patient's choice.
  - *"I just feel pressure – no pain."* – Describing sensation during a procedure.
  - *"I'll go over the charges with you."* – Explaining billing later.
  - *"Thank you for everything."* – Expressing overall gratitude.

- **Practical Speaking Tips:**
  1. **Be honest about pain and symptoms** – If you have pain, describe it clearly: *"I feel a sharp pain when I bite down"* or *"I have sensitivity to cold drinks."* This helps the dentist diagnose accurately.
  2. **Ask about costs upfront** – Dental procedures can be expensive. Ask: *"What will the cost be?"* and *"Does my insurance cover this?"* You can say: *"I'd like to understand the charges before we proceed."*
  3. **Discuss anxiety or fears** – If you're nervous, say: *"I'm a bit anxious about the procedure."* Dentists can offer sedation or explain each step to comfort you. You can also ask: *"Can you explain what you're going to do before you do it?"*
  4. **Learn dental vocabulary** – Knowing terms like *"cavity,"* *"filling,"* *"cleaning,"* *"X-rays,"* and *"plaque"* helps you understand diagnoses and instructions better.
  5. **Ask about numbing** – If you're worried about pain, ask: *"Will I be numb for this?"* and *"How long will the numbness last?"* This helps you prepare for the procedure and recovery.
  6. **Always get after-care instructions** – Before leaving, confirm: *"What should I do after the procedure?"* and *"When can I eat?"* If you receive instructions verbally, repeat them back: *"So I should avoid chewing on that side for an hour – is that right?"*
  7. **Schedule your next visit** – Before leaving, book your next appointment. Ask: *"When should I come back?"* and *"Can I book a cleaning for six months from now?"* Regular visits prevent bigger issues later.
