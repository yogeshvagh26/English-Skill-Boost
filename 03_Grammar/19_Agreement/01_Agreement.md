# Phase 19: Agreement

Welcome to Phase 19! You've completed the comprehensive grammar roadmap (Phases 1-18). Now it's time to explore **Agreement** — the principle that different parts of a sentence must match in number, person, and gender.

Think of it this way: *Agreement is the "harmony" of grammar. Subjects and verbs must agree. Pronouns and antecedents must agree. Numbers must agree. When everything matches, your sentences flow naturally. When they don't, your listener notices instantly.*

## Topic Overview: Types of Agreement

| Type | Definition | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Subject-Verb Agreement** | Subject and verb match in number | She **works** hard. They **work** hard. |
| **Pronoun Agreement** | Pronoun and antecedent match in number/gender | **She** loves **her** job. |
| **Number Agreement** | Countables match with quantifiers | **Many books** are on the shelf. |

## 1. Subject-Verb Agreement

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Subject-verb agreement** means that the subject and verb in a sentence must match in number. Singular subjects take singular verbs; plural subjects take plural verbs.

**Key Points:**
- Singular subject → singular verb (V5)
- Plural subject → plural verb (V1)
- The verb must agree with the subject, not the object
- Watch out for tricky subjects

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Singular Subject + Singular Verb**
```
[SINGULAR SUBJECT] + [SINGULAR VERB]
She + works.
The dog + barks.
```

**Structure 2: Plural Subject + Plural Verb**
```
[PLURAL SUBJECT] + [PLURAL VERB]
They + work.
The dogs + bark.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Basic Subject-Verb Agreement:**

| Singular Subject | Verb | Plural Subject | Verb |
|------------------|------|----------------|------|
| She | **works** | They | **work** |
| He | **sings** | We | **sing** |
| The dog | **barks** | The dogs | **bark** |
| The child | **plays** | The children | **play** |
| It | **is** | They | **are** |

**Agreement with "To Be":**
- "She **is** a doctor." (singular)
- "They **are** doctors." (plural)
- "I **am** happy." (first person singular)

**Tricky Subjects:**

**1. Indefinite Pronouns (Always Singular):**
- "Everyone **is** here." (Not: Everyone are)
- "Somebody **has** called." (Not: Somebody have)
- "Nobody **knows** the answer." (Not: Nobody know)
- "Everything **is** ready." (Not: Everything are)

**2. Collective Nouns (Singular or Plural):**
- "The team **is** winning." (as one unit)
- "The team **are** arguing." (as individuals)
- "The family **is** large." (as one unit)
- "The family **are** having dinner." (as individuals)

**3. Subjects with "Each/Every":**
- "**Each** student **has** a book." (Not: Each have)
- "**Every** person **is** responsible." (Not: Every are)
- "**Each and every** one **is** here." (Not: Every one are)

**4. Subjects with "Either/Neither":**
- "**Either** option **is** acceptable." (Not: Either are)
- "**Neither** answer **is** correct." (Not: Neither are)

**5. Subjects with "Or/Nor":**
- "Neither the teacher nor the students **are** here." (Agrees with students)
- "Neither the students nor the teacher **is** here." (Agrees with teacher)

**6. Subjects with "And" (Compound Subjects):**
- "Tom **and** Jerry **are** friends." (plural)
- "Peanut butter **and** jelly **is** my favorite." (single item)

**7. Amounts and Measurements (Singular):**
- "Five dollars **is** too much." (Not: Five dollars are)
- "Ten miles **is** a long way." (Not: Ten miles are)

**8. Titles (Singular):**
- "The Chronicles of Narnia **is** a great book." (Not: The Chronicles are)

**9. "A number of" vs. "The number of":**
- "A number of students **are** here." (plural)
- "The number of students **is** 50." (singular)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Everyone are here. | Everyone **is** here. |
| Each students have a book. | Each student **has** a book. |
| The team are winning. (US) | The team **is** winning. |
| Neither of them are here. | Neither of them **is** here. |
| Either options are fine. | Either option **is** fine. |
| Five dollars are too much. | Five dollars **is** too much. |
| A number of people is here. | A number of people **are** here. |
| The number of people are 50. | The number of people **is** 50. |

## 2. Pronoun Agreement

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Pronoun agreement** means that a pronoun must agree with its antecedent (the noun it refers to) in number, person, and gender.

**Key Points:**
- Pronoun matches antecedent in number (singular/plural)
- Pronoun matches antecedent in person (1st, 2nd, 3rd)
- Pronoun matches antecedent in gender (male, female, neuter)

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Singular Antecedent + Singular Pronoun**
```
[SINGULAR ANTECEDENT] + [SINGULAR PRONOUN]
She + lost + her + keys.
The dog + wagged + its + tail.
```

**Structure 2: Plural Antecedent + Plural Pronoun**
```
[PLURAL ANTECEDENT] + [PLURAL PRONOUN]
They + lost + their + keys.
The dogs + wagged + their + tails.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Basic Pronoun Agreement:**

| Antecedent | Pronoun | Example |
|------------|---------|---------|
| John (singular, male) | he, him, his | John loves **his** job. |
| Mary (singular, female) | she, her, hers | Mary loves **her** job. |
| The dog (singular, neuter) | it, its | The dog wagged **its** tail. |
| People (plural) | they, them, their | People love **their** jobs. |
| The students (plural) | they, them, their | The students love **their** jobs. |

**Tricky Pronoun Agreement:**

**1. Indefinite Pronouns (Singular):**
- "**Everyone** brought **his** book." (traditional)
- "**Everyone** brought **their** book." (modern, gender-neutral)
- "**Someone** left **their** phone." (singular + plural for inclusivity)
- "**Everybody** should do **their** best."

**2. Collective Nouns (Singular or Plural):**
- "The team celebrated **its** victory." (as one unit)
- "The team celebrated **their** victory." (as individuals)

**3. "Each/Every" (Singular):**
- "**Each** student brought **his** book." (traditional)
- "**Each** student brought **their** book." (modern)

**4. Compound Antecedents:**
- "John and Mary went to **their** home." (both)
- "Either John or Mary will bring **her** book." (agrees with Mary)
- "Neither John nor Mary brought **her** book." (agrees with Mary)

**5. Indefinite Antecedents with "Or/Nor":**
- "Neither the teacher nor the students brought **their** books." (plural)
- "Neither the students nor the teacher brought **her** book." (singular)

**Gender-Neutral Pronoun Usage:**

| Traditional | Modern (Gender-Neutral) |
|-------------|-------------------------|
| Everyone brought **his** book. | Everyone brought **their** book. |
| A doctor should be honest with **his** patients. | A doctor should be honest with **their** patients. |
| Every student should do **his** homework. | Every student should do **their** homework. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Everyone brought his book. (male-centric) | Everyone brought **their** book. |
| Each student brought their book. (modern) | Each student brought **their** book. |
| The team celebrated their victory. (OK) | The team celebrated **their** victory. |
| John and Mary went to his house. | John and Mary went to **their** house. |
| Either John or Mary will bring his book. | Either John or Mary will bring **her** book. |
| A doctor should be honest with her patients. (female-specific) | A doctor should be honest with **their** patients. |

## 3. Number Agreement

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Number agreement** means that countable and uncountable nouns must agree with the appropriate quantifiers, determiners, and verbs.

**Key Points:**
- Countable nouns → many, few, a few, several
- Uncountable nouns → much, little, a little
- Countable/uncountable → some, any, no, enough

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Countable Noun + Quantifier**
```
[MANY/FEW] + [COUNTABLE PLURAL NOUN]
many + books
few + people
```

**Structure 2: Uncountable Noun + Quantifier**
```
[MUCH/LITTLE] + [UNCOUNTABLE NOUN]
much + water
little + time
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Countable Nouns:**
- "I have **many** books." (Not: much books)
- "There are **few** students." (Not: few student)
- "**A few** friends came." (Not: a few friend)
- "**Several** people arrived." (Not: several person)
- "**A number of** people came." (Not: a number of person)

**Uncountable Nouns:**
- "I need **much** water." (Not: many water)
- "There is **little** time." (Not: few time)
- "**A little** sugar is enough." (Not: a few sugar)
- "I have **less** money than you." (Not: fewer money)
- "**A great deal of** work remains." (Not: a great deal of works)

**Nouns That Can Be Both:**

| Noun | Countable | Uncountable |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **chicken** | I bought **a chicken** (the bird). | I ate **some chicken** (the meat). |
| **hair** | There are **hairs** in my soup. | She has **long hair**. |
| **glass** | I drank **two glasses** of water. | The table is made of **glass**. |
| **paper** | I read **a paper** (a newspaper). | I need **some paper** to write on. |
| **time** | I've seen her **three times**. | I need **some time** to think. |

**Countable vs. Uncountable Quantifiers:**

| Quantifier | Countable | Uncountable |
|------------|-----------|-------------|
| many | ✓ | ✗ |
| much | ✗ | ✓ |
| a few | ✓ | ✗ |
| a little | ✗ | ✓ |
| a lot of | ✓ | ✓ |
| some | ✓ | ✓ |
| any | ✓ | ✓ |
| no | ✓ | ✓ |
| enough | ✓ | ✓ |
| plenty of | ✓ | ✓ |
| several | ✓ | ✗ |

**Countable vs. Uncountable: Common Mistakes**

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I have many money. | I have **much** money. |
| I have much friends. | I have **many** friends. |
| There is a few water. | There is **a little** water. |
| There are a little people. | There are **a few** people. |
| I have fewer money. | I have **less** money. |
| I have less books. | I have **fewer** books. |

**Less vs. Fewer:**

| Word | Use | Example |
|------|-----|---------|
| **less** | Uncountable | I have **less** money. |
| **fewer** | Countable | I have **fewer** books. |

**Much vs. Many:**

| Word | Use | Example |
|------|-----|---------|
| **much** | Uncountable | How **much** water? |
| **many** | Countable | How **many** books? |
