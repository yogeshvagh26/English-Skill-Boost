## 1. Situation : Executive Strategy Meeting – The "Metric Inflation" Controversy

**Context:** You are a Senior Data Analyst at a mobile gaming company. The Head of Growth (Priya) proudly reports that "Daily Active Users (DAU) increased 25% this quarter." The CEO (Michael) is pleased—until the CFO (Elena) points out that revenue is flat. Priya argues that the DAU increase is a sign of future growth. You've looked at the data and discovered that the DAU increase is driven by a "definition change"—the marketing team started counting users who open the app for just 1 second, whereas the previous definition required 30 seconds of engagement. You need to expose the "metric inflation" without embarrassing Priya and propose a solution.

## 3. Vocabulary and Expressions

| Word/Phrase                     | Part of Speech | Meaning                                                                                | Example Sentence                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------- | -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Metric inflation**            | Noun Phrase    | Artificially inflating a metric through definition changes or looser inclusion criteria | *Metric inflation erodes trust—stakeholders eventually stop believing the numbers.*               |
| **Definition change**           | Noun Phrase    | Altering the rules for how a metric is calculated                                      | *A definition change can make historical comparisons meaningless.*                               |
| **Retroactive adjustment**      | Noun Phrase    | Recalculating historical data using a new definition to maintain comparability         | *We need to retroactively adjust last year's DAU to match the new definition.*                   |
| **Apples-to-apples comparison** | Noun Phrase    | Comparing data that uses the same definition, methodology, and timeframe               | *Without an apples-to-apples comparison, the growth number is meaningless.*                      |
| **Non-comparable data**         | Noun Phrase    | Data that cannot be compared because the underlying definitions have changed           | *Non-comparable data leads to bad decisions and eroded trust.*                                  |
| **Cohort consistency**          | Noun Phrase    | Ensuring that the same rules apply to different cohorts of users                       | *Cohort consistency is essential for tracking true user behavior changes.*                       |
| **Engagement metric**           | Noun Phrase    | A metric that measures how actively users interact with a product                      | *Engagement metrics (e.g., time spent, actions taken) are more valuable than vanity metrics.*    |
| **Vanity metric**               | Noun Phrase    | A metric that looks good but doesn't correlate with business outcomes                  | *Total DAU can be a vanity metric if it's driven by inactive users.*                             |
| **Data transparency**           | Noun Phrase    | Being open about how metrics are defined, calculated, and changed                      | *Data transparency builds trust and prevents the "metric inflation" trap.*                       |
| **Governance policy**           | Noun Phrase    | A set of rules that govern how data and metrics are managed                            | *We need a governance policy that requires data team approval for any metric definition change.* |
| **Versioning**                  | Noun           | Keeping a history of metric definitions so changes are traceable                       | *Versioning metrics allows us to track the impact of definition changes over time.*              |
| **Correlation with outcomes**   | Noun Phrase    | The statistical relationship between a metric and a business result                    | *Engaged DAU has a 0.92 correlation with revenue—that's why we prioritize it.*                   |

## 4. Key Takeaways

### Common Sentence Patterns (for Exposing Metric Inflation)

| Pattern                                                              | Use                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| *"I think we need to unpack this gap—I've looked at both numbers."*  | To signal you've investigated the discrepancy and found the root cause.                |
| *"The definition changed at the start of this quarter."*             | To call out the specific timing and nature of the change.                              |
| *"That change alone accounts for [X] of the [Y] percentage points of growth."* | To quantify the impact of the definition change.                                      |
| *"The story isn't [inflated headline]. The story is [real growth rate]."* | To reframe the narrative and correct the misperception (anchor pattern).               |
| *"My recommendation: (1) [Retroactive adjustment], (2) [Two metrics], (3) [Governance rule]."* | To provide a clear, multi-part solution that prevents future issues.                  |
| *"From now on, any definition change must be [approved / disclosed / documented]."* | To establish a new rule that ensures transparency going forward.                      |

### Useful Phrases to Remember
- **Exposing the change gently:** *"Priya, I know you were trying to show positive momentum. But when we dig into the numbers, the growth is mostly driven by a definition change—not by real user behavior."*
- **Distinguishing between "total" and "engaged":** *"We need to separate 'users who open the app' from 'users who actually use the app.' They are very different populations."*
- **Introducing "backwards comparability":** *"If we change the definition, we must recalculate the prior period using the new definition—otherwise we're comparing apples to oranges."*
- **Calling out the problem:** *"When we highlight a 25% growth but internally know it's only 7%, we're setting expectations we can't meet. That's dangerous."*
- **Defining a "governance" process:** *"Let's create a simple rule: any metric definition change must be documented, approved by Data, and disclosed in the commentary. This protects everyone."*
- **When stakeholders resist:** *"I know this feels like administrative overhead—but trust is easier to lose than to rebuild. A small upfront effort saves weeks of explanation later."*

### Practical Speaking Tips (for Exposing Metric Inflation)
1.  **Always check for definition changes when you see a sudden spike.** Before celebrating—or panicking—ask: *"Did the definition change? Did we start counting differently?"* This should be your first reflex.
2.  **Build a "Definition Registry."** Maintain a central document (or dashboard) that tracks every metric's definition, calculation logic, and any historical changes. This eliminates ambiguity and empowers you to catch inflation proactively.
3.  **When you find inflation, don't "gotcha" the person responsible.** Priya may have changed the definition in good faith. Approach it as a *process* problem, not a *personality* problem. Say: *"The system didn't flag this change—we need to fix the system."*
4.  **Introduce the concept of "backwards compatibility."** This is a software term that applies beautifully to metrics: when you change a definition, you must make it comparable to the past. If you can't, you must call it out.
5.  **Celebrate the "real" growth.** Even if the headline number is inflated, there may still be real growth underneath. Say: *"Even after adjusting for the definition change, we're at 7% real growth—that's still solid. Let's focus on that."*
6.  **Turn the incident into a learning opportunity.** Say: *"This incident highlights why we need a governance policy. I'll draft one and bring it to you next week."* —this shows you're not just pointing out problems—you're solving them.
7.  **Educate on the difference between "census" and "sample."** Say: *"If we count every open as an 'active,' we're counting the census of anyone who breathed near the app. That's not the same as the sample of engaged users who actually drive revenue."*
8.  **When presenting the "real" number, lead with transparency.** Say: *"The official DAU number is 25% growth. However, after adjusting for the definition change, the comparable growth is 7%. I'll provide both numbers going forward."*
9.  **Don't kill the metric—fix it.** Don't say: *"DAU is a bad metric."* Say: *"DAU is valuable, but we need to define it consistently and pair it with an engagement metric."*
10. **Create a "Metric Change Log."** At the bottom of every dashboard or report, include a small section that says: *"Metric definitions as of [date]. Any changes are logged [here]."* This is a small addition that builds immense trust over time.
