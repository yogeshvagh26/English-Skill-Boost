## 1. Situation : Product Pricing Committee – The "Price Increase" Proposition

**Context:** You are a Senior Data Analyst at a B2B SaaS company. The CEO (Sarah) and the CFO (David) are considering a 15% price increase for the core product. The Head of Sales (Robert) is strongly opposed, arguing it will kill deals. The Head of Product (Maria) is neutral but concerned about churn. You've been asked to model the impact of the price increase. The room is divided. You need to present a balanced, data-driven analysis that considers revenue, churn, competitive positioning, and customer segments—and make a clear recommendation.

## 3. Vocabulary and Expressions

| Word/Phrase                     | Part of Speech | Meaning                                                                                | Example Sentence                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------- | -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Price elasticity**            | Noun Phrase    | How sensitive customer demand is to a price change (high elasticity = price-sensitive) | *SMB customers have high price elasticity—a small price increase causes significant churn.*       |
| **Grandfathering**              | Noun           | Allowing existing customers to keep the old price while new customers pay the new price | *Grandfathering existing customers protects them from the price increase.*                        |
| **Segmented pricing**           | Noun Phrase    | Setting different prices for different customer segments                                | *Segmented pricing maximizes revenue while minimizing churn.*                                    |
| **Value add**                   | Noun Phrase    | An additional feature or service offered to justify a price increase                    | *Adding free training sessions as a value add softens the price increase.*                        |
| **Price sensitivity**           | Noun Phrase    | The degree to which customers react to price changes                                    | *Price sensitivity varies by segment—Enterprise is insensitive; SMB is highly sensitive.*        |
| **Sensitivity analysis**        | Noun Phrase    | Testing how changes in assumptions affect outcomes                                     | *A sensitivity analysis showed that churn rate is the single biggest driver of pricing success.* |
| **Net revenue gain**            | Noun Phrase    | The final increase in revenue after accounting for churn and other losses               | *Net revenue gain is more important than gross price increase.*                                  |
| **Competitive positioning**     | Noun Phrase    | How your product's price compares to competitors                                        | *We're below market average price—we have room to move.*                                         |
| **Win rate impact**             | Noun Phrase    | The effect of a price change on the percentage of deals won                             | *Win rate impact is most severe in SMB—we need to protect that segment.*                          |
| **Churn modeling**              | Noun Phrase    | Predicting the probability of customer churn under different scenarios                  | *Churn modeling shows that feature usage is a stronger predictor of churn than price.*           |
| **Value proposition**           | Noun Phrase    | The perceived value a customer gets from a product relative to its price                | *If the value proposition is strong, customers will accept a price increase.*                    |
| **Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)** | Acronym/Metric | The total revenue expected from a customer over their entire relationship with the company | *LTV helps us decide how much churn we're willing to tolerate.*                                  |
| **Adoption / Usage**            | Noun           | The rate at which customers use the product's features                                  | *Usage intensity (active users, features used) is a leading indicator of churn and price acceptance.* |

## 4. Key Takeaways

### Common Sentence Patterns (for Pricing Strategy Storytelling)

| Pattern                                                              | Use                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| *"Let me walk you through a structured analysis across three dimensions: [X], [Y], and [Z]."* | To set expectations for the scope of the analysis.                                    |
| *"My sensitivity analysis shows three scenarios: Best Case [X], Likely Case [Y], Worst Case [Z]."* | To acknowledge uncertainty while providing actionable ranges.                         |
| *"The risk is concentrated in [segment], not [segment]."*            | To localize the problem and prevent blanket resistance.                                |
| *"I found a mitigating factor: [factor] reduces risk by [X]%."*      | To offer a lever for risk reduction.                                                   |
| *"My recommendation is a segmented approach: (1) [Segment-specific action], (2) [Segment-specific action], (3) [Segment-specific action]."* | To propose a nuanced, tailored recommendation.                                        |
| *"The story isn't [blanket claim]. The story is [segmented insight]."* | To reframe the narrative (anchor pattern).                                            |
| *"Total revenue gain under this approach: [X]—slightly less than the blanker scenario, but with significantly lower churn."* | To balance optimism with realism.                                                     |

### Useful Phrases to Remember
- **Introducing price elasticity:** *"Not all customers are equally sensitive to price—we need to treat them differently."*
- **Handling "why not just raise and see?":** *"A blanket increase is a gamble. Segmenting the approach turns it from a gamble into a calculated strategy."*
- **Introducing the "value" argument:** *"If we increase value along with price, customers will view it as a fair trade, not a gouge."*
- **When someone mentions competition:** *"We need to benchmark our price relative to competitors—if we're below market, we have room to move."*
- **Calling out "churn" risk:** *"The biggest risk isn't revenue loss—it's churn. We need to model churn carefully and protect sensitive segments."*
- **Measuring success:** *"We'll track churn rate, win rate, and revenue growth by segment—if any segment shows high churn, we can adjust."*

### Practical Speaking Tips (for Pricing Strategy Narratives)
1.  **Always model multiple scenarios.** Pricing is inherently uncertain. Show best-case, worst-case, and most-likely scenarios. This builds credibility and helps stakeholders make informed decisions.
2.  **Focus on "Net Revenue Gain," not Gross.** A 15% price increase might seem like 15% more revenue—but if churn increases, the net is lower. Always present the net.
3.  **Protect sensitive segments.** SMB and early-stage customers are often price-sensitive. Grandfathering them or offering lower increases reduces churn risk.
4.  **Be specific about the "Value Add."** If you're raising prices, attach a value add (e.g., new feature, training, support). This helps justify the increase.
5.  **Use a "Pricing Impact Dashboard."** After implementation, track churn, win rates, and revenue by segment. This allows for rapid course correction.
6.  **Segment by "Usage Intensity."** Customers who use more features are more likely to accept a price increase. Segment by usage and tailor the price accordingly.
7.  **Balance short-term revenue vs. long-term trust.** A price increase can generate immediate revenue, but if it damages trust, long-term revenue will suffer. Balance the two.
8.  **Acknowledge the "Sales Friction."** Price increases make sales harder. Acknowledge this and provide sales with tools (e.g., value calculators, competitor comparisons) to overcome objections.
9.  **Phase the implementation.** If you're grandfathering existing customers, tell them they have 12 months before the new price applies—this gives them time to adjust and reduces immediate churn.
10. **Review and iterate.** After 3-6 months, review the impact and adjust. A pricing strategy is never "done"—it needs to evolve with the market.
