## 1. Situation : Customer Experience (CX) Optimization – The "Low NPS" Paradox

**Context:** You are a Senior Data Analyst at a subscription-based meal delivery service. The Head of Customer Experience (Maya) is alarmed—Net Promoter Score (NPS) has dropped from 65 to 48 over the last quarter. The Head of Product (Alex) suggests it's a quality issue—the meals aren't as good. The Head of Operations (David) blames delivery delays. The Head of Marketing (Priya) thinks it's a brand perception problem. You've been asked to analyze the NPS data, customer feedback, and operational metrics to identify the root cause and recommend a fix.

## 3. Vocabulary and Expressions

| Word/Phrase                     | Part of Speech | Meaning                                                                                | Example Sentence                                                                                 |

| **Customer experience (CX)**    | Noun Phrase    | The sum of all interactions a customer has with a brand                               | *CX is the competitive differentiator in subscription businesses.*                                |
| **Text analysis**               | Noun Phrase    | Analyzing open-ended customer feedback to identify themes and patterns                 | *Text analysis revealed that delivery issues are the #1 complaint.*                              |
| **Segment-specific**            | Adjective      | Relating to a particular customer segment                                             | *The NPS drop is segment-specific—it's concentrated among new customers.*                        |
| **Change management**           | Noun Phrase    | The process of managing organizational changes to minimize disruption                  | *Poor change management caused the NPS drop—we made too many changes at once.*                   |
| **Root cause analysis**         | Noun Phrase    | Identifying the underlying cause of a problem                                         | *Root cause analysis showed three issues, not one.*                                              |
| **Recovery plan**               | Noun Phrase    | A set of actions designed to restore a metric to its previous level                   | *Our recovery plan targets delivery, portions, and pricing.*                                     |
| **On-time delivery**            | Noun Phrase    | The percentage of orders delivered within the promised timeframe                       | *On-time delivery dropped from 95% to 88%—a major driver of dissatisfaction.*                   |
| **Portion size**                | Noun Phrase    | The amount of food in a meal                                                           | *Portion size complaints are concentrated among standard-tier customers.*                        |

## 4. Key Takeaways

### Common Sentence Patterns (for CX Storytelling)

| Pattern                                                              | Use                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| *"Everyone has a theory. Let me show you what the data says."*       | To signal that you're going to cut through opinions with facts.                        |
| *"I've analyzed the [problem] across three dimensions."*             | To introduce a structured analysis.                                                    |
| *"First, [Dimension A]—I segmented by [criteria]."*                 | To present segment-specific findings.                                                  |
| *"Second, [Dimension B]—I used [method] to identify [patterns]."*   | To show methodological rigor.                                                          |
| *"Third, [Dimension C]—the timing reveals [insight]."*              | To connect the findings to a timeline.                                                 |
| *"So the drop is not one problem—it's [X] problems."*               | To deliver the key insight: multiple causes.                                           |
| *"The root cause is [diagnosis]."*                                   | To name the underlying issue.                                                          |
| *"I've prioritized the fixes based on impact and effort."*           | To show strategic thinking.                                                            |
| *"The story isn't [singular cause]. The story is [multi-cause solution]."* | To reframe the narrative (anchor pattern).                                             |
| *"I'll build a [metric] dashboard tracking recovery by segment."*    | To commit to measurement and accountability.                                           |

### Useful Phrases to Remember
- **Introducing the segmentation:** *"When we treat all customers the same, we miss the nuance. Let me show you how different segments are reacting differently."*
- **When someone pushes their theory:** *"I hear you—let me show you what the data says about that specific factor."*
- **Introducing the "timing" dimension:** *"The drop didn't happen gradually—it happened exactly when we made three changes."*
- **When proposing the recovery plan:** *"We don't need to fix everything at once—we need to sequence the fixes based on impact."*
- **When the team is overwhelmed:** *"Three problems sounds overwhelming—but we have clear solutions for each one."*
- **When NPS recovers:** *"Our recovery plan worked—NPS is back to 65, and we've learned how to manage change better."*

### Practical Speaking Tips (for CX Narratives)
1.  **Segment, segment, segment.** NPS drops are rarely uniform. Segment by tenure, tier, geography, or behavior to pinpoint the problem.
2.  **Use text analysis for qualitative feedback.** Quantitative (NPS score) tells you *what*, but qualitative (comments) tells you *why*.
3.  **Connect operational changes to customer impact.** If you changed delivery, pricing, or product, link it to the metric drop.
4.  **Prioritize fixes by impact vs. effort.** Not all fixes are equal—quick wins should come first.
5.  **Create a "recovery roadmap."** Show the timeline for each fix and the projected NPS recovery.
6.  **Track NPS by segment weekly.** Segment-level tracking lets you see which segments are recovering—and which aren't.
7.  **Celebrate promoters, analyze detractors.** Promoters are your growth engine; detractors are your warning system.
8.  **Build a "Customer Pulse Dashboard."** Include NPS, CSAT, churn, and complaint volume—updated weekly.
9.  **Involve the whole team in CX.** CX is not just the CX team's job—Product, Ops, and Marketing all affect it.
10. **Learn from the recovery.** When NPS recovers, document what worked—so you don't repeat the same mistakes.
