# Phase 1: Grammar Foundations

## 2. Sentence Basics

Welcome to the next step in your grammar journey! Now that you understand the **parts of speech** (nouns, verbs, adjectives, etc.), it's time to see how they come together to form **sentences**. This section will teach you the core building blocks of every sentence you speak or write.

Think of it this way: *Parts of speech are the bricks; sentence basics are the blueprint for building a house.*

---

## 📖 How to Use This Guide

For every topic, follow this proven learning cycle:

1. **Learn** the concept and rules
2. **Study** sentence structures and formulas
3. **See** simple and real-life examples
4. **Learn** common mistakes and exceptions
5. **Complete** targeted exercises
6. **Practice** through conversations
7. **Write** your own sentences and short paragraphs
8. **Review** using quizzes and spaced repetition

## Topic Overview: The Big Picture

Before we dive into each concept individually, let's see how all these elements fit together:

```
[SENTENCE]
    │
    ├── [SUBJECT] → Who or what the sentence is about
    │
    ├── [PREDICATE] → What the subject does or is
    │       │
    │       ├── [VERB] → The action or state
    │       │
    │       ├── [OBJECT] → Who/what receives the action
    │       │
    │       └── [COMPLEMENT] → Completes the meaning
    │
    └── [PHRASES & CLAUSES] → Groups of words
            │
            ├── [PHRASE] → Group without subject-verb
            │
            └── [CLAUSE] → Group with subject-verb
                    │
                    ├── [INDEPENDENT] → Complete thought
                    │
                    └── [DEPENDENT] → Incomplete thought
```

Now, let's explore each component in detail!

## 1. SUBJECT

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** The **subject** is the person, place, thing, or idea that the sentence is about. It performs the action or is described by the verb.

**Key Points:**
- The subject is usually a noun, pronoun, or noun phrase
- It answers the question: "Who?" or "What?" before the verb
- Every complete sentence has a subject (except in commands/imperatives)

**Types of Subjects:**

| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Simple Subject** | Just the main noun/pronoun | The ***dog*** barked. |
| **Complete Subject** | The main noun + all its modifiers | ***The big brown dog*** barked. |
| **Compound Subject** | Two or more subjects joined | ***Tom and Jerry*** are friends. |
| **Implicit Subject** | Subject is understood (commands) | *(You)* Sit down. |

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Simple Subject**
```
[SIMPLE SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [REST]
The + car + is + fast.
```

**Structure 2: Complete Subject**
```
[COMPLETE SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [REST]
The big, blue car + is + fast.
```

**Structure 3: Compound Subject**
```
[SUBJECT 1] + [CONJUNCTION] + [SUBJECT 2] + [VERB] + [REST]
Sarah + and + John + work + together.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- ***She*** sings beautifully. (pronoun as subject)
- ***Music*** makes me happy. (noun as subject)
- ***Running*** is good for you. (gerund as subject)
- ***To learn*** takes time. (infinitive as subject)

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "***The new employee*** starts tomorrow." (complete subject)
- "***My brother and I*** are going to the game." (compound subject)
- "***It*** is raining outside." (dummy subject)
- "***The book on the shelf*** is mine." (complete subject with prepositional phrase)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Him is going. | He is going. (subject must be subject pronoun) |
| Me and my friend are here. | My friend and I are here. |
| The dogs is barking. | The dogs are barking. (subject-verb agreement) |
| There is two options. | There are two options. |

## 2. PREDICATE

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** The **predicate** is everything in the sentence that is not the subject. It tells us what the subject does, is, or experiences.

**Key Points:**
- The predicate always includes the verb
- It answers: "What about the subject?"
- Every complete sentence has a predicate

**Types of Predicates:**

| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Simple Predicate** | Just the verb/verb phrase | The dog ***barks***. |
| **Complete Predicate** | The verb + all its modifiers/objects | The dog ***barks loudly at strangers***. |
| **Compound Predicate** | Two or more verbs with one subject | The dog ***ran and barked***. |

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Simple Predicate**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB]
The car + stopped.
```

**Structure 2: Complete Predicate**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [OBJECT/COMPLEMENT] + [MODIFIERS]
The car + stopped + suddenly + in the middle of the road.
```

**Structure 3: Compound Predicate**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB 1] + [CONJUNCTION] + [VERB 2] + [REST]
She + opened + the door + and + walked + inside.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- Birds ***fly***. (simple predicate)
- They ***were singing*** in the rain. (verb phrase as simple predicate)
- He ***bought a new car***. (complete predicate)
- We ***studied hard and passed the exam***. (compound predicate)

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "The children ***played in the park all afternoon***." (complete predicate)
- "My mother ***cooked dinner and cleaned the kitchen***." (compound predicate)
- "The meeting ***will be held in the conference room at 3 PM***." (complete predicate with time/place)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She was and went home. | She woke up and went home. |
| He to the store. | He went to the store. (missing verb) |
| The people is happy. | The people are happy. (subject-verb agreement) |
| I finished and then I left. | I finished and then left. (redundant subject) |

### ✅ Complete: Exercises

**Exercise 1: Identify the Complete Predicate**
*Underline the complete predicate:*

1. The sun rises in the east.
2. My grandmother told us a fascinating story.
3. The children laughed and played in the garden.
4. She has been working there for five years.
5. The game was postponed due to rain.

**Exercise 2: Create Compound Predicates**
*Combine each pair:*

1. She opened the window. She looked out.
2. The dog chased the cat. The dog barked loudly.
3. He studied all night. He passed the test.

**Exercise 3: Correct the Errors**
1. He went and he bought.
2. The team has and will be successful.
3. They to the park.

### 💬 Practice: Conversations

**Dialogue: Telling a Story**
- A: "What happened at the party?"
- B: "The music ***played loudly***, and everyone ***danced until midnight***."
- A: "Did you enjoy it?"
- B: "I ***had a great time and met some new people***."

## 3. OBJECT

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** An **object** is a noun, pronoun, or noun phrase that receives the action of the verb or shows the result of the action.

**Types of Objects:**

| Type | Definition | Questions Answered | Examples |
|------|------------|-------------------|----------|
| **Direct Object** | Receives the action directly | Who? What? | She read ***a book***. |
| **Indirect Object** | Shows *to whom* or *for whom* | To whom? For whom? | She gave ***him*** a book. |
| **Object of Preposition** | Object that follows a preposition | Various | She sat ***on the chair***. |

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Direct Object**
```
[SUBJECT] + [TRANSITIVE VERB] + [DIRECT OBJECT]
He + ate + an apple.
```

**Structure 2: Indirect Object**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [INDIRECT OBJECT] + [DIRECT OBJECT]
She + gave + her friend + a gift.
```

**Structure 3: Object of Preposition**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [PREP.] + [OBJECT OF PREPOSITION]
He + sat + on + the chair.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- Direct Object: I bought ***a car***.
- Direct Object: She loves ***pizza***.
- Direct Object: They built ***a house***.
- Indirect Object: He gave ***me*** a present.
- Indirect Object: I cooked ***her*** dinner.
- Object of Preposition: She walked ***to the store***.

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "Could you pass ***me*** ***the salt***?" (indirect + direct)
- "I told ***him*** ***the truth***." (indirect + direct)
- "She carefully placed the vase ***on the table***." (object of preposition)
- "He sent ***his mother*** ***flowers*** for her birthday." (indirect + direct)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I gave to him a gift. | I gave him a gift. |
| She sent to me a letter. | She sent me a letter. |
| He bought for her flowers. | He bought her flowers. |
| I gave him it. | I gave it to him. |

### ✅ Complete: Exercises

**Exercise 1: Identify the Type of Object**
1. She bought flowers. (direct)
2. He gave her a ring. (indirect + direct)
3. They walked to the park. (object of preposition)
4. I sent my friend a message. (indirect + direct)

**Exercise 2: Fill in the Objects**
1. I gave _____ a book. (indirect object)
2. She read _____. (direct object)
3. He threw the ball _____ the wall. (object of preposition)

**Exercise 3: Correct the Errors**
1. I gave to him the keys.
2. She sent to me a letter.
3. He bought for her a gift.

### 💬 Practice: Conversations

**Dialogue: Gift Giving**

- B: "I bought ***her*** ***a beautiful scarf***."
- A: "That's lovely! Did you give ***her*** anything else?"

## 4. COMPLEMENT

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **complement** is a word or phrase that completes the meaning of a sentence. It tells us more about the subject or the object.

**Key Types:**

| Type | Function | Examples |
|------|----------|----------|
| **Subject Complement** | Follows linking verb, describes/redefines subject | She is ***a teacher***. |
| **Object Complement** | Follows direct object, describes/redefines it | They named him ***captain***. |
| **Adjective Complement** | Follows adjective, completes its meaning | He was happy ***to see her***. |

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Subject Complement (Noun)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [LINKING VERB] + [SUBJECT COMPLEMENT]
She + is + a doctor.
```

**Structure 2: Subject Complement (Adjective)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [LINKING VERB] + [SUBJECT COMPLEMENT]
The weather + is + beautiful.
```

**Structure 3: Object Complement**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [OBJECT] + [OBJECT COMPLEMENT]
We + elected + him + president.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- Subject Complement (Noun): He is ***a student***.
- Subject Complement (Adjective): The soup tastes ***delicious***.
- Subject Complement: She seems ***happy***.
- Object Complement: They painted the house ***white***.
- Object Complement: We consider her ***brilliant***.

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "The problem seems ***unsolvable***." (subject complement, adjective)
- "She remains ***our best hope***." (subject complement, noun)
- "The jury found him ***guilty***." (object complement)
- "The weather turned ***cold***." (subject complement)
- "His dream became ***reality***." (subject complement, noun)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She is a teacher and intelligent. | She is a teacher and intelligent. (mixed complement) |
| He feels happily. | He feels happy. (after linking verb, use adjective) |
| I am wanting to go. | I want to go. |
| The food tastes well. | The food tastes good. |

**Key Difference:**
- **Objects** receive the action of the verb
- **Complements** complete the meaning of the verb

### ✅ Complete: Exercises

**Exercise 1: Identify the Complement Type**
1. She became a doctor. (subject complement - noun)
2. The weather is cold. (subject complement - adjective)
3. We painted the room blue. (object complement)
4. He seems tired. (subject complement - adjective)

**Exercise 2: Fill in the Complements**

**Exercise 3: Correct the Errors**

### 💬 Practice: Conversations

- A: "Is she friendly?"
- B: "She's actually ***quite approachable***."
- A: "That sounds ***like a great change***."

## 5. PHRASE

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **phrase** is a group of related words that functions as a single unit within a sentence. Unlike a clause, a phrase does NOT contain a subject-verb pair.

**Types of Phrases:**

| Type | Structure | Function | Examples |
|------|-----------|----------|----------|
| **Noun Phrase** | Noun + modifiers | Acts as a noun | ***The big, red ball*** |
| **Verb Phrase** | Helping + main verb | Acts as verb | ***has been running*** |
| **Adjective Phrase** | Adjective + modifiers | Describes noun | ***very beautiful*** |
| **Adverb Phrase** | Adverb + modifiers | Modifies verb | ***very slowly*** |
| **Prepositional Phrase** | Preposition + object | Shows relationship | ***in the park*** |
| **Infinitive Phrase** | to + verb + object | Acts as noun/adj/adv | ***to learn English*** |
| **Gerund Phrase** | -ing verb + object | Acts as noun | ***running every day*** |
| **Participial Phrase** | -ing/-ed verb + object | Acts as adjective | ***running quickly*** |

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Noun Phrase as Subject**
```
[NOUN PHRASE] + [VERB] + [REST]
The beautiful, old house + stood + on the hill.
```

**Structure 2: Prepositional Phrase**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [PREPOSITIONAL PHRASE]
I + put + the keys on the table.
```

**Structure 3: Gerund Phrase as Subject**
```
[GERUND PHRASE] + [VERB] + [REST]
Running every morning + is + healthy.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- Noun Phrase: ***The tall man*** entered.
- Verb Phrase: They ***have been waiting***.
- Prepositional Phrase: The book is ***on the shelf***.
- Infinitive Phrase: ***To learn a language*** takes time.
- Gerund Phrase: ***Swimming in the ocean*** is fun.
- Participial Phrase: ***Excited about the news***, she called everyone.

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "***The lady in the blue dress*** is my aunt." (noun phrase)
- "She decided ***to take a different route***." (infinitive phrase)
- "***Having finished his homework***, he went out to play." (participial phrase)
- "I enjoy ***listening to classical music***." (gerund phrase)
- "He drove ***with great caution***." (adverb phrase)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| To learn is important. | Learning is important. |
| The man in the car who I saw. | The man in the car who I saw. (not a complete sentence) |
| Running quickly is fun. | Running quickly is fun. (✓) |
| Without a doubt, he is correct. | Without a doubt, he is correct. (✓) |

## 6. CLAUSE

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **clause** is a group of words that contains a subject and a verb.

**Key Point:** Every clause has a subject and a verb. The difference between a phrase and a clause is that a clause has both a subject and a verb; a phrase lacks at least one.

### 📐 Study: Clause Structures

**Structure 1: Basic Clause**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB]
She + sings.
```

**Structure 2: Extended Clause**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [OBJECT/COMPLEMENT] + [MODIFIERS]
She + sings + beautifully + in the choir.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- She runs. (clause with subject + verb)
- The cat is sleeping. (clause)
- She sings beautifully. (clause)
- That he arrived late. (clause - but incomplete thought)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Because I was tired. (fragment) | Because I was tired, I went to bed. |
| Running in the park. (fragment) | I was running in the park. |

### 🔄 Review: Quiz

1. What does every clause contain?
2. How is a clause different from a phrase?

## 7. INDEPENDENT CLAUSE

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** An **independent clause** (also called a main clause) is a group of words that contains a subject and a verb and expresses a complete thought. It can stand alone as a complete sentence.

**Key Points:**
- Contains subject + verb
- Expresses a complete thought
- Can stand alone as a sentence

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Independent Clause Alone**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [REST]
She + went + to the store.
```

**Structure 2: Independent Clause with Multiple Verbs**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB 1] + and + [VERB 2] + [REST]
She + went + to the store + and + bought + milk.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- I like coffee.
- She went home.
- The sun rises in the east.
- He is a doctor.
- They have been studying all night.

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "I'll call you when I arrive." (I'll call you - independent clause)
- "Although he was tired, he finished the work." (he finished the work - independent clause)
- "The train arrived late, but we caught it anyway." (both parts can stand alone)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I like coffee, she prefers tea. | I like coffee, and she prefers tea. |
| Because I was tired. (fragment) | I was tired. (or: Because I was tired, I went to bed.) |

### ✅ Complete: Exercises

**Exercise 1: Identify Independent Clauses**
1. She sings beautifully. (✓)
2. Because she sings beautifully. (✗)
3. I went to the store. (✓)
4. When I went to the store. (✗)

**Exercise 2: Correct the Fragments**
1. Because it was raining.
2. After she finished work.
3. Since I arrived late.

### 💬 Practice: Conversations

**Dialogue: Two Complete Thoughts**
- A: "I think I'll go to the gym today."

- A: "We can go together later."
- B: "That sounds like a good plan."

## 8. DEPENDENT CLAUSE

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **dependent clause** (also called a subordinate clause) is a group of words that contains a subject and a verb but does NOT express a complete thought. It cannot stand alone as a sentence.

**Key Points:**
- Contains subject + verb
- Does NOT express a complete thought
- CANNOT stand alone
- Often begins with a subordinating word (because, although, when, since, etc.)

**Common Subordinating Words:**

| Time | Reason | Condition | Contrast |
|------|--------|-----------|----------|
| when | because | if | although |
| while | since | unless | though |
| after | as | even if | whereas |
| before | so that | provided | while |
| until | in case | whether | despite |

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Dependent + Independent Clause**
```
[DEPENDENT CLAUSE] + [,] + [INDEPENDENT CLAUSE]
Because it was raining, + we stayed home.
```

**Structure 2: Independent + Dependent Clause**
```
[INDEPENDENT CLAUSE] + [DEPENDENT CLAUSE]
We stayed home + because it was raining.
```

**Structure 3: Dependent Clause in Middle**
```
[INDEPENDENT CLAUSE] + [DEPENDENT CLAUSE] + [REST]
The man, + who called earlier, + is here.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- Because she sings beautifully... (incomplete)
- When I went to the store... (incomplete)
- Although he was tired... (incomplete)
- Since she arrived... (incomplete)
- Who lives next door... (incomplete)

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "I'll call you ***when I arrive***."
- "***Because it was raining***, I stayed home."
- "She is the woman ***who lives next door***."
- "I won't go ***unless you come too***."
- "***Although he was sick***, he went to work."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Because I was tired. (fragment) | Because I was tired, I went to bed. |
| When she arrived. (fragment) | When she arrived, we all cheered. |
| I like the car. That is red. | I like the car that is red. |

**Rule for Punctuation:**
- If dependent clause comes FIRST → use a comma
- If dependent clause comes SECOND → no comma needed

### ✅ Complete: Exercises

## 🔗 Relationships Between Topics

Now that you understand each component, let's see how they work together:

```
Independent Clause (can stand alone)
    ├── Subject + Predicate
    │       ├── Subject (who/what)
    │       └── Predicate (verb + objects/complements)
    │
    └── Can contain Phrases:
            ├── Prepositional phrases
            ├── Gerund phrases
            ├── Infinitive phrases
            └── Participial phrases

Dependent Clause (cannot stand alone)
    ├── Subject + Predicate (like independent clause)
    ├── BUT + Subordinating word
    └── Cannot stand alone

Sentence = Independent Clause
    ├── + Dependent Clause(s)
    ├── + Phrases
    └── + Components (subject, predicate, objects, complements)
```

## 📚 Quick Reference Card

| Component | Definition | Example |
|-----------|------------|---------|
| **Subject** | Who/what sentence is about | ***The cat*** slept. |
| **Predicate** | What subject does/is | The cat ***slept on the mat***. |
| **Direct Object** | Receives action | I threw ***the ball***. |
| **Indirect Object** | To/for whom | I gave ***him*** the ball. |
| **Complement** | Completes meaning | She is ***a teacher***. |
| **Phrase** | No subject-verb | ***In the garden*** |
| **Clause** | Has subject-verb | ***She sings*** |
| **Independent Clause** | Complete thought | She sings. |
| **Dependent Clause** | Incomplete thought | ***Because she sings*** |
