# Topic: Managing a Corporate Reputation Recovery / Trust Restoration Campaign

**Scenario:** You are the Head of Corporate Communications at a global consumer goods company. Your company has experienced a major reputational crisis—a product safety issue that led to a recall, public backlash, and a 15% drop in stock price. The crisis has subsided, but trust has not fully recovered. The CEO has asked you to lead a comprehensive reputation recovery and trust restoration campaign. You are now presenting the campaign strategy to the executive committee (CEO, CFO, CMO, and Head of CSR) to secure approval and funding. Your goal is to: **acknowledge** the past mistakes, **demonstrate** corrective actions and new commitments, **engage** key stakeholders (customers, employees, investors, regulators), **communicate** a new narrative of transparency and accountability, **measure** the impact on brand perception, and **rebuild** long‑term trust.

---

## Dialogue

| **Speaker** | **Dialogue / Text** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **You** | *(opening the presentation)* Thank you for the time. We've moved past the crisis, but the damage to our reputation persists. Our brand trust scores are still 20% below pre‑crisis levels. I'm here to present a comprehensive reputation recovery and trust restoration campaign—designed to rebuild confidence, demonstrate accountability, and reposition our brand as a leader in ethical business practices. |
| **CEO** | What's the core strategy? |
| **You** | A three‑pillar approach: **#1**—Acknowledge and Apologize (a heartfelt, public recognition of our failures). **#2**—Act and Improve (demonstrating concrete changes in our products, processes, and governance). **#3**—Engage and Rebuild (actively listening to and collaborating with our stakeholders). |
| **CFO** | What's the investment required? |
| **You** | We're proposing a $10M campaign over 18 months: **$4M** for media and communications, **$3M** for stakeholder engagement and partnerships, **$2M** for brand research and measurement, and **$1M** for internal communications and employee engagement. We project a 3x ROI in brand equity recovery. |
| **CMO** | What's the key message? |
| **You** | The message is: "We've learned. We've changed. And we're committed to earning back your trust." We'll back this with evidence—new certifications, third‑party audits, and customer testimonials. |
| **Head of CSR** | And how do we engage stakeholders? |
| **You** | Three channels: **#1**—A CEO‑led listening tour with customers, employees, and community leaders. **#2**—A transparent progress dashboard on our website tracking our commitments. **#3**—A series of "transparency events" where we invite scrutiny and feedback. |
| **CEO** | What about the media narrative? We can't just control the message—we need to earn it. |
| **You** | We'll work with trusted journalists, influencers, and NGOs to tell our story. We'll also invite independent experts to review and validate our progress. |
| **CFO** | How do we measure success? |
| **You** | Five metrics: **#1**—Brand trust scores. **#2**—Net Promoter Score (NPS). **#3**—Media sentiment. **#4**—Employee engagement. **#5**—Stakeholder feedback. We'll track these quarterly and adjust the campaign as needed. |
| **CEO** | I'm convinced. Let's move forward. |
| **You** | Thank you. I'll send a detailed campaign plan with owners, budgets, and timelines by EOD. This is a long journey, but it's the most important one we'll take. |
| *(12 months later – progress update)* | |
| **You** | *(to the executive committee)* I'm pleased to report that our brand trust scores have recovered to 85% of pre‑crisis levels. NPS is up 12 points. Employee engagement is at an all‑time high. The campaign is working. |
| **CMO** | The transparency dashboard has been particularly well‑received. |
| **CEO** | This is proof that honesty and accountability are the best long‑term strategies. Thank you for leading this. |
| **You** | It's been a collective effort. We're not done yet—but we're on the right path. |
