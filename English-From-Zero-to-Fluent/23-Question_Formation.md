Excellent! You've mastered **Conditional Sentences**. Now we move to a practical, everyday skill Question Formation.

Asking questions is how we learn, connect, and get things done. If you can't ask questions, you can't have conversations. This topic will teach you the different types of questions and how to form them correctly.

# Topic 23: Question Formation

## 1. Concept Explanation

Questions are sentences that ask for information. In English, there are four main types of questions:


1. **Yes/No Questions (Closed Questions)** – Expect a "yes" or "no" answer.

    * Do you like coffee? → Yes / No.

2. **Wh- Questions (Open Questions)** – Ask for specific information using question words (Who, What, Where, When, Why, How).

    * Where do you live? → "I live in Mumbai."

3. **Choice Questions** – Offer two or more options.

    * Do you want tea or coffee? → "Coffee, please."

4. **Tag Questions** – Add a short question at the end of a statement to confirm information.

    * You like coffee, don't you? → "Yes, I do."


#### Difficult words explained:

* **Interrogative** = A question.

* **Auxiliary verb** = Helping verb (be, do, have, will, can, etc.).

#### The 80/20 Rule for Question Formation:

80% of the time, you will use **Yes/No Questions** and **Wh- Questions**. Master these two, and you'll be able to ask almost anything. The key is the **inversion** rule—putting the helping verb before the subject.

## 2. Rules (The Inversion Rule)

### Yes/No Questions:

**Formula:** `[Auxiliary Verb] + [Subject] + [Base Verb / Rest of Sentence]?`

| **Tense** | **Statement (Positive)** | **Question (Yes/No)** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Simple Present (be)** | She **is** a teacher. | **Is** she a teacher? |
| **Simple Present (action)** | She **works** hard. | **Does** she work hard? |
| **Simple Past (be)** | They **were** late. | **Were** they late? |
| **Simple Past (action)** | She **went** home. | **Did** she go home? |
| **Present Continuous** | She **is** working. | **Is** she working? |
| **Present Perfect** | She **has** gone. | **Has** she gone? |
| **Future (will)** | She **will** come. | **Will** she come? |
| **Modals** | She **can** swim. | **Can** she swim? |


### Wh- Questions:

**Formula:** `[Wh- Word] + [Auxiliary Verb] + [Subject] + [Base Verb / Rest of Sentence]?`

| **Wh- Word** | **Asks About** | **Example** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Who** | A person (subject) | **Who** called you? |
| **Whom** | A person (object) | **Whom** did you call? (Formal) |
| **What** | A thing, idea, or action | **What** are you doing? |
| **When** | Time | **When** will you come? |
| **Where** | Place | **Where** do you live? |
| **Why** | Reason | **Why** are you sad? |
| **How** | Manner, condition, or degree | **How** do you do that? |
| **Which** | Choice (specific options) | **Which** one do you want? |
| **Whose** | Ownership | **Whose** book is this? |


### Special Cases:

* **Who/What as Subject:** When *who* or *what* is the subject of the sentence, DO NOT invert. Use statement order.
    * *Who called you?* (**Who** is the subject; no auxiliary before it)
    * *What happened?* (**What** is the subject)
    * Compare: *Who did you call?* (**Who** is the object; auxiliary before subject)

### Tag Questions:

**Formula:**
> [Statement (Positive/Negative)] + [ , ] + [Auxiliary Verb (opposite polarity)] + [Subject]?

* **If the statement is positive, the tag is negative.**
    * *You are coming, aren't you?*
* **If the statement is negative, the tag is positive.**
    * *You aren't coming, are you?*

| **Statement** | **Tag** | **Full Question** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| She is a doctor, | isn't she? | She is a doctor, isn't she? |
| They work hard, | don't they? | They work hard, don't they? |
| You don't like pizza, | do you? | You don't like pizza, do you? |
| He hasn't called, | has he? | He hasn't called, has he? |
| We can go, | can't we? | We can go, can't we? |

**Intonation:**

* **Rising intonation (↗) = Genuine question (you don't know the answer).**
* **Falling intonation (↘) = Confirmation (you expect the answer to be yes).**

## 3. Structure Formula

### Yes/No Question:
> [Auxiliary Verb] + [Subject] + [Main Verb (base form / appropriate form)] + [Object/Complement]?

* *Does + she + eat + meat?*
* *Are + you + coming + to the party?*

### Wh- Question:
> [Wh- Word] + [Auxiliary Verb] + [Subject] + [Main Verb] + [Object/Complement]?

* *Where + do + you + live?*
* *Why + are + you + late?*

### Tag Question:
> [Statement] + [,] + [Auxiliary (opposite)] + [Subject]?

* *It's a beautiful day, + isn't + it?*

---

## 4. Examples

### Yes/No Questions (Positive)

1. **Are** you a student?
2. **Does** she like coffee?
3. **Did** you go to the party?
4. **Have** you eaten dinner?
5. **Will** you come tomorrow?

### Yes/No Questions (Negative)

1. **Aren't** you a student?
2. **Doesn't** she like coffee?
3. **Didn't** you go to the party?
4. **Haven't** you eaten dinner?
5. **Won't** you come tomorrow?

### Wh- Questions

1. **What** is your name?
2. **Where** do you live?
3. **When** did you arrive?
4. **Why** are you sad?
5. **How** do you know him?
6. **Who** called you? (*Who* = subject, no auxiliary)
7. **Whom** did you call? (*Whom* = object, auxiliary needed)
8. **Which** one do you prefer?
9. **Whose** phone is this?
10. **How much** does it cost?

### Tag Questions

1. You're a student, **aren't you?**
2. She likes coffee, **doesn't she?**
3. You didn't go to the party, **did you?**
4. He hasn't eaten yet, **has he?**
5. They won't come, **will they?**

## 6. Common Mistakes

| **Common Mistake** | **Why it is wrong** | **Correct Version** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **You are** coming? (Without inversion) | In English, we invert the subject and auxiliary for questions. | **Are you** coming? |
| **Where you** live? | Missing auxiliary '**do**'. | **Where do you** live? |
| **What you are** doing? | In Wh- questions, auxiliary comes before subject. | **What are you** doing? |
| **Who did called** you? | After '**did**', use the base verb. | **Who called you?** (No auxiliary needed if '**who**' is subject) OR **Who did you call?** (If '**who**' is object) |
| You like pizza, **isn't it?** <br><br>*(Actually correct!) Let's do: You like pizza, isn't it?* | The tag must match the auxiliary/verb in the main clause. '**Like**' uses '**do**'. | You like pizza, **don't you?** |
| She is coming, **is she?** (If you mean as a genuine question) <br><br>For a tag, if the statement is positive, the tag should be negative. If you say "She is coming, is she?" it sounds like surprise or disbelief. But for a standard tag, use negative. | Tag should be opposite polarity. | She is coming, **isn't she?** |
