# Phase 3: Verbs

## Types of Verbs

Welcome to Phase 3! After mastering **tenses**, it's time to dive deeper into the most important part of any sentence: **VERBS**. Understanding verb types is crucial because different verbs behave differently, and knowing these differences will dramatically improve your accuracy and fluency.

Think of it this way: *Tenses tell you WHEN an action happens. Verb types tell you WHAT KIND of action it is and HOW it works in a sentence. Together, they give you complete control over your English expression.*

---

## 📖 How to Use This Guide

For every topic, follow this proven learning cycle:

1. **Learn** the concept and rules
2. **Study** sentence structures and formulas
3. **See** simple and real-life examples
4. **Learn** common mistakes and exceptions
5. **Complete** targeted exercises
6. **Practice** through conversations
7. **Write** your own sentences and short paragraphs
8. **Review** using quizzes and spaced repetition

## Topic Overview: Types of Verbs

| Category | Definition | Examples |
|----------|------------|----------|
| **Main Verbs** | The primary verb showing action/state | She **runs** every day. |
| **Helping Verbs** | Assist the main verb to form tenses | She **has** been running. |
| **Auxiliary Verbs** | Help form questions, negatives, tenses | **Do** you like it? |
| **Modal Verbs** | Express possibility, necessity, ability | She **can** swim. |
| **Linking Verbs** | Connect subject to a description | She **is** a doctor. |
| **Action Verbs** | Show physical or mental action | She **runs** / **thinks**. |
| **Stative Verbs** | Describe states, not actions | She **knows** the answer. |
| **Transitive Verbs** | Take a direct object | She **bought** a car. |
| **Intransitive Verbs** | Don't take a direct object | She **sleeps**. |

## 1. MAIN VERBS

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **main verb** (also called a lexical verb) is the primary verb in a sentence that carries the core meaning. It expresses the main action or state of being.

**Key Points:**
- Every sentence has at least one main verb
- Main verbs carry the meaning of the sentence
- They can stand alone or be helped by helping verbs
- They are the most important word for understanding the action

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Main Verb Alone**
```
[SUBJECT] + [MAIN VERB] + [REST]
She + sings + beautifully.
```

**Structure 2: Main Verb with Helping Verb**
```
[SUBJECT] + [HELPING VERB] + [MAIN VERB] + [REST]
She + has + been + singing + all day.
```

**Structure 3: Multiple Main Verbs**
```
[SUBJECT] + [MAIN VERB 1] + and + [MAIN VERB 2] + [REST]
She + sings + and + dances + well.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- "She **works** at a hospital." (main verb - action)
- "He **feels** tired." (main verb - state)
- "They **have** a new car." (main verb - possession)
- "I **think** you're right." (main verb - mental action)
- "We **will eat** dinner soon." (main verb with helping verb)

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "I **need** some help with this project."
- "She **lives** in a small apartment downtown."
- "They **built** their house from scratch."
- "He **makes** everyone feel welcome."
- "I **understand** your concern."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I am like coffee. | I **like** coffee. |
| She is need help. | She **needs** help. |
| They are want to go. | They **want** to go. |
| I am agree. | I **agree**. |

## 2. HELPING VERBS

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **helping verb** (also called an auxiliary verb) is a verb that "helps" the main verb to express tense, mood, or voice. It does not carry the main meaning but adds grammatical information.

**Key Points:**
- Helping verbs come before the main verb
- They help form tenses (has been working)
- They help form questions (Do you like it?)
- They help form negatives (I don't like it.)

**Common Helping Verbs:**

| Type | Examples |
|------|----------|
| **Be verbs** | am, is, are, was, were |
| **Have verbs** | have, has, had |
| **Do verbs** | do, does, did |
| **Modals** | can, could, will, would, shall, should, may, might, must |

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Helping Verb + Main Verb**
```
[SUBJECT] + [HELPING VERB] + [MAIN VERB] + [REST]
She + is + working + now.
They + have + eaten + dinner.
```

**Structure 2: Multiple Helping Verbs**
```
[SUBJECT] + [HELPING 1] + [HELPING 2] + [MAIN VERB] + [REST]
She + has + been + working + all day.
It + might + have + been + stolen.
```

**Structure 3: Negative with Helping Verb**
```
[SUBJECT] + [HELPING] + [NOT] + [MAIN VERB] + [REST]
She + is + not + coming + today.
They + have + not + finished + yet.
```

**Structure 4: Question with Helping Verb**
```
[HELPING] + [SUBJECT] + [MAIN VERB] + [REST]?
Is + she + coming + today?
Have + you + finished + yet?
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- Be verbs: "She **is** sleeping." / "They **are** coming."
- Have verbs: "I **have** finished." / "She **has** called."
- Do verbs: "**Do** you like it?" / "I **don't** know."
- Modals: "I **can** swim." / "She **will** come."

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "She **has been** working here for five years."
- "They **are going to** move next month."
- "**Does** your brother speak English?"
- "I **would have called** you if I had known."
- "The car **must have been** stolen."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She doesn't works. | She doesn't **work**. |
| I have went. | I have **gone**. |
| She is cook dinner. | She **is cooking** dinner. |
| They have finished? | **Have** they finished? |

## 3. AUXILIARY VERBS

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Auxiliary verbs** are helping verbs used to form tenses, questions, negatives, and the passive voice. They are "helper" verbs that support the main verb.

**Key Points:**
- Auxiliary verbs are a subset of helping verbs
- They include: be, do, have (and their forms)
- They do NOT include modals (those are a separate category)
- They are essential for forming many grammatical structures

**The Three Primary Auxiliary Verbs:**

| Auxiliary | Forms | Uses |
|-----------|-------|------|
| **Be** | am, is, are, was, were, been, being | Continuous tenses, passive voice |
| **Have** | have, has, had | Perfect tenses |
| **Do** | do, does, did | Questions, negatives, emphasis |

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Be as Auxiliary (Continuous)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [BE] + [VERB + ING]
She + is + reading.
They + were + playing.
```

**Structure 2: Be as Auxiliary (Passive)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [BE] + [PAST PARTICIPLE]
The book + was + written.
The house + is + being + built.
```

**Structure 3: Have as Auxiliary (Perfect)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [HAVE] + [PAST PARTICIPLE]
She + has + left.
They + had + finished.
```

**Structure 4: Do as Auxiliary (Questions/Negatives)**
```
[DO] + [SUBJECT] + [BASE VERB]?
Do + you + like + coffee?

[SUBJECT] + [DO NOT] + [BASE VERB]
She + does not + like + coffee.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- Be (continuous): "She **is** singing." / "They **were** playing."
- Be (passive): "The cake **was** eaten." / "The house **is being** cleaned."
- Have (perfect): "I **have** seen that." / "She **had** left."
- Do (question): "**Do** you know him?" / "**Does** she work here?"
- Do (negative): "I **don't** like it." / "She **doesn't** want to go."

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "The film **is being** shown at the cinema."
- "I **have been** waiting for you."
- "**Did** you remember to lock the door?"
- "She **does** like chocolate!" (emphasis)
- "The new system **was being** developed when the company closed."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She don't like it. | She **doesn't** like it. |
| Have you went? | **Have** you **gone**? |
| She is come. | She **has come**. |
| What you want? | What **do** you want? |

**Be vs. Have vs. Do:**

| Auxiliary | Use | Example |
|-----------|-----|---------|
| **Be** | Continuous tenses | She **is** working. |
| **Be** | Passive voice | It **was** done. |
| **Have** | Perfect tenses | I **have** seen it. |
| **Do** | Questions | **Do** you like it? |
| **Do** | Negatives | I **don't** like it. |

## 4. MODAL VERBS

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Modal verbs** are special auxiliary verbs that express possibility, necessity, ability, permission, obligation, or advice. They modify the meaning of the main verb.

**Key Points:**
- Modal verbs never change form (no -s, -ing, -ed)
- They always come before the base form of the verb
- They express the speaker's attitude
- They don't need "to" after them (except "ought to")

**The Nine Core Modal Verbs:**

| Modal | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **Can** | Ability, permission | I **can** swim. |
| **Could** | Past ability, polite request | **Could** you help me? |
| **May** | Permission, possibility | **May** I come in? |
| **Might** | Possibility (less certain) | It **might** rain. |
| **Will** | Future, willingness | I **will** help you. |
| **Would** | Polite request, hypothetical | **Would** you like tea? |
| **Shall** | Suggestion (with I/we) | **Shall** I open the window? |
| **Should** | Advice, obligation | You **should** rest. |
| **Must** | Strong obligation | You **must** leave now. |

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Modal + Base Verb**
```
[SUBJECT] + [MODAL] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
She + can + speak + French.
I + should + go + now.
```

**Structure 2: Negative Modal**
```
[SUBJECT] + [MODAL] + [NOT] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
She + cannot + speak + French.
I + should + not + go + now.
```

**Structure 3: Modal Questions**
```
[MODAL] + [SUBJECT] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]?
Can + she + speak + French?
Should + I + go + now?
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- Ability: "I **can** swim." / "I **could** swim when I was 5."
- Permission: "**May** I leave now?" / "You **can** use my phone."
- Possibility: "It **may** rain." / "He **might** be late."
- Obligation: "You **must** wear a seatbelt."
- Advice: "You **should** see a doctor."
- Request: "**Would** you open the door?" / "**Could** you help me?"
- Future: "I **will** call you tomorrow."

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "You **must** be kidding!" (certainty)
- "I **should have called** you earlier." (past regret)
- "**Can** I borrow your pen?" (permission)
- "She **might have forgotten** the meeting." (past possibility)
- "You **ought to** apologize." (advice - using "ought to")

**Modal Verb Meanings Chart:**

| Modal | Past Form | Negative | Past Negative |
|-------|-----------|----------|---------------|
| can | could | cannot / can't | couldn't |
| may | might | may not | might not |
| must | had to | must not / mustn't | didn't have to |
| should | should have | should not / shouldn't | shouldn't have |
| will | would | will not / won't | wouldn't |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She cans swim. | She **can swim**. |
| I must to go. | I **must go**. |
| He shoulds work harder. | He **should work** harder. |
| I can't to come. | I **can't come**. |
| May you help me? | **Can/Could** you help me? |

**Modal Verb Rules:**
1. No -s for third person: ❌ He cans ✓ He can
2. No "to" after modal: ❌ I must to go ✓ I must go
3. Use base verb after modal: ❌ She should going ✓ She should go
4. Use "have + past participle" for past: ❌ I should went ✓ I should have gone

## 5. LINKING VERBS

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **linking verb** connects the subject of a sentence to a word or phrase that describes or identifies it. Unlike action verbs, linking verbs don't show action; they show a state of being or a condition.

**Key Points:**
- Linking verbs connect subject to a complement
- They don't express action
- The complement (subject complement) describes or renames the subject
- The most common linking verb is "be" (am, is, are, was, were, been, being)

**Common Linking Verbs:**

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **Be verbs** | am, is, are, was, were, been, being |
| **Sensory verbs** | look, smell, taste, feel, sound |
| **State verbs** | seem, appear, become, remain, stay, grow, turn |

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Linking Verb + Noun Complement**
```
[SUBJECT] + [LINKING VERB] + [NOUN COMPLEMENT]
She + is + a doctor.
He + became + president.
```

**Structure 2: Linking Verb + Adjective Complement**
```
[SUBJECT] + [LINKING VERB] + [ADJECTIVE COMPLEMENT]
She + is + happy.
The food + tastes + delicious.
```

**Structure 3: Linking Verb + Prepositional Phrase**
```
[SUBJECT] + [LINKING VERB] + [PREPOSITIONAL PHRASE]
The keys + are + on the table.
She + is + in the kitchen.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- "She **is** a teacher." (noun complement)
- "He **feels** tired." (adjective complement)
- "The soup **tastes** great." (adjective complement)
- "They **seem** nice." (adjective complement)
- "She **became** a doctor." (noun complement)

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "He **looks** tired after the long journey."
- "The flowers **smell** wonderful."
- "She **appears** to be very confident."
- "The weather **turned** cold."
- "They **remained** silent throughout the meeting."

**Sensory Linking Verbs vs. Action Verbs:**

| Linking Verb (State) | Action Verb (Action) |
|----------------------|----------------------|
| She **looks** beautiful. (adjective describes her) | She **looks** at the painting. (action of looking) |
| The soup **smells** good. (adjective describes soup) | She **smells** the soup. (action of smelling) |
| He **feels** happy. (adjective describes him) | He **feels** the fabric. (action of touching) |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| He feels happily. | He feels **happy**. (adjective, not adverb) |
| The soup tastes well. | The soup tastes **good**. |
| She looks beautifully. | She looks **beautiful**. |
| I am want to go. | I **want** to go. (want is an action, not linking) |

**Linking Verbs vs. Action Verbs:**

| Sentence | Type | Test |
|----------|------|------|
| She is a teacher. | Linking | Can replace with "=" |
| She runs fast. | Action | Not linking |
| He looks tired. | Linking | Describes subject |
| He looks at the window. | Action | Physical action |

## 6. ACTION VERBS

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** An **action verb** expresses a physical or mental action performed by the subject. It tells what the subject does.

**Key Points:**
- Shows physical or mental action
- Can be transitive (takes an object) or intransitive (no object)
- Most common type of verb
- Answers the question "What is the subject doing?"

**Types of Action Verbs:**

| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Physical** | Actions you can see | run, walk, eat, write, build |
| **Mental** | Actions you can't see | think, believe, know, want, understand |

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Action Verb + Object**
```
[SUBJECT] + [ACTION VERB] + [OBJECT]
She + bought + a car.
He + wrote + a letter.
```

**Structure 2: Action Verb Alone**
```
[SUBJECT] + [ACTION VERB] + [ADVERB]
She + runs + quickly.
He + sleeps + peacefully.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- Physical: "She **runs** every morning."
- Physical: "He **eats** breakfast at 7 AM."
- Mental: "I **think** you're right."
- Mental: "She **believes** in hard work."
- Physical: "They **built** a house."
- Mental: "I **understand** the problem."

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "I **woke up** early and **went** to the gym."
- "She **studied** all night for her exam."
- "They **traveled** to Europe last summer."
- "We **need** to make a decision quickly."
- "He **wrote** a beautiful poem."

**Action Verbs vs. Linking Verbs Test:**

If you can replace the verb with "is," "am," or "are" and the sentence still makes sense, it's a linking verb.

- She **runs** fast. → She **is** fast. (Not the same meaning → Action)
- She **is** fast. → Linking (describes her)
- He **feels** the fabric. → He **is** the fabric. (Nonsense → Action)
- He **feels** tired. → He **is** tired. (Makes sense → Linking)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She is sleep. | She **sleeps**. |
| I am agree. | I **agree**. |
| He is want to go. | He **wants** to go. |
| They are need help. | They **need** help. |

## 7. STATIVE VERBS

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **stative verb** describes a state, condition, or situation rather than an action. It tells how things are, not what is happening.

**Key Points:**
- Describe states, not actions
- Usually NOT used in continuous tenses
- Express thoughts, feelings, senses, possession, etc.

**Categories of Stative Verbs:**

| Category | Verbs | Examples |
|----------|-------|----------|
| **Thoughts/Opinions** | believe, think, know, understand, suppose | I **believe** you. |
| **Emotions/Feelings** | love, hate, like, prefer, want, need | She **loves** chocolate. |
| **Senses** | see, hear, smell, taste, feel | I **see** the car. |
| **Possession** | have, own, possess, belong, contain | He **has** a car. |
| **Measurement** | weigh, measure, cost | The book **costs** $20. |
| **Others** | seem, appear, be, consist, depend | It **seems** difficult. |

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Stative Verb (Simple Form)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [STATIVE VERB] + [REST]
I + believe + you.
She + loves + chocolate.
```

**Structure 2: Stative Verb (Not Continuous)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [STATIVE VERB] + [REST]
(I am believing you) ❌
I believe you. ✓
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- Thoughts: "I **know** the answer." (NOT: I am knowing)
- Feelings: "She **likes** coffee." (NOT: She is liking)
- Senses: "I **see** the bird." (NOT: I am seeing)
- Possession: "He **has** a car." (NOT: He is having)
- Measurement: "It **weighs** 5 kg." (NOT: It is weighing)

**Stative vs. Action Verbs:**

| Stative (State) | Action (Dynamic) |
|-----------------|------------------|
| I **have** a car. (possession) | I **am having** lunch. (eating) |
| I **think** you're right. (opinion) | I **am thinking** about you. (mental process) |
| She **looks** beautiful. (appearance) | She **is looking** at the picture. (action) |
| I **see** the bird. (sense) | I **am seeing** a doctor. (meeting) |

**Important:** Some verbs can be both stative AND action depending on context:

| Verb | Stative Use | Action Use |
|------|-------------|------------|
| **have** | I have a car. (possess) | I am having lunch. (eating) |
| **think** | I think you're right. (opinion) | I am thinking about it. (process) |
| **look** | She looks beautiful. (appearance) | She is looking at the window. (action) |
| **see** | I see the problem. (understand) | I am seeing a friend. (meeting) |
| **taste** | The soup tastes good. (quality) | She is tasting the soup. (action) |
| **feel** | I feel tired. (state) | I am feeling the fabric. (action) |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I am knowing the answer. | I **know** the answer. |
| She is loving chocolate. | She **loves** chocolate. |
| I am wanting to go. | I **want** to go. |
| I am understanding you. | I **understand** you. |
| He is having a car. | He **has** a car. |

## 8. TRANSITIVE VERBS

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **transitive verb** is an action verb that requires a direct object to complete its meaning. The action transfers from the subject to the object.

**Key Points:**
- Requires a direct object
- Answers "what?" or "whom?" after the verb
- The action "transfers" to something/someone

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Transitive Verb + Object**
```
[SUBJECT] + [TRANSITIVE VERB] + [DIRECT OBJECT]
She + bought + a car.
He + wrote + a letter.
They + built + a house.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- "She **bought** a car." (What did she buy? → a car)
- "He **wrote** a letter." (What did he write? → a letter)
- "I **need** help." (What do I need? → help)
- "They **built** a house." (What did they build? → a house)

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "I **ate** an apple for breakfast." (What did I eat? → an apple)
- "She **likes** classical music." (What does she like? → classical music)
- "He **hit** the ball." (What did he hit? → the ball)
- "We **visited** our grandparents." (Whom did we visit? → our grandparents)

**Transitive Verb Test:**
Ask "what?" or "whom?" after the verb. If you get an answer, it's transitive.

- She bought... **what?** → a car. (transitive)
- He wrote... **what?** → a letter. (transitive)
- She smiled. (No question → intransitive)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I bought. (missing object) | I bought **a car**. |
| She likes. (missing object) | She likes **pizza**. |
| He wrote. (missing object) | He wrote **a letter**. |

## 9. INTRANSITIVE VERBS

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** An **intransitive verb** is an action verb that does NOT take a direct object. The action does not transfer to anything.

**Key Points:**
- Does NOT require a direct object
- The action is complete without an object
- Often followed by adverbs or prepositional phrases

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Intransitive Verb Alone**
```
[SUBJECT] + [INTRANSITIVE VERB] + [ADVERB]
She + runs + quickly.
He + slept + peacefully.
```

**Structure 2: Intransitive Verb + Prepositional Phrase**
```
[SUBJECT] + [INTRANSITIVE VERB] + [PREPOSITIONAL PHRASE]
She + runs + in the park.
He + sleeps + on the sofa.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- "She **sleeps**." (Complete meaning)
- "He **cried**." (No object needed)
- "They **arrived**." (No object needed)
- "She **smiled**." (No object needed)

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "I **woke up** early this morning."
- "He **laughed** at the joke."
- "They **traveled** to Europe."
- "She **ran** quickly to catch the bus."

**Intransitive Verb Test:**
Ask "what?" or "whom?" after the verb. If you can't answer, it's intransitive.

- She ran... what? (No answer → intransitive)
- He slept... what? (No answer → intransitive)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I bought. (should have object) | I **bought a car**. |
| She arrived the station. | She arrived **at** the station. |
| They reached to the hotel. | They **reached** the hotel. (transitive) |

**Common Intransitive Verbs:**

| Verb | Example |
|------|---------|
| arrive | She arrived at 5 PM. |
| sleep | He sleeps for 8 hours. |
| run | She runs every morning. |
| laugh | They laughed loudly. |
| cry | He cried. |
| work | I work hard. |
| happen | It happened quickly. |

## 📚 Quick Reference: Verb Types Summary

| Verb Type | Definition | Example |
|-----------|------------|---------|
| **Main Verb** | Core meaning of sentence | She **works**. |
| **Helping Verb** | Assists main verb | She **is** working. |
| **Auxiliary Verb** | Forms tenses, questions | **Do** you like it? |
| **Modal Verb** | Expresses possibility/necessity | She **can** swim. |
| **Linking Verb** | Connects subject to description | She **is** tired. |
| **Action Verb** | Shows physical/mental action | She **runs**. |
| **Stative Verb** | Describes a state | She **knows**. |
| **Transitive Verb** | Takes a direct object | She **bought** a car. |
| **Intransitive Verb** | Takes no object | She **sleeps**. |
