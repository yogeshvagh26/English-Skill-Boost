# Phase 3: Verbs

## Subject-Verb Agreement

Welcome to Subject-Verb Agreement! This is one of the most important and frequently tested areas of English grammar. **Subject-Verb Agreement** means that the subject and verb in a sentence must match in number — singular subjects take singular verbs, and plural subjects take plural verbs.

Think of it this way: *You've learned about verb types (what verbs are) and verb forms (how verbs change). Now you need to learn how to make verbs AGREE with their subjects. This is essential for speaking and writing correctly.*

## Topic Overview: The Golden Rule

### 📚 The Basic Rule

**The Golden Rule of Subject-Verb Agreement:**

> A **singular** subject takes a **singular** verb.
> A **plural** subject takes a **plural** verb.

| Subject | Verb | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| Singular | Singular (V5) | She **works** hard. |
| Plural | Plural (V1) | They **work** hard. |

**Quick Reference:**

| Subject Type | Verb Form | Example |
|--------------|-----------|---------|
| I | V1 | I **work**. |
| You (singular) | V1 | You **work**. |
| He/She/It | V5 (V + s/es) | She **works**. |
| We | V1 | We **work**. |
| You (plural) | V1 | You **work**. |
| They | V1 | They **work**. |
| Singular Noun | V5 | The dog **barks**. |
| Plural Noun | V1 | The dogs **bark**. |

## 📐 Detailed Rules with Examples

### Rule 1: Singular vs. Plural Subjects

| Singular Subject | Singular Verb | Plural Subject | Plural Verb |
|------------------|---------------|----------------|-------------|
| The dog | **barks** | The dogs | **bark** |
| She | **sings** | They | **sing** |
| The child | **plays** | The children | **play** |
| He | **goes** | They | **go** |
| It | **works** | They | **work** |

**Examples:**
- "The cat **sleeps** on the sofa." (singular)
- "The cats **sleep** on the sofa." (plural)
- "She **studies** every day." (singular)
- "They **study** every day." (plural)

---

### Rule 2: Compound Subjects with "And"

**Rule:** Subjects joined by "and" are usually plural and take a plural verb.

**Structure:**
```
[SUBJECT 1] + [AND] + [SUBJECT 2] + [PLURAL VERB]
Tom + and + Jerry + are + friends.
```

**Examples:**
- "Tom and Jerry **are** friends." ✓
- "My brother and sister **live** in London." ✓
- "The teacher and the student **are** in the classroom." ✓

**Exception:** When the compound subject refers to one person/thing or is considered a single unit:
- "Peanut butter and jelly **is** my favorite sandwich." (one item)
- "The president and CEO **is** arriving soon." (one person)

---

### Rule 3: Compound Subjects with "Or" / "Nor"

**Rule:** When subjects are joined by "or" or "nor," the verb agrees with the subject closest to it.

**Structure:**
```
[SUBJECT 1] + [OR/NOR] + [SUBJECT 2] + [VERB AGREES WITH SUBJECT 2]
Neither + the teacher + nor + the students + are + here.
Either + the students + or + the teacher + is + here.
```

**Examples:**
- "Neither the teacher nor the students **are** here." (students = plural)
- "Either the students or the teacher **is** here." (teacher = singular)
- "Neither John nor his friends **are** coming." (friends = plural)
- "Neither his friends nor John **is** coming." (John = singular)

---

### Rule 4: Indefinite Pronouns

**Rule:** Most indefinite pronouns are singular and take singular verbs.

**Singular Indefinite Pronouns:**

| Pronoun | Example |
|---------|---------|
| everyone | Everyone **is** here. |
| everybody | Everybody **enjoys** the party. |
| everything | Everything **is** ready. |
| someone | Someone **is** at the door. |
| somebody | Somebody **has** called. |
| something | Something **smells** good. |
| anyone | Anyone **is** welcome. |
| anybody | Anybody **can** do it. |
| anything | Anything **is** possible. |
| no one | No one **knows** the answer. |
| nobody | Nobody **likes** waiting. |
| nothing | Nothing **matters** more. |
| each | Each **has** a different opinion. |
| either | Either **is** acceptable. |
| neither | Neither **works** correctly. |

**Plural Indefinite Pronouns:**

| Pronoun | Example |
|---------|---------|
| both | Both **are** correct. |
| few | Few **know** the truth. |
| many | Many **have** tried. |
| several | Several **are** available. |

**Indefinite Pronouns That Can Be Singular or Plural:**

| Pronoun | Singular | Plural |
|---------|----------|--------|
| all | All **is** lost. | All **are** coming. |
| some | Some **is** left. | Some **are** here. |
| none | None **is** perfect. | None **are** perfect. |
| any | Any **is** fine. | Any **are** welcome. |

---

### Rule 5: Collective Nouns

**Rule:** Collective nouns can be singular OR plural depending on meaning.

**Common Collective Nouns:** team, family, group, committee, audience, class, staff, crowd, jury, government

| Usage | Verb | Example |
|-------|------|---------|
| Acting as ONE unit | Singular | The team **is** winning. |
| Acting as individuals | Plural | The team **are** arguing among themselves. |

**Examples:**
- "The family **is** planning a vacation." (one unit)
- "The family **are** having dinner together." (individuals)
- "The committee **has** made a decision." (one decision)
- "The committee **have** different opinions." (different members)
- "The audience **is** applauding." (one unit)
- "The audience **are** taking their seats." (individuals)

**Note:** In American English, collective nouns are usually treated as singular. In British English, they can be plural.

---

### Rule 6: Singular Subjects with Plural Forms

**Rule:** Some nouns look plural but are actually singular.

| Noun | Verb | Example |
|------|------|---------|
| news | Singular | The news **is** good. |
| mathematics | Singular | Mathematics **is** difficult. |
| physics | Singular | Physics **is** fascinating. |
| economics | Singular | Economics **is** my major. |
| politics | Singular | Politics **is** complicated. |
| athletics | Singular | Athletics **is** important. |
| gymnastics | Singular | Gymnastics **is** fun to watch. |
| measles | Singular | Measles **is** contagious. |
| mumps | Singular | Mumps **is** a childhood disease. |

**Examples:**
- "The news **is** very interesting today."
- "Mathematics **is** not my favorite subject."
- "Politics **is** a controversial topic."
- "Physics **is** a challenging subject."

---

### Rule 7: Plural Subjects with Singular Forms

**Rule:** Some nouns look singular but are actually plural.

| Noun | Verb | Example |
|------|------|---------|
| people | Plural | People **are** friendly. |
| police | Plural | The police **are** investigating. |
| cattle | Plural | The cattle **are** grazing. |
| clothes | Plural | Her clothes **are** expensive. |
| scissors | Plural | The scissors **are** sharp. |
| trousers | Plural | My trousers **are** new. |
| glasses | Plural | His glasses **are** broken. |
| jeans | Plural | My jeans **are** too tight. |

**Examples:**
- "People **are** generally kind."
- "The police **are** looking for the suspect."
- "These scissors **are** very sharp."
- "My glasses **are** on the table."

---

### Rule 8: Titles and Names

**Rule:** Titles of books, movies, etc., are always singular.

**Examples:**
- "The Chronicles of Narnia **is** a great book series." (title = singular)
- "The United States **is** a large country." (country = singular)
- "The Philippines **is** an island nation." (country = singular)
- "The Beatles **is** my favorite band." (band = singular)

---

### Rule 9: Amounts and Measurements

**Rule:** Amounts of time, money, distance, weight, etc., are usually singular when considered as a whole.

**Examples:**
- "Five hundred dollars **is** a lot of money." (one amount)
- "Ten miles **is** a long distance." (one distance)
- "Three years **is** a long time to wait." (one period)
- "Five kilograms **is** heavy." (one weight)
- "Half of the pizza **is** mine." (one part)

**But:** "Five dollars **are** on the table." (individual bills, not one amount)

---

### Rule 10: Fractions and Percentages

**Rule:** The verb agrees with the noun after "of."

**Structure:**
```
[FRACTION/PERCENTAGE] + [OF] + [NOUN] + [VERB]
Half + of + the cake + is + gone.
Half + of + the students + are + present.
```

**Examples:**
- "Half of the water **is** gone." (water = singular)
- "Half of the students **are** present." (students = plural)
- "50% of the population **is** vaccinated." (population = singular)
- "50% of the people **are** vaccinated." (people = plural)
- "A third of the pie **is** left." (pie = singular)
- "A third of the pies **are** left." (pies = plural)

---

### Rule 11: "There is" / "There are"

**Rule:** The verb agrees with the noun that follows "there."

**Structure:**
```
[THERE] + [VERB] + [NOUN]
There + is + a book + on the table.
There + are + books + on the table.
```

**Examples:**
- "There **is** a cat in the garden."
- "There **are** cats in the garden."
- "There **is** some milk in the fridge."
- "There **are** some eggs in the fridge."
- "There **is** a problem with the car."
- "There **are** problems with the car."

---

### Rule 12: Relative Clauses (Who, Which, That)

**Rule:** The verb in a relative clause agrees with the antecedent (the word it refers to).

**Examples:**
- "She is one of the students **who speak** French." (students = plural)
- "She is the only student **who speaks** French." (student = singular)
- "The book **that is** on the table is mine." (book = singular)
- "The books **that are** on the table are mine." (books = plural)

---

### Rule 13: "Each" and "Every"

**Rule:** "Each" and "every" take singular verbs.

**Examples:**
- "Each student **has** a book."
- "Every student **has** a book."
- "Each of the students **has** a book."
- "Every one of the students **is** here."
- "Each and every student **is** responsible."

---

### Rule 14: "A number of" vs. "The number of"

| Expression | Verb | Example |
|------------|------|---------|
| **A number of** (some) | Plural | A number of students **are** here. |
| **The number of** (the total) | Singular | The number of students **is** 50. |

**Examples:**
- "A number of people **have** applied for the job." (some people)
- "The number of applicants **has** increased." (the total)
- "A number of issues **need** attention." (some issues)
- "The number of issues **is** growing." (the total)

---

### Rule 15: Inverted Sentences (Questions)

**Rule:** In questions, the verb agrees with the subject that comes after it.

**Examples:**
- "**Is** she coming?" (she = singular)
- "**Are** they coming?" (they = plural)
- "**Does** he work here?" (he = singular)
- "**Do** they work here?" (they = plural)
- "**Has** she finished?" (she = singular)
- "**Have** they finished?" (they = plural)

| There **is** many problems. | There **are** many problems. |

## 💬 Practice: Conversations

### Dialogue 1: Talking About People

- A: "Everyone **is** here except Sarah."
- B: "**Are** the others coming later?"
- A: "The Smith family **is** planning to come."
- B: "**Does** anyone know what time?"
- A: "Neither John nor his sisters **know** the time."

### Dialogue 2: At Work

- A: "The news **is** not good today."
- B: "**Is** there anything we can do?"
- A: "The management **is** discussing the issue."
- B: "**Are** they going to make a decision soon?"
- A: "The number of problems **is** growing."

### Dialogue 3: Making Plans

- A: "Neither of the options **seems** good."
- B: "**Is** there a better alternative?"
- A: "A number of people **have** suggested alternatives."
- B: "**Are** they all valid?"
- A: "Each one **has** its own merits."
