# Phase 1: Grammar Foundations

## 4. Sentence Structure

Welcome to the next crucial step in your grammar journey! You've learned the **parts of speech** (the building blocks), **sentence basics** (the foundation), and **types of sentences** (the purposes). Now it's time to understand **sentence structure** — how clauses combine to create different kinds of sentences.

Think of it this way: *You know the bricks (words), you know the blueprint (subject, predicate, objects), and you know what you want to build (statement, question, command, exclamation). Now you need to know how to assemble complex structures — from simple huts to towering skyscrapers.*

## Topic Overview: The Four Sentence Structures

| Structure | Definition | Number of Clauses | Example |
|-----------|------------|-------------------|---------|
| **Simple Sentence** | One independent clause | 1 independent | I like coffee. |
| **Compound Sentence** | Two+ independent clauses | 2+ independent | I like coffee, but she prefers tea. |
| **Complex Sentence** | One independent + one+ dependent | 1 independent + 1+ dependent | Although I like coffee, I prefer tea. |
| **Compound-Complex Sentence** | Two+ independent + one+ dependent | 2+ independent + 1+ dependent | I like coffee, but she prefers tea because it keeps her awake. |

## 1. SIMPLE SENTENCE

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **simple sentence** consists of one independent clause. It has one subject and one predicate (verb phrase) and expresses a complete thought.

**Key Points:**
- Contains ONE independent clause
- Has ONE subject and ONE predicate (though each may be compound)
- Expresses one complete thought
- Can be short or long

**Important:** A simple sentence can have:
- **Compound subject** (two or more subjects with one verb)
- **Compound predicate** (two or more verbs with one subject)
- Both compound subject and compound predicate
- Modifiers, phrases, and objects

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Basic Simple Sentence**
```
[SUBJECT] + [PREDICATE/VERB]
Birds + fly.
```

**Structure 2: Simple Sentence with Object**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [OBJECT]
She + bought + a car.
```

**Structure 3: Simple Sentence with Compound Subject**
```
[SUBJECT 1] + and + [SUBJECT 2] + [VERB] + [REST]
Tom + and + Jerry + are + friends.
```

**Structure 4: Simple Sentence with Compound Predicate**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB 1] + and + [VERB 2] + [OBJECT]
She + opened + the door + and + walked + in.
```

**Structure 5: Simple Sentence with Both Compound Subject and Predicate**
```
[SUB 1] + and + [SUB 2] + [VERB 1] + and + [VERB 2] + [OBJECT]
Tom + and + Jerry + ran + and + played.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- Basic: He sleeps.
- With object: I bought a book.
- With compound subject: Mary and John work together.
- With compound predicate: She ran and jumped.
- With both: My brother and sister studied and passed.

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "The sun is shining brightly today." (simple with modifiers)
- "My best friend and I went to the movies." (compound subject)
- "She sang and danced on stage." (compound predicate)
- "The new employee completed the project ahead of schedule." (simple with modifiers)
- "Children need love and attention." (simple with compound object)

**Simple Sentences Can Be Long:**
```
[SUBJECT]                 [VERB]    [OBJECT]              [MODIFIERS]
The tall man in the suit + bought + a brand new car + from the dealership.
```

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She likes coffee, he likes tea. | She likes coffee. He likes tea. |
| He went to the store, and he bought milk. (compound, not simple) | He went to the store and bought milk. |
| The dog, the cat, the bird are friends. | The dog, cat, and bird are friends. |

2. I like coffee, and she likes tea. (Not simple)
3. Tom and Jerry are friends. (Simple - compound subject)

### 💬 Practice: Conversations

## 2. COMPOUND SENTENCE

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **compound sentence** consists of two or more independent clauses joined together. Each clause could stand alone as its own sentence.

**Key Points:**
- Contains TWO or MORE independent clauses
- No dependent clauses
- Clauses must be joined correctly with a conjunction or punctuation
- Expresses two or more complete thoughts

**Ways to Join Independent Clauses:**

| Method | Example |
|--------|---------|
| **Coordinating Conjunction** (FANBOYS) | I like coffee, **but** she likes tea. |
| **Semicolon (;)** | I like coffee; she likes tea. |
| **Semicolon + Conjunctive Adverb** | I like coffee; **however,** she likes tea. |
| **Colon (:)** (for explanation) | I have one rule: never give up. |

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Coordinating Conjunction**
```
[IND. CLAUSE 1] + [,] + [CONJUNCTION] + [IND. CLAUSE 2]
She likes coffee, + but + he prefers tea.
```

**Structure 2: Semicolon**
```
[IND. CLAUSE 1] + [;] + [IND. CLAUSE 2]
She likes coffee; + he prefers tea.
```

**Structure 3: Semicolon + Conjunctive Adverb**
```
[IND. CLAUSE 1] + [;] + [CONJUNCTIVE ADVERB] + [,] + [IND. CLAUSE 2]
She likes coffee; + however, + he prefers tea.
```

**Common Conjunctive Adverbs:**
- however, therefore, moreover, furthermore, nevertheless, consequently, meanwhile, otherwise, indeed, similarly, additionally

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- Coordinating conjunction: I wanted to go, but I was tired.
- Semicolon: I wanted to go; I was tired.
- Conjunctive adverb: I wanted to go; however, I was tired.
- Multiple clauses: I like coffee, she likes tea, and they like juice.

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "She studied hard for the exam, so she passed with flying colors."
- "I have a meeting at 3 PM; I'll call you afterwards."
- "He loves traveling; however, he rarely has time to go."
- "You can come with us, or you can stay home."
- "The project was challenging; nevertheless, we completed it successfully."

**FANBOYS Guide:**

| Conjunction | Function | Example |
|-------------|----------|---------|
| **For** | Reason | She stayed home, for she was tired. |
| **And** | Addition | I like coffee, and she likes tea. |
| **Nor** | Negative addition | He didn't call, nor did he text. |
| **But** | Contrast | I want to go, but I can't. |
| **Or** | Choice | You can come, or you can stay. |
| **Yet** | Contrast (unexpected) | She was tired, yet she continued. |
| **So** | Result | I was hungry, so I ate. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I like coffee, she likes tea. (comma splice) | I like coffee, and she likes tea. |
| I like coffee she likes tea. (run-on) | I like coffee; she likes tea. |
| I went to the store and bought milk. (not compound) | I went to the store, and I bought milk. |
| I was tired, so I went home. (✓) | Correct! |

**Comma Splice vs. Run-on:**
- **Comma splice:** Two independent clauses joined only by a comma (incorrect)
  - Incorrect: *I like coffee, she likes tea.*
  - Correct: *I like coffee, and she likes tea.*
- **Run-on:** Two independent clauses joined with no punctuation (incorrect)
  - Incorrect: *I like coffee she likes tea.*
  - Correct: *I like coffee; she likes tea.*

### ✅ Complete: Exercises

**Exercise 1: Identify Compound Sentences**

2. She went home because she was tired. (Not compound)
3. He studied hard; however, he failed. (Compound)

**Exercise 2: Join the Clauses**

2. She studied hard. She passed. → She studied hard, so she passed.
3. He didn't call. He didn't text. → He didn't call, nor did he text.

**Exercise 3: Correct the Errors**

2. I like coffee she likes tea.
3. He went to the store and he bought milk. (Correct as compound, or simplify)

**Exercise 4: Use Conjunctive Adverbs**
1. She was tired. She continued working. → She was tired; however, she continued working.
2. He practiced daily. He improved quickly. → He practiced daily; therefore, he improved quickly.

## 3. COMPLEX SENTENCE

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **complex sentence** consists of one independent clause and at least one dependent clause. The clauses are connected by subordinating conjunctions or relative pronouns.

**Key Points:**
- Contains ONE independent clause
- Contains ONE or MORE dependent clauses
- Dependent clauses cannot stand alone
- Expresses a main idea plus additional information

**Ways to Form Complex Sentences:**

| Method | Subordinating Words | Example |
|--------|---------------------|---------|
| **Subordinating Conjunction** | because, although, while, when, since, etc. | I stayed home **because it was raining**. |
| **Relative Pronoun** | who, whom, which, that | The man **who lives next door** is a doctor. |
| **Relative Adverb** | where, when, why | The house **where I grew up** is for sale. |

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Dependent Clause First**
```
[DEPENDENT CLAUSE] + [,] + [INDEPENDENT CLAUSE]
Because it was raining, + I stayed home.
```

**Structure 2: Independent Clause First**
```
[INDEPENDENT CLAUSE] + [DEPENDENT CLAUSE]
I stayed home + because it was raining.
```

**Structure 3: Dependent Clause in Middle**
```
[INDEPENDENT CLAUSE START] + [DEPENDENT CLAUSE] + [INDEPENDENT CLAUSE END]
The man, + who lives next door, + is a doctor.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- Subordinating conjunction: Although she was tired, she continued working.
- Relative pronoun: The woman who called you is my sister.
- Relative adverb: This is the place where we met.
- In the middle: The student, who studied hard, passed the exam.

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "I'll call you when I arrive."
- "Because he was late, he missed the bus."
- "The book that I borrowed is excellent."
- "Although it was cold, they went swimming."
- "She decided to leave since the meeting was over."
- "The man whose car was stolen reported it to the police."

**Common Subordinating Conjunctions:**

| Type | Conjunctions |
|------|-------------|
| **Time** | when, while, after, before, since, until, as soon as, whenever |
| **Reason** | because, since, as, so that, in order that |
| **Condition** | if, unless, provided that, as long as, even if |
| **Contrast** | although, though, even though, whereas, while |
| **Place** | where, wherever |
| **Manner** | as, as if, as though |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Because I was tired. (fragment) | Because I was tired, I went to bed. |
| The man who lives next door. (fragment) | The man who lives next door is a doctor. |
| I went home, because I was tired. | I went home because I was tired. (no comma when dependent comes second) |
| Although I was tired, but I went. | Although I was tired, I went. |

**Punctuation Rules:**
- **Dependent clause FIRST:** Use a comma
  - *Because it was raining, I stayed home.*
- **Dependent clause SECOND:** No comma
  - *I stayed home because it was raining.*
- **Dependent clause in MIDDLE:** Use commas
  - *The man, who lives next door, is a doctor.*

## 4. COMPOUND-COMPLEX SENTENCE

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **compound-complex sentence** contains two or more independent clauses AND at least one dependent clause. It combines elements of both compound and complex sentences.

**Key Points:**
- Contains TWO or MORE independent clauses
- Contains ONE or MORE dependent clauses
- Expresses multiple complete thoughts with additional information
- Most complex sentence type

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Dependant + Independent + Conjunction + Independent**
```
[DEPENDENT CLAUSE] + [,] + [IND. CLAUSE 1] + [,] + [CONJ.] + [IND. CLAUSE 2]
Because it was raining, + I stayed home, + but + she went out.
```

**Structure 2: Independent + Dependant + Conjunction + Independent**
```
[IND. CLAUSE 1] + [DEPENDENT CLAUSE] + [,] + [CONJ.] + [IND. CLAUSE 2]
I stayed home + because it was raining, + but + she went out.
```

**Structure 3: Multiple Dependents**
```
[IND. CLAUSE 1] + [DEP.] + [,] + [CONJ.] + [IND. CLAUSE 2] + [DEP.]
She went to the store, + where she bought milk, + and + he went home, + which was nearby.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- "Because I was tired, I went to bed, and I slept well."
- "I went to bed because I was tired, but I couldn't sleep."
- "Although he studied hard, he failed the test, so he was disappointed."
- "The man who called earlier is here, and he wants to see you."

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "Because it was getting late, we decided to leave, and we made it home just before midnight."
- "I enjoy reading books, which help me relax, but I don't have much time for it."
- "When she arrived at the airport, her flight was delayed, so she had to wait for three hours."
- "The student who won the scholarship is from Brazil, and his sister, who is also a student, will join him next year."

**Analyzing Compound-Complex Sentences:**

```
[Because I was tired]        [I went to bed]          [and]        [I slept well]
   DEPENDENT CLAUSE        INDEPENDENT CLAUSE        CONJ.       INDEPENDENT CLAUSE
     (reason)                 (main action)                       (result)
```

```
[She went to the store]           [where she bought milk]             [and]         [she came home]
   INDEPENDENT CLAUSE              DEPENDENT CLAUSE                  CONJ.       INDEPENDENT CLAUSE
      (action)                      (additional info)                            (result)
```

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Because I was tired, I went to bed, and I slept well. (✓) | Correct! |
| I went to bed because I was tired but I couldn't sleep. | I went to bed because I was tired, but I couldn't sleep. |
| Although he studied hard and he failed. (fragment) | Although he studied hard, he failed, and he was disappointed. |
| The man who called earlier, and he wants to see you. | The man who called earlier is here, and he wants to see you. |

### ✅ Complete: Exercises

## 🔗 Comparing All Four Structures

| Feature | Simple | Compound | Complex | Compound-Complex |
|---------|--------|----------|---------|------------------|
| **Number of Independent Clauses** | 1 | 2+ | 1 | 2+ |
| **Number of Dependent Clauses** | 0 | 0 | 1+ | 1+ |
| **Example** | I like coffee. | I like coffee, but she likes tea. | Although I like coffee, I prefer tea. | Although I like coffee, she likes tea, and we compromise. |
| **Complexity** | Lowest | Low | Medium | Highest |
