# Phase 3: Verbs

## Infinitives

Welcome to Infinitives! You've learned about **verb types**, **verb forms**, **subject-verb agreement**, **irregular verbs**, and **gerunds**. Now it's time to master **infinitives** — another essential verb form that functions as a noun, adjective, or adverb.

Think of it this way: *Gerunds (V4) and infinitives (to + V1) are both verb forms that act like nouns, but they have different uses and rules. Understanding when to use each one is a key skill in English.*

## 1. What Is an Infinitive?

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** An **infinitive** is the base form of a verb (V1) preceded by the word "to." It can function as a noun, adjective, or adverb in a sentence.

**Key Points:**
- The structure is "to + V1" (e.g., to go, to eat, to write)
- It does NOT function as the main verb
- It can act as a noun, adjective, or adverb
- It can have its own object and modifiers

**The Two Types of Infinitives:**

| Type | Structure | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Full Infinitive** | to + V1 | I want **to go**. |
| **Bare Infinitive** | V1 (without "to") | I can **go**. |

### 📐 Study: Infinitive Formation

**Formula:**
```
[TO] + [BASE VERB (V1)] = [INFINITIVE]
to + work = to work
to + study = to study
to + run = to run
to + write = to write
```

**Infinitive Phrases:**

An infinitive phrase includes the infinitive plus its modifiers and/or objects.

**Structure:**
```
[TO] + [V1] + [OBJECT/COMPLEMENT] + [MODIFIERS]
to + read + books + every day
to + speak + English + fluently
```

## 2. Functions of Infinitives

### 📚 The Five Main Functions

**Function 1: Infinitive as Subject**

**Structure:**
```
[INFINITIVE] + [VERB] + [REST]
To swim + is + fun.
To learn + takes + time.
```

**Examples:**
- "**To travel** is my biggest dream."
- "**To succeed** requires hard work."
- "**To understand** is to forgive."
- "**To love** is to be vulnerable."
- "**To be** or not to be, that is the question."

**Note:** In modern English, gerunds are more common as subjects than infinitives.

---

**Function 2: Infinitive as Direct Object**

**Structure:**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [INFINITIVE]
I + want + to go.
She + loves + to dance.
```

**Examples:**
- "I want **to learn** Chinese."
- "She hopes **to become** a doctor."
- "They decided **to leave** early."
- "I need **to finish** this project."
- "He promised **to call** me."

---

**Function 3: Infinitive as Subject Complement**

**Structure:**
```
[SUBJECT] + [LINKING VERB] + [INFINITIVE]
My dream + is + to travel.
His goal + is + to become + a doctor.
```

**Examples:**
- "My goal is **to become** a teacher."
- "Her dream is **to travel** the world."
- "The plan is **to leave** at 6 AM."
- "His job is **to manage** the team."
- "The solution is **to work** harder."

---

**Function 4: Infinitive as Adjective (Modifying a Noun)**

**Structure:**
```
[NOUN] + [INFINITIVE] + [REST]
I have + work + to do.
She needs + help + to finish.
```

**Examples:**
- "I have a book **to read**."
- "She has a lot of work **to do**."
- "Is there anything **to eat**?"
- "I need someone **to help** me."
- "We have no time **to waste**."

---

**Function 5: Infinitive as Adverb (Modifying a Verb)**

**Structure:**
```
[VERB] + [INFINITIVE] + [PURPOSE/REASON]
She left + to catch + the train.
He studied + to pass + the exam.
```

**Examples (Purpose):**
- "I went to the store **to buy** milk." (Why? To buy milk)
- "She studied hard **to pass** the exam." (Why? To pass the exam)
- "He called **to confirm** the reservation." (Why? To confirm)
- "I saved money **to travel** abroad." (Why? To travel abroad)
- "They exercise **to stay** healthy." (Why? To stay healthy)

**Examples (Result):**
- "She arrived **to find** the door locked." (Result)
- "He worked hard **to become** successful." (Result)
- "I woke up **to see** the sunrise." (Result)

**Examples (Reason):**
- "I was happy **to see** her." (Reason for being happy)
- "She was surprised **to hear** the news." (Reason for surprise)
- "We were lucky **to find** a seat." (Reason for luck)

## 4. Verbs Followed by Object + Infinitive

### 📚 Learn: Verbs with an Object

**Rule:** Some verbs are followed by an object + infinitive.

**Structure:**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [OBJECT] + [INFINITIVE]
She + told + me + to go.
He + wants + her + to leave.
```

**List of Common Verbs:**

| Verb | Example |
|------|---------|
| advise | I advise you **to wait**. |
| allow | She allowed me **to stay**. |
| ask | He asked me **to help** him. |
| beg | I beg you **to help** me. |
| cause | What caused her **to leave**? |
| challenge | I challenge you **to beat** me. |
| choose | She chose him **to lead** the team. |
| command | The officer commanded us **to stop**. |
| convince | I convinced her **to come**. |
| encourage | She encouraged me **to apply**. |
| expect | I expect you **to finish** on time. |
| forbid | He forbids me **to go**. |
| force | She forced him **to confess**. |
| help | He helped me **to learn** Spanish. |
| invite | She invited me **to attend**. |
| need | I need you **to help** me. |
| order | The commander ordered them **to surrender**. |
| persuade | I persuaded him **to stay**. |
| remind | Please remind me **to call** her. |
| tell | She told me **to wait**. |
| urge | I urged her **to apply**. |
| want | I want you **to be** here. |
| warn | I warned him **to be** careful. |

**Examples:**
- "She **told me to wait** outside."
- "He **wants me to help** him."
- "I **advise you to reconsider**."
- "They **allowed us to enter**."
- "She **encouraged me to apply** for the job."

## 5. Verbs Followed by Gerund or Infinitive

### 📚 Learn: Verbs with Both Options

**Rule:** Some verbs can be followed by EITHER a gerund OR an infinitive.

**Group 1: Same Meaning**

| Verb | Gerund | Infinitive |
|------|--------|------------|
| begin | I began **working**. | I began **to work**. |
| start | I started **working**. | I started **to work**. |
| continue | I continued **working**. | I continued **to work**. |
| like | I like **swimming**. | I like **to swim**. |
| love | I love **dancing**. | I love **to dance**. |
| hate | I hate **getting** up. | I hate **to get** up. |
| prefer | I prefer **reading**. | I prefer **to read**. |

**Group 2: Different Meaning**

| Verb | Gerund | Infinitive |
|------|--------|------------|
| stop | I stopped **smoking**. (quit) | I stopped **to smoke**. (paused) |
| remember | I remember **meeting** her. (recall) | I remembered **to meet** her. (didn't forget) |
| forget | I forgot **meeting** him. (don't recall) | I forgot **to meet** him. (didn't remember) |
| regret | I regret **saying** that. (sorry for past) | I regret **to say** that... (sorry to say) |
| try | I tried **opening** the window. (experimented) | I tried **to open** the window. (made effort) |
| mean | Success means **working** hard. (involves) | I meant **to call** you. (intended) |
| go on | She went on **talking**. (continued) | She went on **to talk**. (moved to next) |

## 6. Infinitives with "To" vs. Bare Infinitives

### 📚 Learn: When to Drop "To"

**Rule:** After certain verbs, we use the bare infinitive (without "to").

**After Modal Verbs:**

| Modal | Example |
|-------|---------|
| can | She **can swim**. |
| could | He **could run** fast. |
| will | I **will help** you. |
| would | She **would like** to come. |
| shall | I **shall return**. |
| should | You **should go**. |
| may | I **may come** later. |
| might | He **might arrive** early. |
| must | You **must leave** now. |

**After Certain Verbs:**

| Verb | Example |
|------|---------|
| feel | I **feel** the ground **shake**. |
| hear | I **heard** her **sing**. |
| see | I **saw** him **leave**. |
| watch | She **watched** them **play**. |
| let | She **let** me **go**. |
| make | He **made** me **cry**. |
| help | She **helped** me **finish**. (with or without "to") |

**After "Had Better" and "Would Rather":**

| Structure | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| had better | You **had better** **leave** now. |
| would rather | I **would rather** **stay** home. |
| would sooner | I **would sooner** **die** than surrender. |

**After "Why" Questions:**

| Structure | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| Why + bare infinitive | **Why** **wait**? Let's go now. |
| Why not + bare infinitive | **Why not** **try** again? |

## 7. Negative Infinitives

### 📚 Learn: Forming Negatives

**Structure:**
```
[TO] + [NOT] + [V1]
to + not + go = not to go
to + not + eat = not to eat
```

**Examples:**
- "She decided **not to go**." (She decided to stay.)
- "I told him **not to wait** for me."
- "He promised **not to be** late."
- "She asked me **not to tell** anyone."
- "I regret **not to have** studied harder." (or: "I regret not having studied harder.")

**Alternative Structure (split infinitive):**
- "I told him **to not wait**." (Less formal, but common)

---

## 8. Split Infinitives

### 📚 Learn: What They Are

**Definition:** A **split infinitive** occurs when an adverb is placed between "to" and the verb.

**Examples:**
- "She decided **to quickly leave**." (Split infinitive)
- "He wants **to completely finish** the project." (Split infinitive)

**Rule:** Split infinitives are acceptable in modern English, but many traditionalists avoid them. They are often used for emphasis.

**Formal vs. Informal:**

| Formal (Traditional) | Informal (Modern) |
|----------------------|-------------------|
| She decided **to leave quickly**. | She decided **to quickly leave**. |
| He wants **to finish completely**. | He wants **to completely finish**. |
| I need **to study hard**. | I need **to really study**. |

---

## 9. Common Mistakes with Infinitives

### ⚠️ Learn: The Top Errors

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I want **go** home. | I want **to go** home. |
| She enjoys **to read**. | She enjoys **reading**. |
| I made him **to go**. | I made him **go**. |
| I saw her **to leave**. | I saw her **leave**. |
| She can **to swim**. | She can **swim**. |
| I need **to** go home? | **Do** I need **to** go home? |
| He told me **not to** go. | He told me **not to** go. (correct) |
| I decided **not go**. | I decided **not to go**. |

**The "To" Rule:**
- After modals → NO "to" (can swim, must go)
- After certain verbs (make, let, see, hear) → NO "to" (made me cry, saw him leave)
- After most other verbs → WITH "to" (want to go, need to study)

## 10. Infinitive vs. Gerund Summary

### 📚 Quick Comparison Chart

| Situation | Use Gerund | Use Infinitive |
|-----------|------------|----------------|
| **As subject** | ✓ (more common) | ✓ (possible) |
| **After preposition** | ✓ | ✗ |
| **For purpose** | ✗ | ✓ |
| **After modals** | ✗ | ✓ (bare infinitive) |
| **After "enjoy"** | ✓ | ✗ |
| **After "want"** | ✗ | ✓ |
| **As adjective** | ✓ | ✓ |
| **As adverb** | ✗ | ✓ |

**Key Differences:**

| Gerund | Infinitive |
|--------|------------|
| **Swimming** is fun. | **To swim** is fun. (less common) |
| She is good at **swimming**. | ✗ She is good to swim. |
| ✗ I went swimming to buy milk. | I went **to buy** milk. (purpose) |
| She enjoys **swimming**. | ✗ She enjoys to swim. |
| ✗ She wants swimming. | She wants **to swim**. |

## 💬 Practice: Conversations

### Dialogue 1: Making Plans

- A: "What do you want **to do** this weekend?"
- B: "I'd like **to go** to the beach."
- A: "I'd love **to go** too! We need **to pack** some food."
- B: "I'll remember **to bring** drinks."
- A: "I have **to buy** a new swimsuit."
- B: "I can **drive** us there."

### Dialogue 2: Giving Advice

- A: "I don't know what **to do** about my job."
- B: "You should **talk** to your manager."
- A: "I tried **to talk** to her, but she was busy."
- B: "Maybe you need **to write** an email."
- A: "That's a good idea. I'll try **to write** one today."
- B: "I can **help** you **write** it."

### Dialogue 3: Life Goals

- A: "What do you hope **to achieve** in the next five years?"
- B: "I want **to start** my own business."
- A: "That's a great goal! What do you need **to do** first?"
- B: "I need **to save** money and **learn** more about entrepreneurship."
- A: "I can **help** you **research** business ideas."
- B: "That would be amazing! I'd love **to have** a partner."
