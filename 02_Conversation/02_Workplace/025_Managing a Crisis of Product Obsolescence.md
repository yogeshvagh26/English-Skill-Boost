# Topic: Managing a Crisis of Product Obsolescence / Market Disruption

**Scenario:** You are the Chief Product Officer (CPO) at a consumer electronics company. Your flagship product—a smart home hub—has been the market leader for 5 years. However, a new entrant has launched a competing product that is cheaper, more feature‑rich, and integrates with a wider range of devices. Your sales have dropped 30% in the last quarter, and major retailers are reducing shelf space. The CEO has called an emergency meeting with the executive team (CEO, CFO, CMO, Head of R&D, and Head of Operations) to develop a response strategy. Your goal is to: **assess** the competitive threat and market dynamics, **evaluate** options (pivot, sunset, innovate, partner), **develop** a recovery plan, **address** the financial and operational impact, **communicate** the strategy to stakeholders, and **secure** the executive team's commitment to a bold path forward.

---

## Dialogue

| **Speaker** | **Dialogue / Text** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **CEO** | *(opening the meeting)* We have a crisis. Our sales are down 30%, and we're losing shelf space. The new competitor is eating our lunch. I need a plan—and I need it now. |
| **You** | I've done a rapid assessment. The competitor's product is cheaper, has better integration, and is more feature‑rich. We have three options: **#1**—Sunset the product and exit the market. **#2**—Pivot to a new, differentiated product. **#3**—Innovate aggressively to regain leadership. |
| **CFO** | What about the financials? We've invested $50M in this product line. |
| **You** | Option 1 would be a write‑off. Option 2 would require a $20M investment and 12 months. Option 3 would require $30M and 18 months—but would give us a first‑mover advantage in a new category. |
| **CMO** | What about a partnership? Could we integrate with the competitor or a complementary player? |
| **You** | We've explored that. There's a potential partnership with a smart home ecosystem provider—they're interested in our user base. That could accelerate our pivot. |
| **Head of R&D** | I believe we can develop a next‑gen product that leapfrogs the competitor. We have the talent and the IP. |
| **CFO** | The cost is significant. We'd need to reprioritize other projects. |
| **CEO** | What's the risk of each option? |
| **You** | Option 1: immediate loss of revenue and market position. Option 2: moderate risk—if we execute well, we can regain share. Option 3: high risk, high reward—if we succeed, we re‑establish leadership; if we fail, we lose it all. |
| **CEO** | I'm leaning toward Option 3—innovation. But I want a phased approach: a quick pivot to a differentiated product, followed by a longer‑term innovation roadmap. |
| **You** | I support that. We'll launch an MVP within 6 months and a full product within 18 months. We'll also pursue the partnership to expand our ecosystem. |
| **CMO** | And how do we communicate this to the market? We need to manage expectations. |
| **You** | We'll be transparent: *"We've listened to our customers and are building the next generation of smart home technology. We're investing in innovation to deliver the best experience."* |
| **CFO** | What about the financial restatement? We'll need to adjust our revenue projections. |
| **You** | I'll work with you on a revised forecast and present it to the board. |
| **You** | *(closing)* This is a defining moment for our company. We have the talent, the resources, and the will to reinvent ourselves. Let's commit to a bold, innovative path—and let's execute with speed. |
| *(6 months later – progress update)* | |
| **You** | *(to the executive team)* The MVP is launched. Early feedback is positive—customers love the new features. The partnership is also progressing well. We're on track to regain market share by next year. |
| **CEO** | This is the right move. We're back in the game. |
| **You** | We'll continue to innovate and execute. This is just the beginning. |

## Vocabulary

| **Word / Phrase** | **Part of Speech** | **Meaning** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Product obsolescence** | Noun Phrase | When a product becomes outdated or less competitive. |
| **Market disruption** | Noun Phrase | A significant change in market dynamics caused by new entrants or technology. |
| **Sunset** | Verb | To gradually phase out a product. |
| **Pivot** | Verb | To change direction or strategy. |
| **First‑mover advantage** | Noun Phrase | The benefit of being the first to enter a market. |
| **MVP (Minimum Viable Product)** | Noun | A product with just enough features to test the market. |
| **Leapfrog** | Verb | To surpass competitors by offering superior technology. |
| **Reprioritize** | Verb | To change the order of importance of projects. |
| **Partnership** | Noun | A collaborative relationship with another company. |
| **Ecosystem** | Noun | The network of devices, platforms, and services a product integrates with. |
| **Write‑off** | Noun | The elimination of an asset's value from financial records. |
| **Innovation roadmap** | Noun Phrase | A plan for developing new products or features. |
| **Phased approach** | Noun Phrase | Implementing changes in stages over time. |
| **Revenue projections** | Noun Phrase | Forecasts of future income. |
| **Defining moment** | Noun Phrase | A critical point that shapes the future. |
| **Reinvent** | Verb | To significantly change and improve. |
| **Regain market share** | Phrase | To recover lost market position. |
| **Competitive threat** | Noun Phrase | A risk posed by competitors. |
| **Consumer sentiment** | Noun Phrase | Public opinion about a brand or product. |
| **Agility** | Noun | The ability to move quickly and adapt. |
