# Topic: Managing a Corporate University / Learning & Development Strategy

**Scenario:** You are the Chief Talent Officer at a global professional services firm. The CEO has approved the creation of a Corporate University—a dedicated learning and development function to upskill employees at all levels, build leadership pipelines, and align learning with strategic business goals. You are now presenting the Corporate University strategy to the executive committee (CEO, CFO, COO, and CHRO) to secure final approval and funding. Your goal is to: **articulate** the vision and business case, **present** the curriculum design and delivery model, **address** concerns about cost and ROI, **define** success metrics, **outline** the governance structure, and **secure** the committee's commitment to invest in the program.

---

## Dialogue

| **Speaker** | **Dialogue / Text** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **You** | *(opening the presentation)* Thank you for the time. I'm excited to present our Corporate University strategy. This is not just about training—it's about building a culture of continuous learning that drives business performance. Our goal is to develop talent at every level, from new hires to senior leaders, and to ensure we have the skills we need to execute our strategy. |
| **CEO** | What's the business case? I need to see the ROI. |
| **You** | Three key drivers: **#1**—We've identified critical skill gaps in digital, data, and leadership. **#2**—Our talent retention is at risk—we're losing high-potential employees who feel they're not growing. **#3**—A strong learning culture increases productivity and innovation by 15-20%. We project a 3x return on investment over 3 years. |
| **CFO** | What's the investment required? |
| **You** | We're proposing a $3M annual budget for the first 3 years, covering curriculum development, technology platform, faculty, and program administration. After that, we expect the program to be self-sustaining through reduced external training costs. |
| **COO** | How will the curriculum be structured? |
| **You** | *(sharing a slide)* Three core pillars: **#1**—Core skills (functional and technical training for all employees). **#2**—Leadership development (for managers and senior leaders). **#3**—Strategic skills (future-focused capabilities like AI, sustainability, and digital transformation). We'll also offer personalized learning paths. |
| **CHRO** | What about delivery? We have employees in 15 countries. |
| **You** | We'll use a blended model: online self-paced courses, virtual instructor-led sessions, and in-person workshops for key programs. We'll also partner with external universities and industry experts. |
| **CEO** | How do we measure success? |
| **You** | Five metrics: **#1**—Course completion rates. **#2**—Skill acquisition (measured through assessments). **#3**—Employee engagement and retention. **#4**—Internal promotion rates. **#5**—Business impact (e.g., project outcomes, productivity improvements). We'll report progress quarterly. |
| **CFO** | What about governance? Who owns this? |
| **You** | I'll lead a Corporate University steering committee with representatives from each business unit. We'll also have a faculty advisory board to ensure curriculum quality and relevance. |
| **You** | *(closing)* This is a significant investment in our people—and our future. I'm confident it will pay dividends in talent retention, innovation, and business performance. I'm asking for your approval to move forward. |
| **CEO** | *(nodding)* I'm convinced. Let's proceed. |
| **You** | Thank you. I'll send the detailed implementation plan with a phased rollout starting in 6 months. |
| *(12 months later – progress update)* | |
| **You** | *(to the executive committee)* The Corporate University is fully operational. We've launched 20 programs, enrolled 1,500 employees, and achieved a 90% course completion rate. Early feedback is excellent, and we're already seeing a 10% increase in internal promotions. |
| **CFO** | The ROI is tracking ahead of projections. |
| **You** | This is just the beginning. We'll continue to expand and evolve the curriculum based on business needs. |

| **Corporate University** | Noun Phrase | A dedicated internal organization for employee learning and development. |
| **Learning & Development (L&D)** | Noun | The function responsible for employee training and growth. |
| **Continuous learning** | Noun Phrase | An ongoing process of acquiring new knowledge and skills. |
| **Skill gaps** | Noun Phrase | Differences between current and required competencies. |
| **High-potential employees** | Noun Phrase | Employees identified as having the capacity for leadership. |
| **Leadership pipeline** | Noun Phrase | A pool of candidates ready for leadership roles. |

| **Instructor-led sessions** | Noun Phrase | Training led by a live instructor. |
| **Faculty advisory board** | Noun Phrase | A group of experts ensuring curriculum quality. |
| **Phased rollout** | Noun Phrase | Gradual implementation over time. |
| **Personalized learning paths** | Noun Phrase | Customized training plans for individual employees. |
| **Success metrics** | Noun Phrase | Measurable indicators of achievement. |
| **Internal promotions** | Noun Phrase | Advancing existing employees to higher roles. |
| **Curriculum design** | Noun Phrase | The process of developing educational content and structure. |
| **Talent retention** | Noun Phrase | The ability to keep skilled employees. |
