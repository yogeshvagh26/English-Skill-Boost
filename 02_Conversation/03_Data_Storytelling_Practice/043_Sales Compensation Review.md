## 1. Situation : Sales Compensation Review – The "Incentive Misalignment" Crisis

**Context:** You are a Senior Data Analyst at a B2B SaaS company. The VP of Sales (Robert) is pushing for a new compensation plan that rewards sales reps for closing *any* deal, regardless of size or profitability. The CFO (Elena) is concerned about margin erosion—she wants to incentivize *high-quality* deals. The CEO (Sarah) wants a data-driven solution that balances growth and profitability. You've analyzed the impact of the current compensation plan on sales behavior, deal quality, and company profitability. You need to present your findings and recommend a compensation structure that aligns sales incentives with business goals.

| **Compensation plan**           | Noun Phrase    | The structure of sales incentives (base salary, commission, bonuses)                   | *A well-designed compensation plan aligns sales behavior with business goals.*                   |
| **Sales commission**            | Noun Phrase    | A percentage of the deal value paid to the sales rep                                   | *Commission rates vary by deal size to encourage larger deals.*                                  |
| **Quota**                       | Noun           | The target revenue a sales rep must achieve to earn full commission                    | *Quota attainment dropped slightly under the new plan—but deal quality improved.*                |
| **Accelerator**                 | Noun           | A higher commission rate for deals that exceed a certain threshold                     | *We introduced an accelerator for deals over $50k to encourage bigger wins.*                     |
| **Deal size**                   | Noun Phrase    | The value of a single sales deal                                                       | *Average deal size dropped 30% under the old comp plan.*                                         |
| **Margin**                      | Noun           | The profit percentage of a deal (revenue minus cost)                                   | *We're offering a 5% bonus for deals with margins over 60%.*                                     |
| **CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)** | Acronym/Metric | The cost of acquiring a new customer (sales + marketing spend)                         | *CAC increased from $1,200 to $1,800—a sign of inefficiency.*                                    |
| **LTV (Lifetime Value)**        | Acronym/Metric | The total revenue expected from a customer over their lifetime                         | *Small deals have lower LTV—they're less valuable over time.*                                    |
| **Misaligned incentives**       | Noun Phrase    | When sales compensation encourages behavior that doesn't serve the company's goals     | *Misaligned incentives are the root cause of shrinking margins.*                                 |
| **Tiered commission**           | Noun Phrase    | Different commission rates for different deal sizes or types                           | *A tiered commission structure rewards reps for larger, more profitable deals.*                 |
| **Quota attainment**            | Noun Phrase    | The percentage of reps who meet or exceed their quota                                  | *Quota attainment dropped slightly—but deal quality improved.*                                   |
| **Sales productivity**          | Noun Phrase    | The efficiency of the sales team in generating revenue                                 | *A well-designed comp plan increases sales productivity by focusing on the right deals.*        |

## 4. Key Takeaways

### Common Sentence Patterns (for Sales Compensation Storytelling)

| Pattern                                                              | Use                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| *"I've analyzed the impact of the current comp plan across [X] dimensions."* | To introduce a structured analysis.                                                    |
| *"First, [Dimension A]—here's what the data shows."*                 | To present findings one at a time.                                                     |
| *"Second, [Dimension B]—I found [concerning trend]."*                | To build the case with additional evidence.                                            |
| *"Third, [Dimension C]—the [misalignment] is clear."*                | To deliver the diagnosis.                                                              |
| *"The story isn't [blame]. The story is [diagnosis]."*               | To reframe the narrative (anchor pattern).                                             |
| *"My recommendation is a [compensation structure]."*                 | To propose a specific, actionable solution.                                            |
| *"I've modeled the impact: [Before] vs. [After]."*                   | To show the projected outcome of the new plan.                                         |
| *"Let's pilot this with one region for [X] days."*                   | To propose a low-risk validation step.                                                 |
| *"I'll build a dashboard tracking [metrics] by rep."*                | To commit to measurement and accountability.                                           |

### Useful Phrases to Remember
- **Introducing the "misalignment" concept:** *"The current comp plan is rewarding reps for doing the wrong thing—not because they're bad, but because the incentives are misaligned."*
- **When someone says "but reps are hitting quota":** *"They're hitting quota—but they're hitting it with smaller, less profitable deals."*
- **Introducing the "tiered" approach:** *"We don't need to pay reps less—we need to pay them more for the right kind of business."*
- **When proposing the pilot:** *"A 90-day pilot gives us data without committing the entire team."*
- **When rep earnings are a concern:** *"Under the new plan, top performers earn more—bottom performers earn less, but we'll provide coaching to help them shift to larger deals."*
- **When the pilot succeeds:** *"The pilot region saw deal size increase 40% and margins improve 15%. We're rolling out the plan company-wide."*

### Practical Speaking Tips (for Sales Compensation Narratives)
1.  **Link compensation directly to business outcomes.** Show how the current plan drives behavior that hurts margins—and how the new plan fixes it.
2.  **Use data to "diagnose" before you "prescribe."** Show the problem clearly before proposing the solution.
3.  **Model the impact on rep earnings.** Sales leadership cares about rep retention—show how earnings change for top vs. bottom performers.
4.  **Propose a "pilot" to reduce risk.** A small-scale test builds buy-in and reduces resistance.
5.  **Include a "transition" plan.** If reps will earn less initially, provide training and support to help them adapt.
6.  **Track "deal quality" metrics.** Don't just track revenue—track margin, LTV, and retention by deal size.
7.  **Involve sales leadership in the design.** If sales leadership feels heard, they're more likely to support the change.
8.  **Communicate the "why."** Explain to the sales team *why* the comp plan is changing—and how it benefits them in the long run.
9.  **Celebrate wins.** When a rep closes a large, profitable deal, celebrate it. This reinforces the desired behavior.
10. **Review and iterate.** Compensation plans aren't set in stone—review them quarterly and adjust based on data.
