# Phase 11: Modals

Welcome to Phase 11! You've mastered **verbs** (types, forms, agreement, irregulars, gerunds, infinitives, participles), **nouns** (all types), **pronouns** (all types), **adjectives** (types, order, comparisons), **adverbs** (all types), **articles & determiners**, **prepositions** (all types), and **conjunctions** (all types). Now it's time to explore **modals** — special auxiliary verbs that express ability, permission, possibility, necessity, obligation, and more.

Think of it this way: *Main verbs tell you WHAT happens. Modals tell you the ATTITUDE toward what happens — whether it's possible, necessary, allowed, or certain. "I go" vs. "I can go" vs. "I must go" vs. "I should go." Modals add nuance and precision to your language.*

| Modal | Main Functions | Example |
|-------|----------------|---------|
| **Can** | Ability, permission, possibility | I **can** swim. |
| **Could** | Past ability, polite request, possibility | I **could** swim when I was 5. |
| **May** | Permission, possibility | **May** I come in? |
| **Might** | Possibility (less certain) | It **might** rain. |
| **Must** | Strong obligation, certainty | You **must** leave now. |

| **Dare** | Courage (question/negative) | How **dare** you? |
| **Ought to** | Advice, obligation (should) | You **ought to** apologize. |

## The Golden Rules of Modals

### 📚 Learn: The Rules

**Rule 1: Modals Never Change Form**
- No -s for third person: ❌ She **cans** swim. ✅ She **can** swim.
- No -ing form: ❌ He is **canning** go. ✅ He **can** go.
- No -ed form: ❌ She **coulded** go. ✅ She **could** go.

**Rule 2: Modals Are Followed by the Base Verb**
- No "to" after modals: ❌ I must **to** go. ✅ I must **go**.
- No -ing after modals: ❌ She can **going**. ✅ She can **go**.
- No past participle: ❌ He must **gone**. ✅ He must **go**.

**Rule 3: Modals Use "Not" for Negatives**
- "I **cannot** go." / "I **can't** go."
- "She **should not** go." / "She **shouldn't** go."

**Rule 4: Modals Invert for Questions**
- "**Can** I go?"
- "**Should** she go?"

**Rule 5: Modals Have No Infinitive or Participle Forms**
- No "to can" (incorrect)
- No "canning" (incorrect)
- No "canned" (incorrect)

## 1. Can

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "Can" is used to express ability, permission, and possibility. It is one of the most common modals.

**Key Uses:**
- **Ability** (physical or mental)
- **Permission** (informal)
- **Possibility** (general)
- **Requests** (informal)

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Ability**
```
[SUBJECT] + [CAN] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
I + can + swim.
She + can + speak + three languages.
```

**Structure 2: Permission**
```
[SUBJECT] + [CAN] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
You + can + go + now.
Can + I + come + in?
```

**Structure 3: Possibility**
```
[SUBJECT] + [CAN] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
It + can + be + expensive.
Anything + can + happen.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Ability:**
- "I **can** swim."
- "She **can** speak French fluently."
- "He **can** run very fast."
- "I **can't** drive a car." (negative)
- "**Can** you play the piano?" (question)

**Permission:**
- "You **can** go now."
- "**Can** I borrow your pen?"
- "You **can't** park here." (negative)
- "**Can** we leave early?" (question)

**Possibility:**
- "It **can** be very cold here."
- "Anything **can** happen."
- "He **can** be difficult sometimes."
- "This **can't** be right." (negative)
- "**Can** that be true?" (question)

**Requests (Informal):**
- "**Can** you help me?"
- "**Can** you pass the salt?"
- "**Can** I have some water?"

**Can vs. Could (Politeness):**
- "**Can** you help me?" (informal)
- "**Could** you help me?" (more polite)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She cans swim. | She **can** swim. |
| I can to swim. | I **can** swim. |
| I can swimming. | I **can** swim. |
| I can't to help you. | I **can't** help you. |

## 2. Could

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "Could" is the past form of "can." It is used for past ability, polite requests, and possibility.

**Key Uses:**
- **Past ability** (general ability in the past)
- **Polite requests** (more polite than "can")
- **Possibility** (less certain than "can")
- **Suggestions** (giving options)

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Past Ability**
```
[SUBJECT] + [COULD] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
I + could + swim + when I was 5.
She + could + speak + French before she moved.
```

**Structure 2: Polite Request**
```
[SUBJECT] + [COULD] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
Could + you + help + me?
Could + I + borrow + your pen?
```

**Structure 3: Possibility**
```
[SUBJECT] + [COULD] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
It + could + rain + tomorrow.
He + could + be + at work.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Past Ability:**
- "I **could** swim when I was 5."
- "She **could** speak French before she moved."
- "He **could** run very fast when he was young."
- "I **couldn't** swim until I was 10." (negative)

**Polite Requests:**
- "**Could** you help me, please?"
- "**Could** I borrow your pen?"
- "**Could** you pass the salt?"
- "**Could** I have some more water?"

**Possibility:**
- "It **could** rain tomorrow."
- "He **could** be at work."
- "She **could** have forgotten."
- "It **could** be true." (less certain than "can")
- "He **could** arrive late." (possibility)

**Suggestions:**
- "You **could** try calling her."
- "We **could** go to the park."
- "She **could** ask for help."
- "You **could** take a taxi."

**Could vs. Can (Politeness):**
- "**Can** you help me?" (informal)
- "**Could** you help me?" (more polite)
- "**Could** I have some water?" (very polite)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I could swimmed. | I **could** swim. |
| I could to swim. | I **could** swim. |
| Could I to go? | **Could** I go? |
| I could have went. | I **could have gone**. |

## 3. May

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "May" is used to express permission and possibility. It is more formal than "can."

**Key Uses:**
- **Permission** (formal)
- **Possibility** (formal)
- **Wishes** (formal)

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Permission**
```
[SUBJECT] + [MAY] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
May + I + come + in?
You + may + leave + now.
```

**Structure 2: Possibility**
```
[SUBJECT] + [MAY] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
It + may + rain + tomorrow.

```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Permission:**
- "**May** I come in?"
- "**May** I use your phone?"
- "You **may** leave now."
- "Students **may** use the library."
- "**May** I speak to the manager?"

**Possibility:**
- "It **may** rain tomorrow."
- "She **may** be at home."

- "**May** I come in?" (formal)

## 4. Might

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "Might" is the past form of "may" and expresses possibility. It indicates a lower probability than "may" or "could."

**Key Uses:**
- **Possibility** (less certain than "may")
- **Conditional possibility**
- **Polite suggestion**

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Possibility**
```
[SUBJECT] + [MIGHT] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
It + might + rain + tomorrow.
She + might + be + at home.
```

**Structure 2: Conditional**
```
[SUBJECT] + [MIGHT] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
If I ask, she + might + help + me.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Possibility:**
- "It **might** rain tomorrow."
- "She **might** be at home."
- "He **might** arrive late."
- "They **might** have left already."
- "I **might** not come to the party." (negative)

**Conditional Possibility:**
- "If I ask, she **might** help me."
- "If you study, you **might** pass."
- "If we leave now, we **might** catch the train."

**Polite Suggestions:**
- "You **might** try calling her."
- "We **might** go to the park."
- "She **might** ask for help."

**Might vs. May (Possibility):**
- "It **may** rain." (50% chance — more certain)
- "It **might** rain." (30% chance — less certain)

**Might vs. Could (Possibility):**
- "It **might** rain." (just a possibility)
- "It **could** rain." (a real possibility)

**Might Have (Past Possibility):**
- "She **might have** forgotten."
- "He **might have** left already."
- "They **might have** missed the bus."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| It might to rain. | It **might** rain. |
| She might goes. | She **might** go. |
| I might have went. | I **might have gone**. |
| Might I to come in? | **Might** I come in? (more formal than may) |

## 5. Must

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "Must" is used to express strong obligation, necessity, or certainty. It indicates that something is required or almost certain.

**Key Uses:**
- **Strong obligation** (internal/self-imposed)
- **Certainty** (logical conclusion)
- **Rules/regulations** (formal)

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Obligation**
```
[SUBJECT] + [MUST] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
You + must + leave + now.
She + must + study + harder.
```

**Structure 2: Certainty**
```
[SUBJECT] + [MUST] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
She + must + be + tired.
He + must + be + home.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Obligation:**
- "You **must** leave now."
- "She **must** study harder."
- "We **must** pay our taxes."
- "I **must** call my mother."
- "You **mustn't** smoke here." (negative prohibition)

**Certainty (Logical Conclusion):**
- "She **must** be tired." (I can see she's yawning)
- "He **must** be home." (The lights are on)
- "They **must** have left." (Their car is gone)
- "This **must** be the place." (The address matches)

**Rules/Regulations:**
- "Visitors **must** show their ID."
- "All passengers **must** fasten their seatbelts."
- "Students **must** wear uniforms."
- "You **must** not enter without permission." (negative)

**Must vs. Have To:**
- "I **must** go." (I feel I need to)
- "I **have to** go." (Someone else requires it)

**Must vs. Should:**
- "You **must** go." (strong obligation)
- "You **should** go." (advice/recommendation)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She must to go. | She **must** go. |
| I must going. | I **must** go. |
| I must have went. | I **must have gone**. |
| You mustn't to smoke. | You **mustn't** smoke. |

**Must vs. Have to:**

| Must (Internal) | Have to (External) |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| I **must** study hard. | I **have to** study hard. (teacher said so) |
| I **must** go now. | I **have to** go now. (my boss is calling) |

## 6. Shall

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "Shall" is used to make suggestions, offers, or to express future actions (formal, mostly with I/we).

**Key Points:**
- Used with I and We (mostly)
- Offers and suggestions
- Formal future (less common now)
- Questions for offers/suggestions

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Suggestion**
```
[SHALL] + [I/WE] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
Shall + I + open + the window?
Shall + we + go + now?
```

**Structure 2: Offer**
```
[SHALL] + [I/WE] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
Shall + I + help + you?
Shall + we + wait + for them?
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Suggestions:**
- "**Shall** I open the window?"
- "**Shall** we go now?"
- "**Shall** we have dinner?"
- "**Shall** I call you later?"
- "What **shall** we do?"

**Offers:**
- "**Shall** I help you?"
- "**Shall** I carry that for you?"
- "**Shall** we wait for them?"
- "**Shall** I get you a drink?"

**Formal Future (British English):**
- "I **shall** arrive at 6 PM." (formal)
- "We **shall** be delighted to attend."
- "I **shall** never forget you."

**Shall vs. Will:**
- "I **shall** go." (formal, British, less common)
- "I **will** go." (more common)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Shall he go? (not used for third person) | **Shall** I go? |
| I shall to go. | I **shall** go. |
| Shall I to help you? | **Shall** I help you? |
| I shall going. | I **shall** go. |

## 7. Should

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "Should" is used to express advice, recommendation, or mild obligation. It indicates what is the right or best thing to do.

**Key Uses:**
- **Advice** (what is good to do)
- **Recommendation** (what is recommended)
- **Mild obligation** (what is expected)
- **Expectation** (what is likely)

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Advice**
```
[SUBJECT] + [SHOULD] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
You + should + see + a doctor.
She + should + study + harder.
```

**Structure 2: Obligation**
```
[SUBJECT] + [SHOULD] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
Students + should + arrive + on time.
You + should + pay + your bills.
```

**Structure 3: Expectation**
```
[SUBJECT] + [SHOULD] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
He + should + be + here by now.
The train + should + arrive + soon.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Advice:**
- "You **should** see a doctor."
- "She **should** study harder."
- "We **should** leave early."
- "You **shouldn't** eat so much sugar." (negative)
- "**Should** I call her?" (question)

**Recommendation:**
- "You **should** try this restaurant."
- "She **should** read this book."
- "We **should** visit the museum."
- "You **should** watch that movie."

**Mild Obligation:**
- "Students **should** arrive on time."
- "You **should** pay your bills."
- "We **should** respect others."
- "People **should** recycle."

**Expectation:**
- "He **should** be here by now."
- "The train **should** arrive soon."
- "She **should** have called by now."
- "The package **should** arrive today."

**Should Have (Past Regret):**
- "I **should have** studied harder." (but I didn't)
- "She **should have** called." (but she didn't)
- "We **should have** left earlier." (but we didn't)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| You should to go. | You **should** go. |
| She should goes. | She **should** go. |
| I should have went. | I **should have gone**. |
| Should I to go? | **Should** I go? |

## 8. Will

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "Will" is used to express the future, willingness, promises, and predictions. It is one of the most common modals.

**Key Uses:**
- **Future actions** (what will happen)
- **Predictions** (what is expected)
- **Promises** (commitments)
- **Willingness** (voluntary actions)
- **Offer** (spontaneous help)

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Future**
```
[SUBJECT] + [WILL] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
I + will + call + you tomorrow.
She + will + be + here soon.
```

**Structure 2: Prediction**
```
[SUBJECT] + [WILL] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
It + will + rain + tomorrow.
They + will + win + the game.
```

**Structure 3: Promise**
```
[SUBJECT] + [WILL] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
I + will + help + you.
I + will + never + forget + you.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Future Actions:**
- "I **will** call you tomorrow."
- "She **will** be here soon."
- "We **will** arrive at 6 PM."
- "They **will** move to London."

**Predictions:**
- "It **will** rain tomorrow."
- "They **will** win the game."
- "She **will** become a doctor."
- "The economy **will** improve."

**Promises:**
- "I **will** help you."
- "I **will** never forget you."
- "She **will** always love you."
- "We **will** be there for you."

**Willingness/Offers:**
- "I **will** carry that for you."
- "I **will** help you with the project."

- "I **won't** let you down." (negative)

**Decisions (Spontaneous):**
- "I **will** have the steak." (deciding at the moment)
- "I **will** take that job." (spontaneous decision)

**Will vs. Be Going To:**
- "I **will** call you." (spontaneous decision)
- "I **am going to** call you." (already planned)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I will to go. | I **will** go. |
| She will goes. | She **will** go. |
| I will have went. | I **will have gone**. |
| Will I to go? | **Will** I go? |

## 9. Would

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "Would" is used for polite requests, hypothetical situations, past habits, and conditional statements. It is the past form of "will."

**Key Uses:**
- **Polite requests** (more polite than "will")
- **Hypothetical situations** (imagined)
- **Past habits** (used to)
- **Conditional** (if...would)
- **Preferences** (would rather, would like)

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Polite Request**
```
[SUBJECT] + [WOULD] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
Would + you + help + me?
Would + you + like + some tea?
```

**Structure 2: Hypothetical**
```
[SUBJECT] + [WOULD] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
I + would + go + if I had time.
She + would + be + happy if she knew.
```

**Structure 3: Past Habit**
```
[SUBJECT] + [WOULD] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
When I was young, I + would + play + outside.
She + would + visit + us every Sunday.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Polite Requests:**
- "**Would** you help me, please?"
- "**Would** you like some tea?"
- "**Would** you mind opening the door?"
- "**Would** you please be quiet?"
- "I **would** like a coffee, please." (polite request)

**Hypothetical Situations:**
- "I **would** go if I had time."
- "She **would** be happy if she knew."
- "What **would** you do if you won the lottery?"
- "I **wouldn't** do that if I were you." (advice)

**Past Habits:**
- "When I was young, I **would** play outside."
- "She **would** visit us every Sunday."
- "He **would** always help me."
- "We **would** go to the beach every summer."

**Conditional Statements:**
- "If I had money, I **would** travel."
- "If she studied, she **would** pass."
- "If they arrived, we **would** leave."
- "If you asked, she **would** help."

**Preferences:**
- "I **would rather** stay home."
- "I **would like** a cup of tea."
- "She **would prefer** to leave early."
- "**Would** you **rather** have coffee or tea?"

**Would Have (Past Unreal):**
- "I **would have** helped if I had known."
- "She **would have** called if she could."
- "We **would have** won if we had tried harder."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I would to go. | I **would** go. |
| She would goes. | She **would** go. |
| I would have went. | I **would have gone**. |
| Would I to go? | **Would** I go? |

## 10. Need

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "Need" can function as a modal (in questions and negatives) or as a main verb. As a modal, it expresses necessity.

**Key Uses:**
- **Necessity** (in questions and negatives)
- **Lack of necessity** (don't need to)

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Modal Need (Question)**
```
[NEED] + [SUBJECT] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
Need + I + go + now?
Need + she + wait + for us?
```

**Structure 2: Modal Need (Negative)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [NEED] + [NOT] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
You + need + not + go + now.
She + need + not + wait + for us.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Modal Need (Questions):**
- "**Need** I go now?"
- "**Need** she wait for us?"
- "**Need** they come early?"
- "**Need** I say more?"

**Modal Need (Negatives):**
- "You **need** not go now."
- "She **need** not wait for us."
- "They **need** not come early."
- "You **needn't** worry." (contracted)

**Main Verb Need (Affirmative):**
- "I **need** to go now."
- "She **needs** to study harder."
- "We **need** more time."

**Main Verb Need vs. Modal Need:**
- Affirmative: "I **need** to go." (main verb)
- Negative: "You **need** not go." (modal)
- Question: "**Need** I go?" (modal)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I need not to go. (modal) | I **need** not go. |
| Need I to go? | **Need** I go? |
| She need not goes. | She **need** not go. |
| I need not went. | I **need** not go. |

## 11. Dare

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "Dare" can function as a modal (in questions and negatives) or as a main verb. As a modal, it expresses courage or challenge.

**Key Uses:**
- **Courage** (in questions and negatives)
- **Challenge** (dare someone to do something)

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Modal Dare (Question)**
```
[DARE] + [SUBJECT] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
Dare + I + go + in?
Dare + she + speak + to him?
```

**Structure 2: Modal Dare (Negative)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [DARE] + [NOT] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
I + dare + not + go + in.
She + dare + not + speak + to him.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Modal Dare (Questions):**
- "**Dare** I go in?"
- "**Dare** she speak to him?"
- "**Dare** they ask for more?"
- "How **dare** you?" (common expression)

**Modal Dare (Negatives):**
- "I **dare** not go in."
- "She **dare** not speak to him."
- "They **dare** not ask for more."
- "He **daren't** tell her." (contracted)

**Main Verb Dare (Affirmative):**
- "I **dare** to go in."
- "She **dares** to speak to him."
- "We **dare** to ask for more."
- "I **dared** him to jump." (to challenge)

**Main Verb Dare vs. Modal Dare:**
- Affirmative: "I **dare** to go." (main verb)
- Negative: "I **dare** not go." (modal)
- Question: "**Dare** I go?" (modal)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I dare not to go. (modal) | I **dare** not go. |
| Dare I to go? | **Dare** I go? |
| She dare not goes. | She **dare** not go. |

## 12. Ought to

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "Ought to" is used to express advice, moral obligation, or expectation. It is similar to "should" but slightly more formal.

**Key Points:**
- Similar to "should"
- Slightly more formal
- Expresses moral duty or expectation
- Always followed by "to" + base verb

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Advice/Obligation**
```
[SUBJECT] + [OUGHT TO] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
You + ought to + see + a doctor.
She + ought to + study + harder.
```

**Structure 2: Expectation**
```
[SUBJECT] + [OUGHT TO] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]
He + ought to + be + here by now.
The train + ought to + arrive + soon.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Advice:**
- "You **ought to** see a doctor."
- "She **ought to** study harder."
- "We **ought to** leave early."
- "He **ought to** apologize."

**Moral Obligation:**
- "We **ought to** help the poor."
- "People **ought to** be honest."
- "You **ought to** tell the truth."
- "She **ought to** respect her parents."

**Expectation:**
- "He **ought to** be here by now."
- "The train **ought to** arrive soon."
- "They **ought to** have called by now."
- "The package **ought to** arrive today."

**Ought to vs. Should:**
- "You **ought to** go." (formal, moral duty)
- "You **should** go." (advice, less formal)

**Ought to Have (Past Regret):**
- "I **ought to have** studied harder." (but I didn't)
- "She **ought to have** called." (but she didn't)
- "We **ought to have** left earlier." (but we didn't)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I ought to went. | I **ought to go**. |
| I ought have studied. | I **ought to have** studied. |
| She ought to goes. | She **ought to go**. |
| Ought I to go? | **Ought** I to go? |

## 💬 Practice: Conversations

### Dialogue 1: Using Can and Could

- A: "**Can** you help me with this?"
- B: "I **can't** right now. I'm busy."
- A: "**Could** you help me later?"
- B: "Yes, I **could** help you at 5 PM."
- A: "Great! I **could** finish this by then."
- B: "I **can** wait until then."

### Dialogue 2: Using May, Might, and Must

- A: "**May** I come in?"
- B: "Yes, you **may**."
- A: "Where is she? She **must** be here by now."
- B: "She **might** be running late."
- A: "It **may** be traffic."
- B: "She **might** have forgotten."

### Dialogue 3: Using Should and Ought to

- A: "I have a headache."
- B: "You **should** see a doctor."
- A: "You **ought to** take some medicine."
- B: "I **should have** taken some earlier."
- A: "You **ought to have** seen a doctor yesterday."
- B: "I know. I **should have** gone."

### Dialogue 4: Using Will and Would

- A: "I **will** help you with that."
- B: "**Would** you really do that?"
- A: "Yes, I **will**. I **would** do anything for you."
- B: "**Would** you like some tea?"
- A: "I **would** love some. I **will** have a cup."
- B: "I **would** have offered earlier if I had known."

### Dialogue 5: Using Need and Dare

- A: "**Need** I go now?"
- B: "No, you **needn't**. You can stay."
- A: "How **dare** you speak to me like that?"
- B: "I **dare** to tell the truth."
- A: "You **dare** not say that again."
- B: "I **dare** to say what I think."
