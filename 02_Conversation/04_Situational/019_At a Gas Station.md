### 4. Key Takeaways

- **Common Sentence Patterns:**
  - *"I need to fill up my tank – [type of fuel], please."* – Requesting gas.
  - *"Could you check my [tire pressure / oil level]?"* – Asking for a car check.
  - *"What pump number are you at?"* – Identifying the gas pump.
  - *"Would you like a full tank or just a certain amount?"* – Asking how much gas.
  - *"The recommended PSI is printed on a sticker inside the driver's door."* – Explaining where to find tire info.
  - *"That'll be [amount] for the gas and [amount] for [item] – total [amount]."* – Giving the total cost.

- **Useful Phrases to Remember:**
  - *"Sure thing."* – A friendly way to agree to help.
  - *"No problem."* – Responding to thanks.
  - *"I've been meaning to [do something]."* – Explaining you intended to do it earlier.
  - *"That's a relief."* – Expressing relief after a good check result.
  - *"You're good to go."* – Meaning everything is fine and you can leave.
  - *"Have a safe trip!"* – A kind farewell for someone going on a journey.

- **Practical Speaking Tips:**
  1. **Know your car's fuel type** – Gas stations offer *"regular unleaded,"* *"mid-grade,"* and *"premium."* Know which one your car needs. If you're unsure, check the owner's manual or ask: *"What fuel does my car take?"* Many attendants are knowledgeable.
  2. **Ask for services proactively** – Many stations offer free tire pressure checks, air, and even oil checks. Don't hesitate to ask: *"Could you check my tire pressure?"* or *"Can you check my oil?"* It's part of their service.
  3. **Know your tire PSI** – The recommended PSI is usually on a sticker inside the driver's door frame, the glove box, or the fuel cap. If you don't know, say: *"I'm not sure – could you check the sticker?"* Attendants will help.
  4. **Understand payment methods** – You can pay at the pump (with card) or inside the store (with cash or card). Ask: *"Do I pay at the pump or inside?"* Some stations require prepayment before pumping – ask: *"Do I need to prepay?"*
  5. **Check your washer fluid and oil regularly** – Before long trips, it's smart to have these checked. Use phrases like: *"Could you also check my windshield washer fluid?"* and *"Can you check my oil level?"* It only takes a minute and can prevent issues on the road.
  6. **Ask about air for tires** – Air is often free, but some stations charge. Ask: *"Is there a charge for air?"* If there is, they'll direct you to a coin-operated machine or a free hose. Knowing this saves you surprise fees.
