# Topic: Managing a Post-Merger Organizational Design / Structure Project

**Scenario:** You are the Chief People Officer (CPO) at a company that has just completed a major acquisition. The two organizations are now one legal entity, but the combined workforce of 5,000 people operates with overlapping roles, duplicated functions, and unclear reporting lines. You have been tasked with leading a post-merger organizational design project to: **define** the new organizational structure, **align** roles and responsibilities, **design** reporting lines and governance, **address** role duplication and redundancies, **communicate** the new structure transparently, and **support** employees through the transition—all while maintaining business continuity and minimizing disruption.

---

## Dialogue

| **Speaker** | **Dialogue / Text** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **You** | *(opening the design workshop)* Thank you all for joining. We're here to design the organizational structure for our combined company. This is not just about boxes on an org chart—it's about creating clarity, accountability, and alignment around our strategic priorities. I want us to be thoughtful, transparent, and inclusive in our approach. |
| **CEO** | What's the starting point? |
| **You** | We've mapped the current structures of both organizations. We'll start by defining our strategic priorities—what are the critical capabilities we need to execute our 3‑year plan? Then we'll design a structure that enables those capabilities. |
| **COO** | And how do we address overlapping roles? |
| **You** | We'll review each function and identify areas of duplication. For each, we'll decide on: **#1**—Consolidation (combining teams). **#2**—Integration (merging with a clear lead). **#3**—Redundancy (eliminating roles where there's genuine overlap). We'll be guided by business needs, not just cost savings. |
| **Head of Strategy** | What about the governance model? Who makes what decisions? |
| **You** | We'll design a clear decision‑rights framework—specifying who is accountable, who is consulted, and who must be informed for each major decision type. This prevents confusion and bottlenecks. |
| **HR Lead** | And what about the people impact? This is going to be unsettling for many. |
| **You** | We'll communicate early and often. We'll share the high‑level structure first, then detailed role changes, and finally individual placement decisions. We'll also offer career transition support, coaching, and outplacement services. |
| **Finance Lead** | What's the timeline? |
| **You** | 6 months: **Phase 1**—Design the high‑level structure (2 months). **Phase 2**—Define roles and responsibilities (2 months). **Phase 3**—Communicate and implement (2 months). We'll have checkpoints at each phase. |
| **CFO** | What about cost? |
| **You** | We'll achieve cost synergies through consolidation, but we're prioritizing quality and business continuity over pure cost savings. I'll provide a detailed budget for the transition. |
| **You** | *(closing)* This is a complex but essential project. Getting the structure right will enable us to realize the full potential of this merger. I'll send a detailed project plan with owners, milestones, and timelines. Thank you for your collaboration and leadership. |
| *(6 months later – follow-up)* | |
| **You** | *(to the executive team)* The new structure is fully implemented. We've defined clear roles and reporting lines, eliminated 15% of overlapping positions through a fair and transparent process, and achieved a 90% retention rate among key talent. |
| **COO** | The clarity in decision‑making has made a significant difference—we're moving faster. |
| **HR Lead** | Employee feedback has been positive—people appreciate the transparency and the support provided. |
| **You** | This is a great outcome. Now we'll focus on embedding the new structure and supporting leaders in their new roles. |

## Vocabulary

| **Word / Phrase** | **Part of Speech** | **Meaning** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Organizational design** | Noun Phrase | The process of structuring roles, teams, and reporting lines. |
| **Post-merger** | Adjective | Occurring after a merger or acquisition. |
| **Overlapping roles** | Noun Phrase | Positions with similar or duplicate responsibilities. |
| **Reporting lines** | Noun Phrase | The hierarchy of who reports to whom. |
| **Governance** | Noun | The system of rules and practices for decision-making. |
| **Decision‑rights framework** | Noun Phrase | A model specifying who makes which decisions. |
| **Duplication** | Noun | Unnecessary repetition of roles or functions. |
| **Consolidation** | Noun | Combining teams or functions into a single unit. |
| **Integration** | Noun | Merging teams with a clear lead and shared goals. |
| **Redundancy** | Noun | Elimination of positions that are no longer needed. |
| **Business continuity** | Noun Phrase | Maintaining critical operations during change. |
| **Outplacement services** | Noun Phrase | Support for employees leaving the organization. |
| **Career transition support** | Noun Phrase | Coaching and resources for employees changing roles. |
| **Synergies** | Noun | Benefits achieved through the combination of two organizations. |
| **Transparent** | Adjective | Open and honest about the process. |
| **Accountability** | Noun | Clear responsibility for outcomes. |
| **Strategic priorities** | Noun Phrase | The most important objectives for the combined organization. |
| **Clarity** | Noun | The quality of being clear and easy to understand. |
| **Alignment** | Noun | Agreement and coordination across the organization. |
| **Embedding** | Noun | The process of making a new structure fully operational. |
