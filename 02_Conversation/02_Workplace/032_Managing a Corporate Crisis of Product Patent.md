# Topic: Managing a Corporate Crisis of Product Patent / IP Theft

**Scenario:** You are the Chief Legal Officer at a biotech company. A competitor has launched a product that appears to infringe on one of your key patents—a critical formulation that underpins your flagship medication. The competitor's product is already gaining market share, and investors are asking questions. You need to convene an emergency meeting with the CEO, Head of R&D, Head of Product, Head of PR, and General Counsel to: **assess** the validity and scope of the patent infringement, **evaluate** legal options (litigation, licensing, negotiation), **assess** the business impact (market share, revenue, reputation), **develop** a communication strategy for investors, customers, and employees, **decide** on a course of action, **coordinate** with external counsel, and **protect** the company's competitive position and intellectual property portfolio.

---

## Dialogue

| **Speaker** | **Dialogue / Text** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **CEO** | *(opening the emergency meeting)* We have a serious situation. A competitor has launched a product that we believe infringes our core patent. Our patent is our crown jewel. We need to decide how to respond. |
| **You** | I've reviewed the competitor's product documentation with our patent counsel. The infringement appears clear—they are using the same formulation and delivery mechanism covered by our patent. We have a strong case. |
| **Head of R&D** | Our patent is valid until 2032. We have the original research and documentation to prove our innovation. |
| **General Counsel** | We have three options: **#1**—File a patent infringement lawsuit immediately. **#2**—Pursue a licensing agreement. **#3**—Engage in settlement negotiations. |
| **CEO** | What's the risk of each? |
| **You** | Option 1 is costly—$5M-$10M in legal fees—and could take 2-3 years. But it sends a strong signal and protects our IP. Option 2 would generate revenue through licensing fees but requires negotiation. Option 3 might end the conflict quickly but could set a precedent that weakens our position. |
| **Head of Product** | What about the market? The competitor is already gaining share. We need to act fast. |
| **CEO** | I'm leaning toward litigation. We need to defend our IP and deter other potential infringers. |
| **You** | I support that. We'll file for a preliminary injunction to stop the competitor from selling the product while the case proceeds. That will send a strong message. |
| **Head of PR** | What about our external communication? We need to reassure investors and customers. |
| **You** | We'll issue a statement: *"We have identified a potential infringement of our intellectual property. We are taking appropriate legal action to protect our innovation and our competitive position. We remain confident in our patent portfolio and our ability to defend it."* |
| **Head of Product** | What about our customers? They may be worried about supply continuity. |
| **You** | We'll reassure them that our supply chain is unaffected and that we remain committed to delivering high‑quality products. |
| **CEO** | And what about our employees? They'll be anxious. |
| **You** | We'll send an internal memo: *"We are taking action to protect our intellectual property. Our patents are strong, and we are confident in our legal position. We will keep you informed."* |
| **You** | *(closing)* To summarize: we're filing a lawsuit and seeking a preliminary injunction. We'll also explore licensing as a fallback. I'll coordinate with external counsel and provide regular updates to the board. |
| *(3 months later – follow‑up)* | |
| **You** | *(to the leadership team)* The preliminary injunction has been granted. The competitor is temporarily barred from selling the product. We've started settlement discussions and are confident we can reach a favorable resolution. |
| **CEO** | This is a significant victory. It sends a strong message to the market. |
| **Head of PR** | The media coverage has been positive—we're seen as protecting our innovation. |
| **You** | We'll continue to monitor and enforce our IP rights. This case underscores the importance of our patent strategy. |

## Vocabulary

| **Word / Phrase** | **Part of Speech** | **Meaning** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Patent infringement** | Noun Phrase | Unauthorized use of a patented invention. |
| **Intellectual property (IP)** | Noun | Creations of the mind, such as patents and trademarks. |
| **Preliminary injunction** | Noun Phrase | A court order to stop an action until a final decision is made. |
| **Litigation** | Noun | The process of taking legal action. |
| **Licensing** | Noun | Granting permission to use IP in exchange for payment. |
| **Settlement** | Noun | An agreement reached between parties to resolve a dispute. |
| **Patent portfolio** | Noun Phrase | A collection of patents owned by a company. |
| **External counsel** | Noun Phrase | Lawyers from an outside law firm. |
| **Competitive position** | Noun Phrase | A company's standing relative to competitors. |
| **Legal fees** | Noun Phrase | The cost of hiring lawyers and pursuing legal action. |
| **Infringe** | Verb | To violate a law or right. |
| **Deter** | Verb | To discourage someone from taking an action. |
| **Supply chain** | Noun Phrase | The network of suppliers and distributors. |
| **Market share** | Noun Phrase | The percentage of a market controlled by a company. |
| **Board** | Noun | The group of directors overseeing the company. |
| **Confidence** | Noun | A feeling of certainty about a positive outcome. |
| **Innovation** | Noun | The introduction of new ideas and methods. |
| **Competitor** | Noun | A company that offers similar products or services. |
| **Fallback** | Noun | A backup plan. |
| **Favorable resolution** | Noun Phrase | A positive outcome to a dispute. |
