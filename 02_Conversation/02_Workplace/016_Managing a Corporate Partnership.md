# Topic: Managing a Corporate Partnership / Joint Venture Performance Review

**Scenario:** You are the Director of Strategic Alliances at a global pharmaceutical company. Your company has a 5‑year joint venture (JV) with a biotech firm to develop and commercialize a new class of oncology drugs. The JV is now in its third year, and while the science is promising, the partnership is experiencing friction: missed milestones, cost overruns, and differing strategic priorities. You are now leading a joint performance review meeting with the JV's leadership team (your CEO, the partner's CEO, the JV General Manager, and the Head of R&D) to: **review** performance against the original business plan, **identify** root causes of underperformance, **renegotiate** the governance and resource allocation, **align** on revised priorities and timelines, **address** cultural and operational differences, **and secure** a renewed commitment to the partnership.

---

## Dialogue

| **Speaker** | **Dialogue / Text** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **You** | *(opening the review)* Thank you both for making time. This JV has the potential to be a game‑changer for both our companies—but we're not where we expected to be at this stage. Missed milestones and rising costs are putting the partnership at risk. I want us to be honest about the challenges and work together to course‑correct. |
| **Partner CEO** | I agree. Our teams are working hard, but we're not aligned on priorities. Our R&D team feels pressure to move fast; your team is more focused on regulatory compliance. This tension is slowing us down. |
| **Your CEO** | That's a fair observation. We need to find a way to accelerate without compromising safety and quality—that's non‑negotiable for us. |
| **JV General Manager** | The root cause is unclear decision‑making. We have too many layers of approval, and both parents are second‑guessing operational decisions. |
| **You** | I propose we streamline the governance: **#1**—Delegate operational decisions to the JV General Manager within a defined budget and scope. **#2**—Establish a joint steering committee with decision‑rights on strategy and major investments. **#3**—Set a 30‑day decision turnaround for all escalations. |
| **Partner CEO** | I support that—it gives clarity and speed. |
| **Head of R&D** | What about resource allocation? We need more senior scientists dedicated to the JV. |
| **You** | We'll commit to assigning 3 additional senior scientists for 12 months. Partner, can you match that? |
| **Partner CEO** | We can commit to 2, with the option to add a third if the program hits its milestones. |
| **Your CEO** | That's a reasonable compromise. |
| **You** | Now let's talk about the revised timeline. I propose we extend the development phase by 6 months, and we add a joint risk review every quarter to stay ahead of issues. |
| **Partner CEO** | Agreed. We need to focus on execution and trust. |
| **You** | *(closing)* To summarize: we've identified root causes, streamlined governance, agreed on resource allocation, extended the timeline, and committed to quarterly risk reviews. I'll send a revised JV operating agreement and project plan by EOD. This partnership is too important to fail—let's make it work. |
| *(6 months later – follow‑up)* | |
| **Your CEO** | *(to the JV leadership)* The governance changes have made a real difference. Decision‑making is faster, and the teams are more aligned. We've hit our last two milestones on time and on budget. |
| **Partner CEO** | I'm pleased with the progress. The new structure is working. |
| **You** | This is the partnership we envisioned. Let's keep the momentum and continue to build on this success. |

## Vocabulary

| **Word / Phrase** | **Part of Speech** | **Meaning** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Joint venture (JV)** | Noun | A business arrangement where two or more parties combine resources for a specific project. |
| **Performance review** | Noun Phrase | A formal assessment of how a partnership or project is progressing. |
| **Governance** | Noun | The system of rules and practices for decision‑making. |
| **Steering committee** | Noun Phrase | A group that oversees the direction of a partnership. |
| **Decision‑rights** | Noun Phrase | The authority to make specific types of decisions. |
| **Escalation** | Noun | The process of raising an issue to a higher level of authority. |
| **Resource allocation** | Noun Phrase | The distribution of people, budget, and tools to tasks. |
| **Milestones** | Noun | Significant checkpoints in a project. |
| **Risk review** | Noun Phrase | A regular assessment of potential problems. |
| **Operating agreement** | Noun Phrase | A document defining how a partnership will be managed. |
| **Strategic alignment** | Noun Phrase | Ensuring goals are consistent across partners. |
| **Cultural differences** | Noun Phrase | Varied values and behaviors between organizations. |
| **Non‑negotiable** | Adjective | Not open to discussion or compromise. |
| **Compromise** | Noun | An agreement reached through mutual concessions. |
| **Accountability** | Noun | The obligation to take responsibility for outcomes. |
| **Transparency** | Noun | Openness and honesty about actions and decisions. |
| **Execution** | Noun | The act of carrying out a plan. |
| **Momentum** | Noun | The force gained by continued progress. |
| **Partnership** | Noun | A collaborative, mutually beneficial relationship. |
| **Course‑correct** | Verb | To make changes to get back on track. |
