# Topic 26: Email Writing


## 1. Concept Explanation

**Email Writing** is the art of composing clear, concise, and professional messages for various purposes: **work communication**, **client correspondence**, **job applications**, **networking**, and more.

#### Difficult words explained:

* ***Concise*** = Brief, to the point, using few words.

* ***Professional*** = Appropriate for a work or formal setting.

* ***Tone*** = The attitude or feeling conveyed in writing (*friendly, formal, urgent, etc.*).


#### The 80/20 Rule for Email Writing:

**80%** of effective emails follow a simple structure:

1. **Clear Subject Line** – Summarizes the purpose.

2. **Appropriate Greeting** – Sets the tone.

3. **Brief Body** – States the purpose clearly.

4. **Call to Action** – Tells the reader what to do next.

5. **Professional Closing** – Ends politely.


Master this structure, and you'll write **effective emails** for any situation.

## 2. Rules (The Anatomy of an Email)

### Part 1: Subject Line

> The subject line is the **first thing** the recipient sees. It should be **clear**, **specific**, and **actionable**.

| Type | Example |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Informative** | *Meeting Rescheduled: Friday 10 AM* |
| **Action Required** | *Action Needed: Please Review Attached Document* |
| **Question** | *Question About Project Timeline* |
| **Follow-up** | *Following Up on Our Previous Conversation* |
| **Confirmation** | *Confirming Our Meeting on Tuesday* |

<br>

### Part 2: Greeting (Salutation)

> The greeting **sets the tone**. Match it to your **relationship** with the recipient.

| Formality | Greeting | When to Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Formal** | *Dear Mr. Smith, / Dear Dr. Patel,* | First time, unknown recipient, formal settings |
| **Semi-Formal** | *Dear John, / Hi John,* | Colleagues, known contacts, professional but friendly |
| **Informal** | *Hi John, / Hello John,* | Close colleagues, friends, casual settings |
| **Very Formal** | *To Whom It May Concern,* | When you don't know the recipient's name |

<br>

### Part 3: Opening

> Start with a **polite opening** to build rapport.

| Situation | Example |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Standard** | *I hope this email finds you well.* |
| **Follow-up** | *I hope you're having a good week.* |
| **Acknowledging** | *Thank you for your prompt response.* |
| **Apologizing** | *I apologize for the delay in responding.* |

<br>

### Part 4: Body

> The body is the **main content** of your email. Follow the **BLOT structure**:

* **Be Logical:** Organize your thoughts clearly.
* **One Topic:** Stick to **one main topic** per email.

**Tip:** Use **short paragraphs (2-4 sentences)** to make it easy to read.

<br>

### Part 5: Call to Action (CTA)

> Clearly state **what you want** the recipient to do.

| Type | Example |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Request** | *Could you please review the attached report and share your feedback?* |
| **Suggestion** | *Please let me know your availability for a meeting next week.* |
| **Question** | *Do you have any questions about the proposal?* |
| **Confirmation** | *Please confirm your attendance by Friday.* |



### Part 6: Closing

> End with a **professional sign-off**.

| Formality | Closing | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Formal** | *Yours sincerely, / Yours faithfully,* | *Yours sincerely, John Smith* |
| **Semi-Formal** | *Best regards, / Kind regards,* | *Best regards, Anna* |
| **Informal** | *Best, / Thanks, / Cheers,* | *Thanks, Ben* |
| **Action-Oriented** | *Looking forward to your response,* | *Looking forward to your response, Maria* |

<br>

### Part 7: Signature

> Include your **contact information** for professionalism.

**Example:**

```text
John Smith
Marketing Manager
ABC Corporation
Phone: +91 00000 00000
Email: john.smith@abc.com
Website: ://abc.com
```

## 3. Structure (Full Email Template)

```text

[Subject Line]

[Greeting],

[Opening - Polite Introduction]

[Body - Main Content]
- Paragraph 1: State the purpose clearly.
- Paragraph 2: Provide details, context, or explanation.
- Paragraph 3: Mention any attachments or additional information.

[Call to Action - What you want the recipient to do]

[Closing],

[Your Name]
[Your Title]
[Your Contact Information]

```

## 4. Examples (Full Emails)

#### Example 1: Formal Email (Job Application)

```text

Subject: Application for Marketing Manager Position - John Smith

Dear Hiring Manager,

I hope this email finds you well. I am writing to apply for the Marketing Manager position at ABC Corporation, as advertised on LinkedIn.

I have over 5 years of experience in digital marketing, with a proven track record of increasing brand visibility and driving sales. I am confident that my skills and experience align perfectly with your requirements.

Please find attached my resume and cover letter for your review. I would welcome the opportunity to discuss my application further.

Thank you for your time and consideration. I look forward to hearing from you.

Yours sincerely,
John Smith
Marketing Specialist
Phone: +91 00000 00000
Email: john.smith@email.com

```


#### Example 2: Professional Email (Colleague)

```text
Subject: Project Update - Q3 Report

Dear Priya,

I hope you're having a good week.

I wanted to provide you with a quick update on the Q3 project report. I have completed the initial draft and attached it for your review. Please take a look when you have a moment.

Specifically, could you please review the financial data on pages 5-7? I want to ensure the numbers are accurate before we present it to the client.

Let me know if you have any questions or suggestions. I'd appreciate your feedback by Friday, if possible.

Best regards,
Rahul
Project Manager
Phone: +91 00000 00000
```

#### Example 3: Semi-Formal Email (Networking)

```text
Subject: Following Up on Our Conversation at the Conference

Dear Ms. Sharma,

It was a pleasure meeting you at the Digital Marketing Conference last week. I really enjoyed our conversation about content strategy.

I wanted to follow up on your offer to connect me with your colleague in the marketing department. If you're still open to it, I would be very grateful for an introduction.

Please let me know if there's anything I can do in return.

Looking forward to your response.

Kind regards,
Amit Patel
Content Writer
Phone: +91 00000 00000
```


#### Example 4: Informal Email (Friend/Close Colleague)

```text
Subject: Lunch Tomorrow?

Hey Ananya,

How are you? Hope you're doing well.

Are you free for lunch tomorrow? I was thinking we could try that new Italian place near the office. Let me know if you're interested!

Also, I wanted to ask if you could help me with something. I'm working on a presentation and could use your expertise.

Let me know what you think.

Cheers,
Vikram
```

#### Example 5: Follow-up Email

```text

Subject: Following Up on Job Application - Marketing Manager Position

Dear Hiring Manager,

I hope this email finds you well. I am writing to follow up on my application for the Marketing Manager position, which I submitted on [date].

I am still very interested in this opportunity and would like to inquire about the status of my application. If you need any additional information from me, please do not hesitate to ask.

Thank you for your time and consideration. I look forward to hearing from you.

Best regards,
John Smith
Phone: +91 00000 00000
```

## 6. Common Mistakes (Email Writing)

| Common Mistake | Why it is wrong | Correct Version |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **"I am writing this email to..."** | **Redundant**; state the purpose directly. | ***I am writing to apply for the position.*** |
| **No subject line** | Emails with no subject line often get **ignored**. | **Always include a clear subject line.** |
| **"Please revert back to me."** | **'Revert'** means to go back to a previous state. Use **'reply'** or **'respond'**. | ***Please reply to me as soon as possible.*** |
| **All caps** | ALL CAPS IS PERCEIVED AS **SHOUTING** AND IS **UNPROFESSIONAL**. | **Use standard capitalization.** |
| **Too casual** | Using **slang** or **abbreviations** in formal emails. | Use formal language: ***I am writing to...*** *(not "I'm writing to...")* |
| **Vague call to action** | The recipient **doesn't know** what you want. | Be specific: ***Please review the attached document and send your feedback by Friday.*** |
| **Forgetting attachment** | Sending an email about an attachment and **forgetting it**. | **Double-check before sending.** Write: ***Attached is the document for your review.*** |
| **Typos and errors** | Poor grammar and spelling create a **negative impression**. | **Proofread before sending.** |

## 7. Vocabulary (10 Useful Email Phrases)

| Phrase | Use | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. "I am writing to..."** | State your **purpose** | *I am writing to apply for the position.* |
| **2. "I hope this email finds you well."** | Polite **opening** | *I hope this email finds you well.* |
| **3. "Please find attached..."** | Mention an **attachment** | *Please find attached the report.* |
| **4. "Thank you for your prompt response."** | Acknowledging **quick reply** | *Thank you for your prompt response.* |
| **5. "I apologize for the delay."** | Apologizing for **late reply** | *I apologize for the delay in responding.* |
| **6. "Could you please...?"** | Polite **request** | *Could you please review the document?* |
| **7. "I look forward to hearing from you."** | Ending with **anticipation** | *I look forward to hearing from you.* |
| **8. "Please do not hesitate to contact me."** | Encouraging **follow-up** | *Please do not hesitate to contact me.* |
| **9. "In the meantime..."** | Transition to **another point** | *In the meantime, please review the proposal.* |
| **10. "Thank you for your time and consideration."** | Showing **gratitude** | *Thank you for your time and consideration.* |

## 14. Answers (with explanations)

1. It should be **clear**, **specific**, and **summarize the purpose**. *(The subject line is the first thing the recipient sees.)*

2. **"Dear Mr./Ms./Dr. [Last Name]"** or **"To Whom It May Concern"** *(Formal greetings for unknown recipients.)*

3. To tell the reader **what action** they need to take. *(A call to action makes your request clear.)*

4. **"Please find attached..."** *(This is the standard phrase for mentioning attachments.)*

5. **Proofread it.** *(Always check for typos, errors, and missing attachments.)*

6. **"Best regards,"** or **"Kind regards,"** *(Semi-formal closings for colleagues and known contacts.)*

7. It's **all caps** and uses **exclamation points**—it's aggressive and unprofessional. *(Use standard capitalization.)*

8. **"I apologize for the delay in responding."** *(This is a polite and professional way to apologize.)*

9. **Slang**, **abbreviations**, **ALL CAPS**, and **emojis**. *(Keep professional emails formal and clear.)*

10. **"Yours sincerely,"** or **"Yours faithfully,"** *(These are the most formal closings.)*
