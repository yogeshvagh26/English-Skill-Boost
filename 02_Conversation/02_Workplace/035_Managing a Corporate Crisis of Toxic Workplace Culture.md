# Topic: Managing a Corporate Crisis of Toxic Workplace Culture / Employee Suffering

**Scenario:** You are the Chief People Officer (CPO) at a global consulting firm. An anonymous employee survey has revealed shocking levels of burnout, fear, and harassment across multiple teams. Employees describe a culture of "constant pressure," "public humiliation," and "no psychological safety." Several high‑profile exits have occurred, and a group of current employees has sent a letter to the board demanding immediate change. The CEO has asked you to lead a cultural transformation initiative to: **investigate** the root causes of the toxic culture, **engage** with employees to understand their experiences, **address** the most urgent issues (e.g., harassment, burnout, retaliation), **design** a new set of behavioral standards, **communicate** the changes transparently, **hold** leaders accountable, **measure** progress, and **restore** trust in the organization. You are now presenting the plan to the executive committee.

---

## Dialogue

| **Speaker** | **Dialogue / Text** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **CEO** | *(opening the meeting)* We have a serious problem. The survey results are alarming. Our people are suffering, and it's our responsibility to fix it. I need a plan to transform our culture. |
| **You** | I've analyzed the data. The key issues are: **#1**—Fear of speaking up (72% of employees say they're afraid to raise concerns). **#2**—Burnout and overwork (65% say they work more than 60 hours a week). **#3**—Harassment and discrimination (40% have experienced or witnessed it). This is not acceptable. |
| **CFO** | What's the business impact? |
| **You** | Turnover is 20% higher than our competitors, costing us $15M annually. Productivity is down 15%. Our brand is suffering—we're struggling to attract talent. |
| **COO** | What's the plan to address this? |
| **You** | *(sharing a slide)* Three pillars: **#1**—Safety: Establish a zero‑tolerance policy for harassment and retaliation. Create multiple safe channels for reporting concerns. **#2**—Well‑being: Implement a sustainable work model—protect weekends, enforce a "no‑meeting" day, and expand mental health support. **#3**—Accountability: Hold leaders accountable for culture. Include culture metrics in performance reviews and tie compensation to improvements. |
| **CHRO** | How do we ensure this is more than just a PowerPoint? |
| **You** | We'll launch a 90‑day listening tour—town halls, focus groups, and 1‑on‑1s. We'll also appoint a team of "culture ambassadors" from across the organization to guide the change. |
| **CEO** | What about the specific cases of harassment and discrimination? |
| **You** | We'll hire an external investigator to review all complaints and recommend actions. We'll also provide confidential support for affected employees. |
| **Head of Legal** | We need to be careful about legal exposure. But we also need to act decisively. |
| **You** | We'll be transparent about our actions. We'll publish a progress report every quarter—showing what we've done and what we're still working on. |
| **CEO** | I'm committed to this. We cannot afford to fail. |
| **You** | *(closing)* This is a defining moment for our company. We have the opportunity to build a culture of respect, trust, and well‑being. I'll send a detailed transformation plan with owners, budgets, and timelines. Let's get to work. |
| *(12 months later – progress update)* | |
| **You** | *(to the executive committee)* I'm pleased to report significant progress: harassment complaints are down 50%, employee engagement is up 20%, and turnover has decreased by 15%. The listening tour and culture ambassadors have been instrumental. |
| **CEO** | This is proof that real change is possible. Thank you for your leadership. |
| **You** | We're not done yet—but we're on the right path. We'll continue to monitor and evolve. |
