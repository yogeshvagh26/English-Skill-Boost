## 1. Situation : Customer Churn Prediction – The "Model ROI" Debate

**Context:** You are a Senior Data Analyst at a subscription-based fitness app. You've built a machine learning model that predicts customer churn with 85% accuracy. The Head of Marketing (Priya) wants to use it to launch targeted retention campaigns. The CFO (David) is skeptical—he wants to know the ROI before investing in operationalizing the model. The CEO (Sarah) has asked you to present the business case. You need to translate model performance into business impact and build a compelling case for implementation.

## 4. Key Takeaways

### Common Sentence Patterns (for Model ROI Storytelling)

| Pattern                                                              | Use                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| *"I've analyzed the model across three dimensions."*                 | To introduce a structured, balanced assessment.                                        |
| *"First, model performance—here's our accuracy and key trade-offs."* | To address technical questions while managing expectations.                            |
| *"Second, business impact—let me show you the math."*               | To translate model metrics into dollars.                                               |
| *"Third, implementation cost and ROI."*                             | To provide a complete financial picture.                                               |
| *"The cost of a false positive is [X], the cost of a false negative is [Y]."* | To explain the business trade-off.                                                    |
| *"My recommendation: Proceed with a pilot."*                        | To propose a low-risk, high-learning first step.                                      |
| *"If the pilot achieves ≥[X]% of projected ROI, we scale."*         | To set clear success criteria.                                                         |
| *"The story isn't [technical claim]. The story is [business case]."* | To reframe the narrative (anchor pattern).                                             |

### Useful Phrases to Remember
- **Introducing the model:** *"We've built a model that predicts churn—but more importantly, it helps us prevent it."*
- **Managing expectations:** *"No model is perfect—but the business value is clear even after accounting for false positives."*
- **Introducing the "cost trade-off":** *"A false positive costs us $50 (an offer). A false negative costs us $500 (lost LTV). We're better off with some false positives."*
- **When someone says "what about false alarms?":** *"We'd rather offer a discount to someone who would stay anyway than lose someone we could have saved."*
- **Introducing the pilot:** *"The pilot is a safety net—we test, learn, and scale only if it works."*
- **When the model is successful:** *"Our pilot saved $108k in 2 months—we're now scaling to full implementation."*

### Practical Speaking Tips (for Model ROI Narratives)
1.  **Start with the "why" (business problem), not the "how" (model details).** Stakeholders care about churn and revenue, not algorithms.
2.  **Translate model metrics into business metrics.** Accuracy → false positives → cost. Recall → false negatives → lost revenue. Precision → wasted effort.
3.  **Always show the "cost trade-off."** Explain the cost of false positives vs. false negatives to justify the model.
4.  **Use a simple "before vs. after" scenario.** Show current churn cost and projected savings. The contrast is powerful.
5.  **Propose a low-risk pilot.** A pilot validates the model with minimal risk and cost.
6.  **Set clear success metrics before starting.** Define what "success" looks like before the pilot begins.
7.  **Build a dashboard for real-time monitoring.** Stakeholders want to see the model's impact, not just hear about it.
8.  **Involve operations early.** Marketing and engineering need to be aligned before the model goes live.
9.  **Celebrate the wins.** When the pilot succeeds, shout it from the rooftops—it builds trust and momentum.
10. **Iterate and improve.** Models are never perfect—use feedback from the pilot to improve the model and the process.
