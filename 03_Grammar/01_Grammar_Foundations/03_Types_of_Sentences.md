# Phase 1: Grammar Foundations

## 3. Types of Sentences

Congratulations on making it this far! You've learned the **parts of speech** (the building blocks) and **sentence basics** (the structure). Now it's time to explore the four **types of sentences** — the different ways we express ourselves in English.

Think of it this way: *You have the words, you have the structure, now you need to know how to use them for different purposes — to state facts, ask questions, give commands, or express emotions.*

---

## 📖 How to Use This Guide

For every topic, follow this proven learning cycle:

1. **Learn** the concept and rules
2. **Study** sentence structures and formulas
3. **See** simple and real-life examples
4. **Learn** common mistakes and exceptions
5. **Complete** targeted exercises
6. **Practice** through conversations
7. **Write** your own sentences and short paragraphs
8. **Review** using quizzes and spaced repetition

---

## Topic Overview: The Four Types

| Type | Purpose | Punctuation | Example |
|------|---------|-------------|---------|
| **Declarative** | Make a statement | Period (.) | The sun is shining. |
| **Interrogative** | Ask a question | Question mark (?) | Is it raining? |
| **Imperative** | Give a command/request | Period or exclamation | Please sit down. |
| **Exclamatory** | Express strong emotion | Exclamation point (!) | What a beautiful day! |

Now, let's explore each type in detail!

## 1. DECLARATIVE SENTENCES

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **declarative sentence** makes a statement, provides information, or expresses an opinion. It is the most common type of sentence.

**Key Points:**
- States a fact, opinion, or idea
- Ends with a period (.)
- Can be positive or negative
- Can be simple or complex
- The subject usually comes before the verb

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Positive Declarative**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [REST]
The sun + rises + in the east.
```

**Structure 2: Negative Declarative**
```
[SUBJECT] + [AUXILIARY VERB] + [NOT] + [MAIN VERB] + [REST]
She + does + not + like + coffee.
```

**Structure 3: Declarative with Object/Complement**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [OBJECT/COMPLEMENT]
He + is + a teacher.
They + built + a house.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- I like pizza.
- She lives in New York.
- The weather is beautiful today.
- He doesn't speak French.
- They have finished their homework.

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "I need to go to the supermarket after work."
- "The meeting has been postponed until next Tuesday."
- "She has been working at this company for five years."
- "We don't have any milk left."
- "It's going to rain later this afternoon."

**Declarative Sentences in Different Tenses:**

| Tense | Example |
|-------|---------|
| Present Simple | I drink coffee every morning. |
| Past Simple | She visited Paris last year. |
| Future Simple | We will go to the beach tomorrow. |
| Present Continuous | They are watching a movie. |
| Present Perfect | He has already eaten lunch. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I no like it. | I don't like it. |
| She not coming. | She is not coming. |
| He have a car. | He has a car. |
| They was happy. | They were happy. |
| She don't understand. | She doesn't understand. |

### ✅ Complete: Exercises

**Exercise 1: Identify as Declarative or Not**
1. The sky is blue. (Declarative)
2. Are you coming? (Not declarative)
3. Please sit down. (Not declarative)
4. She sings beautifully. (Declarative)
5. What a mess! (Not declarative)

**Exercise 2: Convert to Negative Declarative**
1. I like chocolate. → I don't like chocolate.
2. She speaks Spanish. → She doesn't speak Spanish.
3. They have finished. → They haven't finished.
4. He is coming. → He isn't coming.

**Exercise 3: Complete the Declarative Sentences**
1. The sun _____ in the east. (rises)
2. She _____ to school every day. (goes)
3. They _____ a new car last week. (bought)
4. I _____ finish the project tomorrow. (will)

### 💬 Practice: Conversations

**Dialogue: Sharing News**
- A: "I got a promotion at work today." (declarative)
- B: "That's wonderful news!" (declarative)
- A: "I've been waiting for this for months." (declarative)
- B: "You deserve it. You work really hard." (declarative)
- A: "I'm going to celebrate with dinner tonight." (declarative)

### ✍️ Write: Practice

**Task 1:** Write 5 declarative sentences about your morning routine.

**Task 2:** Write a short paragraph (5-6 sentences) about your favorite hobby. Use only declarative sentences.

### 🔄 Review: Quiz

1. What punctuation does a declarative sentence end with?
2. Write a negative declarative sentence about your job.
3. Identify: "She doesn't eat meat." — What type of sentence?
4. Correct: "She no like to swim."

## 2. INTERROGATIVE SENTENCES

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** An **interrogative sentence** asks a question. It is used to request information, confirm facts, or invite a response.

**Key Points:**
- Asks a question
- Ends with a question mark (?)
- Often begins with a question word (who, what, when, where, why, how) or an auxiliary verb (do, does, did, is, are, was, were, have, has, had, will, can, should)

**Types of Interrogative Sentences:**

| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Yes/No Questions** | Answer with yes/no | Do you like coffee? |
| **Wh- Questions** | Ask for specific info | Where do you live? |
| **Tag Questions** | Confirm something | You're coming, aren't you? |
| **Choice Questions** | Offer options | Do you want tea or coffee? |

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Yes/No Question**
```
[AUXILIARY VERB] + [SUBJECT] + [MAIN VERB] + [REST]?
Do + you + like + coffee?
```

**Structure 2: Wh- Question**
```
[WH- WORD] + [AUXILIARY VERB] + [SUBJECT] + [MAIN VERB] + [REST]?
Where + do + you + live?
```

**Structure 3: Tag Question**
```
[STATEMENT] + [,] + [AUXILIARY VERB] + [SUBJECT]?
You're coming, + aren't + you?
```

**Structure 4: Choice Question**
```
[AUXILIARY VERB] + [SUBJECT] + [OPTION 1] + or + [OPTION 2]?
Do + you + want tea + or + coffee?
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- Yes/No: Are you ready?
- Wh-: What is your name?
- Wh-: When will they arrive?
- Wh-: Why did you leave?
- Tag: You're going, aren't you?

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "Could you please tell me where the nearest bank is?"
- "Have you ever been to Japan?"
- "Why did you decide to study English?"
- "You haven't finished yet, have you?"
- "Would you like some more coffee?"
- "What time does the movie start?"

**Question Words Guide:**

| Question Word | Asks About | Example |
|---------------|------------|---------|
| Who | Person | Who is she? |
| What | Thing/Idea | What is that? |
| When | Time | When will you arrive? |
| Where | Place | Where do you live? |
| Why | Reason | Why are you late? |
| How | Manner | How do you do it? |
| Which | Choice | Which one is yours? |
| Whose | Possession | Whose book is this? |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| You like coffee? | Do you like coffee? |
| What you want? | What do you want? |
| Where she lives? | Where does she live? |
| You are coming, don't you? | You are coming, aren't you? |
| Why you did that? | Why did you do that? |

**Word Order in Questions:**
- **Yes/No questions:** Verb + Subject + Rest
- **Wh- questions:** Question word + Verb + Subject + Rest

### ✅ Complete: Exercises

**Exercise 1: Change to Questions**
1. You like pizza. → Do you like pizza?
2. She lives in London. → Does she live in London?
3. They have finished. → Have they finished?
4. He is coming. → Is he coming?

**Exercise 2: Create Wh- Questions**
1. I live in New York. → Where do you live?
2. He's coming at 5 PM. → When is he coming?
3. She bought a car. → What did she buy?
4. He left because he was tired. → Why did he leave?

**Exercise 3: Add Tag Questions**
1. You're coming, _____? (aren't you)
2. She likes coffee, _____? (doesn't she)
3. We're late, _____? (aren't we)
4. He hasn't eaten, _____? (has he)

**Exercise 4: Correct the Errors**
1. You have a car? → Do you have a car?
2. What you want? → What do you want?
3. Where she went? → Where did she go?

### 💬 Practice: Conversations

**Dialogue: Asking Questions**
- A: "Where are you from?" (Wh- question)
- B: "I'm from Canada. And you?"
- A: "I'm from England. Have you ever been there?" (Yes/No question)
- B: "No, I haven't. Is it beautiful?" (Yes/No question)
- A: "It is, but it rains a lot. Do you speak French?" (Yes/No question)
- B: "A little bit. Why do you ask?" (Wh- question)

### ✍️ Write: Practice

**Task 1:** Write 10 questions you would ask a new friend (include all types of interrogative sentences).

**Task 2:** Write a dialogue (10-12 exchanges) between a tourist and a local. Use at least:
- 3 Yes/No questions
- 3 Wh- questions
- 1 Tag question
- 1 Choice question

### 🔄 Review: Quiz

1. What punctuation ends an interrogative sentence?
2. Correct: "You are going to the store?"
3. What's the tag question for: "She hasn't called yet, _____?"
4. Write a Wh- question asking about time.

## 3. IMPERATIVE SENTENCES

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** An **imperative sentence** gives a command, makes a request, offers advice, or gives instructions. The subject is usually "you" (understood) and is not stated.

**Key Points:**
- Gives a command or request
- Can end with a period (.) or exclamation point (!)
- The subject is implied "you"
- Often begins with a verb
- Can be positive (do something) or negative (don't do something)

**Types of Imperative Sentences:**

| Type | Purpose | Examples |
|------|---------|----------|
| **Commands** | Direct order | Stop! |
| **Requests** | Polite ask | Please sit down. |
| **Instructions** | How-to | Mix the ingredients. |
| **Advice** | Suggestion | Take a break. |
| **Warnings** | Caution | Watch out! |

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Positive Imperative**
```
[VERB] + [REST]
Sit + down.
```

**Structure 2: Negative Imperative**
```
[DO NOT] + [VERB] + [REST]
Don't + sit + there.
```

**Structure 3: Polite Imperative**
```
[PLEASE] + [VERB] + [REST]
Please + come + in.
```

**Structure 4: Imperative with "Let's"**
```
[LET'S] + [VERB] + [REST]
Let's + go + to the park.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- Commands: Close the door. Sit down.
- Requests: Please pass the salt.
- Instructions: Mix the flour and water.
- Advice: Get some rest.
- Warnings: Be careful! Watch out!
- Negative: Don't touch that! Don't be late.

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "Please turn off your cell phones during the performance." (polite request)
- "Don't forget to bring your ID." (negative instruction)
- "Let's meet at 3 PM outside the cafe." (suggestion)
- "Careful! That's hot!" (warning)
- "Write your name at the top of the page." (instruction)

**Polite Imperatives:**
```
[Please] + [verb] + [rest]
Please + help + me.

[Would you] + [please] + [verb] + [rest]?
Would you + please + help + me?
```

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| You sit down. (informal) | Sit down. |
| Don't you do that. | Don't do that. |
| Please to help me. | Please help me. |
| Let's we go. | Let's go. |
| No touch! | Don't touch! |

### ✅ Complete: Exercises

**Exercise 1: Change to Imperative**
1. You should take a break. → Take a break.
2. You need to mix the ingredients. → Mix the ingredients.
3. You must be careful. → Be careful.
4. You should not touch that. → Don't touch that.

**Exercise 2: Make Negative Imperatives**
1. Touch the paint. → Don't touch the paint.
2. Go there. → Don't go there.
3. Eat that. → Don't eat that.
4. Forget your keys. → Don't forget your keys.

**Exercise 3: Make Polite Imperatives**
1. Help me. → Please help me.
2. Pass the salt. → Please pass the salt.
3. Wait here. → Please wait here.
4. Repeat that. → Please repeat that.

### 💬 Practice: Conversations

**Dialogue: Giving Instructions**
- A: "Let's cook dinner together." (suggestion)
- B: "Okay. Please chop the vegetables." (polite imperative)
- A: "Do I need to wash them first?"
- B: "Yes, wash them thoroughly." (instruction)
- A: "Don't forget to peel the carrots." (negative)
- B: "Thanks! Be careful with the knife!" (warning)

### ✍️ Write: Practice

**Task 1:** Write a recipe for your favorite dish using 8+ imperative sentences.

**Task 2:** Write a set of instructions for someone visiting your home (where to park, how to enter, what to do). Use:
- 3 positive imperatives
- 3 negative imperatives
- 2 polite imperatives
- 1 "Let's" imperative

### 🔄 Review: Quiz

1. What is the implied subject in imperative sentences?
2. Correct: "Please to wait here."
3. Write a negative imperative warning.
4. What's the difference between "Sit down." and "Please sit down."?

## 4. EXCLAMATORY SENTENCES

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** An **exclamatory sentence** expresses strong emotion such as excitement, surprise, anger, joy, or frustration. It conveys the speaker's intense feelings.

**Key Points:**
- Expresses strong emotion
- Ends with an exclamation point (!)
- Often begins with "What" or "How" (exclamatory structure)
- Can be a single word or a longer sentence
- Shows the speaker's attitude

**Common Emotions in Exclamatory Sentences:**

| Emotion | Examples |
|---------|----------|
| **Surprise** | What a shock! |
| **Joy** | How wonderful! |
| **Anger** | That's so annoying! |
| **Fear** | Run! |
| **Excitement** | I can't believe it! |
| **Frustration** | Oh no! |
| **Admiration** | What a beautiful view! |

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: "What" Exclamatory**
```
[WHAT] + [A/AN] + [ADJECTIVE] + [NOUN]!
What + a + beautiful + day!
```

**Structure 2: "How" Exclamatory**
```
[HOW] + [ADJECTIVE] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB]!
How + beautiful + she + is!
```

**Structure 3: Single Word Exclamatory**
```
[WORD]!
Wow!
```

**Structure 4: Exclamatory with "So/Such"**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [SO/SUCH] + [ADJECTIVE]!
That + is + so + cool!
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Examples:**
- What a shame!
- How wonderful!
- Wow! That's amazing!
- I can't believe it!
- Oh no! Not again!
- That's incredible!

**Real-Life Examples:**
- "What a gorgeous sunset!" (admiration)
- "How could you do this to me?!" (anger/frustration)
- "I got the job!" (excitement)
- "Watch out! There's a car coming!" (warning)
- "I can't believe we won!" (surprise)
- "What an amazing performance!" (admiration)

**Exclamatory vs. Declarative:**

| Declarative (Statement) | Exclamatory (Emotion) |
|-------------------------|----------------------|
| It's a beautiful day. | What a beautiful day! |
| She sings well. | She sings so well! |
| That's amazing. | That's amazing! |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| What beautiful day! | What a beautiful day! (if countable singular) |
| How a beautiful day! | What a beautiful day! |
| I'm so happy! (statement) | I'm so happy! (can be exclamatory with emotion) |
| Overusing exclamation points | Use sparingly in formal writing |

**Rule for "What" vs. "How":**

| Structure | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| What + a/an + adjective + noun (singular countable) | What a beautiful day! |
| What + adjective + noun (plural/uncountable) | What beautiful flowers! |
| How + adjective + subject + verb | How beautiful she looks! |
| How + adverb + subject + verb | How quickly he runs! |

### ✅ Complete: Exercises

**Exercise 1: Change to Exclamatory**
1. It's a beautiful day. → What a beautiful day!
2. She is so talented. → She's so talented!
3. That's amazing. → That's amazing!
4. It's so cold. → It's so cold!

**Exercise 2: Complete with "What" or "How"**
1. _____ a cute puppy! (What)
2. _____ beautiful she is! (How)
3. _____ amazing news! (What)
4. _____ quickly he moves! (How)

**Exercise 3: Match the Emotion**
1. I won the lottery! (joy)
2. What a disaster! (frustration)
3. Look out! (warning)
4. That's incredible! (surprise)

### 💬 Practice: Conversations

**Dialogue: Reacting to News**
- A: "I won a free trip to Hawaii!" (exclamatory: excitement)
- B: "What wonderful news!" (exclamatory: joy)
- A: "How exciting is that!" (exclamatory: excitement)
- B: "I can't believe it! When do you leave?" (exclamatory: surprise)
- A: "Next week! I'm so happy!" (exclamatory: joy)

### ✍️ Write: Practice

**Task 1:** Write 10 exclamatory sentences expressing different emotions (surprise, joy, anger, fear, excitement, etc.).

**Task 2:** Write a dialogue between two friends who just received amazing news. Use at least:
- 5 exclamatory sentences
- 3 different emotions
- Mix of "What," "How," and single-word exclamations

### 🔄 Review: Quiz

1. What punctuation ends an exclamatory sentence?
2. Correct: "What beautiful day it is!"
3. When should you use "How" instead of "What"?
4. Write an exclamatory sentence showing surprise.

## 🔗 Putting It All Together: Comparison Chart

| Feature | Declarative | Interrogative | Imperative | Exclamatory |
|---------|-------------|---------------|------------|-------------|
| **Purpose** | State fact | Ask question | Give command | Express emotion |
| **Ending** | Period (.) | Question mark (?) | Period/Exclamation | Exclamation (!) |
| **Subject** | Usually explicit | Explicit | Implicit "you" | Explicit or implicit |
| **Examples** | I like pizza. | Do you like pizza? | Eat your pizza! | What a pizza! |
| **Formal Use** | Very common | Common | Common | Rare |
