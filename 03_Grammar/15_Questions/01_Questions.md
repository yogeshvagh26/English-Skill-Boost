# Phase 15: Questions

Welcome to Phase 15! You've mastered **verbs**, **nouns**, **pronouns**, **adjectives**, **adverbs**, **articles & determiners**, **prepositions**, **conjunctions**, **modals**, **voice**, **narration**, and **conditionals**. Now it's time to explore **questions** — the key to communication, conversation, and gathering information.

Think of it this way: *Questions are how we learn, connect, and understand. Without questions, conversations would be one-sided and limited. Mastering question forms in English — from simple yes/no questions to complex embedded questions — will transform your ability to communicate naturally.*

| Type | Function | Example |
|------|----------|---------|
| **Yes/No Questions** | Ask for confirmation (yes/no answer) | **Do** you like coffee? |

## 1. Yes/No Questions

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Yes/No questions** are questions that can be answered with "yes" or "no." They are used to ask for confirmation, permission, or basic information.

**Key Points:**
- Can be answered with yes/no
- Require auxiliary verb before the subject
- No question word (wh- word)
- Rising intonation in speech

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: With Auxiliary Verb (Be, Do, Have)**
```
[AUXILIARY] + [SUBJECT] + [MAIN VERB] + [REST]?
Do + you + like + coffee?
Is + she + coming + today?
Have + you + finished + your homework?
```

**Structure 2: With Modal Verbs**
```
[MODAL] + [SUBJECT] + [BASE VERB] + [REST]?
Can + you + help + me?
Should + I + go + now?
Will + she + come + to the party?
```

**Structure 3: With "To Be"**
```
[BE] + [SUBJECT] + [COMPLEMENT]?
Are + you + tired?
Is + she + a doctor?
```

**Structure 4: Negative Questions**
```
[AUXILIARY] + [NOT] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [REST]?
Don't + you + like + coffee?
Isn't + she + coming + today?
Can't + you + help + me?
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**With "Do":**
- "**Do** you like coffee?"
- "**Does** she speak French?"
- "**Did** you see the movie?"
- "**Don't** you like chocolate?" (negative)
- "**Doesn't** she work here?" (negative)

**With "Be":**
- "**Are** you ready?"
- "**Is** she coming?"
- "**Were** they at the party?"
- "**Aren't** you tired?" (negative)
- "**Isn't** she a doctor?" (negative)

**With "Have":**
- "**Have** you finished?"
- "**Has** she called?"
- "**Had** they arrived?"
- "**Haven't** you eaten?" (negative)
- "**Hasn't** she left?" (negative)

**With Modals:**
- "**Can** you help me?"
- "**Could** you open the door?"
- "**Will** she come?"
- "**Would** you like some tea?"
- "**Should** I call her?"
- "**Can't** you see?" (negative)
- "**Won't** you stay?" (negative)

**Answering Yes/No Questions:**

| Question | Yes Answer | No Answer |
|----------|------------|-----------|
| Do you like coffee? | Yes, I **do**. | No, I **don't**. |
| Is she coming? | Yes, she **is**. | No, she **isn't**. |
| Have you finished? | Yes, I **have**. | No, I **haven't**. |
| Can you help me? | Yes, I **can**. | No, I **can't**. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| You like coffee? (informal) | **Do** you like coffee? |
| She is coming? (informal) | **Is** she coming? |
| What you like? (WH) | **Do** you like coffee? |
| You have finished? (informal) | **Have** you finished? |
| Yes, I like. (incomplete) | Yes, I **do**. |

## 2. WH Questions

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **WH questions** ask for specific information and cannot be answered with yes/no. They begin with question words (who, what, when, where, why, how).

**Key Points:**
- Ask for specific information
- Begin with WH words
- Require a complete answer
- Use auxiliary verb before the subject

### 📐 Study: WH Question Words

| Word | Asks About | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Who** | Person (subject) | **Who** called you? |
| **Whom** | Person (object) | **Whom** did you see? |
| **What** | Thing/Idea | **What** is that? |
| **When** | Time | **When** will you arrive? |
| **Where** | Place | **Where** do you live? |
| **Why** | Reason | **Why** are you late? |
| **How** | Manner | **How** do you do it? |
| **Which** | Choice | **Which** one is yours? |
| **Whose** | Possession | **Whose** book is this? |

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: WH + Auxiliary + Subject + Verb**
```
[WH WORD] + [AUXILIARY] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [REST]?
Where + do + you + live?
What + are + you + doing?
Why + did + she + leave?
```

**Structure 2: WH + Verb (When "Who" is the Subject)**
```
[WH WORD] + [VERB] + [REST]?
Who + called + you?
What + happened + yesterday?
Which + is + yours?
```

**Structure 3: WH + Preposition (Formal)**
```
[WH WORD] + [AUXILIARY] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [PREPOSITION]?
Who + did + you + talk + to?
(Formal: To whom did you talk?)
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Who (Subject):**
- "**Who** called you?"
- "**Who** is coming to the party?"
- "**Who** wrote this book?"
- "**Who** lives in that house?"

**Whom (Object - Formal):**
- "**Whom** did you see?" (formal)
- "**Whom** are you waiting for?" (formal)
- "To **whom** should I address this letter?" (formal)

**What:**
- "**What** is your name?"
- "**What** are you doing?"
- "**What** do you want?"
- "**What** happened?" (subject)

**When:**
- "**When** will you arrive?"
- "**When** did she leave?"
- "**When** is the meeting?"
- "**When** does the train leave?"

**Where:**
- "**Where** do you live?"
- "**Where** is she?"
- "**Where** are you going?"
- "**Where** did you buy that?"

**Why:**
- "**Why** are you late?"
- "**Why** did she leave?"
- "**Why** don't you come?"
- "**Why** is he angry?"

**How:**
- "**How** are you?"
- "**How** do you do it?"
- "**How** much does it cost?"
- "**How** long have you been here?"
- "**How** often do you exercise?"
- "**How** far is the station?"

**Which:**
- "**Which** one is yours?"
- "**Which** color do you prefer?"
- "**Which** book is better?"
- "**Which** of these is correct?"

**Whose:**
- "**Whose** book is this?"
- "**Whose** car is parked outside?"
- "**Whose** idea was that?"

**WH Questions with Prepositions:**
- "**Who** are you talking **to**?"
- "**What** are you looking **at**?"
- "**Where** are you from?"
- "**Who** did you go **with**?"

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Where you live? | **Where do** you live? |
| What you are doing? | What **are** you doing? |
| Why she left? | Why **did** she leave? |
| Where is the station? (✓) | Where **is** the station? |
| Who you saw? | Who **did** you see? |
| Where you are from? | Where **are** you from? |

## 3. Question Tags

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Question tags** are short questions added to the end of a statement. They are used to seek confirmation, agreement, or to keep a conversation flowing.

**Key Points:**
- Added to the end of a statement
- Use the auxiliary verb from the statement
- Positive statement → Negative tag
- Negative statement → Positive tag
- Use the same subject (as a pronoun)

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Positive Statement + Negative Tag**
```
[POSITIVE STATEMENT] + [,] + [NEGATIVE AUXILIARY] + [SUBJECT]?
You like coffee, + don't + you?
She is coming, + isn't + she?
They have finished, + haven't + they?
```

**Structure 2: Negative Statement + Positive Tag**
```
[NEGATIVE STATEMENT] + [,] + [POSITIVE AUXILIARY] + [SUBJECT]?
You don't like coffee, + do + you?
She isn't coming, + is + she?
They haven't finished, + have + they?
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**With "Be":**
- "You are tired, **aren't you**?"
- "She is a doctor, **isn't she**?"
- "They aren't coming, **are they**?"
- "It isn't raining, **is it**?"

**With "Do":**
- "You like coffee, **don't you**?"
- "She speaks French, **doesn't she**?"
- "They don't work here, **do they**?"
- "He doesn't like pizza, **does he**?"

**With "Have":**
- "You have finished, **haven't you**?"
- "She has called, **hasn't she**?"
- "They haven't arrived, **have they**?"
- "He hasn't eaten, **has he**?"

**With Modals:**
- "You can swim, **can't you**?"
- "She will come, **won't she**?"
- "They should wait, **shouldn't they**?"
- "He couldn't come, **could he**?"

**With "I Am":**
- "I am right, **aren't I**?"
- "I am late, **aren't I**?"

**With "Let's":**
- "Let's go, **shall we**?"
- "Let's eat, **shall we**?"

**With Imperatives:**
- "Open the door, **will you**?"
- "Don't forget, **will you**?"
- "Help me, **can you**?"

**Special Cases:**

| Statement | Tag |
|-----------|-----|
| I am right | **aren't I**? |
| Let's go | **shall we**? |
| Open the door | **will you**? |
| Don't forget | **will you**? |
| There is a book | **isn't there**? |
| There are books | **aren't there**? |

**Intonation in Question Tags:**

| Intonation | Meaning |
|------------|---------|
| **Falling** (↓) | You are sure; seeking confirmation |
| **Rising** (↑) | You are unsure; asking a real question |

**Examples:**
- "You like coffee, don't you?" (falling = I think you do)
- "You like coffee, don't you?" (rising = I'm not sure, do you?)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| You like coffee, don't you? (✓) | You like coffee, **don't you**? |
| She is coming, isn't she? (✓) | She is coming, **isn't she**? |
| You don't like coffee, don't you? | You don't like coffee, **do you**? |
| She isn't coming, isn't she? | She isn't coming, **is she**? |
| I am right, amn't I? | I am right, **aren't I**? |
| Let's go, don't we? | Let's go, **shall we**? |

## 4. Embedded Questions

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Embedded questions** (also called indirect questions) are questions that are placed inside a statement or another question. They are used to sound more polite or formal.

**Key Points:**
- Question inside a statement or question
- Use statement word order (subject + verb)
- No auxiliary verb before the subject
- Often introduced by: I wonder, Do you know, Could you tell me

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Embedded Question in a Statement**
```
[INTRODUCTORY PHRASE] + [QUESTION WORD] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB]
I wonder + where + she + is.
I don't know + what + he + wants.
```

**Structure 2: Embedded Question in a Question**
```
[INTRODUCTORY QUESTION] + [QUESTION WORD] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB]?
Do you know + where + she + is?
Could you tell me + what + he + wants?
```

**Structure 3: Yes/No Embedded Question**
```
[INTRODUCTORY PHRASE] + [IF/WHETHER] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB]
I wonder + if + she + is coming.
Do you know + whether + he + has arrived?
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Embedded Questions in Statements:**
- "I wonder **where she is**."
- "I don't know **what he wants**."
- "I'm not sure **why she left**."
- "I forget **when the meeting starts**."
- "I can't remember **how to do it**."

**Embedded Questions in Questions:**
- "**Do you know** where she is?"
- "**Could you tell me** what he wants?"
- "**Can you tell me** why she left?"
- "**Do you remember** when the meeting starts?"
- "**Could you show me** how to do it?"

**Yes/No Embedded Questions:**
- "I wonder **if she is coming**."
- "Do you know **whether he has arrived**?"
- "I'm not sure **if I should go**."
- "Can you tell me **if this is correct**?"
- "I don't know **whether to go or stay**."

**Embedded Question with "To":**
- "I don't know **how to do it**."
- "I can't decide **what to eat**."
- "She doesn't know **where to go**."
- "Tell me **when to start**."
- "I'm not sure **whether to stay**."

**Direct vs. Embedded Questions:**

| Direct Question | Embedded Question |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| Where is she? | I wonder **where she is**. |
| What does he want? | Do you know **what he wants**? |
| Is she coming? | I wonder **if she is coming**. |
| Why did she leave? | Can you tell me **why she left**? |
| When does the train leave? | Could you tell me **when the train leaves**? |

**Common Introductory Phrases:**

| Phrase | Example |
|--------|---------|
| I wonder | I wonder **where he is**. |
| I don't know | I don't know **what to do**. |
| I'm not sure | I'm not sure **if she's coming**. |
| Can you tell me | Can you tell me **where the station is**? |
| Could you tell me | Could you tell me **what time it is**? |
| Do you know | Do you know **how to get there**? |
| Do you remember | Do you remember **when we met**? |
| I can't remember | I can't remember **where I put it**. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I wonder where is she. | I wonder **where she is**. |
| Do you know what time is it? | Do you know **what time it is**? |
| I wonder if is she coming. | I wonder **if she is coming**. |
| Can you tell me where is the station? | Can you tell me **where the station is**? |
| I don't know what do I do. | I don't know **what to do**. |

**Embedded Question Word Order:**

| Incorrect | Correct |
|-----------|---------|
| I wonder where **is she**. | I wonder where she **is**. |
| Do you know what time **is it**? | Do you know what time it **is**? |
| Can you tell me where **is the station**? | Can you tell me where the station **is**? |

## 💬 Practice: Conversations

### Dialogue 1: Using Yes/No Questions

- A: "**Do** you like coffee?"
- B: "Yes, I **do**. **Do** you?"
- A: "No, I **don't**. I prefer tea."
- B: "**Is** she coming to the party?"
- A: "Yes, she **is**. She told me yesterday."
- B: "**Has** she arrived yet?"
- A: "No, she **hasn't**."

### Dialogue 2: Using WH Questions

- A: "**Where** do you live?"
- B: "I live in London."
- A: "**How long** have you lived there?"
- B: "I have lived there for five years."
- A: "**Why** did you move there?"
- B: "I moved there for work."
- A: "**What** do you do there?"
- B: "I work as a teacher."

### Dialogue 3: Using Question Tags

- A: "You like coffee, **don't you**?"
- B: "Yes, I do. You don't like it, **do you**?"
- A: "No, I don't. She is coming tonight, **isn't she**?"
- B: "Yes, she is. They haven't arrived yet, **have they**?"
- A: "No, they haven't. I am right, **aren't I**?"
- B: "Yes, you are."

### Dialogue 4: Using Embedded Questions

- A: "**Can you tell me** where the station is?"
- B: "Yes, it's just down the street."
- A: "**Do you know** when the next train leaves?"
- B: "I'm not sure, but I **wonder if** we can check online."
- A: "**Could you tell me** how to get to Platform 3?"
- B: "**Can you tell me** if this is the right train?"
- A: "I **don't know** whether to go or wait."
