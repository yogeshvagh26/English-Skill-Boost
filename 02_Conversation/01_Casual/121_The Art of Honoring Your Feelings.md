## Topic: The Art of Honoring Your Feelings

### Dialogue

| Speaker | Line |
|---------|------|
| **Alexis** | I've been feeling a lot lately—sadness, anger, confusion—and I keep trying to push it away. I feel like I shouldn't be feeling this way. |
| **Alexa** | That's such a common reaction. But your feelings aren't wrong or bad—they're simply signals. They're telling you something important. |
| **Alexis** | I was taught to be strong, to keep it together. So when I feel overwhelmed, I tell myself to just get over it. |
| **Alexa** | I understand that pressure. But being strong doesn't mean ignoring your feelings—it means facing them with courage. |
| **Alexis** | So I'm allowed to feel sad without needing to fix it right away? |
| **Alexa** | Absolutely. You don't have to fix your feelings. You just have to let them be there. They're temporary. |
| **Alexis** | I think I've been running from my emotions because they feel too big. |
| **Alexa** | That makes sense. But when you run, they follow. When you stop and face them, they start to soften. |
| **Alexis** | What does it mean to "honor" my feelings? |
| **Alexa** | It means acknowledging them without judgment. Saying, "I feel angry, and that's okay. I feel sad, and that's human." |
| **Alexis** | I've been so hard on myself for feeling down. I tell myself I should be grateful instead. |
| **Alexa** | You can be both. You can feel grief and gratitude at the same time. They're not mutually exclusive. |
| **Alexis** | I never thought of it that way. I thought I had to pick one. |
| **Alexa** | No, you can hold both. That's the richness of being human. |
| **Alexis** | I think I've been afraid that if I let myself feel, I'll fall apart. |
| **Alexa** | That's a real fear. But what if you don't fall apart? What if you just feel, and then it passes, and you're still standing? |
| **Alexis** | I want to try that. To let myself feel without judging it. |
| **Alexa** | That's a brave step. You can start by just naming the feeling. "I am feeling anxious right now." |
| **Alexis** | That sounds so simple. But it's a big shift for me. |
| **Alexa** | It is. And it's the beginning of emotional freedom. |
| **Alexis** | I'm going to try to sit with my feelings tonight. Just let them be there. |
| **Alexa** | I'll be here if you want to share what comes up. No pressure, no fixing—just listening. |
| **Alexis** | That means more than you know. |
| **Alexa** | You're not alone in this. Your feelings are welcome here. |

### Vocabulary with Meanings

| Word / Phrase | Type | Meaning |
|---------------|------|---------|
| **Honoring** (your feelings) | Verb | Acknowledging and respecting your emotions without judgment |
| **Signals** (emotions as) | Noun (plural) | Messages from your body and mind that tell you something about your needs |
| **Courage** | Noun | The ability to face fear and difficulty without backing down |
| **Temporary** | Adjective | Lasting for a limited time; not permanent |
| **Soften** (emotions) | Verb | Become less intense or sharp |
| **Acknowledging** | Verb | Recognizing and accepting something as true |
| **Judgment** (without) | Noun | Avoiding being critical or evaluative about your feelings |
| **Grief and gratitude** | Noun | Two emotions that can coexist in the same moment |
| **Mutually exclusive** | Phrase | Two things that cannot exist at the same time |
| **Richness** (of being human) | Noun | The depth and complexity of human emotions and experiences |
| **Fall apart** (feeling like) | Phrasal Verb | To feel emotionally overwhelmed and unable to cope |
| **Emotional freedom** | Noun / Phrase | The state of being able to feel without being controlled by your emotions |
| **Sit with** (my feelings) | Phrase | To allow yourself to experience an emotion without trying to change or escape it |
