## 1. Situation : Customer Support Review – The "Ticket Backlog" Crisis

**Context:** You are a Senior Data Analyst at a B2B SaaS company with 10,000 customers. The Head of Support (Maya) is overwhelmed—ticket backlog has grown 200% over the past quarter, and average resolution time has increased from 4 hours to 18 hours. Customer Satisfaction (CSAT) scores have dropped from 4.8 to 4.2 (out of 5). The CEO (Sarah) is concerned about churn risk. Maya blames the increase in ticket volume on a new product launch. Engineering blames the support team for being inefficient. You've been asked to investigate and present a data-backed plan.

## 4. Key Takeaways

### Common Sentence Patterns (for Support Operations Storytelling)

| Pattern                                                              | Use                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| *"I've analyzed the support data from multiple angles."*             | To introduce a comprehensive analysis.                                                 |
| *"First, [Dimension A]—here's what I found."*                       | To present findings one at a time.                                                     |
| *"Second, [Dimension B]—I discovered that [insight]."*              | To build the case with additional layers.                                              |
| *"Third, [Dimension C]—we're inefficiently using our [resource]."*  | To identify operational inefficiencies.                                                |
| *"My recommendation is a [X]-stage approach: (1) [Action], (2) [Action], (3) [Action]."* | To provide a structured improvement plan.                                              |
| *"I've modeled the impact—here's the projected improvement."*       | To show the potential ROI of the recommendations.                                      |
| *"The story isn't [blame]. The story is [process improvement]."*    | To reframe the narrative (anchor pattern).                                             |
| *"I'll build a dashboard to track [metrics] in real-time."*         | To commit to measurement and accountability.                                           |

### Useful Phrases to Remember
- **Introducing the problem:** *"Our support team is overwhelmed, but the data reveals the root cause."*
- **Acknowledging the team's effort:** *"Maya's team is working hard—the problem is the process, not the people."*
- **Introducing "deflection":** *"The best support ticket is the one that never gets opened. Self-service is key."*
- **When someone says "we need more headcount":** *"Let's first optimize the existing process—then we can evaluate headcount needs based on data."*
- **Calling out "repeat tickets":** *"40% of our tickets are repeat questions—that's a knowledge base opportunity."*
- **When proposing automation:** *"Automation isn't about replacing humans—it's about freeing them to do higher-value work."*

### Practical Speaking Tips (for Support Operations Narratives)
1.  **Classify tickets first.** Before you can solve the problem, understand what type of tickets are flooding the system. Category analysis reveals where the real issues lie.
2.  **Focus on "deflection" opportunities.** Self-service (knowledge base, chatbots, FAQs) reduces ticket volume without adding headcount.
3.  **Use a "Tiered Model."** Separate basic questions from complex issues. This optimizes your senior agents' time.
4.  **Calculate the "Cost per Ticket."** Show the financial impact of support efficiency—this gets executive attention.
5.  **Monitor "First Contact Resolution (FCR)."** High FCR leads to higher CSAT and lower effort—it's a critical metric.
6.  **Build a Support Dashboard.** Include: ticket volume, resolution time, CSAT, deflection rate, and agent productivity. Review it weekly.
7.  **Celebrate quick wins.** A knowledge base article on the top 10 questions can have immediate impact. Show the results.
8.  **Involve Product in the solution.** If tickets are about product confusion, Product should improve onboarding and UI.
9.  **Set clear SLAs (Service Level Agreements).** Define expected resolution times by ticket type—this sets expectations and improves focus.
10. **Review agent performance with empathy.** Use data to identify coaching opportunities, not to criticize individuals.
