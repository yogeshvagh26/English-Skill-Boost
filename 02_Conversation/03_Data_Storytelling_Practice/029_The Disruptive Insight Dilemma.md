## 1. Situation : Boardroom Showdown – The "Disruptive Insight" Dilemma

**Context:** You are a Senior Data Analyst at a publicly-traded retail company. The CEO (Jennifer) is presenting a "growth story" to the board—a narrative about how the company is successfully expanding into new markets. Your data shows something different: the new markets are underperforming, and the "growth" is actually coming from cannibalizing existing markets. You need to present this disruptive insight to the board—but you must do so in a way that doesn't embarrass the CEO or undermine her credibility. Your job depends on getting this right.

---

## 2. Conversation

| Speaker       | Dialogue                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **CEO (Jennifer)** | I'm thrilled to share our Q3 results. We've grown revenue 8% year-over-year, driven by our successful expansion into the European market. Our strategy is working, and we're on track for another record year.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Board Member (Marcus)** | Impressive numbers, Jennifer. Can we see the breakdown by region?                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **CEO (Jennifer)** | *(flips to a slide)* As you can see, Europe is up 25%, North America is flat, and Asia is up 5%. Our total growth is driven by Europe.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **You (Analyst)** | *(raises hand carefully)* Jennifer, if I may—I have a slightly different read on this data. I've done a deeper dive into the European numbers, and I'd like to share what I found.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **CEO (Jennifer)** | *(surprised)* Go ahead.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **You (Analyst)** | Jennifer's data is accurate—Europe is up 25%. But when I segment that growth by customer type, I found that 80% of the European growth is coming from **existing North American customers** who opened European offices and bought through our new subsidiary. Only 20% is from new European-native customers.                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Board Member (Marcus)** | So we're not actually gaining new customers—we're just moving revenue from one column to another?                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **You (Analyst)** | That's the concern. In fact, our total customer base grew only 2% year-over-year. The 8% revenue growth is driven by these customers buying more—not by net new customers. That's not necessarily bad—but it's a different story than "new market expansion."                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **CEO (Jennifer)** | *(defensive)* I think the Analyst is oversimplifying. Even if the revenue is from existing customers, it's still revenue. And it validates our model in Europe.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **You (Analyst)** | Jennifer, I completely agree it's real revenue—and I'm not saying the European strategy is failing. I'm saying the **narrative** needs to be more precise. If we tell investors "we're growing through European expansion," they'll expect European customer acquisition. When they look at the data and see it's mostly existing customers, they'll lose trust.                                                                                                              |
| **Board Member (Sarah)** | The Analyst has a point. Jennifer, what's the real story here?                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **You (Analyst)** | The real story is: *"We're growing revenue by deepening our relationship with our existing customers, who are expanding globally. We're not failing to acquire new Europeans—we're just not there yet. The European expansion is a medium-term play, not a short-term win."* That's a story we can own.                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **CEO (Jennifer)** | *(long pause)* ...I see your point. We've been telling the wrong story. Let's reframe the narrative. We'll use your segmentation in the investor presentation.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **You (Analyst)** | I'll work with you to build the new narrative. My **recommendation**: (1) Show total growth (8%), (2) Segment it—"Revenue from new international customers" vs. "Revenue from existing customers expanding internationally," (3) Set clear metrics for *net new customer acquisition* in Europe as a goal for next year.                                                                                                                                                       |
| **CEO (Jennifer)** | Let's do it. And Analyst—thank you for the candor. This could have been an embarrassing conversation with investors.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **You (Analyst)** | The story isn't *"We're growing in Europe."* The story is *"Our existing customers are growing, and we're growing with them—and we have a clear plan to acquire net new customers in Europe next year."* That's a powerful, honest story.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |

| **Cannibalization**             | Noun           | When growth in one area comes at the expense of another                                | *The European growth was actually cannibalizing our North American revenue.*                     |
| **Narrative reframing**         | Noun Phrase    | Changing the story being told about the data to be more accurate                       | *Narrative reframing is essential when the initial narrative is misleading.*                     |
| **Net new customer acquisition**| Noun Phrase    | The number of new customers minus churned customers                                    | *Net new customer acquisition should be the metric for "growth" success.*                        |

| **Investor confidence**         | Noun Phrase    | The trust investors have in the company's leadership and strategy                      | *Investor confidence is fragile—one misleading story can damage it permanently.*                |

## 4. Key Takeaways

### Common Sentence Patterns (for Delivering a Disruptive Insight)

| Pattern                                                              | Use                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| *"If I may—I have a slightly different read on this data."*         | To respectfully introduce a challenging perspective.                                   |
| *"Jennifer's data is accurate—but I found something different when I segmented it by [dimension]."* | To validate the person's work while adding nuance.                                     |
| *"That's the concern. Our [metric] is actually [X]—not [Y]."*       | To present the counter-narrative with concrete numbers.                                |
| *"I'm not saying the strategy is failing. I'm saying the narrative needs to be more precise."* | To separate the strategy from the story—you can support one while correcting the other. |
| *"The real story is: [new narrative]."*                              | To propose the alternative, more accurate story.                                       |
| *"My recommendation: (1) Show [X], (2) Segment it by [Y], (3) Set [Z] as our new goal."* | To provide a clear action plan.                                                        |
| *"The story isn't [old narrative]. The story is [new narrative]."*   | To reframe the narrative (anchor pattern).                                             |
| *"This could have been an embarrassing conversation with investors—thank you for the candor."* | To reinforce the value of honesty and build trust.                                     |

### Useful Phrases to Remember
- **Introducing a disruptive insight gently:** *"I've been diving deeper into this data—and I found something interesting that I think we should discuss."*
- **Validating the existing narrative:** *"You're absolutely right that the revenue is there. But the *source* of that revenue tells a different story."*
- **When the leader gets defensive:** *"I'm not questioning your strategy—I'm questioning the story we're telling about it. We need the story to match the data."*
- **Distinguishing "strategy" from "story":** *"The strategy might still be right. But the story we tell about it needs to be accurate—otherwise we lose trust."*
- **Calling out "cannibalization":** *"We're not adding new customers—we're moving revenue from one column to another. That's not growth, that's shuffling."*
- **When the leader agrees:** *"Thank you for being open to this. I'll work with you to build the new narrative."*

### Practical Speaking Tips (for Delivering Disruptive Insights)
1.  **Validate first, then correct.** Start by acknowledging what's right (e.g., "Your data is accurate"). Then introduce the nuance. This reduces defensiveness.
2.  **Distinguish between "strategy" and "story."** The strategy might be right, but the story might be wrong. This allows the leader to save face while correcting the narrative.
3.  **Show the "hidden" data.** If the leader is looking at aggregate data, show the segmented data. The granularity reveals the truth.
4.  **Use "we" language.** Say: *"We've been telling the wrong story"*—not "you've been telling the wrong story." This makes it a shared problem, not a personal attack.
5.  **Provide a "new narrative" upfront.** Don't just say "that's wrong." Say: *"Here's the story we should be telling instead."* This makes you a solution provider, not a critic.
6.  **Quantify the risk of the wrong narrative.** Say: *"If we go to investors with this story, they'll expect X. When X doesn't happen, we lose credibility."*
7.  **Offer to help rebuild the narrative.** Don't just point out the problem—offer to help fix it. Say: *"I'll work with you to build a more accurate deck."*
8.  **Practice the "sandwich" method (but use it sparingly).** Praise → Criticism → Praise. For example: *"Your overall strategy is solid. However, this specific data point is misleading. But I'm confident we can fix it."*
9.  **Be prepared for backlash.** Disruptive insights can be uncomfortable. Stay calm, stick to the data, and be patient.
10. **Follow up with a "reframed" document.** After the meeting, send a short document with the new narrative, supporting data, and clear metrics. This makes the change official and actionable.
