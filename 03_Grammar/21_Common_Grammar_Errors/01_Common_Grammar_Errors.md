## 1. Double Negatives

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **double negative** occurs when two negative words are used in the same clause, creating a positive meaning (or confusion). In standard English, double negatives are considered incorrect.

**Key Points:**
- Two negatives in one clause
- Creates a positive meaning
- Avoid in standard English
- Use one negative only

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Incorrect (Double Negative)**
```
[NEGATIVE VERB] + [NEGATIVE WORD]
I + don't + have + no + money.
She + didn't + see + nobody.
```

**Structure 2: Correct (Single Negative)**
```
[NEGATIVE VERB] + [POSITIVE WORD]
I + don't + have + any + money.
She + didn't + see + anybody.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Incorrect (Double Negative):**
- "I don't have **no** money." → Correct: "I don't have **any** money."
- "She didn't see **nobody**." → Correct: "She didn't see **anybody**."
- "He doesn't want **nothing**." → Correct: "He doesn't want **anything**."
- "I can't find **nowhere**." → Correct: "I can't find **anywhere**."
- "She never goes **nowhere**." → Correct: "She never goes **anywhere**."

**Correct Alternatives:**

| Incorrect | Correct |
|-----------|---------|
| I don't have no money. | I don't have **any** money. / I have **no** money. |
| She didn't see nobody. | She didn't see **anybody**. / She saw **nobody**. |
| He doesn't want nothing. | He doesn't want **anything**. / He wants **nothing**. |
| I can't find nowhere. | I can't find **anywhere**. / I can find **nowhere**. |
| She never goes nowhere. | She never goes **anywhere**. / She goes **nowhere**. |

**Exceptions (Informal/Non-Standard):**
- In some dialects, double negatives are used for emphasis.
- In standard English, avoid double negatives.

**Common Negative Words to Avoid in Double Negatives:**

| Negative Word | Positive Replacement |
|---------------|---------------------|
| no | any |
| none | any |
| nothing | anything |
| nobody | anybody |
| nowhere | anywhere |
| never | ever |
| neither | either |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I don't have no money. | I don't have **any** money. |
| She didn't see nobody. | She didn't see **anybody**. |
| He doesn't want nothing. | He doesn't want **anything**. |
| I can't find nowhere. | I can't find **anywhere**. |
| She never goes nowhere. | She never goes **anywhere**. |

## 2. Dangling Modifiers

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **dangling modifier** is a word or phrase that modifies a word not clearly stated in the sentence. It "dangles" because the subject it modifies is missing.

**Key Points:**
- Modifier has no clear subject
- Creates confusion or unintended meaning
- Often at the beginning of a sentence

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Incorrect (Dangling)**
```
[MODIFIER] + [,] + [MAIN CLAUSE WITH WRONG SUBJECT]
Walking to work, the rain started.
(Who was walking? The rain?)
```

**Structure 2: Correct (Clear Subject)**
```
[MODIFIER] + [,] + [MAIN CLAUSE WITH CLEAR SUBJECT]
Walking to work, I felt the rain start.
(Now the subject "I" is clear.)
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Incorrect (Dangling Modifiers):**
- "**Walking to work**, the rain started." (Who was walking?)
- "**Reading the book**, the characters seemed real." (Who was reading?)
- "**Looking out the window**, the trees were swaying." (Who was looking?)
- "**Having studied all night**, the exam was easy." (Who studied?)
- "**Broken by the storm**, I saw the tree." (What was broken?)

**Corrected Sentences:**

| Incorrect | Correct |
|-----------|---------|
| Walking to work, the rain started. | **While I was walking** to work, the rain started. |
| Reading the book, the characters seemed real. | **Reading the book**, I felt the characters seemed real. |
| Looking out the window, the trees were swaying. | **Looking out the window**, I saw the trees swaying. |
| Having studied all night, the exam was easy. | **Having studied** all night, I found the exam easy. |
| Broken by the storm, I saw the tree. | I saw the tree that **had been broken by the storm**. |

**Fixing Dangling Modifiers:**

**Method 1: Add the Subject**
- "**Walking to work**, I felt the rain start."

**Method 2: Change the Modifier to a Clause**
- "**While I was walking** to work, the rain started."

**Method 3: Put the Subject First**
- "I saw the tree **that had been broken** by the storm."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Walking to work, the rain started. | **While I was walking** to work, the rain started. |
| Reading the book, the characters seemed real. | **Reading the book**, I felt the characters seemed real. |
| Having studied all night, the exam was easy. | **Having studied** all night, I found the exam easy. |

## 3. Split Infinitives

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **split infinitive** occurs when an adverb is placed between "to" and the base verb (e.g., "to quickly go"). While traditionally considered incorrect, it is now widely accepted in modern English.

**Key Points:**
- Adverb between "to" and verb
- Traditional grammar: avoid
- Modern English: acceptable
- Can be awkward if overused

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Incorrect (Traditional)**
```
[TO] + [ADVERB] + [VERB]
to + quickly + go
to + slowly + eat
```

**Structure 2: Correct (Traditional)**
```
[TO] + [VERB] + [ADVERB]
to + go + quickly
to + eat + slowly
```

**Structure 3: Acceptable (Modern)**
```
[TO] + [ADVERB] + [VERB]
to + quickly + go
(Now widely accepted)
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Traditional (Avoid Splitting):**
- "She decided **to go quickly**." (Not: to quickly go)
- "I want **to eat slowly**." (Not: to slowly eat)
- "He tried **to run fast**." (Not: to fast run)
- "She plans **to leave soon**." (Not: to soon leave)

**Modern (Acceptable Splitting):**
- "She decided **to quickly go**." (Acceptable)
- "I want **to slowly eat**." (Acceptable)
- "He tried **to fast run**." (Acceptable)
- "She plans **to soon leave**." (Acceptable)

**When to Avoid Splitting Infinitives:**
- In formal writing
- When it sounds awkward
- When it creates confusion

**When Splitting is Preferred:**
- For emphasis: "I want **to really understand**."
- For clarity: "She needs **to fully explain**."
- In informal speech: "I'm going **to quickly grab** a coffee."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake (Traditional) | Correction |
|----------------------|-----------|
| To quickly go | **To go quickly** |
| To slowly eat | **To eat slowly** |
| To fast run | **To run fast** |
| To soon leave | **To leave soon** |

**Note:** In modern English, split infinitives are widely accepted and often preferred for clarity and emphasis.

## 4. Run-on Sentences

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **run-on sentence** occurs when two or more independent clauses are joined without proper punctuation or conjunctions.

**Key Points:**
- Two or more independent clauses
- No proper punctuation
- No conjunction
- Confusing and grammatically incorrect

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Incorrect (Run-on)**
```
[CLAUSE 1] + [CLAUSE 2]
I like coffee she likes tea.
```

**Structure 2: Correct Options**
```
[CLAUSE 1] + [.] + [CLAUSE 2]
I like coffee. She likes tea.

[CLAUSE 1] + [,] + [CONJUNCTION] + [CLAUSE 2]
I like coffee, and she likes tea.

[CLAUSE 1] + [;] + [CLAUSE 2]
I like coffee; she likes tea.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Incorrect (Run-on Sentences):**
- "I like coffee she likes tea."
- "She wanted to go but she was tired." (missing comma)
- "I went to the store I bought milk."
- "He studied hard he passed the exam."
- "The sun was setting the birds were singing."

**Corrected Sentences:**

| Incorrect | Correct |
|-----------|---------|
| I like coffee she likes tea. | I like coffee, **and** she likes tea. |
| She wanted to go but she was tired. | She wanted to go, **but** she was tired. |
| I went to the store I bought milk. | I went to the store **and** I bought milk. |
| He studied hard he passed the exam. | He studied hard, **so** he passed the exam. |
| The sun was setting the birds were singing. | The sun was setting; the birds were singing. |

**Ways to Fix Run-on Sentences:**

| Method | Example |
|--------|---------|
| **Period** | I like coffee. She likes tea. |
| **Comma + Conjunction** | I like coffee, and she likes tea. |
| **Semicolon** | I like coffee; she likes tea. |
| **Semicolon + Conjunctive Adverb** | I like coffee; however, she likes tea. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I like coffee she likes tea. | I like coffee, **and** she likes tea. |
| She wanted to go but she was tired. | She wanted to go, **but** she was tired. |
| I went to the store I bought milk. | I went to the store **and** I bought milk. |
| He studied hard he passed the exam. | He studied hard, **so** he passed the exam. |

## 5. Sentence Fragments

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **sentence fragment** is an incomplete sentence that lacks a subject, a verb, or a complete thought. It is often a dependent clause standing alone.

**Key Points:**
- Incomplete sentence
- Lacks subject, verb, or complete thought
- Often a dependent clause
- Cannot stand alone

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Incorrect (Fragment)**
```
[DEPENDENT CLAUSE]
Because I was tired.
```

**Structure 2: Correct (Complete Sentence)**
```
[DEPENDENT CLAUSE] + [,] + [INDEPENDENT CLAUSE]
Because I was tired, I went to bed.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Incorrect (Sentence Fragments):**
- "Because I was tired."
- "When she arrived."
- "After the meeting was over."
- "The man who lives next door."
- "Running quickly down the street."

**Corrected Sentences:**

| Fragment | Complete Sentence |
|----------|------------------|
| Because I was tired. | **Because I was tired**, I went to bed. |
| When she arrived. | **When she arrived**, we started the meeting. |
| After the meeting was over. | **After the meeting was over**, we went home. |
| The man who lives next door. | **The man who lives next door** is a doctor. |
| Running quickly down the street. | I saw her **running quickly down the street**. |

**Ways to Fix Sentence Fragments:**

| Method | Example |
|--------|---------|
| **Add a subject** | The man **is** a doctor. |
| **Add a verb** | The dog **barked**. |
| **Combine with a main clause** | Because I was tired, I went to bed. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Because I was tired. | **Because I was tired**, I went to bed. |
| When she arrived. | **When she arrived**, we started. |
| After the meeting. | **After the meeting**, we went home. |
| The man who lives next door. | The man who lives next door **is a doctor**. |

## 6. Misplaced Modifiers

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **misplaced modifier** is a word or phrase that is placed too far from the word it modifies, creating confusion or unintended meaning.

**Key Points:**
- Modifier in wrong position
- Confuses the meaning
- Creates unintended humor
- Should be placed near the word it modifies

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Incorrect (Misplaced)**
```
[MODIFIER] + [WRONG WORD]
She almost drove her kids to school.
(She almost drove them? Or she almost drove to school?)
```

**Structure 2: Correct (Clear)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [MODIFIER] + [OBJECT]
She drove her kids almost to school.
(Now it's clear: she drove them near school.)
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Incorrect (Misplaced Modifiers):**
- "She **almost** drove her kids to school." (Did she almost drive them, or did she almost get there?)
- "He **nearly** ate the whole pizza." (Did he nearly eat it, or did he eat nearly the whole pizza?)
- "I **only** ate the sandwich." (Did I only eat it, or was it the only thing I ate?)
- "The man was walking the dog **in a red shirt**." (Who wore the red shirt?)
- "She saw a cat **running down the street**." (Who was running?)

**Corrected Sentences:**

| Incorrect | Correct |
|-----------|---------|
| She almost drove her kids to school. | She drove her kids **almost to school**. |
| He nearly ate the whole pizza. | He ate **nearly the whole pizza**. |
| I only ate the sandwich. | **Only I** ate the sandwich. / I ate **only the sandwich**. |
| The man was walking the dog in a red shirt. | **The man in a red shirt** was walking the dog. |
| She saw a cat running down the street. | She saw a cat **that was running down the street**. |

**Placement of "Only":**

| Position | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| **Only** I ate the sandwich. | No one else ate it. |
| I **only** ate the sandwich. | I only ate it (didn't do anything else). |
| I ate **only** the sandwich. | I ate nothing else. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She almost drove her kids to school. | She drove her kids **almost to school**. |
| He nearly ate the whole pizza. | He ate **nearly the whole pizza**. |
| I only ate the sandwich. | **Only I** ate the sandwich. / I ate **only the sandwich**. |
| The man was walking the dog in a red shirt. | **The man in a red shirt** was walking the dog. |

## 7. Wrong Prepositions

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Wrong prepositions** occur when the incorrect preposition is used in a phrase. Many verbs and adjectives require specific prepositions.

**Key Points:**
- Common source of errors
- Verbs require specific prepositions
- Adjectives require specific prepositions
- Memorization is often required

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Incorrect Preposition**
```
[VERB/ADJECTIVE] + [WRONG PREPOSITION]
She is interested + in + art. (Correct)
She is interested + for + art. (Wrong)
```

**Structure 2: Correct Preposition**
```
[VERB/ADJECTIVE] + [CORRECT PREPOSITION]
She is interested + in + art.
He is good + at + math.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Common Verb + Preposition Errors:**

| Incorrect | Correct |
|-----------|---------|
| She depends **of** me. | She depends **on** me. |
| I apologize **for** being late. (✓) | I apologize **for** being late. |
| He is married **with** her. | He is married **to** her. |
| We arrived **to** London. | We arrived **in** London. |
| I discussed **about** the problem. | I discussed the problem. (no preposition) |
| She entered **into** the room. | She entered the room. (no preposition) |

**Common Adjective + Preposition Errors:**

| Incorrect | Correct |
|-----------|---------|
| She is good **in** math. | She is good **at** math. |
| I am afraid **from** spiders. | I am afraid **of** spiders. |
| She is interested **for** art. | She is interested **in** art. |
| He is proud **for** his son. | He is proud **of** his son. |
| I am tired **for** waiting. | I am tired **of** waiting. |
| She is angry **on** him. | She is angry **with** him. |

**Common Preposition Combinations:**

| Verb + Preposition | Example |
|--------------------|---------|
| depend **on** | I depend **on** you. |
| apologize **for** | She apologized **for** being late. |
| believe **in** | I believe **in** hard work. |
| look **at** | Look **at** this picture. |
| wait **for** | I am waiting **for** you. |
| listen **to** | Listen **to** me. |
| speak **to** | I spoke **to** him. |
| think **about** | I am thinking **about** you. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She depends of me. | She depends **on** me. |
| She is good in math. | She is good **at** math. |
| I am afraid from spiders. | I am afraid **of** spiders. |
| She is interested for art. | She is interested **in** art. |
| He is married with her. | He is married **to** her. |
| We arrived to London. | We arrived **in** London. |

## 8. Article Errors

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Article errors** occur when the wrong article (a, an, the) is used or when an article is omitted when needed.

**Key Points:**
- "A" before consonant sounds
- "An" before vowel sounds
- "The" for specific items
- Zero article for general/plural/uncountable

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Incorrect Article**
```
[WRONG ARTICLE] + [NOUN]
I saw a apple. → I saw **an** apple.
She is a honest person. → She is **an** honest person.
```

**Structure 2: Correct Article**
```
[CORRECT ARTICLE] + [NOUN]
I saw an apple.
She is an honest person.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**A vs. An Errors:**

| Incorrect | Correct |
|-----------|---------|
| I saw **a** apple. | I saw **an** apple. |
| She is **a** honest person. | She is **an** honest person. |
| I need **a** hour. | I need **an** hour. |
| She is **an** university student. | She is **a** university student. |
| I have **a** umbrella. | I have **an** umbrella. |

**The vs. Zero Article Errors:**

| Incorrect | Correct |
|-----------|---------|
| I saw **the** dog. (general) | I saw **a** dog. |
| Dogs are loyal. (general) | Dogs are loyal. (zero article) |
| Water is essential. (general) | Water is essential. (zero article) |
| I read **the** book you gave me. (specific) | I read **the** book you gave me. |

**Common Article Rules:**

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| **A** before consonant sound | **a** book, **a** university |
| **An** before vowel sound | **an** apple, **an** hour |
| **The** for specific/unique | **the** sun, **the** book you gave me |
| **Zero** for general plural | ___ Books are useful. |
| **Zero** for general uncountable | ___ Water is essential. |
| **Zero** for proper nouns | ___ Sarah is here. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I saw a apple. | I saw **an** apple. |
| She is a honest person. | She is **an** honest person. |
| I need a hour. | I need **an** hour. |
| She is an university student. | She is **a** university student. |
| I saw the dog. (general) | I saw **a** dog. |

## 9. Tense Shifts

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **tense shift** occurs when the verb tense changes unnecessarily within a sentence or paragraph, creating confusion.

**Key Points:**
- Unnecessary change in tense
- Creates confusion
- Avoid within sentences
- Maintain consistency

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Incorrect (Tense Shift)**
```
[PAST TENSE] + [PRESENT TENSE]
She **walked** to school and **runs** home.
```

**Structure 2: Correct (Consistent)**
```
[PAST TENSE] + [PAST TENSE]
She **walked** to school and **ran** home.

[PRESENT TENSE] + [PRESENT TENSE]
She **walks** to school and **runs** home.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Incorrect (Tense Shifts):**
- "She **walks** to school every day and **ran** home." (Inconsistent)
- "He **was** happy because he **is** successful." (Inconsistent)
- "I **went** to the store and **buy** milk." (Inconsistent)
- "She **studies** hard and **passed** the exam." (Inconsistent)

**Corrected Sentences:**

| Incorrect | Correct |
|-----------|---------|
| She walks to school and ran home. | She **walks** to school and **runs** home. |
| He was happy because he is successful. | He **is** happy because he **is** successful. |
| I went to the store and buy milk. | I **went** to the store and **bought** milk. |
| She studies hard and passed the exam. | She **studied** hard and **passed** the exam. |

**When Tense Shifts Are Acceptable:**

| Situation | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| **Different time frames** | She **was** a student, but now she **is** a doctor. |
| **Fact/General truth** | He **said** that water **boils** at 100°C. |
| **Sequence of events** | I **woke up**, **ate** breakfast, and **went** to work. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She walks to school and ran home. | She **walks** to school and **runs** home. |
| He was happy because he is successful. | He **is** happy because he **is** successful. |
| I went to the store and buy milk. | I **went** to the store and **bought** milk. |
| She studies hard and passed the exam. | She **studied** hard and **passed** the exam. |

## 10. Pronoun Errors

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Pronoun errors** occur when the wrong pronoun is used, including subject/object confusion, unclear antecedents, and agreement errors.

**Key Points:**
- Subject vs. object pronouns
- Unclear antecedents
- Agreement errors
- Pronoun case errors

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Subject Pronoun**
```
[SUBJECT PRONOUN] + [VERB]
She + is + happy.
I + am + here.
```

**Structure 2: Object Pronoun**
```
[VERB] + [OBJECT PRONOUN]
She gave it to me.
I saw him.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Subject vs. Object Pronoun Errors:**

| Incorrect | Correct |
|-----------|---------|
| **Me** and John are friends. | **John and I** are friends. |
| **Her** and I are going. | **She and I** are going. |
| I gave it to **she**. | I gave it to **her**. |
| **Him** is a doctor. | **He** is a doctor. |
| Between you and **I** | Between you and **me** |

**Unclear Antecedents:**

| Incorrect | Correct |
|-----------|---------|
| When Sarah talked to Mary, she was happy. (Who was happy?) | Sarah was happy when she talked to Mary. |
| The book is on the table, and it is red. (What is red?) | The book on the table is red. |

**Pronoun Agreement Errors:**

| Incorrect | Correct |
|-----------|---------|
| Everyone brought **his** book. (male-centric) | Everyone brought **their** book. |
| Each student should do **his** homework. | Each student should do **their** homework. |
| The team celebrated **its** victory. (US: singular) | The team celebrated **their** victory. (UK: plural) |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Me and John are friends. | **John and I** are friends. |
| Her and I are going. | **She and I** are going. |
| I gave it to she. | I gave it to **her**. |
| Him is a doctor. | **He** is a doctor. |
| Between you and I | Between you and **me** |
| Everyone brought his book. | Everyone brought **their** book. |

## 📚 Quick Reference Card

| Error | Problem | Example | Correction |
|-------|---------|---------|------------|
| **Double Negative** | Two negatives | I don't have no money. | I don't have **any** money. |
| **Dangling Modifier** | No clear subject | Walking to work, the rain started. | **While walking**, I felt the rain. |
| **Split Infinitive** | Adverb between "to" and verb | To quickly go | **To go quickly** |
| **Run-on Sentence** | Two sentences joined | I like coffee she likes tea. | I like coffee, **and** she likes tea. |
| **Sentence Fragment** | Incomplete sentence | Because I was tired. | **Because I was tired**, I went to bed. |
| **Misplaced Modifier** | Modifier in wrong position | She almost drove her kids to school. | She drove her kids **almost to school**. |
| **Wrong Preposition** | Incorrect preposition | She is good in math. | She is good **at** math. |
| **Article Error** | Incorrect article | I saw a apple. | I saw **an** apple. |
| **Tense Shift** | Inconsistent tense | She walks and ran. | She **walks** and **runs**. |
| **Pronoun Error** | Incorrect pronoun | Me and John are friends. | **John and I** are friends. |
