# Topic: Managing a Corporate Brand Refresh / Rebranding Initiative

**Scenario:** You are the Chief Marketing Officer (CMO) at a mid-sized technology company. Your company was founded 20 years ago and has grown significantly, but the brand identity has not evolved to reflect your current offerings, values, and market position. The CEO has approved a brand refresh initiative—including a new logo, updated visual identity, revised messaging, and a new website. You are now leading a cross-functional task force (Marketing, Design, Product, Sales, and HR) to: **define** the new brand strategy and positioning, **develop** the visual identity and messaging framework, **plan** the internal and external launch, **address** concerns about brand equity loss, **manage** the transition timeline, and **secure** cross-functional commitment—all while ensuring the refresh strengthens rather than dilutes the brand.

---

## Dialogue

| **Speaker** | **Dialogue / Text** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **You** | *(opening the task force meeting)* Thank you all for joining. Our brand has served us well for two decades, but it no longer reflects who we are today—or where we're going. This refresh is about aligning our brand with our modern, innovative, and customer-centric identity. We're not changing our values—we're clarifying and modernizing them. |
| **Design Lead** | What's the scope of the refresh? Are we changing everything? |
| **You** | We're updating: **#1**—Logo and visual identity. **#2**—Tagline and messaging framework. **#3**—Website and digital presence. **#4**—Internal brand materials. We're also refining our mission, vision, and values. |
| **Sales Lead** | I'm concerned about brand equity. Our current logo is recognized—changing it could confuse customers. |
| **You** | That's a valid concern. We'll roll out the refresh with a clear narrative explaining why we're evolving and what's staying the same. We'll also maintain brand recognition through colour and typography continuity. |
| **Product Lead** | How do we ensure the new brand reflects our products accurately? |
| **You** | We'll work with Product to ensure our messaging is aligned with our product features and customer benefits. We'll also test the new messaging with a customer advisory panel. |
| **HR Lead** | What about the internal launch? Our employees are brand ambassadors. |
| **You** | We'll launch internally first—with a town hall, training sessions, and a brand toolkit. Employees will get early access to the new materials and be invited to provide feedback. |
| **Marketing Lead** | How long is the timeline? |
| **You** | 12 months, broken into 4 phases: **Phase 1**—Discovery and strategy (3 months). **Phase 2**—Creative development (4 months). **Phase 3**—Internal launch and training (2 months). **Phase 4**—External launch (3 months). |
| **Design Lead** | And what about the cost? |
| **You** | We've budgeted $500K for the refresh—covering external agency fees, internal resources, and launch activities. I'll provide a detailed breakdown. |
| **You** | *(assigning tasks)* **Design**—lead the creative development. **Marketing**—manage the launch and communications. **HR**—lead the internal rollout. **Product**—ensure messaging alignment. **Sales**—provide customer insights. I'll oversee the overall initiative and report to the CEO quarterly. |
| **You** | *(closing)* This refresh is an exciting opportunity to tell our story in a more compelling and authentic way. I'm confident it will strengthen our market position and deepen customer loyalty. Let's work together to make it a success. |
| *(12 months later – launch day)* | |
| **You** | *(to the task force)* The new brand is live. The website, logo, and materials are out. Early feedback is overwhelmingly positive—customers and employees are embracing the change. |
| **Sales Lead** | Our customers are excited—they see it as a sign of our growth and innovation. |
| **HR Lead** | And employees are proud—they feel part of something fresh and forward-looking. |
| **You** | This is exactly what we hoped for. Now let's continue to embed the new brand into everything we do. |

## Vocabulary

| **Word / Phrase** | **Part of Speech** | **Meaning** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Brand refresh** | Noun Phrase | Updating a brand's visual and messaging identity. |
| **Rebranding** | Noun | A comprehensive change to a company's brand identity. |
| **Visual identity** | Noun Phrase | The visual elements of a brand, such as logo, color, and typography. |
| **Messaging framework** | Noun Phrase | A structured set of brand messages and value propositions. |
| **Brand equity** | Noun Phrase | The value derived from brand recognition and reputation. |
| **Tagline** | Noun | A short, memorable brand slogan. |
| **Customer advisory panel** | Noun Phrase | A group of customers who provide feedback. |
| **Brand ambassadors** | Noun Phrase | Employees and advocates who represent the brand. |
| **Town hall** | Noun | A company-wide meeting for communication. |
| **Brand toolkit** | Noun Phrase | A set of resources for consistent brand implementation. |
| **Continuity** | Noun | Consistency and uninterrupted connection. |
| **Stakeholder** | Noun | Anyone with an interest in the brand refresh. |
| **Authentic** | Adjective | Genuine and true to the company's values. |
| **Compelling** | Adjective | Evoking interest and conviction. |
| **Differentiate** | Verb | To make a brand stand out from competitors. |
| **Mission** | Noun | The company's core purpose and objectives. |
| **Vision** | Noun | The long-term aspiration of the company. |
| **Values** | Noun | The fundamental beliefs that guide behavior. |
| **Launch campaign** | Noun Phrase | A coordinated effort to introduce the new brand. |
| **Forward-looking** | Adjective | Focused on the future and growth. |
