### 1. Situation Name : At a Hospital / Clinic – Seeing a Doctor

### 2. Conversation

| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Receptionist**    | Good morning! Welcome to Green Valley Clinic. Do you have an appointment?                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Emma**            | Yes, I do. My name is Emma Wilson. I have an appointment with Dr. Martinez at 10:30 AM.                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Receptionist**    | Let me check. … Yes, I see you here. Could you please fill out this registration form? We need your contact information, insurance details, and a brief description of your symptoms.                                                    |
| **Emma**            | Of course. Here's the completed form.                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Receptionist**    | Thank you. Please have a seat in the waiting area. The nurse will call you in shortly.                                                                                                                                                   |
| *(20 minutes later – Nurse calls Emma)*                                                                                                                                                                                                 |

| **Emma**            | Sure.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Nurse**           | I'm going to check your temperature, blood pressure, and heart rate. May I have your arm? *(wraps the cuff)* … Your blood pressure is a little elevated, but nothing too concerning. Your temperature is 99.8°F, so you have a mild fever. What symptoms have you been experiencing? |
| **Emma**            | I've had a persistent cough for about five days, and I feel really tired. I also have a sore throat and a headache.                                                                                                                      |
| **Nurse**           | I see. The doctor will be with you in a few minutes. She'll review everything.                                                                                                                                                           |
| *(Dr. Martinez enters)*                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Doctor**          | Good morning, Emma. I'm Dr. Martinez. I've looked at your chart. Tell me more about how you've been feeling.                                                                                                                             |
| **Emma**            | Well, it started with a scratchy throat, and then I developed this cough. It's a dry cough – no mucus. I also feel very achy and exhausted, even after sleeping well.                                                                   |
| **Doctor**          | Any fever, chills, or shortness of breath?                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Emma**            | I did have chills last night, and my temperature was a bit high this morning. But no shortness of breath, thankfully.                                                                                                                    |
| **Doctor**          | Okay. Let me listen to your lungs. *(uses stethoscope)* … Take a deep breath for me. … And again. … Good. Your lungs sound clear, which is reassuring. I'd like to do a quick strep test to check for a bacterial infection. It's just a quick throat swab. |

| *(Doctor performs the strep test)*                                                                                                                                                                                                       |

| **Emma**            | So no prescription medication?                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **Doctor**          | Not for the virus itself, but I can give you a prescription for a cough syrup to help with the symptoms if the cough becomes bothersome. I'll also write you a doctor's note if you need to take a day or two off work.                |

| **Doctor**          | I'll write that for you. The most important thing is to rest and stay hydrated. Your body needs time to fight off the virus. If you get worse or develop difficulty breathing, come back immediately or go to the emergency room.       |

| **Doctor**          | You're welcome. The nurse will give you the prescription and the doctor's note at the front desk. Take care, and get well soon!                                                                                                          |
| **Emma**            | Thanks, I will.                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |

### 4. Key Takeaways

- **Common Sentence Patterns:**
  - *"I have an appointment with [Doctor Name] at [time]."* – Stating your scheduled visit.
  - *"What symptoms have you been experiencing?"* – The doctor asking about your condition.
  - *"Tell me more about how you've been feeling."* – An open-ended question to get details.
  - *"I recommend [treatment/rest/medicine]."* – The doctor giving advice.
  - *"Take a deep breath for me."* – An instruction during a physical examination.
  - *"If you get worse, come back immediately."* – Warning signs to watch for.

- **Useful Phrases to Remember:**
  - *"Nothing too concerning."* – Reassuring the patient that things are not serious.
  - *"Whatever you think is best."* – A way to trust the doctor's professional judgment.
  - *"Your lungs sound clear."* – A positive phrase meaning no infection in the lungs.
  - *"Get well soon!"* – A common, kind wish for someone who is sick.
  - *"Take care."* – A warm, caring farewell.

- **Practical Speaking Tips:**
  1. **Describe symptoms clearly** – When the doctor asks, be specific: use phrases like *"I have a persistent cough"*, *"it's a dry cough"*, or *"I feel achy"*. Mention when it started (e.g., *"about five days ago"*). This helps the doctor diagnose you faster.
  2. **Use correct severity words** – Distinguish between *"mild"* (slight), *"moderate"* (not too bad), and *"severe"* (very bad). Example: *"I have a mild headache, but the cough is moderate."*
  3. **Don't be afraid to ask questions** – If the doctor uses medical terms you don't understand, say: *"Could you explain that in simpler terms?"* or *"What exactly does that mean?"*
  4. **Know the difference between "symptoms" and "diagnosis"** – *Symptoms* are what you feel (cough, fever). A *diagnosis* is the doctor's conclusion about what you have (flu, infection). Being able to distinguish helps you communicate what you need.
  5. **Confirm instructions at the end** – Before you leave, summarize what the doctor said to check you understood: *"So I should rest, drink fluids, and come back if the cough gets worse – is that right?"*
