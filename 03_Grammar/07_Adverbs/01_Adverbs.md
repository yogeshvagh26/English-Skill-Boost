# Phase 7: Adverbs

Welcome to Phase 7! You've mastered **verbs** (types, forms, agreement, irregulars, gerunds, infinitives, participles), **nouns** (all types), **pronouns** (all types), and **adjectives** (types, order, comparisons). Now it's time to explore **adverbs** — the words that modify verbs, adjectives, and other adverbs.

Think of it this way: *Adjectives describe nouns. Adverbs describe everything else — verbs, adjectives, and other adverbs. "She runs" vs. "She runs **quickly**." "It's **very** hot." Adverbs add precision and detail to your language.*

---

## 1. Adverbs of Time

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Adverbs of time** tell us when something happens. They answer the question "When?"

**Key Points:**
- Answer the question: When?
- Can be specific or general
- Can be at the beginning or end of a sentence

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Time Adverb at the End**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [OBJECT] + [ADVERB OF TIME]
I + will call + you + tomorrow.
She + arrived + + yesterday.
```

**Structure 2: Time Adverb at the Beginning**
```
[ADVERB OF TIME] + [,] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [OBJECT]
Tomorrow, + I + will call + you.
Yesterday, + she + arrived.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Common Adverbs of Time:**

| Adverb | Meaning | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| now | at this moment | I am working **now**. |
| then | at that time | I will see you **then**. |
| soon | in the near future | I will call you **soon**. |
| later | after some time | I will do it **later**. |
| yesterday | the day before today | I went **yesterday**. |
| today | this day | I am busy **today**. |
| tomorrow | the day after today | I will come **tomorrow**. |
| tonight | this evening/night | I will call **tonight**. |
| already | before now | I have **already** eaten. |
| yet | up to now (negative/question) | I haven't finished **yet**. |
| just | a moment ago | I have **just** arrived. |
| recently | not long ago | I **recently** moved. |
| never | not ever | I have **never** been there. |
| ever | at any time | Have you **ever** been there? |
| always | all the time | I **always** drink coffee. |

**Examples:**
- "I will call you **tomorrow**."
- "She arrived **yesterday**."
- "I have **already** eaten."
- "I haven't finished **yet**."
- "I have **just** arrived."
- "I **never** eat meat."
- "I **always** brush my teeth."

**Time Adverb Position:**
- At the end: "I will call you **tomorrow**."
- At the beginning: "**Tomorrow**, I will call you."
- Before the main verb: "I **already** ate."

## 2. Adverbs of Place

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Adverbs of place** tell us where something happens. They answer the question "Where?"

**Key Points:**
- Answer the question: Where?
- Often follow the verb or object
- Can indicate direction or location

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Place Adverb After the Verb**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [ADVERB OF PLACE]
She + is + here.
He + went + outside.
```

**Structure 2: Place Adverb After the Object**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [OBJECT] + [ADVERB OF PLACE]
I + put + the book + here.
She + saw + him + there.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Common Adverbs of Place:**

| Adverb | Meaning | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| here | in this place | She is **here**. |
| there | in that place | I saw him **there**. |
| everywhere | in all places | I looked **everywhere**. |
| nowhere | in no place | I can find it **nowhere**. |
| anywhere | in any place | I can't find it **anywhere**. |
| inside | in the interior | Come **inside**. |
| outside | in the exterior | She is **outside**. |
| upstairs | to a higher floor | Go **upstairs**. |
| downstairs | to a lower floor | Go **downstairs**. |
| home | to/at home | I went **home**. |
| away | to/at a distance | She went **away**. |
| abroad | to/at a foreign country | I studied **abroad**. |
| near | close by | He lives **near**. |
| far | at a distance | She lives **far**. |

**Examples:**
- "She is **here**."
- "I saw him **there**."
- "I looked **everywhere**."
- "Come **inside**."
- "I went **home**."
- "She lives **near**."

**Place Adverb Position:**
- Usually after the verb: "She is **here**."
- Usually after the object: "I put the book **there**."

## 3. Adverbs of Frequency

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Adverbs of frequency** tell us how often something happens. They answer the question "How often?"

**Key Points:**
- Answer the question: How often?
- Usually before the main verb
- Usually after the verb "to be"

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Frequency Adverb Before Main Verb**
```
[SUBJECT] + [ADVERB] + [MAIN VERB] + [REST]
I + always + drink + coffee.
She + often + goes + to the gym.
```

**Structure 2: Frequency Adverb After "To Be"**
```
[SUBJECT] + [BE] + [ADVERB] + [REST]
She + is + always + happy.
They + are + never + late.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Common Adverbs of Frequency:**

| Adverb | Percentage | Example |
|--------|------------|---------|
| always | 100% | I **always** drink coffee. |
| usually | ~90% | I **usually** wake up early. |
| often | ~70% | I **often** go to the gym. |
| sometimes | ~50% | I **sometimes** eat out. |
| occasionally | ~30% | I **occasionally** watch TV. |
| rarely | ~10% | I **rarely** eat meat. |
| never | 0% | I **never** smoke. |
| ever | (in questions) | Have you **ever** been there? |

**Examples:**
- "I **always** drink coffee in the morning."
- "She **usually** arrives on time."
- "He **often** goes to the gym."
- "I **sometimes** eat out for lunch."
- "They **rarely** watch TV."
- "I **never** eat meat."
- "Have you **ever** been to Paris?"

**Other Frequency Expressions:**
- once a day, twice a week, three times a month
- every day, every week, every year
- every other day, every now and then

**Examples:**
- "I go to the gym **three times a week**."
- "She calls me **every day**."
- "I visit my parents **every other week**."

**Frequency Adverb Position:**
- Before the main verb: "I **always** drink coffee."
- After "to be": "She is **always** happy."
- Between auxiliary and main verb: "I have **never** been there."
- At the beginning or end: "**Sometimes**, I eat out." / "I eat out **sometimes**."

## 4. Adverbs of Degree

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Adverbs of degree** tell us to what extent or how much something happens. They answer the question "To what extent?"

**Key Points:**
- Answer the question: To what extent?
- Modify adjectives, verbs, and other adverbs
- Often come before the word they modify

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Degree Adverb Modifying Adjective**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [ADVERB] + [ADJECTIVE]
She + is + very + beautiful.
It + is + too + hot.
```

**Structure 2: Degree Adverb Modifying Verb**
```
[SUBJECT] + [ADVERB] + [VERB] + [REST]
I + really + like + it.
She + almost + finished + it.
```

**Structure 3: Degree Adverb Modifying Adverb**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [ADVERB] + [ADVERB]
She + runs + very + quickly.
He + speaks + too + quietly.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Common Adverbs of Degree:**

| Adverb | Meaning | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| very | to a high degree | She is **very** kind. |
| too | more than enough | It is **too** hot. |
| quite | to a moderate degree | I am **quite** tired. |
| really | truly, very | I am **really** happy. |
| extremely | to a very high degree | It is **extremely** important. |
| almost | nearly | I **almost** finished. |
| nearly | almost | She **nearly** cried. |
| hardly | almost not | I can **hardly** hear you. |
| barely | almost not | I **barely** passed. |
| completely | entirely | I **completely** agree. |
| totally | entirely | I **totally** disagree. |
| absolutely | completely | I **absolutely** love it. |
| so | to such a degree | I am **so** tired. |
| enough | sufficient | I have **enough** time. |
| rather | to a moderate degree | It is **rather** cold. |

**Examples:**
- "She is **very** beautiful."
- "It is **too** hot outside."
- "I am **really** happy to see you."
- "It is **extremely** important."
- "I **almost** finished the project."
- "I **completely** agree with you."
- "I can **hardly** believe it."

**Degree Adverb Position:**
- Before the adjective: "She is **very** nice."
- Before the main verb: "I **really** like it."
- Before the adverb: "She runs **very** quickly."
- After the verb: "I work **enough**."

## 5. Adverbs of Manner

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Adverbs of manner** tell us how something is done. They answer the question "How?"

**Key Points:**
- Answer the question: How?
- Usually formed by adding -ly to adjectives
- Usually come after the verb or object

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Manner Adverb After the Verb**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [ADVERB]
She + speaks + clearly.
He + runs + fast.
```

**Structure 2: Manner Adverb After the Object**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [OBJECT] + [ADVERB]
She + speaks + English + clearly.
He + drives + the car + carefully.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Common Adverbs of Manner:**

| Adjective → Adverb | Example |
|--------------------|---------|
| quick → quickly | She runs **quickly**. |
| slow → slowly | He speaks **slowly**. |
| careful → carefully | Drive **carefully**. |
| beautiful → beautifully | She sings **beautifully**. |
| bad → badly | He plays **badly**. |
| good → well | She sings **well**. |
| fast → fast (no -ly) | He runs **fast**. |
| hard → hard (no -ly) | She works **hard**. |
| early → early (no -ly) | I woke up **early**. |
| late → late (no -ly) | He arrived **late**. |

**Examples:**
- "She drives **carefully**."
- "He speaks **slowly**."
- "She sings **beautifully**."
- "He plays the piano **well**."
- "She runs **fast**."
- "He works **hard**."
- "I arrived **late**."

**Forming Adverbs of Manner:**

| Rule | Adjective | Adverb |
|------|-----------|--------|
| Most: add -ly | quick | quickly |
| Ends in -y: change to -ily | happy | happily |
| Ends in -ic: add -ally | basic | basically |
| Ends in -le: change to -ly | gentle | gently |
| Irregular | good | well |
| No change | fast | fast |

**Adverbs of Manner Position:**
- After the verb: "She speaks **clearly**."
- After the object: "She speaks English **clearly**."
- At the beginning (for emphasis): "**Carefully**, she opened the door."

## 6. Sentence Adverbs

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Sentence adverbs** express the speaker's attitude or opinion about the whole sentence. They comment on the entire statement.

**Key Points:**
- Comment on the whole sentence
- Usually at the beginning of the sentence
- Often set off with a comma

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Sentence Adverb at the Beginning**
```
[SENTENCE ADVERB] + [,] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [REST]
Fortunately, + we + arrived + on time.
Frankly, + I + don't + care.
```

**Structure 2: Sentence Adverb in the Middle**
```
[SUBJECT] + [,] + [SENTENCE ADVERB] + [,] + [VERB] + [REST]
We, + fortunately, + arrived + on time.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Common Sentence Adverbs:**

| Adverb | Meaning | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| fortunately | luckily | **Fortunately**, no one was hurt. |
| unfortunately | unluckily | **Unfortunately**, I can't come. |
| frankly | honestly | **Frankly**, I don't care. |
| honestly | truthfully | **Honestly**, I didn't know. |
| seriously | not joking | **Seriously**, I need help. |
| obviously | clearly | **Obviously**, she was upset. |
| clearly | without doubt | **Clearly**, he is the best. |
| apparently | it seems | **Apparently**, they left early. |
| generally | in general | **Generally**, people are kind. |
| honestly | truthfully | **Honestly**, I don't remember. |
| personally | in my opinion | **Personally**, I agree. |
| surprisingly | unexpectedly | **Surprisingly**, he passed. |
| obviously | clearly | **Obviously**, she was wrong. |

**Examples:**
- "**Fortunately**, we arrived on time."
- "**Unfortunately**, I can't come to the party."
- "**Frankly**, I don't care what they think."
- "**Honestly**, I didn't know about the meeting."
- "**Seriously**, you need to reconsider."
- "**Obviously**, she was upset about the news."
- "**Personally**, I think it's a bad idea."

**Sentence Adverb Position:**
- At the beginning: "**Fortunately**, we won."
- In the middle: "We, **fortunately**, won."
- At the end (less common): "We won, **fortunately**."

## 7. Position of Adverbs

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** The **position of adverbs** in a sentence depends on the type of adverb and what it modifies. Understanding correct placement is essential for natural English.

**Key Points:**
- Different types of adverbs have different positions
- Correct position changes the meaning
- In English, adverbs usually follow a specific order

### 📐 Study: Adverb Position Rules

**Rule 1: Frequency Adverbs (Before the Main Verb)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [FREQUENCY ADVERB] + [MAIN VERB] + [REST]
I + always + drink + coffee.
She + never + eats + meat.
```

**Rule 2: Frequency Adverbs (After "To Be")**
```
[SUBJECT] + [BE] + [FREQUENCY ADVERB] + [REST]
She + is + always + happy.
They + are + never + late.
```

**Rule 3: Manner Adverbs (After the Verb/Object)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [MANNER ADVERB]
She + drives + carefully.

[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [OBJECT] + [MANNER ADVERB]
She + drives + the car + carefully.
```

**Rule 4: Place Adverbs (After the Verb/Object)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [PLACE ADVERB]
She + lives + here.

[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [OBJECT] + [PLACE ADVERB]
I + put + the book + there.
```

**Rule 5: Time Adverbs (At the End or Beginning)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [OBJECT] + [TIME ADVERB]
I + will call + you + tomorrow.

[TIME ADVERB] + [,] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [OBJECT]
Tomorrow, + I + will call + you.
```

**Rule 6: Degree Adverbs (Before the Word They Modify)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [DEGREE ADVERB] + [ADJECTIVE]
She + is + very + beautiful.

[SUBJECT] + [DEGREE ADVERB] + [VERB] + [REST]
I + really + like + it.
```

**Rule 7: Sentence Adverbs (At the Beginning)**
```
[SENTENCE ADVERB] + [,] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [REST]
Fortunately, + we + arrived + on time.
```

**Multiple Adverbs (General Order):**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [PLACE] + [TIME] + [MANNER]
She + works + at the office + every day + hard.
(But this is flexible)
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Correct Positions:**
- "I **always** drink coffee." (frequency before main verb)
- "She is **never** late." (frequency after 'to be')
- "She drove **carefully**." (manner after verb)
- "She lives **here**." (place after verb)
- "I will call you **tomorrow**." (time at the end)
- "**Tomorrow**, I will call you." (time at the beginning)
- "She is **very** beautiful." (degree before adjective)
- "**Fortunately**, we won." (sentence adverb at beginning)

**Multiple Adverbs:**
- "She works **hard** (manner) **at the office** (place) **every day** (time)."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I drink always coffee. | I **always** drink coffee. |
| She is happy always. | She is **always** happy. |
| She drove careful. | She drove **carefully**. |
| I have seen never that. | I have **never** seen that. |
| I like very much it. | I like it **very much**. |

## 💬 Practice: Conversations

### Dialogue 1: Using Adverbs of Frequency

- A: "How **often** do you exercise?"
- B: "I **usually** go to the gym three times a week."
- A: "I **never** go to the gym. I **always** feel tired."
- B: "You should **sometimes** try going. I **often** feel more energetic."
- A: "I **rarely** have time."
- B: "I **occasionally** go in the morning."

### Dialogue 2: Using Adverbs of Manner

- A: "She sings **beautifully**, doesn't she?"
- B: "Yes, she sings **very well**."
- A: "She plays the piano **perfectly** too."
- B: "Does she practice **regularly**?"
- A: "She practices **hard** every day."
- B: "She **clearly** has a lot of talent."

### Dialogue 3: Using Sentence Adverbs

- A: "**Unfortunately**, I can't come to the party."
- B: "**That's too bad**. **Obviously**, it won't be as fun without you."
- A: "**Frankly**, I have too much work."
- B: "**Seriously**? I thought you finished the project."
- A: "**Honestly**, I still have a lot to do."

### Dialogue 4: Using Adverbs of Time and Place

- A: "Where are you going **now**?"
- B: "I'm going **home** **soon**."
- A: "Are you coming **here** **tomorrow**?"
- B: "Yes, I'll be **here** **then**."
- A: "I'll see you **there**."
- B: "Great! I'll be **there** **on time**."
