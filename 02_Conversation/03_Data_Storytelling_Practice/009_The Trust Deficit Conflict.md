## 1. Situation : Monthly Sales Review – The "Trust Deficit" Conflict

**Context:** You are a Data Analyst at a B2B software company. The VP of Sales (Lisa) and the CFO (David) are in a heated argument. Lisa's Salesforce dashboard shows 120 new customers acquired this month. David's ERP system shows only 95 new customers. Marketing is reporting 150 leads that converted. Everyone has a different number. The CEO (Sarah) looks at you and says: *"You're the data person. Which number is right?"* You need to reconcile the data, explain the discrepancies, and establish a single source of truth – without making anyone look incompetent.

## 2. Conversation

| Speaker       | Dialogue                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **CEO (Sarah)** | I have three different numbers for new customers this month – 120, 95, and 150. This is becoming a crisis of trust. Which one is correct?                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **VP Sales (Lisa)** | My Salesforce pipeline shows 120 closed-won deals. That's what my team closed. That's the real number.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| **CFO (David)** | My ERP shows only 95 actual invoices generated. If we closed 120 deals, why are we only billing 95? Someone is counting deals before they're signed.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **You (Analyst)** | *(calmly)* Let me step in here. I've traced the lineage of all three numbers, and here's the honest truth: **all three numbers are correct – they're just answering different questions.** Let me reconcile them.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Sarah** | Explain.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **You (Analyst)** | Lisa's 120 is based on Salesforce "Closed-Won" status – this triggers the moment a contract is verbally agreed and logged by the sales rep. David's 95 comes from the ERP, which only counts customers after a signed contract is received and processed by finance – which can take 3-5 business days. The 5-day lag means 25 deals are "in flight" – verbally won but not yet invoiced. Marketing's 150 counts trial conversions, which includes free trials that haven't yet become paid contracts. |
| **Lisa** | So I'm not wrong – I'm just looking at a different stage.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| **You (Analyst)** | Exactly. The story isn't *"who is right"*. The story is *"we have three definitions of 'new customer' at three stages of the customer lifecycle."* We need to align on a **single, official definition**. My **recommendation**: (1) Adopt "Contract Signed" (ERP invoiced) as the official company metric for revenue recognition. (2) Use Salesforce "Closed-Won" as a leading indicator for pipeline health. (3) Create a **reconciliation dashboard** that shows all three numbers side-by-side with the 5-day lag explicitly documented, so everyone can see the discrepancy and understand *why* it exists. |
| **David** | That reconciliation dashboard would solve so many of these arguments.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Sarah** | Do it. And from now on, when we say "new customers," we mean the ERP number. But we track the Salesforce number as our leading indicator. This is exactly the clarity we needed.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |

## 3. Vocabulary and Expressions

| Word/Phrase                     | Part of Speech | Meaning                                                                                | Example Sentence                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------- | -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Single source of truth (SSOT)** | Noun Phrase    | A single, agreed-upon system or definition that everyone uses for reporting           | *We need a single source of truth for revenue – no more debating numbers.*                       |
| **Data reconciliation**         | Noun           | The process of comparing data from different sources to find and explain discrepancies | *Reconciliation revealed the 5-day lag between Salesforce and ERP.*                              |
| **Data lineage**                | Noun           | The documented journey of data from its source to its final destination                | *Tracing data lineage helped me understand why the numbers didn't match.*                        |
| **Definitional alignment**      | Noun Phrase    | Getting all stakeholders to agree on the same definition for a metric                  | *Definitional alignment is harder than building the dashboard – but more important.*             |
| **Leading indicator**           | Noun Phrase    | A metric that predicts future performance (e.g., Salesforce Closed-Won predicts revenue) | *We use Salesforce Closed-Won as a leading indicator of next month's revenue.*                   |
| **Lagging indicator**           | Noun Phrase    | A metric that reflects past performance (e.g., ERP invoiced revenue)                   | *ERP revenue is a lagging indicator – it tells us what already happened.*                        |
| **Granularity**                 | Noun           | The level of detail in your data (e.g., daily, weekly, monthly, per-customer)          | *We need daily granularity to catch lags, not just monthly totals.*                              |
| **Data quality**                | Noun Phrase    | The accuracy, completeness, and consistency of data                                    | *Data quality issues often cause these trust deficits – we need to clean upstream.*              |
| **ETL (Extract, Transform, Load)** | Acronym        | The process of extracting data from sources, transforming it, and loading it into a warehouse | *Our ETL pipeline runs every night, but it doesn't account for the 5-day lag.*                   |
| **Data governance**             | Noun Phrase    | The policies and processes for managing data quality, security, and usage              | *We need a data governance policy that defines who owns each metric.*                            |
| **Upstream / Downstream**       | Adj/Noun       | Upstream = before your system (source); Downstream = after your system (destination)   | *The data quality issue is upstream in Salesforce – we need to fix it there.*                    |
| **Audit trail**                 | Noun Phrase    | A documented record of who changed what data and when                                  | *Having an audit trail helps us trace back any discrepancies to the source transaction.*         |
| **Reconciliation dashboard**    | Noun Phrase    | A dashboard that displays numbers from multiple sources side-by-side with explanations | *The reconciliation dashboard shows Salesforce (120), ERP (95), and the 25-deal gap (in-flight).* |

## 4. Key Takeaways

### Common Sentence Patterns (for Data Reconciliation Storytelling)

| Pattern                                                              | Use                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| *"I traced the lineage of all three numbers – and here's the honest truth: all are correct, they're just answering different questions."* | To de-escalate conflict and establish credibility.                                     |
| *"The discrepancy is [actual difference], and the root cause is [explanation – e.g., timing, definition, or system lag]."* | To explain the "why" behind the gap without blaming anyone.                           |
| *"So the story isn't 'who is right'. The story is 'we have [X] definitions at [Y] stages of the lifecycle'."* | To reframe the narrative and move from blame to process.                               |
| *"My recommendation: (1) Adopt [Definition A] as the official metric, (2) Use [Definition B] as a leading indicator, (3) Build a [Reconciliation Dashboard] to make the gap visible."* | To propose a clear, multi-part solution.                                               |
| *"This isn't a data problem – it's a definitional problem. Once we align on definitions, the data will align."* | To shift the conversation from "bad data" to "misaligned expectations."                |
| *"Let's document the official definition and get sign-off from all stakeholders."* | To ensure long-term alignment and prevent future disputes.                             |

### Useful Phrases to Remember
- **Acknowledging the frustration:** *"I understand why this is frustrating – seeing different numbers from different systems erodes trust. Let me fix that."*
- **Introducing the concept of "timing lag":** *"The gap isn't an error – it's a timing difference. System A records at event X; System B records at event Y, which happens 5 days later."*
- **Preventing future fights:** *"Once we all agree on the 'official' number, any other number should be explicitly labeled as 'preliminary' or 'operational'."*
- **Handling "whose system is right?":** *"The question isn't which system is right – the question is which system answers which business question. Let's map that out."*
- **Creating a "Data Dictionary":** *"I'll create a data dictionary that defines every metric, its source, its calculation, and its refresh cadence. This will be our reference document."*
- **Calling out data quality:** *"If we see mismatches beyond timing, we may have data quality issues in the source system – I can run a quality audit if you want."*

### Practical Speaking Tips (for Building Trust in Data)
1.  **Start with "empathy" before "analysis".** Say: *"I know seeing different numbers is confusing and frustrating. Let me walk you through why this happens – it's actually a common issue in fast-growing companies."* – this validates their feelings and positions you as a problem-solver.
2.  **Draw a "Data Flow" diagram on the whiteboard.** Show: *"Sales rep logs deal (Salesforce) → Contract sent → Contract signed → Finance processes invoice (ERP)."* Point to the 5-day gap and say: *"That's where the discrepancy lives."* This visual explanation is powerful.
3.  **Avoid saying "you're wrong" – say "you're measuring at a different point".** Never call out a stakeholder's number as "wrong." Say: *"Your number is correct for your system's timestamp. The other number is correct for a later timestamp. Let's align on the timestamp."*
4.  **Propose a "Golden Record"** – a single table or dashboard that everyone agrees is the definitive source. Say: *"Going forward, any discussion about new customers starts with this dashboard. If you want a different number, you must explain why."*
5.  **Document the "Why" explicitly.** Don't just show the discrepancy – show the *reason* (e.g., "25 deals are in the 5-day invoicing window"). This transforms a "bug" into a "process feature." It builds trust because you're being transparent about how the sausage is made.
6.  **Set up a "Monthly Data Review"** where you walk stakeholders through the reconciliation dashboard and flag any unexplained variance. This turns data reconciliation from a crisis response into a routine governance process.
