# Phase 16: Clauses

Welcome to Phase 16! You've mastered **verbs**, **nouns**, **pronouns**, **adjectives**, **adverbs**, **articles & determiners**, **prepositions**, **conjunctions**, **modals**, **voice**, **narration**, **conditionals**, and **questions**. Now it's time to explore **clauses** — the building blocks of complex sentences.

Think of it this way: *A clause is a group of words with a subject and a verb. Clauses are the "engines" of sentences. Understanding the four types of clauses — noun, adjective, adverb, and relative — will allow you to create sophisticated, nuanced, and precise sentences.*

| Type | Function | Example |
|------|----------|---------|
| **Noun Clause** | Acts as a noun | I know **that she is coming**. |
| **Adjective Clause** | Modifies a noun | The woman **who lives next door** is a doctor. |

## 1. What Is a Clause?

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **clause** is a group of words that contains a subject and a verb. Clauses are the building blocks of sentences.

**Key Points:**
- Every clause has a subject and a verb
- Clauses can be independent or dependent
- Independent clauses can stand alone
- Dependent clauses cannot stand alone

**Clause vs. Phrase:**

| Feature | Clause | Phrase |
|---------|--------|--------|
| **Subject + Verb** | Yes | No |
| **Can Stand Alone** | Sometimes | No |
| **Example** | She runs. | Running quickly |

**Independent vs. Dependent Clauses:**

| Type | Can Stand Alone? | Example |
|------|------------------|---------|
| **Independent** | Yes | She runs. |
| **Dependent** | No | Because she runs |

## 2. Noun Clause

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **noun clause** is a dependent clause that functions as a noun in a sentence. It can act as a subject, object, or complement.

**Key Points:**
- Functions as a noun
- Can be a subject, object, or complement
- Often introduced by: that, if, whether, wh- words

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Noun Clause as Subject**
```
[NOUN CLAUSE] + [VERB] + [REST]
That she is coming + is + certain.
Where he lives + is + unknown.
```

**Structure 2: Noun Clause as Object**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [NOUN CLAUSE]
I + know + that she is coming.
She + said + that she would call.
```

**Structure 3: Noun Clause as Complement**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [NOUN CLAUSE]
The truth + is + that she is coming.
My concern + is + whether she will arrive on time.
```

**Structure 4: Noun Clause as Object of Preposition**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [PREPOSITION] + [NOUN CLAUSE]
I + worry + about + whether she is safe.
She + is + interested + in + what he has to say.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Noun Clause as Subject:**
- "**That she is coming** is certain."
- "**Where he lives** is unknown."
- "**What she said** surprised me."
- "**Whether he will come** is uncertain."
- "**How she did it** remains a mystery."

**Noun Clause as Object:**
- "I know **that she is coming**."
- "She said **that she would call**."
- "I don't know **where he lives**."
- "Can you tell me **what time it is**?"
- "I wonder **if she will come**."

**Noun Clause as Complement:**
- "The truth is **that she is coming**."
- "My concern is **whether she will arrive on time**."
- "His belief is **that hard work pays off**."
- "Her dream is **that she will become a doctor**."
- "The problem is **that we don't have enough time**."

**Noun Clause as Object of Preposition:**
- "I worry about **whether she is safe**."
- "She is interested in **what he has to say**."
- "We talked about **how we could improve**."
- "I'm thinking about **what I should do**."
- "They are concerned about **whether they will pass**."

**Common Introductory Words for Noun Clauses:**

| Word | Example |
|------|---------|
| that | I know **that** she is coming. |
| if/whether | I wonder **if** she is coming. |
| who | I know **who** she is. |
| what | I know **what** she wants. |
| where | I know **where** she lives. |
| when | I know **when** she arrives. |
| why | I know **why** she left. |
| how | I know **how** she did it. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| That she is coming is certain. (✓) | **That she is coming** is certain. |
| I know that is she coming. | I know **that she is coming**. |
| I don't know where is he. | I don't know **where he is**. |
| I wonder if is she coming. | I wonder **if she is coming**. |

## 3. Adjective Clause

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** An **adjective clause** (also called a relative clause) is a dependent clause that modifies a noun or pronoun. It provides additional information about the noun.

**Key Points:**
- Modifies a noun or pronoun
- Often introduced by relative pronouns: who, whom, which, that, whose
- Can be restrictive (essential) or non-restrictive (non-essential)

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Adjective Clause with Relative Pronoun**
```
[NOUN] + [RELATIVE PRONOUN] + [CLAUSE]
The woman + who + lives next door + is a doctor.
The book + that + I read + is great.
```

**Structure 2: Restrictive (Essential) Clause**
```
[NOUN] + [RELATIVE CLAUSE] + [REST]
The man + who called you + is my uncle.
(Without it, the meaning changes)
```

**Structure 3: Non-Restrictive (Non-Essential) Clause**
```
[NOUN] + [,] + [RELATIVE CLAUSE] + [,] + [REST]
My brother, + who lives in London, + is a doctor.
(The clause adds extra information)
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**With "Who" (People - Subject):**
- "The woman **who lives next door** is a doctor."
- "The man **who called you** is my uncle."
- "She is the student **who won the award**."
- "People **who work hard** succeed."

**With "Whom" (People - Object):**
- "The woman **whom I met** is a lawyer."
- "The man **whom you called** is my uncle."
- "She is the student **whom the teacher praised**."
- "The person **whom I saw** was leaving."

**With "Which" (Things):**
- "The book **which I read** is great."
- "The car **which he bought** is red."
- "The movie **which we watched** was exciting."
- "The house **which they built** is beautiful."

**With "That" (People or Things):**
- "The book **that I read** is great."
- "The woman **that lives next door** is a doctor."
- "The car **that he bought** is red."
- "People **that work hard** succeed."

**With "Whose" (Possession):**
- "The man **whose car was stolen** is angry."
- "The woman **whose son won** is proud."
- "The company **whose products are popular** is growing."
- "The student **whose book is missing** is upset."

**Restrictive vs. Non-Restrictive Clauses:**

| Type | Punctuation | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Restrictive** | No commas | The woman **who lives next door** is a doctor. |
| **Non-Restrictive** | Commas | My mother, **who lives in London**, is a doctor. |

**Who vs. Whom:**

| Pronoun | Use | Example |
|---------|-----|---------|
| **who** | Subject | The woman **who** called you is my sister. |
| **whom** | Object | The woman **whom** you called is my sister. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| The man which lives here is my uncle. | The man **who** lives here is my uncle. |
| The book who I read is great. | The book **which/that** I read is great. |
| The woman who I saw is my aunt. | The woman **whom** I saw is my aunt. |
| My brother, who lives in London is a doctor. | My brother, who lives in London, **is** a doctor. (missing comma) |

## 4. Adverb Clause

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** An **adverb clause** is a dependent clause that modifies a verb, adjective, or adverb. It provides information about time, place, reason, condition, contrast, or manner.

**Key Points:**
- Modifies a verb, adjective, or adverb
- Answers questions like: when, where, why, how, under what conditions
- Often introduced by subordinating conjunctions

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Adverb Clause at the Beginning**
```
[ADVERB CLAUSE] + [,] + [MAIN CLAUSE]
Because it was raining, + I stayed home.
Although she was tired, + she continued working.
```

**Structure 2: Adverb Clause at the End**
```
[MAIN CLAUSE] + [ADVERB CLAUSE]
I stayed home + because it was raining.
She continued working + although she was tired.
```

**Structure 3: Adverb Clause in the Middle**
```
[MAIN CLAUSE START] + [ADVERB CLAUSE] + [MAIN CLAUSE END]
The student, + because he studied hard, + passed the exam.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Time Clauses:**
- "I was sleeping **when you called**."
- "She read **while he cooked dinner**."
- "**After I finished** my work, I went home."
- "**Before I leave**, I need to say goodbye."
- "I waited **until you arrived**."
- "Call me **as soon as you arrive**."

**Reason Clauses:**
- "I stayed home **because it was raining**."
- "**Since you asked**, I'll tell you the truth."
- "**As I was tired**, I went to bed early."

**Condition Clauses:**
- "I will go **if you come with me**."
- "I won't go **unless you come**."
- "I will go **provided that you come**."
- "I will go **as long as you come**."

**Contrast Clauses:**
- "**Although he was tired**, he continued working."
- "**Though it was cold**, we went for a walk."
- "**Even though it rained**, we went to the beach."
- "I like coffee, **whereas she likes tea**."

**Place Clauses:**
- "This is **where I grew up**."
- "I will go **wherever you go**."

**Manner Clauses:**
- "He acts **as if he owns the place**."
- "She looked **as though she had seen a ghost**."

**Purpose Clauses:**
- "I studied hard **so that I could pass**."
- "She saved money **in order that she could travel**."

**Result Clauses:**
- "She was **so tired that she fell asleep**."
- "He ran **so fast that he won the race**."

**Adverb Clause Subordinating Conjunctions:**

| Category | Conjunctions |
|----------|--------------|
| **Time** | when, while, after, before, since, until, as soon as |
| **Reason** | because, since, as |
| **Condition** | if, unless, provided that, as long as |
| **Contrast** | although, though, even though, whereas |
| **Place** | where, wherever |
| **Manner** | as if, as though |
| **Purpose** | so that, in order that |
| **Result** | so...that, such...that |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Because I was tired. (fragment) | **Because I was tired**, I went to bed. |
| Although I was tired, but I went. (double) | **Although** I was tired, I went. |
| I went home because I was tired. (✓) | I went home **because** I was tired. |
| I will go if you will come. (future) | I will go if you **come**. |

## 5. Relative Clause

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **relative clause** is a type of adjective clause that modifies a noun. It is introduced by a relative pronoun (who, whom, which, that, whose) or a relative adverb (when, where, why).

**Key Points:**
- Same as adjective clause
- Modifies a noun (the antecedent)
- Introduced by relative pronouns or adverbs
- Can be restrictive or non-restrictive

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Relative Pronoun as Subject**
```
[NOUN] + [RELATIVE PRONOUN] + [VERB] + [REST]
The woman + who + lives + next door is a doctor.
```

**Structure 2: Relative Pronoun as Object**
```
[NOUN] + [RELATIVE PRONOUN] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB]
The book + that + I + read + is great.
```

**Structure 3: Relative Adverb**
```
[NOUN] + [RELATIVE ADVERB] + [CLAUSE]
The house + where + I grew up + is for sale.
The day + when + we met + was special.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Relative Pronouns:**

| Pronoun | Antecedent | Example |
|---------|------------|---------|
| **who** | People (subject) | The woman **who** lives next door is a doctor. |
| **whom** | People (object) | The woman **whom** I saw is a doctor. |
| **which** | Things/animals | The book **which** I read is great. |
| **that** | People or things | The book **that** I read is great. |
| **whose** | Possession | The man **whose** car was stolen is angry. |

**Relative Adverbs:**

| Adverb | Use | Example |
|--------|-----|---------|
| **when** | Time | The day **when** we met was special. |
| **where** | Place | The house **where** I grew up is for sale. |
| **why** | Reason | The reason **why** I left is personal. |

**Restrictive vs. Non-Restrictive Relative Clauses:**

| Type | Punctuation | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Restrictive** | No commas | The book **that I read** is on the table. |
| **Non-Restrictive** | Commas | My book, **which I read yesterday**, is on the table. |

**"That" vs. "Which":**

| Pronoun | Use | Example |
|---------|-----|---------|
| **that** | Restrictive clauses | The book **that** I read is great. |
| **which** | Non-restrictive clauses | The book, **which** I read yesterday, is great. |

**Omission of Relative Pronoun:**
- "The book **that I read** is great." → "The book **I read** is great." (That can be omitted when it's the object)

**Examples:**
- "The woman **who lives** next door is a doctor."
- "The book **that I read** is great."
- "The man **whose car was stolen** is angry."
- "The house **where I grew up** is for sale."
- "The day **when we met** was special."
- "The reason **why I left** is personal."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| The man which lives here. | The man **who** lives here. |
| The book who I read. | The book **which/that** I read. |
| The man whose car was stolen. (✓) | The man **whose** car was stolen. |
| The day which we met. | The day **when** we met. |
| The house where I grew up. (✓) | The house **where** I grew up. |

## 💬 Practice: Conversations

### Dialogue 1: Using Noun Clauses

- A: "**Do you know** where she lives?"
- B: "No, I don't know **where she lives**."
- A: "**I wonder** if she is coming to the party."
- B: "She said **that she would come**."
- A: "**What she said** is that she would be late."
- B: "The problem is **that we don't know when**."

### Dialogue 2: Using Relative Clauses

- A: "The woman **who lives next door** is a doctor."
- B: "The book **that I read** was written by her."
- A: "The man **whose car was stolen** is her husband."
- B: "The house **where they live** is beautiful."
- A: "The day **when we met** was special."
- B: "The reason **why I like them** is their kindness."

### Dialogue 3: Using Adverb Clauses

- A: "**Because it was raining**, I stayed home."
- B: "**Although he was tired**, he continued working."
- A: "I will go **if you come with me**."
- B: "I was sleeping **when you called**."
- A: "**After I finish** my work, I will call you."
- B: "I waited **until you arrived**."
