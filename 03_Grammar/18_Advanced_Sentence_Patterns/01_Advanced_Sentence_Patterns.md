## 1. Parallelism

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Parallelism** (also called parallel structure) means using the same grammatical form for items in a list, series, or comparison. It creates balance, rhythm, and clarity.

**Key Points:**
- Use the same grammatical form for parallel items
- Creates balance and rhythm
- Improves clarity and readability
- Applies to words, phrases, and clauses

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Parallel Words**
```
[NOUN 1] + [CONJUNCTION] + [NOUN 2]
coffee + and + tea

[ADJECTIVE 1] + [CONJUNCTION] + [ADJECTIVE 2]
tall + and + handsome

[VERB 1] + [CONJUNCTION] + [VERB 2]
run + and + jump
```

**Structure 2: Parallel Phrases**
```
[PHRASE 1] + [CONJUNCTION] + [PHRASE 2]
running quickly + and + jumping high
to study hard + and + to work diligently
```

**Structure 3: Parallel Clauses**
```
[CLAUSE 1] + [CONJUNCTION] + [CLAUSE 2]
She came + and + she saw.
He studied + because he wanted to pass.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Correct Parallelism:**

**With Nouns:**
- "I like **coffee**, **tea**, and **juice**." (nouns)
- "She is **kind**, **smart**, and **beautiful**." (adjectives)
- "He **runs**, **swims**, and **cycles**." (verbs)

**With Phrases:**
- "She likes **reading books**, **watching movies**, and **listening to music**." (gerund phrases)
- "I want **to study hard**, **to work diligently**, and **to succeed**." (infinitive phrases)
- "He is **in the office**, **on the phone**, and **at his desk**." (prepositional phrases)

**With Clauses:**
- "**She came**, **she saw**, and **she conquered**."
- "**I said** I would go, and **I did go**."
- "**The weather was cold**, but **the sun was shining**."

**In Comparisons:**
- "She is **as smart as** she is **kind**."
- "I would rather **swim** than **run**."
- "It is better **to love** than **to be loved**."

**In Lists:**
- "I need to **buy milk**, **bread**, and **eggs**."
- "She is **responsible**, **hardworking**, and **reliable**."
- "He **woke up**, **ate breakfast**, and **left for work**."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake (Non-Parallel) | Correction (Parallel) |
|------------------------|----------------------|
| I like swimming, running, and to read. | I like **swimming**, **running**, and **reading**. |
| She is kind, smart, and has beauty. | She is **kind**, **smart**, and **beautiful**. |
| He runs, swims, and cycling. | He **runs**, **swims**, and **cycles**. |
| I want to study hard, working hard, and succeed. | I want **to study** hard, **to work** hard, and **to succeed**. |
| She likes reading and to write. | She likes **reading** and **writing**. |
| He is tall, strong, and has confidence. | He is **tall**, **strong**, and **confident**. |

## 2. Inversion

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Inversion** means reversing the normal word order of a sentence (subject + verb becomes verb + subject). It is used for emphasis, drama, or in certain grammatical structures.

**Key Points:**
- Reverses normal word order
- Used for emphasis or drama
- Used with negative adverbials, conditionals, and comparisons

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Inversion with Negative Adverbials**
```
[NEGATIVE ADVERBIAL] + [AUXILIARY] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB]
Never + have + I + seen + such beauty.
Not only + does + she + sing, but she also dances.
```

**Structure 2: Inversion in Conditionals**
```
[SHOULD/WERE/HAD] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB]
Were + I + richer, I would travel.
Had + I + known, I would have helped.
```

**Structure 3: Inversion with "So/Neither/Nor"**
```
[SO/NEITHER/NOR] + [AUXILIARY] + [SUBJECT]
So + do + I.
Neither + do + I.
```

**Structure 4: Inversion with Comparisons**
```
[COMPARATIVE] + [AUXILIARY] + [SUBJECT]
So beautiful + was + she + that everyone stared.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Inversion with Negative Adverbials:**
- "**Never** have I seen such beauty."
- "**Rarely** does she complain."
- "**Hardly** had I arrived when it started raining."
- "**No sooner** had I left than he arrived."
- "**Not only** does she sing, but she also dances."
- "**Seldom** have I felt so happy."
- "**Little** did I know what would happen."
- "**Under no circumstances** should you go there."

**Inversion in Conditionals:**
- "**Were** I richer, I would travel the world." (If I were richer)
- "**Had** I known, I would have helped." (If I had known)
- "**Should** you need help, call me." (If you should need help)
- "**Were** she to come, she would help." (If she were to come)

**Inversion with "So/Neither/Nor":**
- "I love coffee." "**So do I.** "
- "I don't like tea." "**Neither do I.** "
- "She can swim." "**So can I.** "
- "He hasn't arrived." "**Nor have I.** "

**Inversion with Comparisons:**
- "**So beautiful** was she that everyone stared."
- "**So fast** did he run that he won the race."
- "**Such** was her talent that she became famous."

**Inversion with Place Adverbials:**
- "**Here comes** the train."
- "**There goes** the bus."
- "**On the table sat** a beautiful vase."
- "**In the garden stood** a tall tree."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Never I have seen such beauty. | **Never have I** seen such beauty. |
| Rarely she complains. | **Rarely does she** complain. |
| If I were richer, I would travel. (no inversion) | **Were I** richer, I would travel. |
| If I had known, I would have helped. (no inversion) | **Had I** known, I would have helped. |
| I love coffee. So I do. | I love coffee. **So do I.** |

## 3. Cleft Sentences

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **cleft sentence** is a sentence that is divided (cleft) to emphasize a particular part. It uses structures like "It is...that" or "What...is" to focus on specific information.

**Key Points:**
- Divides a sentence to emphasize one part
- Uses "It is/was...that/who" or "What...is/was"
- Focuses attention on specific information

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: It-Cleft**
```
[IT] + [BE] + [FOCUS] + [THAT/WHO] + [REST]
It + was + John + who + called.
It + is + coffee + that + I want.
```

**Structure 2: What-Cleft**
```
[WHAT] + [BE] + [FOCUS]
What + I need + is + rest.
What + I want + is + coffee.
```

**Structure 3: All-Cleft**
```
[ALL] + [BE] + [FOCUS]
All + I need + is + you.
All + she wants + is + love.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**It-Cleft Sentences (Focus on Subject):**
- "**It was John** who called me." (Emphasizes John)
- "**It is she** who is responsible." (Emphasizes she)
- "**It was the teacher** who helped me." (Emphasizes teacher)

**It-Cleft Sentences (Focus on Object):**
- "**It is coffee** that I want." (Emphasizes coffee)
- "**It was the book** that I needed." (Emphasizes book)
- "**It is you** that I love." (Emphasizes you)

**It-Cleft Sentences (Focus on Time):**
- "**It was yesterday** that I saw her." (Emphasizes yesterday)
- "**It was at 5 PM** that we met." (Emphasizes 5 PM)
- "**It was in London** that we met." (Emphasizes location)

**It-Cleft Sentences (Focus on Reason):**
- "**It was because I was tired** that I left." (Emphasizes reason)
- "**It was for you** that I did it." (Emphasizes purpose)

**What-Cleft Sentences:**
- "**What I need** is rest."
- "**What I want** is coffee."
- "**What she said** surprised me."
- "**What I love** is music."
- "**What I don't understand** is why he left."

**All-Cleft Sentences:**
- "**All I need** is you."
- "**All she wants** is love."
- "**All I ask** is your honesty."
- "**All he does** is sleep."

**Transforming Simple Sentences to Cleft Sentences:**

| Simple | Cleft |
|--------|-------|
| John called me. | **It was John** who called me. |
| I want coffee. | **It is coffee** that I want. |
| I need rest. | **What I need** is rest. |
| I need you. | **All I need** is you. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| It was John who he called me. | **It was John** who called me. |
| It is coffee I want. (missing that) | **It is coffee** that I want. |
| What I need is rest. (✓) | **What I need** is rest. |
| All I need is you. (✓) | **All I need** is you. |

## 4. Fronting

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Fronting** means moving an element (object, complement, adverb, or prepositional phrase) to the beginning of the sentence for emphasis.

**Key Points:**
- Moves an element to the front
- Creates emphasis
- Can change the rhythm of the sentence

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Fronting an Object**
```
[OBJECT] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB]
That + I + love.
Coffee + I + want.
```

**Structure 2: Fronting a Complement**
```
[COMPLEMENT] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB]
Happy + I + am.
Tired + she + was.
```

**Structure 3: Fronting an Adverb/Prepositional Phrase**
```
[ADVERB/PP] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB]
In the morning + I + will call you.
Slowly + he + walked away.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Fronting an Object:**
- "**That** I love." (Emphasizes "that")
- "**Coffee** I want, not tea." (Emphasizes "coffee")
- "**Him** she loves." (Emphasizes "him")
- "**The book** I read." (Emphasizes "the book")

**Fronting a Complement:**
- "**Happy** I am." (Emphasizes "happy")
- "**Tired** she was after the trip." (Emphasizes "tired")
- "**Beautiful** she is." (Emphasizes "beautiful")
- "**Frustrated** he became." (Emphasizes "frustrated")

**Fronting an Adverb/Prepositional Phrase:**
- "**In the morning** I will call you." (Emphasizes time)
- "**Slowly** he walked away." (Emphasizes manner)
- "**On the table** she placed the book." (Emphasizes place)
- "**After the meeting** we will eat." (Emphasizes time)

**Fronting with Inversion (Optional):**
- "**Into the room** walked the teacher." (Inversion: walked + the teacher)
- "**On the table** sat a beautiful vase." (Inversion: sat + a vase)
- "**Down the street** ran the dog." (Inversion: ran + the dog)

**Fronting vs. Normal Order:**

| Normal Order | Fronting |
|--------------|----------|
| I love that. | **That** I love. |
| I want coffee. | **Coffee** I want. |
| She loves him. | **Him** she loves. |
| I am happy. | **Happy** I am. |
| She was tired. | **Tired** she was. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| That I love it. | **That** I love. |
| Coffee I want it. | **Coffee** I want. |
| Happy I am. (✓) | **Happy** I am. |
| In the morning will call you. | **In the morning** I will call you. |

## 5. Emphasis

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Emphasis** refers to various ways of highlighting important information in a sentence. This can be done through word choice, word order, or grammatical structures.

**Key Points:**
- Highlights important information
- Can use auxiliaries, adverbs, or repetition
- Creates impact and clarity

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Emphasis with "Do/Does/Did"**
```
[DO/DOES/DID] + [MAIN VERB]
I + do + love + you.
She + does + work + hard.
```

**Structure 2: Emphasis with Adverbs**
```
[ADVERB] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB]
Certainly + she + is + correct.
Indeed + he + came + on time.
```

**Structure 3: Emphasis with Repetition**
```
[REPEATED WORD]
It was a long, long time.
She loves him, really loves him.
```

**Structure 4: Emphasis with Double Negatives**
```
[DOUBLE NEGATIVE]
I don't not care.
She didn't say nothing. (informal)
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Emphasis with "Do/Does/Did":**
- "I **do** love you." (Emphasizes love)
- "She **does** work hard." (Emphasizes work)
- "They **did** arrive on time." (Emphasizes arrival)
- "He **does** want to help." (Emphasizes desire)
- "I **did** remember to call." (Emphasizes memory)

**Emphasis with Adverbs:**
- "**Certainly** she is the best candidate."
- "**Indeed** he came on time."
- "**Absolutely** I agree with you."
- "**Definitely** she will come."
- "**Really** she is talented."

**Emphasis with Repetition:**
- "It was a **long, long** time ago."
- "She loves him, **really loves him**."
- "I waited **and waited** for her."
- "It was **very, very** cold."
- "He looked **again and again**."

**Emphasis with Double Negatives (Informal):**
- "I **don't not** care." (I care)
- "She **didn't say nothing**." (She said something)
- "It **ain't not** nothing." (It's something)

**Emphasis with Cleft Sentences and Fronting:**
- "**It was John** who called." (Cleft)
- "**That** I love." (Fronting)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I do love you. (✓) | I **do** love you. |
| She does works hard. | She **does work** hard. |
| I didn't say nothing. (informal) | I **didn't say** anything. (formal) |
| I don't not care. (informal) | I **do** care. (formal) |

## 6. Ellipsis

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Ellipsis** means omitting words that are understood from the context. It makes sentences shorter and more efficient.

**Key Points:**
- Omits understood words
- Creates shorter sentences
- Common in informal speech and writing

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Ellipsis of Subject**
```
[ELLIPSIS] + [VERB] + [REST]
(She) + loves + him.
(I) + will + call + you tomorrow.
```

**Structure 2: Ellipsis of Verb/Phrase**
```
[SUBJECT] + (VERB) + [REST]
She + (went) + home.
He + (is) + here.
```

**Structure 3: Ellipsis of Whole Clause**
```
[CLAUSE 1] + [CLAUSE 2] + (CLAUSE 3)
She came. I came + (too).
I went. She went + (too).
```

**Structure 4: Gapping**
```
[SUBJECT A] + [VERB] + [OBJECT A] + [CONJUNCTION] + [SUBJECT B] + (VERB) + [OBJECT B]
I + like + coffee + and + she + (likes) + tea.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Ellipsis of Subject:**
- "(**I**) Love you." (short for "I love you")
- "(**She**) Called me yesterday."
- "(**He**) Will call you later."
- "(**We**) Need to talk."

**Ellipsis of Verb/Phrase:**
- "She is happy, and I (am) too."
- "I can swim, and she can (swim) too."
- "He went home, and I went (home) too."
- "She has arrived, and he has (arrived) too."

**Ellipsis in Responses:**
- "Who called?" "**John**." (John called)
- "What happened?" "**An accident**." (An accident happened)
- "Where are you going?" "**Home**." (I am going home)
- "What time is it?" "**5 PM**." (It is 5 PM)

**Gapping:**
- "I like coffee, and she **tea**." (she likes tea)
- "He reads books, and she **magazines**." (she reads magazines)
- "John went to the store, and Mary **home**." (Mary went home)

**Ellipsis with "To" (Infinitive):**
- "I want to go, but I don't want to." (I don't want to go)
- "She asked me to come, and I will." (I will come)
- "I told him to leave, and he did." (he left)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She is happy, and I too. | She is happy, and I **am** too. |
| I can swim, and she can too. (✓) | I can swim, and she can **too**. |
| I want to go, but I don't want to. (✓) | I want to go, but I don't want **to**. |
| He went home, and I went. | He went home, and I went **too**. |

## 7. Substitution

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Substitution** means replacing a word or phrase with a shorter word to avoid repetition. Common substitutes include "do," "so," "one," "that," and "it."

**Key Points:**
- Replaces repeated words/phrases
- Creates more natural speech
- Avoids repetition

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Substitution with "Do"**
```
[SUBJECT] + [DO] + [OBJECT]
She + does + (so).
I + will + do + (it).
```

**Structure 2: Substitution with "So"**
```
[SUBJECT] + [SAY/BELIEVE/THINK] + [SO]
I + think + so.
She + said + so.
```

**Structure 3: Substitution with "One/Ones"**
```
[DETERMINER] + [ONE/ONES]
the + red + one
these + blue + ones
```

**Structure 4: Substitution with "That/Those"**
```
[SUBJECT] + [VERB] + [THAT/THOSE]
This book is better than + that.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Substitution with "Do":**
- "I love coffee." "**I do too**." (love coffee)
- "She speaks French." "**He does too**." (speaks French)
- "I don't like tea." "**I don't either**." (don't like tea)
- "He can swim." "**She can too**." (can swim)

**Substitution with "So":**
- "I **think so**." (I think it is true)
- "She **said so**." (She said it was true)
- "I **hope so**." (I hope it happens)
- "He **believes so**." (He believes it is true)
- "I **don't think so**." (I don't think it is true)

**Substitution with "One/Ones":**
- "I like the red **one**." (the red book/car/etc.)
- "These shoes are cheaper than those **ones**."
- "Which **one** is yours?"
- "I prefer the blue **ones**."

**Substitution with "That/Those":**
- "My house is bigger than **that**." (that house)
- "Your ideas are better than **those**." (those ideas)
- "This book is more interesting than **that**." (that book)
- "The weather here is colder than **that** in London."

**Substitution with "It/They":**
- "I need help, and I know **it**." (I know I need help)
- "She loves him, and I see **that**." (I see she loves him)
- "They are leaving, and I heard **so**." (I heard they are leaving)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I like coffee. I do too. (✓) | I like coffee. I **do too**. |
| I think so. (✓) | I **think so**. |
| I like the red one. (✓) | I like the red **one**. |
| This book is better than that one. (✓) | This book is better than **that one**. |

## 📚 Quick Reference Card

| Pattern | Formula | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Parallelism** | A, B, and C (same form) | I like **swimming**, **running**, and **reading**. |
| **Inversion** | Neg Adv + Aux + Sub + V | **Never** have I seen such beauty. |
| **Cleft** | It + be + focus + that/who | **It was John** who called. |
| **Cleft** | What + Sub + V + be + focus | **What I need** is rest. |
| **Fronting** | Fronted + Sub + V | **That** I love. |
| **Emphasis** | Sub + do/does/did + V | I **do** love you. |
| **Ellipsis** | Omitting understood words | She can (sing), and I can too. |
| **Substitution** | Replacing with do/so/one | I think **so**. |
