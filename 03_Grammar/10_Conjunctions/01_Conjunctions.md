Welcome to Phase 10! ## 1. Coordinating Conjunctions

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Coordinating conjunctions** connect words, phrases, or clauses of equal grammatical rank. They join elements that are grammatically similar.

**Key Points:**
- Connect equal elements (word + word, phrase + phrase, clause + clause)
- FANBOYS = For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So
- When joining two independent clauses, use a comma before the conjunction

### 📐 Study: The FANBOYS

| Conjunction | Function | Example |
|-------------|----------|---------|
| **F**or | Reason | I stayed home, **for** I was tired. |
| **A**nd | Addition | I like coffee **and** tea. |
| **N**or | Negative addition | He didn't call, **nor** did he text. |
| **B**ut | Contrast | I wanted to go, **but** I was tired. |
| **O**r | Choice | You can come **or** stay home. |
| **Y**et | Contrast (unexpected) | She was tired, **yet** she continued. |
| **S**o | Result | I was hungry, **so** I ate. |

### 👀 See: Examples

**For (Reason):**
- "I stayed home, **for** I was feeling unwell."
- "She was happy, **for** she had received good news."
- "He left early, **for** he had a meeting."

**And (Addition):**
- "I like coffee **and** tea."
- "She is smart **and** kind."
- "We went to the park **and** had a picnic."
- "He works hard **and** plays hard."

**Nor (Negative Addition):**
- "He didn't call, **nor** did he text."
- "She doesn't eat meat, **nor** does she eat fish."
- "I have never been to Paris, **nor** have I been to London."
- "He wasn't happy, **nor** was he sad."

**But (Contrast):**
- "I wanted to go, **but** I was too tired."
- "She is rich **but** unhappy."
- "The food was cheap **but** delicious."
- "He tried hard **but** failed."

**Or (Choice):**
- "You can have tea **or** coffee."
- "Should I stay **or** should I go?"
- "We can eat now **or** wait until later."
- "Is that a dog **or** a cat?"

**Yet (Contrast - Unexpected):**
- "She was tired, **yet** she continued working."
- "The weather was cold, **yet** we went swimming."
- "He is very old, **yet** he is still active."
- "The task was difficult, **yet** they completed it."

**So (Result):**
- "I was hungry, **so** I made a sandwich."
- "She studied hard, **so** she passed the exam."
- "It was raining, **so** we stayed inside."
- "He worked late, **so** he missed the bus."

**Coordinating Conjunction Punctuation Rules:**
- When joining two independent clauses → use a comma before the conjunction
  - "I like coffee, **and** she likes tea."
- When joining words or phrases → no comma needed
  - "I like coffee **and** tea."

**Structure: Joining Independent Clauses**
```
[CLAUSE 1] + [,] + [CONJUNCTION] + [CLAUSE 2]
I like coffee, + and + she likes tea.
```

**Structure: Joining Words/Phrases**
```
[WORD 1] + [CONJUNCTION] + [WORD 2]
coffee + and + tea
```

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I like coffee, tea. (comma splice) | I like coffee **and** tea. |
| I like coffee she likes tea. (run-on) | I like coffee, **and** she likes tea. |
| I wanted to go, but I was tired. (✓) | I wanted to go, **but** I was tired. |
| She is rich, yet unhappy. (✓) | She is rich, **yet** unhappy. |

## 2. Subordinating Conjunctions

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Subordinating conjunctions** connect an independent clause with a dependent clause. They show a relationship such as time, reason, condition, contrast, or place.

**Key Points:**
- Connect unequal elements (independent + dependent)
- Show relationships (time, reason, condition, contrast, place)
- The dependent clause cannot stand alone
- Punctuation depends on clause order

### 📐 Study: Common Subordinating Conjunctions

**Time:**

| Conjunction | Example |
|-------------|---------|
| when | I was sleeping **when** you called. |
| while | She read **while** he cooked. |
| after | **After** I finished, I left. |
| before | **Before** I go, I need to call. |
| since | I have been happy **since** I moved here. |
| until | I waited **until** you arrived. |
| as soon as | Call me **as soon as** you arrive. |
| whenever | **Whenever** I see her, I smile. |
| as | I saw her **as** I was leaving. |

**Reason/Cause:**

| Conjunction | Example |
|-------------|---------|
| because | I stayed home **because** it was raining. |
| since | **Since** you asked, I will tell you. |
| as | **As** I was tired, I went to bed. |

**Condition:**

| Conjunction | Example |
|-------------|---------|
| if | I will go **if** you come with me. |
| unless | I won't go **unless** you come. |
| provided that | I will go **provided that** you come. |
| as long as | I will go **as long as** you come. |
| even if | I will go **even if** it rains. |

**Contrast/Concession:**

| Conjunction | Example |
|-------------|---------|
| although | **Although** he was tired, he continued. |
| though | **Though** it was cold, we went out. |
| even though | **Even though** it rained, we went. |
| whereas | I like coffee, **whereas** she likes tea. |
| while | I like coffee, **while** she likes tea. |

**Place:**

| Conjunction | Example |
|-------------|---------|
| where | This is **where** I live. |
| wherever | I will go **wherever** you go. |

**Manner:**

| Conjunction | Example |
|-------------|---------|
| as if | He acts **as if** he owns the place. |
| as though | She looked **as though** she had seen a ghost. |

**Purpose:**

| Conjunction | Example |
|-------------|---------|
| so that | I studied hard **so that** I could pass. |
| in order that | She saved money **in order that** she could travel. |

### 👀 See: Examples

**Time:**
- "I was sleeping **when** you called."
- "She read **while** he cooked dinner."
- "**After** I finished my work, I went home."
- "**Before** I leave, I need to say goodbye."
- "I have been happy **since** I moved here."
- "I waited **until** you arrived."
- "Call me **as soon as** you get this message."
- "**Whenever** I see her, I smile."

**Reason:**
- "I stayed home **because** it was raining."
- "**Since** you asked, I'll tell you the truth."
- "**As** I was tired, I went to bed early."

**Condition:**
- "I will go **if** you come with me."
- "I won't go **unless** you come with me."
- "I will go **provided that** you come."
- "I will go **as long as** you come."

**Contrast:**
- "**Although** he was tired, he continued working."
- "**Though** it was cold, we went for a walk."
- "**Even though** it rained, we went to the beach."
- "I like coffee, **whereas** she likes tea."

**Place:**
- "This is **where** I grew up."
- "I will go **wherever** you go."

**Purpose:**
- "I studied hard **so that** I could pass the exam."
- "She saved money **in order that** she could travel."

**Subordinating Conjunction Punctuation Rules:**
- When dependent clause comes FIRST → use a comma
  - "**Because** I was tired, I went to bed."
- When dependent clause comes SECOND → no comma
  - "I went to bed **because** I was tired."

**Structure 1: Dependent Clause First**
```
[DEPENDENT CLAUSE] + [,] + [INDEPENDENT CLAUSE]
Because it was raining, + I stayed home.
```

**Structure 2: Independent Clause First**
```
[INDEPENDENT CLAUSE] + [DEPENDENT CLAUSE]
I stayed home + because it was raining.
```

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Because I was tired. (fragment) | **Because** I was tired, I went to bed. |
| Although I was tired, but I went. (double conjunction) | **Although** I was tired, I went. |
| I went home because I was tired. (✓) | I went home **because** I was tired. |
| Since I moved here, I am happy. | **Since** I moved here, I have been happy. |

## 3. Correlative Conjunctions

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Correlative conjunctions** are pairs of conjunctions that work together to connect equal elements. They come in pairs and are always used together.

**Key Points:**
- Work in pairs
- Connect equal elements
- Must be used together
- Show relationships between paired items

### 📐 Study: Common Correlative Conjunctions

| Pair | Function | Example |
|------|----------|---------|
| **both...and** | Two things included | **Both** John **and** Mary are coming. |
| **either...or** | One of two choices | You can have **either** tea **or** coffee. |
| **neither...nor** | Not one of two choices | I like **neither** tea **nor** coffee. |
| **not only...but also** | Additional emphasis | She is **not only** smart **but also** kind. |
| **whether...or** | Choice or uncertainty | I don't know **whether** to go **or** stay. |
| **no sooner...than** | One happened just after another | **No sooner** had I arrived **than** it started raining. |
| **such...that** | Result/degree | It was **such** a good movie **that** I watched it twice. |
| **so...that** | Result/degree | She was **so** tired **that** she fell asleep. |

### 👀 See: Examples

**Both...and (Inclusion):**
- "**Both** John **and** Mary are coming to the party."
- "She is **both** intelligent **and** beautiful."
- "I enjoy **both** reading **and** writing."
- "**Both** the teacher **and** the students were happy."

**Either...or (Choice):**
- "You can have **either** tea **or** coffee."
- "**Either** you come with me **or** you stay here."
- "She can **either** accept the offer **or** look for another job."
- "I will **either** call you **or** send an email."

**Neither...nor (Negative Choice):**
- "I like **neither** tea **nor** coffee."
- "**Neither** John **nor** Mary is coming."
- "She is **neither** happy **nor** sad."
- "He can **neither** speak English **nor** understand it."

**Not only...but also (Additional Emphasis):**
- "She is **not only** intelligent **but also** kind."
- "He **not only** speaks Spanish **but also** speaks French."
- "The movie was **not only** exciting **but also** educational."
- "She **not only** sings **but also** plays the piano."

**Whether...or (Choice/Uncertainty):**
- "I don't know **whether** to go **or** stay."
- "She can't decide **whether** to accept the job **or** wait for another offer."
- "I will come **whether** you like it **or** not."
- "**Whether** you agree **or** not, I'm going."

**No sooner...than (Immediate sequence):**
- "**No sooner** had I arrived **than** it started raining."
- "**No sooner** did she see me **than** she started laughing."
- "**No sooner** had he finished **than** she arrived."

**Such...that (Result):**
- "It was **such** a good movie **that** I watched it twice."
- "She made **such** a mistake **that** she got fired."
- "He is **such** a kind person **that** everyone loves him."

**So...that (Result):**
- "She was **so** tired **that** she fell asleep."
- "He ran **so** fast **that** he won the race."
- "The food was **so** hot **that** I couldn't eat it."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Either John or Mary are coming. | **Either** John or Mary **is** coming. |
| Neither John nor Mary are coming. | **Neither** John nor Mary **is** coming. |
| She is both intelligent and beautiful. (✓) | She is **both** intelligent **and** beautiful. |
| Not only she is smart, but also kind. | She is **not only** smart **but also** kind. |
| Either you come or stay. | **Either** you come **or** you stay. |

**Subject-Verb Agreement with Correlative Conjunctions:**
- **Either...or** and **Neither...nor**: Verb agrees with the subject closest to it
  - "**Either** John **or** his friends **are** coming." (friends = plural)
  - "**Neither** his friends **nor** John **is** coming." (John = singular)
  - "**Either** the students **or** the teacher **is** responsible." (teacher = singular)

**Both...and**: Always plural
- "**Both** John **and** Mary **are** coming." (plural)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Either John or Mary are coming. | **Either** John or Mary **is** coming. |
| Neither John nor Mary are coming. | **Neither** John nor Mary **is** coming. |
| Not only she is smart, but also kind. | She is **not only** smart **but also** kind. |
| Both John and Mary is coming. | **Both** John and Mary **are** coming. |
| I don't know whether to go. (missing or) | I don't know **whether** to go **or** stay. |

## 5. Punctuation with Conjunctions

### 📚 Quick Reference

**Coordinating Conjunctions (FANBOYS):**
- Joining two independent clauses → use a comma before the conjunction
  - "I like coffee, **and** she likes tea."
- Joining words/phrases → no comma needed
  - "I like coffee **and** tea."

**Subordinating Conjunctions:**
- Dependent clause FIRST → use a comma
  - "**Because** I was tired, I went to bed."
- Dependent clause SECOND → no comma
  - "I went to bed **because** I was tired."

**Correlative Conjunctions:**
- No comma needed between the paired elements
  - "She is **both** smart **and** kind."
  - "**Either** John **or** Mary is coming."

## 💬 Practice: Conversations

### Dialogue 1: Using Coordinating Conjunctions

- A: "I like coffee **and** tea."
- B: "I like coffee, **but** I don't like tea."
- A: "She was tired, **so** she went to bed."
- B: "I wanted to go, **but** I was too busy."
- A: "You can have tea **or** coffee."
- B: "I'll have tea, **for** I prefer it to coffee."

### Dialogue 2: Using Subordinating Conjunctions

- A: "**Because** it was raining, I stayed home."
- B: "**Although** he was tired, he continued working."
- A: "I will go **if** you come with me."
- B: "I was sleeping **when** you called."
- A: "**After** I finish my work, I will go home."
- B: "I waited **until** you arrived."

### Dialogue 3: Using Correlative Conjunctions

- A: "She is **both** intelligent **and** kind."
- B: "You can have **either** tea **or** coffee."
- A: "I like **neither** tea **nor** coffee."
- B: "She is **not only** smart **but also** beautiful."
- A: "**Either** John **or** Mary is coming."
- B: "**Neither** his friends **nor** John is coming."
