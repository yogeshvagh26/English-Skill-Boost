# Phase 3: Verbs

## Irregular Verbs

Welcome to Irregular Verbs! You've learned about **verb types**, **verb forms** (V1, V2, V3, V4, V5), and **subject-verb agreement**. Now it's time to master the most challenging but essential part of English verbs — **irregular verbs**.

Think of it this way: *Regular verbs follow predictable rules (work → worked → worked). Irregular verbs are rebels — they don't follow the rules! You have to learn each one individually. But don't worry — with practice, they become second nature.*

## 1. What Are Irregular Verbs?

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Irregular verbs** are verbs that do not form their past tense (V2) or past participle (V3) by adding -ed. Instead, they change their form in unpredictable ways.

**Regular vs. Irregular:**

| Verb Type | V1 | V2 | V3 | Pattern |
|-----------|----|----|-----|---------|
| **Regular** | work | worked | worked | + -ed |
| **Regular** | play | played | played | + -ed |
| **Irregular** | go | went | gone | No pattern |
| **Irregular** | eat | ate | eaten | No pattern |
| **Irregular** | write | wrote | written | No pattern |

**Why They Matter:**
- They are among the most common verbs in English
- You use them constantly in speaking and writing
- They appear in all tenses and forms
- Making mistakes with them is very noticeable

### 📐 Study: The Three Forms of Irregular Verbs

Every irregular verb has three key forms:

| Form | Name | Example (go) | Example (eat) |
|------|------|--------------|---------------|
| **V1** | Base Form | go | eat |
| **V2** | Past Simple | went | ate |
| **V3** | Past Participle | gone | eaten |

**Patterns of Irregular Verbs:**

Irregular verbs fall into several patterns:

| Pattern | Description | Examples |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| **AAA** | All three forms the same | cut, put, set |
| **ABA** | V1 and V3 same | come, run, become |
| **ABC** | All three forms different | go, eat, write |
| **AAB** | V1 and V2 same | beat |
| **ABB** | V2 and V3 same | think, bring, buy |

## 3. How to Learn Irregular Verbs

### 📚 Effective Strategies

**Strategy 1: Learn by Pattern**
Group verbs by their pattern (AAA, ABB, ABC, etc.) for easier memorization.

**Strategy 2: Learn in Context**
Don't just memorize lists — practice using verbs in sentences.

**Strategy 3: Group by Sound**
Many irregular verbs rhyme and follow similar patterns.

| Group | Example 1 | Example 2 | Example 3 |
|-------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| -ought/-aught | bring → brought | buy → bought | think → thought |
| -eep → -ept | keep → kept | sleep → slept | |
| -ing → -ang → -ung | sing → sang → sung | ring → rang → rung | |
| -ow → -ew → -own | grow → grew → grown | know → knew → known | |

**Strategy 4: Use Flashcards**
Create flashcards with V1 on one side and V2/V3 on the other.

**Strategy 5: Practice Daily**
Use irregular verbs in your daily speaking and writing.

**Strategy 6: Listen and Repeat**
Listen to native speakers and repeat sentences with irregular verbs.

## 4. Common Irregular Verb Mistakes

### ⚠️ Learn: The Top Errors

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I have **went**. | I have **gone**. |
| She has **did** it. | She has **done** it. |
| He **buyed** a car. | He **bought** a car. |
| I **eated** dinner. | I **ate** dinner. |
| She **writed** a letter. | She **wrote** a letter. |
| I have **saw** that. | I have **seen** that. |
| He has **took** it. | He has **taken** it. |
| She has **came** home. | She has **come** home. |
| I have **ran** to work. | I have **run** to work. |
| He **speaked** to me. | He **spoke** to me. |

**Key Warning:** Many learners incorrectly add -ed to irregular verbs:
- ❌ I **goed** home. → ✅ I **went** home.
- ❌ She **doed** it. → ✅ She **did** it.
- ❌ They **comed** late. → ✅ They **came** late.

## 5. Irregular Verbs in Context

### 👀 See: Real-Life Usage

**Present Perfect (V3):**
- "I have **seen** that movie three times."
- "She has **written** five books."
- "They have **gone** to the store."
- "We have **eaten** dinner already."

**Simple Past (V2):**
- "I **saw** that movie three times."
- "She **wrote** five books."
- "They **went** to the store."
- "We **ate** dinner already."

**Passive Voice (V3):**
- "The book was **written** by a famous author."
- "The cake was **eaten** by the children."
- "The car was **stolen** last night."
- "The letter was **sent** yesterday."

**Adjectives (V3):**
- "The **broken** window needs repair."
- "A **written** agreement is better."
- "The **stolen** car was found."
- "The **lost** dog has been found."

## 💬 Practice: Conversations

### Dialogue 1: Telling a Story

- A: "What did you do yesterday?"
- B: "I **went** to the park and **saw** some friends."
- A: "Have you **seen** them recently?"
- B: "No, I hadn't **seen** them for a long time."
- A: "What did you do together?"
- B: "We **ate** lunch and **talked** about old times."
- A: "That sounds nice. Have you **made** plans to meet again?"
- B: "Yes, we've **made** plans for next month."

### Dialogue 2: Travel Experience

- A: "Have you ever **been** to Japan?"
- B: "Yes, I have. I **went** there two years ago."
- A: "What did you **see** there?"
- B: "I **saw** Tokyo and **visited** Kyoto."
- A: "Have you **eaten** authentic Japanese food?"
- B: "Yes, I've **eaten** sushi and ramen."
- A: "Have you **taken** any Japanese classes?"
- B: "No, but I've **learned** a few phrases."

### Dialogue 3: Lost Items

- A: "What's wrong? You look upset."
- B: "I've **lost** my wallet!"
- A: "When did you **lose** it?"
- B: "I **think** I **left** it at the coffee shop."
- A: "Have you **called** them?"
- B: "Yes, I **called** them, but they haven't **found** it."
- A: "Have you **told** the police?"
- B: "Not yet. I haven't **done** that."
