## 1. Situation : M&A Due Diligence – The "Acquisition Gamble"

**Context:** You are a Senior Data Analyst at a mid-sized SaaS company. The CEO (Sarah) is considering acquiring a smaller competitor, "TargetCo," for $50M. The deal is being championed by the Head of Corporate Development (James), who is excited about TargetCo's "fast-growing customer base." The CFO (Elena) is skeptical—she thinks TargetCo's unit economics are weak. You've been asked to analyze TargetCo's data—customer health, retention, LTV, churn, and financials—to determine whether the acquisition makes sense. You need to present your findings objectively, without taking sides.

## 3. Vocabulary and Expressions

| Word/Phrase                     | Part of Speech | Meaning                                                                                | Example Sentence                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------- | -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **M&A (Mergers and Acquisitions)** | Acronym        | The process of combining or acquiring companies                                        | *M&A due diligence requires deep analysis of the target's data.*                                |
| **Due diligence**               | Noun Phrase    | The investigation of a potential acquisition target                                    | *We need to do rigorous due diligence before committing to the deal.*                           |
| **Unit economics**              | Noun Phrase    | The profitability of a single customer (LTV vs. CAC)                                  | *TargetCo's unit economics are weak—they spend almost as much as they earn.*                    |
| **LTV/CAC ratio**               | Noun Phrase    | The ratio of Customer Lifetime Value to Customer Acquisition Cost                       | *A 1.2x LTV/CAC ratio is below the industry standard of 3x+.*                                   |
| **Net Revenue Retention (NRR)** | Acronym/Metric | The percentage of recurring revenue retained from existing customers, including expansions | *TargetCo's NRR is 85%—they're losing revenue from existing customers.*                          |
| **Churn**                       | Noun           | The rate at which customers cancel their subscriptions                                 | *15% annual churn means they lose 15% of their revenue base each year.*                         |
| **Technology debt**             | Noun Phrase    | The cost of maintaining outdated or poorly integrated technology                       | *TargetCo's technology debt will require a $2M investment to fix.*                              |
| **Integration cost**            | Noun Phrase    | The cost of integrating the target company's systems and data                           | *Integration costs must be factored into the acquisition price.*                                |
| **Earn-out**                    | Noun           | A clause in an acquisition agreement where additional payment is contingent on performance | *An earn-out clause protects us if TargetCo's retention doesn't improve.*                       |
| **Synergy**                     | Noun           | The additional value created by combining two companies                                 | *We need to quantify potential synergies—cost savings and revenue cross-sell.*                 |
| **Valuation model**             | Noun Phrase    | A financial model that estimates the fair value of a company                            | *Our valuation model suggests TargetCo is worth $30–35M, not $50M.*                             |
| **Data quality**                | Noun Phrase    | The accuracy, completeness, and consistency of data                                    | *TargetCo's data quality is poor—20% duplication in customer records.*                         |
| **Integration failure**         | Noun Phrase    | When the process of merging two companies fails to achieve expected benefits            | *Integration failure is the biggest risk in M&A—it destroys value.*                             |

## 4. Key Takeaways

### Common Sentence Patterns (for M&A Due Diligence Storytelling)

| Pattern                                                              | Use                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| *"I've analyzed [Target] across [X] dimensions."*                   | To introduce a structured, comprehensive assessment.                                   |
| *"Start with the good news: [positive finding]."*                   | To acknowledge the upside before diving into concerns.                                 |
| *"But here's the problem—and it's a big one."*                      | To signal a significant red flag.                                                      |
| *"I found significant red flags: (1) [X], (2) [Y], (3) [Z]."*       | To present the negative findings clearly.                                              |
| *"I've modeled three scenarios: Best case [X], Likely case [Y], Worst case [Z]."* | To show the range of possible outcomes and manage risk.                                |
| *"The data suggests the fair price is [X]—not [Y]."*                | To deliver the financial recommendation confidently.                                   |
| *"My recommendation: (1) [Action], (2) [Action], (3) [Action]."*    | To provide a clear, actionable plan.                                                   |
| *"The story isn't [simplistic narrative]. The story is [nuanced finding]."* | To reframe the narrative (anchor pattern).                                             |
| *"You just saved us $[X]."*                                          | To demonstrate the value of the analysis directly.                                     |

### Useful Phrases to Remember
- **Introducing the analysis:** *"I've been digging into their data. Here's what I found."*
- **Highlighting a red flag:** *"On the surface, they look great. But the unit economics tell a different story."*
- **When someone is championing the deal:** *"James, I understand why you're excited—but let's make sure we're paying the right price."*
- **Introducing the "best/worst case" scenario:** *"I've modeled best, worst, and likely cases. Even in the best case, we're overpaying."*
- **When proposing negotiation tactics:** *"Let's use the data to negotiate a lower price and include an earn-out clause."*
- **When the deal closes:** *"We acquired TargetCo for $35M—saving $15M based on our analysis. That's a win."*

### Practical Speaking Tips (for M&A and Due Diligence Narratives)
1.  **Be objective.** M&A decisions are emotional. Stick to the data. Don't take sides—just present what the data says.
2.  **Don't just look at top-line growth.** Revenue growth can mask weak unit economics. Always dig into LTV, CAC, retention, and churn.
3.  **Model multiple scenarios.** M&A is uncertain. Best-case, likely-case, and worst-case scenarios give you a range to work with.
4.  **Quantify the "Integration Cost."** The price of the acquisition is just the start. Integration costs (technology, people, data cleanup) can add 20-30% to the total cost.
5.  **Check data quality.** The target company's data may be messy. Duplicate records, missing fields, and inconsistent definitions can signal deeper operational issues.
6.  **Include an "earn-out" clause if needed.** If the target's performance is uncertain, structure the deal with an earn-out—extra payment if they hit specific targets post-acquisition.
7.  **Validate customer health.** Don't just look at revenue—look at customer retention, NPS, and support tickets. Happy customers are sticky customers.
8.  **Use a "Valuation Bridge."** Show how you got from the asking price to your recommended price. For example: "$50M asking – $10M overvaluation based on LTV/CAC – $5M integration cost = $35M fair price."
9.  **Present the "recommendation" clearly.** Don't leave the decision ambiguous. Say: *"My recommendation is to negotiate down to $35M with an earn-out."*
10. **Follow up with a "Due Diligence Checklist."** After the presentation, provide a checklist of all the areas you analyzed—customer health, financials, technology, etc. This builds confidence and shows thoroughness.
