# Phase 9: Prepositions

## Difficult Prepositions (In, On, At, By, Until, Since, For, During, Into, Onto, Among, Between)

Welcome to the second part of Phase 9! In the previous section, you learned about **types of prepositions** (time, place, direction, movement, manner, agent). Now it's time to dive deeper into the **most difficult prepositions** — the ones that English learners (and even native speakers) often confuse.

Think of it this way: *Some prepositions are easy (like "before" and "after"). But others — especially IN, ON, AT, BY, UNTIL, SINCE, FOR, DURING, INTO, ONTO, AMONG, and BETWEEN — have subtle differences that can change your meaning. Mastering these will dramatically improve your accuracy.*

## 1. IN, ON, AT (Place)

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** IN, ON, and AT are the most commonly confused prepositions of place. They show where something is located, but at different levels of specificity.

**Key Points:**
- **IN** = inside, enclosed space
- **ON** = surface, touching
- **AT** = specific point, exact location

### 📐 Study: IN, ON, AT (Place) Chart

| Preposition | Use | Examples |
|-------------|-----|----------|
| **IN** | Enclosed spaces, cities, countries, large areas | in the room, in London, in England |
| **ON** | Surfaces, streets, floors, public transport | on the table, on Main Street, on the bus |
| **AT** | Specific points, addresses, locations | at the door, at 10 Main Street, at the bus stop |

### 👀 See: Examples

**IN (Enclosed space):**
- "The book is **in** the bag."
- "She lives **in** London."
- "I was **in** the car."
- "The keys are **in** my pocket."
- "He is **in** the hospital."

**ON (Surface):**
- "The book is **on** the table."
- "She lives **on** Main Street."
- "The picture is **on** the wall."
- "I sat **on** the chair."
- "He is **on** the bus."

**AT (Specific point):**
- "I am **at** the door."
- "She is **at** the bus stop."
- "We met **at** the restaurant."
- "He works **at** the bank."
- "I'll see you **at** the station."

**IN vs. ON vs. AT (Place) Comparison:**

| Situation | IN | ON | AT |
|-----------|----|----|----|
| **Location** | in the room | on the table | at the door |
| **City** | in London | - | - |
| **Street** | - | on Main Street | - |
| **Address** | - | - | at 10 Main Street |
| **Transport** | in the car | on the bus | - |
| **Building** | in the building | - | at the building |

**Key Rules:**
- **IN** = inside something (enclosed)
- **ON** = on the surface of something (touching)
- **AT** = at a specific point (exact location)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I am in home. | I am **at** home. |
| She lives at London. | She lives **in** London. |
| The book is in the table. | The book is **on** the table. |
| He is in the bus stop. | He is **at** the bus stop. |
| I sat at the chair. | I sat **on** the chair. |
| She is in the bus. | She is **on** the bus. |

## 2. IN, ON, AT (Time)

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** IN, ON, and AT are also used for time, with different levels of specificity.

**Key Points:**
- **IN** = longer periods (months, years, seasons, parts of day)
- **ON** = specific days and dates
- **AT** = exact times and specific points

### 📐 Study: IN, ON, AT (Time) Chart

| Preposition | Use | Examples |
|-------------|-----|----------|
| **IN** | Months, years, seasons, parts of day | in June, in 2024, in summer, in the morning |
| **ON** | Days, dates, specific holidays | on Monday, on July 4th, on Christmas Day |
| **AT** | Exact times, holidays (no day), night | at 5 PM, at Christmas, at night |

### 👀 See: Examples

**IN (Longer periods):**
- "I was born **in** June."
- "She graduated **in** 2020."
- "I like to swim **in** summer."
- "I work **in** the morning."
- "We'll meet **in** the evening."

**ON (Specific days/dates):**
- "I will see you **on** Monday."
- "She was born **on** July 4th."
- "We have a meeting **on** Friday."
- "I work **on** weekends."
- "He arrived **on** Christmas Day."

**AT (Exact times):**
- "I will meet you **at** 5 PM."
- "We celebrate **at** Christmas." (holiday period)
- "She wakes up **at** 7 AM."
- "I work **at** night."
- "He arrived **at** noon."

**IN vs. ON vs. AT (Time) Comparison:**

| Time Frame | IN | ON | AT |
|------------|----|----|----|
| **Time** | - | - | at 5 PM |
| **Day** | - | on Monday | - |
| **Date** | - | on July 4th | - |
| **Month** | in June | - | - |
| **Year** | in 2024 | - | - |
| **Season** | in summer | - | - |
| **Part of day** | in the morning | - | at night |

**Exceptions:**
- "**At** night" (not "in the night")
- "**At** the weekend" (British) / "**On** the weekend" (American)
- "**At** Christmas" (holiday period) / "**On** Christmas Day" (specific day)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I will meet you in 5 PM. | I will meet you **at** 5 PM. |
| I will see you at Monday. | I will see you **on** Monday. |
| She was born in July 4th. | She was born **on** July 4th. |
| I work in the morning. (✓) | I work **in** the morning. |
| I work in night. | I work **at** night. |
| We met in the weekend. (US) | We met **on** the weekend. |

## 3. BY

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "BY" has several important uses: indicating nearness, deadlines, method/means, and agents.

**Key Points:**
- **BY** = near or beside something
- **BY** = not later than (deadline)
- **BY** = method or means
- **BY** = agent (passive voice)

### 📐 Study: BY Chart

| Use | Meaning | Example |
|-----|---------|---------|
| **Near** | beside, next to | Sit **by** me. |
| **Deadline** | not later than | Finish **by** 5 PM. |
| **Method** | means of doing | I travel **by** train. |
| **Agent** | who performs action | The book was written **by** Sarah. |
| **Passing** | past someone/something | He walked **by** me. |

### 👀 See: Examples

**By = Near:**
- "Sit **by** me."
- "The house is **by** the river."
- "I stood **by** the window."
- "She lives **by** the park."

**By = Deadline:**
- "I will finish **by** 5 PM."
- "Please submit the report **by** Friday."
- "I need to arrive **by** 8 AM."
- "The project must be completed **by** December."

**By = Method/Means:**
- "I travel **by** train."
- "She wrote the letter **by** hand."
- "He paid **by** credit card."
- "I learned it **by** experience."

**By = Agent:**
- "The book was written **by** a famous author."
- "The cake was made **by** my mother."
- "The project was completed **by** the team."
- "The letter was sent **by** Sarah."

**By = Passing:**
- "He walked **by** me."
- "She drove **by** the store."
- "I passed **by** your house."
- "The bus went **by** without stopping."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I will finish until 5 PM. | I will finish **by** 5 PM. |
| I travel by a train. | I travel **by** train. |
| She wrote by hand. (✓) | She wrote **by** hand. |
| I am by home. | I am **at** home. |

**By vs. Until:**
- **By** = not later than (deadline)
- **Until** = up to that point (duration)

| Word | Example | Meaning |
|------|---------|---------|
| **by** | Finish **by** 5 PM. | Finish before or at 5 PM. |
| **until** | Stay **until** 5 PM. | Stay from now to 5 PM. |

## 4. UNTIL

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "UNTIL" (and "till") indicates that an action continues up to a specific point in time.

**Key Points:**
- **UNTIL** = up to that point (not after)
- Shows the endpoint of a continuous action
- Can be used with time or events

### 📐 Study: UNTIL Chart

| Use | Example |
|-----|---------|
| **Time** | Stay **until** 5 PM. |
| **Event** | Stay **until** I return. |
| **Not until** | I didn't leave **until** 5 PM. |

### 👀 See: Examples

**Until + Time:**
- "I will stay **until** 5 PM."
- "She worked **until** midnight."
- "The store is open **until** 9 PM."
- "I waited **until** 8 AM."

**Until + Event:**
- "Stay **until** I return."
- "I will wait **until** you finish."
- "Don't move **until** I say so."
- "We will stay **until** the meeting ends."

**Not Until (Emphatic):**
- "I didn't leave **until** 5 PM." (I left at 5 PM)
- "She didn't arrive **until** 8 PM." (She arrived at 8 PM)
- "We didn't eat **until** everyone arrived."

**Until vs. By:**

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **until** | Duration up to a point | I will stay **until** 5 PM. |
| **by** | Deadline (not later than) | I will finish **by** 5 PM. |

**Until vs. By (Comparison):**
- "I will stay **until** 5 PM." (I leave at 5 PM)
- "I will finish **by** 5 PM." (I finish before or at 5 PM)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I will finish until 5 PM. (wrong meaning) | I will finish **by** 5 PM. |
| I will stay by 5 PM. (wrong meaning) | I will stay **until** 5 PM. |
| I wait until you come. (✓) | I will wait **until** you come. |
| I didn't see him until yesterday. (✓) | I didn't see him **until** yesterday. |

## 5. SINCE

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "SINCE" indicates the starting point of an action that continues to the present.

**Key Points:**
- **SINCE** = from a specific point in the past
- Used with present perfect and past perfect
- Always followed by a specific time or event

### 📐 Study: SINCE Chart

| Use | Example |
|-----|---------|
| **Specific point in time** | I have worked here **since** 2020. |
| **Event in the past** | I have been happy **since** I moved here. |

### 👀 See: Examples

**Since + Specific Time:**
- "I have worked here **since** 2020."
- "She has lived here **since** 2018."
- "I have known her **since** 2015."
- "We have been friends **since** childhood."

**Since + Event:**
- "I have been happy **since** I moved here."
- "She has been tired **since** she woke up."
- "I have felt better **since** I started exercising."
- "We have known each other **since** we were children."

**Since vs. For:**

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **since** | Starting point | since 2020, since Monday |
| **for** | Duration | for 5 years, for 3 days |

**Since vs. For (Comparison):**
- "I have worked here **since** 2020." (starting point)
- "I have worked here **for** 5 years." (duration)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I work since 2020. (tense) | I **have worked** since 2020. |
| I have worked since 5 years. (wrong word) | I have worked **for** 5 years. |
| I have worked here for 2020. (wrong word) | I have worked here **since** 2020. |
| I have worked since 5 years ago. (✓) | I have worked since 5 years ago. |

## 6. FOR

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "FOR" indicates the duration of an action. It tells how long something has been happening.

**Key Points:**
- **FOR** = duration (how long)
- Used with present perfect, past, and future
- Followed by a period of time

### 📐 Study: FOR Chart

| Use | Example |
|-----|---------|
| **Duration** | I have worked here **for** 5 years. |
| **Future duration** | I will be there **for** a week. |
| **Past duration** | She lived there **for** 10 years. |

### 👀 See: Examples

**For + Duration:**
- "I have worked here **for** 5 years."
- "She has lived here **for** 10 years."
- "I studied English **for** 2 hours."
- "We waited **for** 30 minutes."

**For + Amount of Time:**
- "I will be on vacation **for** a week."
- "He slept **for** 8 hours."
- "She ran **for** 30 minutes."
- "I have known him **for** a long time."

**For vs. Since (Comparison):**
- "I have worked here **for** 5 years." (duration)
- "I have worked here **since** 2020." (starting point)
- "I have been waiting **for** 2 hours." (duration)
- "I have been waiting **since** 3 PM." (starting point)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I have worked since 5 years. | I have worked **for** 5 years. |
| I have worked here for 2020. | I have worked here **since** 2020. |
| I work for 5 years. (tense) | I **have worked** for 5 years. |
| I will stay for two weeks. (✓) | I will stay **for** two weeks. |

## 7. DURING

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "DURING" indicates that something happens within a particular period of time. It answers "When?" within a time frame.

**Key Points:**
- **DURING** = throughout or at some point in a period
- Followed by a noun/noun phrase (not a clause)
- Shows when something happens

### 📐 Study: DURING Chart

| Use | Example |
|-----|---------|
| **Throughout** | I fell asleep **during** the movie. |
| **At some point** | We met **during** the conference. |
| **Specific event** | He called **during** the meeting. |

### 👀 See: Examples

**During + Event:**
- "I fell asleep **during** the movie."
- "She called **during** the meeting."
- "We met **during** the conference."
- "He arrived **during** the dinner."

**During + Time Period:**
- "I work **during** the day."
- "She sleeps **during** the night."
- "The store is busy **during** the holidays."
- "We traveled **during** the summer."

**During vs. While:**

| Word | Use | Example |
|------|-----|---------|
| **during** | + noun/noun phrase | during the movie |
| **while** | + clause (subject + verb) | while I was watching |

**During vs. For:**

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **during** | When (within a period) | I slept **during** the movie. |
| **for** | Duration (how long) | I slept **for** 2 hours. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I slept during 2 hours. | I slept **for** 2 hours. |
| I fell asleep while the movie. | I fell asleep **during** the movie. |
| During I was watching, I fell asleep. | **While** I was watching, I fell asleep. |
| I met her during the conference. (✓) | I met her **during** the conference. |

## 8. INTO

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "INTO" indicates movement from outside to inside. It shows entry or transformation.

**Key Points:**
- **INTO** = entering (movement)
- Shows going inside something
- Can show transformation

### 📐 Study: INTO Chart

| Use | Example |
|-----|---------|
| **Entering** | He walked **into** the room. |
| **Getting in** | She got **into** the car. |
| **Transformation** | The caterpillar turned **into** a butterfly. |

### 👀 See: Examples

**Into = Entering:**
- "He walked **into** the room."
- "She got **into** the car."
- "I put the money **into** my pocket."
- "The cat jumped **into** the box."
- "We went **into** the building."

**Into = Transformation:**
- "The caterpillar turned **into** a butterfly."
- "The water turned **into** ice."
- "She changed **into** a different person."
- "The prince turned **into** a frog."

**Into vs. In:**

| Word | Use | Example |
|------|-----|---------|
| **into** | Movement (going in) | He walked **into** the room. |
| **in** | Location (already inside) | He is **in** the room. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| He walked in the room. (movement) | He walked **into** the room. |
| She is into the car. (location) | She is **in** the car. |
| I put it into my bag. (✓) | I put it **into** my bag. |
| I got in the car. (movement) | I got **into** the car. |

## 9. ONTO

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "ONTO" indicates movement from one place to a surface. It shows moving to the top of something.

**Key Points:**
- **ONTO** = moving to a surface
- Shows movement, not location
- Related to "on"

### 📐 Study: ONTO Chart

| Use | Example |
|-----|---------|
| **Moving to a surface** | She jumped **onto** the table. |
| **Getting on** | He got **onto** the bus. |
| **Placing on** | I put the book **onto** the shelf. |

### 👀 See: Examples

**Onto = Moving to a Surface:**
- "She jumped **onto** the table."
- "He got **onto** the bus."
- "I put the book **onto** the shelf."
- "The cat jumped **onto** the bed."
- "She climbed **onto** the roof."

**Onto vs. On:**

| Word | Use | Example |
|------|-----|---------|
| **onto** | Movement (to a surface) | She jumped **onto** the table. |
| **on** | Location (already on surface) | She is **on** the table. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She jumped on the table. (movement) | She jumped **onto** the table. |
| She is onto the table. (location) | She is **on** the table. |
| I put it onto the shelf. (✓) | I put it **onto** the shelf. |
| He got on the bus. (movement) | He got **onto** the bus. |

## 10. AMONG

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "AMONG" indicates being in the middle of or surrounded by a group of three or more people or things.

**Key Points:**
- **AMONG** = in a group of 3 or more
- Shows being surrounded by
- Used with plural nouns

### 📐 Study: AMONG Chart

| Use | Example |
|-----|---------|
| **In a group (3+)** | She is **among** her friends. |
| **Surrounded by** | He is **among** the trees. |
| **One of a group** | She is **among** the best students. |

### 👀 See: Examples

**Among + Group (3+):**
- "She is **among** her friends."
- "He is **among** the trees."
- "The teacher walked **among** the students."
- "She is **among** the best in her class."
- "He found peace **among** nature."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| The store is among the bank and the hotel. | The store is **between** the bank and the hotel. |
| She is among her two friends. | She is **between** her two friends. |
| He is among the students. (✓) | He is **among** the students. |

**Among vs. Between:**

| Word | Use | Example |
|------|-----|---------|
| **among** | 3 or more items | **Among** her friends, she is the tallest. |
| **between** | 2 items | **Between** the two buildings, there is a garden. |

## 11. BETWEEN

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** "BETWEEN" indicates being in the middle of two people or things. It shows a position separating two items.

**Key Points:**
- **BETWEEN** = in the middle of two things
- Shows a separating position
- Used with two things (or more when referring to pairs)

### 📐 Study: BETWEEN Chart

| Use | Example |
|-----|---------|
| **Between two things** | The book is **between** the two chairs. |
| **Time interval** | I will call you **between** 5 and 6 PM. |
| **Relationships** | The secret is **between** you and me. |

### 👀 See: Examples

**Between + Two Things:**
- "The book is **between** the two chairs."
- "The store is **between** the bank and the hotel."
- "He sat **between** his two friends."
- "The river flows **between** the two cities."
- "There is a fence **between** the two houses."

**Between + Time:**
- "I will call you **between** 5 and 6 PM."
- "She will arrive **between** 3 and 4 PM."
- "We will meet **between** Monday and Friday."

**Between + Relationships:**
- "The secret is **between** you and me."
- "There is a special bond **between** them."
- "**Between** us, I think he is lying."

**Between vs. Among (Comparison):**

| Word | Number | Example |
|------|--------|---------|
| **between** | 2 (or pairs) | **Between** the two buildings, there is a garden. |
| **among** | 3+ | **Among** her friends, she is the tallest. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| The store is among the bank and the hotel. | The store is **between** the bank and the hotel. |
| She is between her friends. (3+ friends) | She is **among** her friends. |
| The secret is between you and I. | The secret is **between** you and **me**. |
| I will call you among 5 and 6 PM. | I will call you **between** 5 and 6 PM. |

## 📚 Quick Reference Card

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|-------------|-----|---------|
| **In** | Enclosed space, long time | in the room, in June |
| **On** | Surface, specific days | on the table, on Monday |
| **At** | Specific point, exact time | at the door, at 5 PM |
| **By** | Near, deadline, method | by the window, by 5 PM |
| **Until** | Up to a point in time | until 5 PM |
| **Since** | Starting point | since 2020 |
| **For** | Duration | for 5 years |
| **During** | Throughout a period | during the movie |
| **Into** | Entering (movement) | into the room |
| **Onto** | Moving to a surface | onto the table |
| **Among** | In a group (3+) | among friends |
| **Between** | In the middle (2) | between two chairs |
