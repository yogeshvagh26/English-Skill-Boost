## 1. Weekly Revision

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Weekly revision** is the practice of reviewing what you've learned each week. It prevents forgetting and reinforces new knowledge.

**Key Points:**
- Review for 15-30 minutes each week
- Focus on one or two topics
- Use active recall
- Track your progress

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Weekly Revision Schedule**
```
[WEEK 1] → [Tenses]
[WEEK 2] → [Verbs]
[WEEK 3] → [Nouns & Pronouns]
[WEEK 4] → [Adjectives & Adverbs]
```

**Structure 2: Weekly Revision Format**
```
[REVIEW TOPIC] + [EXERCISES] + [QUIZZES] + [ERROR LOG]
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Sample Weekly Revision Plan:**

**Week 1: Tenses**
- Day 1: Review Simple Present & Present Continuous
- Day 2: Review Present Perfect & Present Perfect Continuous
- Day 3: Review Simple Past & Past Continuous
- Day 4: Review Past Perfect & Past Perfect Continuous
- Day 5: Review Simple Future & Future Continuous
- Day 6: Review Future Perfect & Future Perfect Continuous
- Day 7: Complete a mixed tenses quiz

**Week 2: Verbs**
- Day 1: Review Main Verbs & Helping Verbs
- Day 2: Review Modal Verbs
- Day 3: Review Linking Verbs & Action Verbs
- Day 4: Review Transitive & Intransitive Verbs
- Day 5: Review Verb Forms (V1-V5)
- Day 6: Review Irregular Verbs
- Day 7: Complete a verb quiz

**Sample Weekly Revision Routine:**

"Every Sunday, I spend 30 minutes reviewing the grammar I learned during the week. I start by reading my notes, then I complete 10 practice questions, and finally I check my error log."

**Weekly Revision Checklist:**

| Task | Completed? |
|------|------------|
| Review notes from the week | ☐ |
| Complete practice exercises | ☐ |
| Take a quiz | ☐ |
| Update error log | ☐ |
| Identify weak areas | ☐ |
| Plan next week's revision | ☐ |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Skipping weekly revision | Make it a habit |
| Passive revision (just reading) | Use active recall |
| Not tracking progress | Keep a journal |
| Reviewing the same topics | Vary your topics |

## 3. Grammar Quizzes

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Grammar quizzes** are short tests that assess your knowledge of specific grammar topics. They help identify strengths and weaknesses.

**Key Points:**
- Test specific topics
- Identify weaknesses
- Track progress
- Build confidence

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Quiz Format**
```
[TOPIC] + [QUESTIONS] + [ANSWERS] + [SCORE]
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Sample Quiz 1: Tenses**

*Complete the sentences:*

1. I _____ (go) to school every day.
2. She _____ (work) here since 2020.
3. I _____ (see) him yesterday.
4. They _____ (arrive) at 5 PM tomorrow.
5. She _____ (study) for three hours.

**Answers:** 1. go, 2. has worked, 3. saw, 4. will arrive, 5. has been studying

**Sample Quiz 2: Articles**

*Complete the sentences:*

1. I saw _____ apple on the table.
2. She is _____ honest person.
3. I need _____ hour to finish.
4. She is _____ university student.
5. I saw _____ dog in the park.

**Answers:** 1. an, 2. an, 3. an, 4. a, 5. a

**Sample Quiz 3: Error Correction**

*Find and correct the errors:*

1. She don't like coffee.
2. The man which lives here is my uncle.
3. I have went to the store.
4. She is good in math.
5. Me and John are friends.

**Answers:**
1. She **doesn't** like coffee.
2. The man **who** lives here is my uncle.
3. I have **gone** to the store.
4. She is good **at** math.
5. **John and I** are friends.

**Quiz Frequency:**
- Daily: 5-10 questions
- Weekly: 20-30 questions
- Monthly: 50-100 questions

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Not taking quizzes | Quiz yourself regularly |
| Ignoring mistakes | Review and learn from mistakes |
| Taking the same quiz | Vary your quizzes |
| Not tracking scores | Track your progress |

## 4. Mock Tests

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Mock tests** are full-length grammar tests that simulate exam conditions. They help you prepare for real exams and build confidence.

**Key Points:**
- Simulate exam conditions
- Test all topics
- Build exam confidence
- Identify weak areas

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Mock Test Format**
```
[SECTION 1] + [SECTION 2] + [SECTION 3] + [TOTAL SCORE]
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Sample Mock Test (60 Minutes)**

**Section 1: Error Detection (10 questions)**

*Identify the error in each sentence:*

1. She don't like coffee.
2. The man which lives here is my uncle.
3. I have went to the store.
4. She is good in math.
5. Me and John are friends.
6. Everyone are here.
7. This is more better.
8. I saw a apple.
9. Where she lives?
10. The reason why I left is because I was tired.

**Section 2: Fill in the Blanks (10 questions)**

*Complete the sentences:*

1. I _____ (go) to school every day.
2. She _____ (work) here since 2020.
3. I saw _____ apple on the table.
4. She is good _____ math.
5. _____ it was raining, I went out.
6. She _____ (speak) three languages.
7. I _____ (finish) my homework already.
8. We _____ (go) to the park tomorrow.
9. The book _____ (read) by her is famous.
10. I _____ (never/see) such beauty.

**Section 3: Sentence Transformation (10 questions)**

*Transform the sentences:*

1. She writes a letter. (Passive) → _____
2. She said, "I am happy." (Indirect) → _____
3. He asked, "Where are you going?" (Indirect) → _____
4. If it rains, I will stay home. (Unless) → _____
5. She is taller than me. (Comparative) → _____
6. Active → Passive: They built a house.
7. Direct → Indirect: He said, "I will come."
8. Simple → Complex: I like coffee.
9. Conditional: If I had money, I would travel. (Rewrite)
10. Comparative: She is more beautiful than her sister.

**Section 4: Paragraph Writing (1 question)**

*Write a paragraph (5-7 sentences) on one of these topics:*
1. The importance of education
2. My favorite hobby
3. The value of friendship

|-------|------|

| Preposition with "interested" | interested in |

## 6. Grammar Journal

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **grammar journal** is a personal record of your grammar learning journey. It tracks progress, records new rules, and notes common errors.

**Key Points:**
- Track progress
- Record new rules
- Note common errors
- Review regularly

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Grammar Journal Format**
```
[DATE] + [TOPIC] + [RULES] + [EXAMPLES] + [NOTES] + [ERRORS]
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Sample Grammar Journal Entry:**

**Date:** July 27, 2026

**Topic:** Present Perfect Tense

**Rules:**
- Formula: have/has + past participle
- Used for: Experiences, unfinished actions, recent actions
- Signal words: ever, never, already, yet, just, for, since

**Examples:**
- I have visited Paris.
- She has worked here since 2020.
- They have already eaten.

**Common Mistakes:**
- Using past simple instead of present perfect: I have seen him yesterday. → I **saw** him yesterday.
- Using "since" instead of "for": I have worked here since 5 years. → I have worked here **for** 5 years.

**Progress:**
- I understand present perfect better now.
- I need to practice more with "since" and "for."

**Error Log Entry:**
- Date: July 27, 2026
- Error: I have seen him yesterday.
- Correction: I saw him yesterday.
- Rule: Use past simple for specific past time.

**Journal Entry Structure:**

| Section | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Date | When you wrote the entry |
| Topic | What you learned |
| Rules | Key rules and formulas |
| Examples | Your own example sentences |
| Notes | Any additional observations |
| Errors | Mistakes to avoid |
| Progress | How you feel about your progress |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Not writing regularly | Write daily or weekly |
| Just copying rules | Write in your own words |
| Not using examples | Include your own examples |
| Not reviewing | Review your journal regularly |

## 7. Error Log

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** An **error log** is a record of the mistakes you make in your writing and speaking. It helps you identify patterns and focus your revision.

**Key Points:**
- Track every error
- Identify patterns
- Focus revision on weak areas
- Monitor improvement

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Error Log Format**
```
[DATE] + [ERROR] + [CORRECTION] + [RULE] + [NOTES]
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Sample Error Log:**

| Date | Error | Correction | Rule | Notes |
|------|-------|------------|------|-------|
| July 27, 2026 | She don't like coffee. | She **doesn't** like coffee. | Subject-verb agreement | Third person singular |
| July 27, 2026 | The man which lives here... | The man **who** lives here... | Relative pronoun | Use "who" for people |
| July 28, 2026 | I have went home. | I have **gone** home. | Verb form | Past participle of "go" is "gone" |
| July 28, 2026 | She is good in math. | She is good **at** math. | Preposition | "Good at" for skills |
| July 29, 2026 | Me and John are friends. | **John and I** are friends. | Pronoun case | Subject pronoun after "and" |

**Error Log Analysis:**

| Error Type | Count | Date Range |
|------------|-------|------------|
| Subject-Verb Agreement | 5 | July 1-15 |
| Prepositions | 3 | July 1-15 |
| Verb Forms | 4 | July 1-15 |
| Pronouns | 2 | July 1-15 |
| Articles | 1 | July 1-15 |

**Error Log Benefits:**

| Benefit | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Pattern Recognition** | See which errors you make most |
| **Targeted Revision** | Focus on your weak areas |
| **Progress Tracking** | See how you improve over time |
| **Confidence Building** | Celebrate improvements |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Not tracking errors | Log every error |
| Not analyzing patterns | Review your error log regularly |
| Not reviewing | Review errors weekly |
| Not celebrating improvements | Track your progress |

**Definition:** **Spaced repetition** is a learning technique that involves reviewing information at increasing intervals. It optimizes memory and prevents forgetting.

**Key Points:**
- Review at increasing intervals
- Optimize memory retention
- Use active recall
- Use SRS tools

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Spaced Repetition Schedule**

```
[DAY 1] + [DAY 3] + [DAY 7] + [DAY 14] + [DAY 30]
```

| 4th Review | 7 days later | Day 12 |
