# Topic: Managing a Corporate Talent Retention / Engagement Strategy

**Scenario:** You are the Chief Human Resources Officer (CHRO) at a mid‑sized professional services firm. Your company's annual engagement survey shows a concerning trend: voluntary turnover has increased by 12% over the past year, and employee engagement scores have dropped in critical areas like "career growth," "recognition," and "work‑life balance." Exit interviews reveal that people are leaving for better growth opportunities and more flexible work arrangements. The CEO has asked you to lead a Talent Retention Task Force and develop a comprehensive strategy to improve retention and engagement. You are now presenting the strategy to the executive committee (CEO, CFO, COO, and department heads) to secure approval and funding.

---

## Dialogue

| **Speaker** | **Dialogue / Text** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **You** | *(opening the presentation)* Thank you for the time. I'm here to address a critical business challenge: our rising voluntary turnover and declining engagement scores. Our data shows we're losing top talent to competitors who offer clearer growth paths and more flexibility. This is not just an HR issue—it's a business performance issue. |
| **CEO** | What are the numbers? |
| **You** | Voluntary turnover is up 12% year‑over‑year. Our engagement score has dropped from 78% to 71% in 18 months. The top drivers of departure are: **#1**—Limited career growth opportunities (35%). **#2**—Lack of flexible work options (28%). **#3**—Feeling undervalued (22%). |
| **CFO** | What's the cost of this turnover? |
| **You** | We estimate the cost of replacing a single mid‑level professional is 1.5x their annual salary. With 50 voluntary exits last year, that's approximately $3.75M in direct and indirect costs. |
| **COO** | What's your proposed strategy? |
| **You** | *(sharing a slide)* A three‑pillar approach: **#1**—Career Development: We'll create clear career pathways, launch a mentorship program, and invest in leadership development. **#2**—Workplace Flexibility: We'll expand hybrid work options, introduce a compressed workweek pilot, and offer subsidized childcare. **#3**—Recognition and Rewards: We'll implement a peer‑to‑peer recognition platform, introduce spot bonuses, and revise our performance review process to be more frequent and actionable. |
| **Department Head** | What's the investment required? |
| **You** | We're proposing a $1.2M annual investment: $400K for career development programs, $300K for flexibility initiatives, $300K for recognition and rewards, and $200K for program administration and technology. We project a 3x ROI through reduced turnover and increased productivity. |
| **CEO** | What's the timeline? |
| **You** | 12 months, with quarterly milestones: **Phase 1**—Program design and communication (3 months). **Phase 2**—Pilot with a small group (3 months). **Phase 3**—Rollout and training (3 months). **Phase 4**—Evaluation and iteration (3 months). |
| **CFO** | How will we measure success? |
| **You** | Five metrics: **#1**—Voluntary turnover rate. **#2**—Employee engagement score. **#3**—Internal promotion rate. **#4**—Time to fill open roles. **#5**—Manager effectiveness ratings. We'll report progress quarterly. |
| **CEO** | I'm convinced. This is a necessary investment in our people and our future. |
| **You** | Thank you. I'll send a detailed implementation plan with owners, budgets, and timelines by EOD. Let's make our company a place where people want to stay and grow. |
| *(6 months later – progress update)* | |
| **You** | *(to the executive committee)* I'm pleased to report that voluntary turnover has decreased by 8% in the last 6 months. Engagement scores have increased by 5 points. The career development and flexibility programs are receiving positive feedback. |
| **CFO** | The ROI is tracking ahead of projections. |
| **You** | We're on track. Next quarter, we'll expand the programs to all departments and continue to refine based on feedback. |

## Vocabulary

| **Word / Phrase** | **Part of Speech** | **Meaning** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Talent retention** | Noun Phrase | The ability to keep skilled employees. |
| **Employee engagement** | Noun Phrase | The emotional commitment employees feel to their work. |
| **Voluntary turnover** | Noun Phrase | Employees leaving of their own accord. |
| **Exit interviews** | Noun Phrase | Conversations with departing employees to understand their reasons. |
| **Career pathways** | Noun Phrase | Clear routes for advancement within the organization. |
| **Mentorship program** | Noun Phrase | A system where experienced employees guide others. |
| **Hybrid work** | Noun Phrase | A mix of remote and in‑office work. |
| **Compressed workweek** | Noun Phrase | A schedule where employees work longer days but fewer days per week. |
| **Subsidized childcare** | Noun Phrase | Employer‑supported childcare expenses. |
| **Peer‑to‑peer recognition** | Noun Phrase | Employees acknowledging each other's contributions. |
| **Spot bonuses** | Noun Phrase | Immediate, small rewards for exceptional performance. |
| **Performance review** | Noun Phrase | A formal evaluation of an employee's performance. |
| **Actionable** | Adjective | Specific and practical enough to act on. |
| **Program administration** | Noun Phrase | The management and coordination of programs. |
| **Pilot** | Noun | A small‑scale test before a full rollout. |
| **Iteration** | Noun | The process of improving through repeated cycles. |
| **Manager effectiveness** | Noun Phrase | How well managers support their teams. |
| **Time to fill** | Noun Phrase | The duration to hire a new employee. |
| **Internal promotion rate** | Noun Phrase | The percentage of promotions filled from within. |
| **ROI** | Noun | Return on Investment—the financial benefit relative to cost. |
