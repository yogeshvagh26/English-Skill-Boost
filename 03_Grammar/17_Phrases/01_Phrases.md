# Phase 17: Phrases

Welcome to Phase 17! You've mastered **verbs**, **nouns**, **pronouns**, **adjectives**, **adverbs**, **articles & determiners**, **prepositions**, **conjunctions**, **modals**, **voice**, **narration**, **conditionals**, **questions**, and **clauses**. Now it's time to explore **phrases** — groups of words that work together as a single unit but do not contain a subject-verb pair.

Think of it this way: *A clause has a subject and a verb. A phrase does not. Phrases are the "building blocks" within sentences. They add detail, description, and complexity to your language. "The tall man" (Noun Phrase), "is running" (Verb Phrase), "very quickly" (Adverb Phrase). Understanding phrases will elevate your writing and speaking to a higher level.*

## Topic Overview: Types of Phrases

| Type | Definition | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Noun Phrase** | Acts as a noun | **The tall man** is here. |
| **Verb Phrase** | Acts as a verb | She **has been working** hard. |
| **Adjective Phrase** | Modifies a noun | The man **with the red hat** is here. |
| **Adverb Phrase** | Modifies a verb/adjective/adverb | She runs **very quickly**. |
| **Prepositional Phrase** | Shows relationship | The book is **on the table**. |
| **Participial Phrase** | Acts as an adjective | **Running quickly**, she caught the bus. |
| **Infinitive Phrase** | Acts as noun/adjective/adverb | I want **to learn English**. |
| **Gerund Phrase** | Acts as a noun | **Running every day** is healthy. |

## 1. Noun Phrase

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **noun phrase** is a group of words that functions as a noun in a sentence. It includes a noun and its modifiers (determiners, adjectives, prepositional phrases, etc.).

**Key Points:**
- Acts as a noun (subject, object, complement)
- Contains a noun (head) and its modifiers
- Can be short or long

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Determiner + Noun**
```
[DETERMINER] + [NOUN]
the + cat
my + book
```

**Structure 2: Determiner + Adjective + Noun**
```
[DETERMINER] + [ADJECTIVE] + [NOUN]
the + big + cat
my + favorite + book
```

**Structure 3: Determiner + Adjective + Noun + Prepositional Phrase**
```
[DETERMINER] + [ADJECTIVE] + [NOUN] + [PREPOSITIONAL PHRASE]
the + big + cat + on the mat
my + favorite + book + on the shelf
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Noun Phrase as Subject:**
- "**The tall man** is here."
- "**My favorite book** is on the table."
- "**The big cat on the mat** is sleeping."
- "**The woman in the red dress** is my sister."

**Noun Phrase as Object:**
- "I saw **the tall man**."
- "She bought **a beautiful new car**."
- "I read **the book on the shelf**."
- "She met **the man with the blue hat**."

**Noun Phrase as Complement:**
- "She is **a doctor**."
- "He became **the best student**."
- "My favorite hobby is **reading books**."
- "The winner is **the woman in the red dress**."

**Noun Phrase as Object of Preposition:**
- "I sat on **the big red chair**."
- "She walked to **the new store on Main Street**."
- "I talked to **the woman with the blue hat**."

**Complex Noun Phrases:**
- "**The extremely tall man with the black hat** is my uncle."
- "**My favorite book that I read last summer** is on the shelf."
- "**The beautiful old house on the corner** was built in 1920."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| The tall man he is here. | **The tall man** is here. |
| She is doctor. | She is **a doctor**. |
| I read book. | I read **a book**. |
| The woman red dress is my sister. | **The woman in the red dress** is my sister. |

## 2. Verb Phrase

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **verb phrase** is a group of words that functions as a verb in a sentence. It includes the main verb and its helping (auxiliary) verbs.

**Key Points:**
- Acts as a verb (predicate)
- Contains main verb + auxiliary verbs
- Shows tense, mood, voice

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Main Verb Only**
```
[MAIN VERB]
runs
eats
```

**Structure 2: Auxiliary + Main Verb**
```
[AUXILIARY] + [MAIN VERB]
is + running
has + eaten
```

**Structure 3: Multiple Auxiliaries + Main Verb**
```
[AUX 1] + [AUX 2] + [MAIN VERB]
has + been + running
will + have + finished
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Simple Verb Phrase:**
- "She **runs** every day."
- "They **eat** breakfast at 7 AM."
- "He **sings** beautifully."
- "We **work** hard."

**Verb Phrase with Helping Verbs:**
- "She **is running** right now."
- "They **have eaten** dinner."
- "He **will arrive** soon."
- "We **are working** hard."
- "She **has been working** all day."
- "They **will have finished** by 5 PM."
- "He **had been waiting** for hours."
- "We **should have left** earlier."

**Verb Phrase in Questions:**
- "**Is** she **coming**?"
- "**Have** you **finished**?"
- "**Will** they **arrive** soon?"
- "**Can** you **help** me?"

**Verb Phrase in Negatives:**
- "She **is not coming**."
- "They **haven't eaten** yet."
- "He **won't arrive** on time."
- "We **shouldn't have left** so early."

**Verb Phrase in Passive Voice:**
- "The book **was written** by her."
- "The house **was built** in 2020."
- "The car **was stolen** last night."
- "The letter **has been sent**."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She is run. | She **is running**. |
| They have eat. | They **have eaten**. |
| He will arrives. | He **will arrive**. |
| She has been work. | She **has been working**. |

## 3. Adjective Phrase

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** An **adjective phrase** is a group of words that functions as an adjective, modifying a noun or pronoun. It includes an adjective and its modifiers.

**Key Points:**
- Modifies a noun or pronoun
- Contains an adjective and modifiers
- Can come before or after the noun

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Adjective + Modifier**
```
[MODIFIER] + [ADJECTIVE]
very + beautiful
extremely + tall
```

**Structure 2: Adjective + Prepositional Phrase**
```
[ADJECTIVE] + [PREPOSITIONAL PHRASE]
happy + about the news
proud + of her son
```

**Structure 3: Prepositional Phrase as Adjective**
```
[PREPOSITIONAL PHRASE]
on the table
with the red hat
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Adjective Phrase Before Noun:**
- "**The very beautiful** garden is large."
- "**An extremely tall** man entered."
- "**A surprisingly easy** exam was given."
- "**My really good** friend lives here."

**Adjective Phrase After Noun:**
- "The garden, **very beautiful**, is large."
- "The man, **extremely tall**, entered."
- "The exam, **surprisingly easy**, was given."
- "My friend, **really good**, lives here."

**Adjective Phrase with Preposition:**
- "She is **happy about the news**."
- "He is **proud of his son**."
- "I am **tired of waiting**."
- "She is **interested in art**."

**Prepositional Phrase as Adjective:**
- "The book **on the table** is mine."
- "The woman **with the red hat** is my sister."
- "The house **at the end of the street** is empty."
- "The man **in the blue suit** is a doctor."

**Adjective Phrase with "To + Infinitive":**
- "She is **happy to see you**."
- "He is **eager to learn**."
- "I am **ready to go**."
- "We are **excited to start**."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She is happy of the news. | She is **happy about** the news. |
| He is proud for his son. | He is **proud of** his son. |
| I am tired for waiting. | I am **tired of** waiting. |
| She is interested for art. | She is **interested in** art. |

## 4. Adverb Phrase

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** An **adverb phrase** is a group of words that functions as an adverb, modifying a verb, adjective, or another adverb. It includes an adverb and its modifiers.

**Key Points:**
- Modifies a verb, adjective, or adverb
- Contains an adverb and modifiers
- Answers: how, when, where, to what extent

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Adverb + Modifier**
```
[MODIFIER] + [ADVERB]
very + quickly
quite + well
```

**Structure 2: Adverb + Prepositional Phrase**
```
[ADVERB] + [PREPOSITIONAL PHRASE]
carefully + with great attention
slowly + without hurry
```

**Structure 3: Prepositional Phrase as Adverb**
```
[PREPOSITIONAL PHRASE]
in the morning
with great care
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Adverb Phrase Modifying Verb:**
- "She runs **very quickly**."
- "He speaks **quite clearly**."
- "They arrived **very late**."
- "She works **extremely hard**."

**Adverb Phrase Modifying Adjective:**
- "She is **very beautiful**."
- "It is **extremely hot**."
- "He is **quite intelligent**."
- "The food is **really good**."

**Adverb Phrase Modifying Adverb:**
- "She runs **very quickly**."
- "He speaks **quite clearly**."
- "They arrived **very soon**."
- "She works **very hard**."

**Prepositional Phrase as Adverb:**
- "I will call you **in the morning**." (when)
- "She walked **to the store**." (where)
- "He opened the door **with great care**." (how)
- "I was born **in 1990**." (when)

**Adverb Phrase of Frequency:**
- "I go to the gym **three times a week**."
- "She calls her mother **every day**."
- "He visits his parents **once a month**."
- "They meet **every Friday**."

**Adverb Phrase of Manner:**
- "She spoke **with great confidence**."
- "He walked **in a hurry**."
- "They talked **in a friendly manner**."
- "She looked at me **with a smile**."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She runs quick. | She runs **very quickly**. |
| He speaks good. | He speaks **very well**. |
| I will call you on the morning. | I will call you **in the morning**. |
| She walked to store. | She walked **to the store**. |

## 5. Prepositional Phrase

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **prepositional phrase** is a group of words that begins with a preposition and ends with a noun or pronoun (the object of the preposition). It functions as an adjective or adverb.

**Key Points:**
- Begins with a preposition
- Ends with a noun/pronoun (object)
- Functions as adjective or adverb

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Preposition + Noun**
```
[PREPOSITION] + [NOUN]
on + the table
in + the room
```

**Structure 2: Preposition + Determiner + Noun**
```
[PREPOSITION] + [DETERMINER] + [NOUN]
on + the + table
in + my + room
```

**Structure 3: Preposition + Determiner + Adjective + Noun**
```
[PREPOSITION] + [DETERMINER] + [ADJECTIVE] + [NOUN]
on + the + big + table
in + my + small + room
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Prepositional Phrase as Adjective (Modifying Noun):**
- "The book **on the table** is mine."
- "The woman **in the red dress** is my sister."
- "The house **at the end of the street** is empty."
- "The man **with the blue hat** is a doctor."

**Prepositional Phrase as Adverb (Modifying Verb):**
- "I will call you **in the morning**."
- "She walked **to the store**."
- "He opened the door **with great care**."
- "I was born **in 1990**."

**Prepositional Phrase with Multiple Prepositions:**
- "The house **at the end of the street** is empty."
- "The book **on the top shelf of the bookcase** is mine."
- "She walked **from the house to the store**."
- "I sat **on the chair near the window**."

**Common Prepositional Phrases:**

| Phrase | Example |
|--------|---------|
| **in the morning** | I work **in the morning**. |
| **at the end** | She is **at the end**. |
| **on the table** | The book is **on the table**. |
| **by the way** | **By the way**, I like pizza. |
| **in addition** | **In addition**, I need more time. |
| **for example** | **For example**, I like coffee. |
| **on the other hand** | **On the other hand**, I prefer tea. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| The book is on table. | The book is **on the** table. |
| She walked to store. | She walked **to the** store. |
| I work in the morning. (✓) | I work **in the morning**. |
| I will call you on the morning. | I will call you **in the morning**. |

## 6. Participial Phrase

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **participial phrase** is a group of words that contains a participle (present or past) and functions as an adjective. It modifies a noun or pronoun.

**Key Points:**
- Contains a participle (V4 or V3)
- Functions as an adjective
- Can be present or past

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Present Participial Phrase**
```
[PRESENT PARTICIPLE] + [OBJECT/MODIFIER] + [,] + [MAIN CLAUSE]
Running + quickly, + she caught the bus.
Feeling + tired, + I went to bed.
```

**Structure 2: Past Participial Phrase**
```
[PAST PARTICIPLE] + [OBJECT/MODIFIER] + [,] + [MAIN CLAUSE]
Broken + by the storm, + the tree fell.
Written + in 1950, + the book is popular.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Present Participial Phrase:**
- "**Running quickly**, she caught the bus."
- "**Feeling tired**, I went to bed."
- "**Looking out the window**, she saw a beautiful sunset."
- "**Hearing the news**, I immediately called her."
- "**Feeling nervous**, she took a deep breath."

**Past Participial Phrase:**
- "**Broken by the storm**, the tree fell."
- "**Written in 1950**, the book is still popular."
- "**Confused by the question**, he remained silent."
- "**Exhausted from the journey**, she went straight to bed."
- "**Inspired by her mentor**, she decided to become a teacher."

**Participial Phrase in Middle of Sentence:**
- "The woman, **wearing a red dress**, is my sister."
- "The man, **sitting on the bench**, is a doctor."
- "The car, **damaged in the accident**, was towed away."
- "The book, **written by a famous author**, is popular."

**Participial Phrase at End of Sentence:**
- "She caught the bus, **running quickly**."
- "I went to bed, **feeling tired**."
- "He sat down, **exhausted from the journey**."
- "She called me, **hearing the news**."

**Dangling Participles (Common Error):**
- ❌ "**Walking to the store**, the rain started." (Who was walking?)
- ✅ "**Walking to the store**, I felt the rain start."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Running to the store, the rain started. (dangling) | **While I was** running to the store, the rain started. |
| Written in 1950, the book is popular. (✓) | **Written in 1950**, the book is popular. |
| Feeling tired, I went to bed. (✓) | **Feeling tired**, I went to bed. |
| Broken by the storm, the tree fell. (✓) | **Broken by the storm**, the tree fell. |

## 7. Infinitive Phrase

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** An **infinitive phrase** is a group of words that contains an infinitive (to + base verb) and functions as a noun, adjective, or adverb.

**Key Points:**
- Contains "to + base verb"
- Functions as noun, adjective, or adverb
- Can be short or long

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Infinitive + Object**
```
[INFINITIVE] + [OBJECT]
to + read + a book
to + eat + dinner
```

**Structure 2: Infinitive + Modifier + Object**
```
[INFINITIVE] + [MODIFIER] + [OBJECT]
to + read + quickly + a book
to + eat + slowly + dinner
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Infinitive Phrase as Noun (Subject):**
- "**To learn a language** takes time."
- "**To travel the world** is my dream."
- "**To succeed in life** requires hard work."
- "**To understand** is to forgive."

**Infinitive Phrase as Noun (Object):**
- "I want **to learn English**."
- "She hopes **to become a doctor**."
- "They decided **to leave early**."
- "I need **to finish this work**."

**Infinitive Phrase as Noun (Complement):**
- "My goal is **to become a teacher**."
- "Her dream is **to travel the world**."
- "The plan is **to leave at 6 AM**."
- "His job is **to manage the team**."

**Infinitive Phrase as Adjective:**
- "I have a book **to read**."
- "She has a lot of work **to do**."
- "Is there anything **to eat**?"
- "I need someone **to help** me."

**Infinitive Phrase as Adverb (Purpose):**
- "I went to the store **to buy milk**."
- "She studied hard **to pass the exam**."
- "He called **to confirm the reservation**."
- "I saved money **to travel abroad**."

**Infinitive Phrase as Adverb (Reason):**
- "I was happy **to see her**."
- "She was surprised **to hear the news**."
- "We were lucky **to find a seat**."
- "He was eager **to begin the project**."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I want learn English. | I want **to learn** English. |
| To learn a language takes time. (✓) | **To learn a language** takes time. |
| I have a book read. | I have a book **to read**. |
| I went to store buy milk. | I went to the store **to buy** milk. |

## 8. Gerund Phrase

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **gerund phrase** is a group of words that contains a gerund (-ing verb) and functions as a noun in a sentence.

**Key Points:**
- Contains a gerund (-ing verb)
- Functions as a noun
- Can be subject, object, or complement

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Gerund + Object**
```
[GERUND] + [OBJECT]
reading + a book
eating + dinner
```

**Structure 2: Gerund + Modifier + Object**
```
[GERUND] + [MODIFIER] + [OBJECT]
reading + quickly + a book
eating + slowly + dinner
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Gerund Phrase as Subject:**
- "**Reading books** is my favorite hobby."
- "**Running every morning** keeps me healthy."
- "**Learning English** takes practice."
- "**Traveling the world** is my dream."
- "**Eating healthy food** is important."

**Gerund Phrase as Object:**
- "I enjoy **reading books**."
- "She loves **playing the piano**."
- "He hates **waking up early**."
- "They suggested **going to the beach**."
- "I recommend **visiting the museum**."

**Gerund Phrase as Complement:**
- "My hobby is **reading books**."
- "Her passion is **playing the piano**."
- "His favorite activity is **playing chess**."
- "The best thing is **spending time with family**."

**Gerund Phrase as Object of Preposition:**
- "She is good at **playing the piano**."
- "I'm interested in **learning French**."
- "He apologized for **being late**."
- "I'm looking forward to **seeing you**."
- "We succeeded in **completing the project**."

**Gerund Phrase with "Go + Gerund":**
- "Let's go **shopping**."
- "We went **swimming** yesterday."
- "She goes **running** every morning."
- "They went **hiking** in the mountains."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Read books is my hobby. | **Reading books** is my hobby. |
| I enjoy to read books. | I enjoy **reading books**. |
| She is good to play the piano. | She is good **at playing** the piano. |
| I'm interested to learn French. | I'm interested **in learning** French. |

| **Adverb Phrase** | Adverb | Adverb + modifiers | She runs **very quickly**. |
