# Topic: Managing a Digital Ethics / AI Governance Board Discussion

**Scenario:** You are the Chief Ethics & Compliance Officer at a global financial services firm. Your company has been rapidly adopting AI across customer service, credit scoring, and fraud detection. However, recent public scrutiny of AI bias and ethical risks has prompted the board to request a formal review of your AI governance practices. You are now presenting the AI governance framework to the board's Digital Ethics Committee (comprising the CEO, CTO, Head of Risk, and two independent directors). Your goal is to: **explain** the AI governance framework, **address** risks (bias, privacy, transparency), **showcase** current safeguards and controls, **propose** a continuous monitoring and review process, **respond** to concerns about innovation vs. ethics trade‑offs, and **secure** the committee's approval to move forward.

---

## Dialogue

| **Speaker** | **Dialogue / Text** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **You** | *(opening the presentation)* Thank you for the time. AI is becoming central to our operations, and with that comes significant ethical and regulatory risks. I'm here to present our AI governance framework—a set of principles, processes, and controls to ensure our AI systems are fair, transparent, and accountable. |
| **CEO** | What are the key risks we need to manage? |
| **You** | Three main risks: **#1**—Algorithmic bias (unfair outcomes for certain groups). **#2**—Privacy (handling of sensitive customer data). **#3**—Explainability (the ability to understand how decisions are made). Each of these carries regulatory, reputational, and legal exposure. |
| **Independent Director** | How do we ensure our AI is fair and unbiased? |
| **You** | We've implemented a "bias audit" process—every new AI model is tested for fairness across demographic groups before deployment. We also have an independent ethics review board that includes external experts. |
| **CTO** | What about transparency? Some of our models are "black boxes"—even we don't fully understand how they arrive at decisions. |
| **You** | We're investing in explainable AI (XAI) techniques—tools that help us interpret model decisions. We've also set a policy that any high‑risk AI (e.g., credit scoring) must be explainable. If it can't be, we don't deploy it. |
| **Head of Risk** | And what about regulatory compliance? The EU's AI Act is coming. |
| **You** | We've mapped our AI portfolio against the EU AI Act's risk tiers. We're already compliant with the highest‑risk requirements. We'll continue to monitor evolving regulations and adapt. |
| **Independent Director** | How do we balance innovation with ethics? We don't want to slow down our AI initiatives. |
| **You** | We're integrating ethics into the design process—not as a gatekeeper, but as a partner. We've set up an "AI ethics accelerator" that helps teams design ethical AI from the start, reducing the need for last‑minute fixes. |
| **CEO** | What's the governance structure? |
| **You** | Three layers: **#1**—Executive oversight (this committee). **#2**—An AI governance board (cross‑functional leaders). **#3**—Operational working groups (implementing and monitoring). We'll report on AI governance quarterly. |
| **CTO** | I'm comfortable with the framework. It's robust and practical. |
| **CEO** | Let's approve it and move forward with implementation. |
| **You** | Thank you. I'll send the detailed governance policy, the risk assessment, and the implementation plan by EOD. We'll also schedule a quarterly review with the committee. |
| *(3 months later – follow‑up)* | |
| **You** | *(to the committee)* The AI governance framework is now operational. We've completed bias audits on all high‑risk models, established the ethics review board, and launched the explainability initiative. Early feedback is positive—teams appreciate the clarity and support. |
| **CEO** | This has strengthened our reputation as a responsible AI leader. |
| **You** | That's the goal—innovation with integrity. We'll continue to evolve the framework as technology and regulations change. |

## Vocabulary

| **Word / Phrase** | **Part of Speech** | **Meaning** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Digital ethics** | Noun Phrase | The application of moral principles to digital technology. |
| **AI governance** | Noun Phrase | The framework of rules and practices for responsible AI development. |
| **Algorithmic bias** | Noun Phrase | Systematic and unfair discrimination in AI systems. |
| **Explainable AI (XAI)** | Noun | AI systems that can be understood and interpreted by humans. |
| **Black box** | Noun Phrase | A system whose internal workings are opaque. |
| **Bias audit** | Noun Phrase | A process to detect and measure bias in AI models. |
| **Ethics review board** | Noun Phrase | A group that assesses the ethical implications of AI projects. |
| **EU AI Act** | Noun | Proposed EU regulation for AI systems based on risk tiers. |
| **High‑risk AI** | Noun Phrase | AI systems that pose significant risks to safety or fundamental rights. |
| **Explainability** | Noun | The ability to understand how a model makes decisions. |
| **Regulatory exposure** | Noun Phrase | The risk of non‑compliance with laws and regulations. |
| **Reputational risk** | Noun Phrase | The potential damage to a company's public image. |
| **Accountability** | Noun | The obligation to take responsibility for AI outcomes. |
| **Transparency** | Noun | Openness about how AI systems work. |
| **Ethics accelerator** | Noun Phrase | A program to embed ethics into the design process. |
| **Gatekeeper** | Noun | A person or process that controls access or approval. |
| **Operational working groups** | Noun Phrase | Teams that implement and monitor governance practices. |
| **Quarterly reporting** | Noun Phrase | Providing updates every three months. |
| **Responsible AI** | Noun Phrase | AI developed with fairness, transparency, and accountability. |
| **Integrity** | Noun | The quality of being honest and having strong moral principles. |
