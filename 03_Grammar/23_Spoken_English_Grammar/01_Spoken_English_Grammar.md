# Phase 24: Spoken English Grammar

Welcome to Phase 24 — the penultimate phase of your grammar journey! You've mastered **formal grammar**, **advanced structures**, **punctuation**, and **common errors**. Now it's time to explore **Spoken English Grammar** — the natural, everyday language that native speakers use in conversations.

Think of it this way: *Written English is formal and structured. Spoken English is fluid, natural, and full of shortcuts. You've learned the "textbook" rules. Now it's time to learn how native speakers actually talk — with contractions, short answers, fillers, and natural sentence patterns. This is the key to sounding fluent and natural.*

## 1. Contractions

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Contractions** are shortened forms of words or phrases created by combining words and omitting letters. They are very common in spoken English and informal writing.

**Key Points:**
- Shorten words in speech
- Combine auxiliary verbs with "not"
- Combine pronouns with auxiliary verbs
- Make speech sound natural and fluent

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Pronoun + Auxiliary Verb**
```
[PRONOUN] + [AUXILIARY] → [CONTRACTION]
I + am → I'm
You + are → You're
He + is → He's
We + have → We've
```

**Structure 2: Auxiliary Verb + Not**
```
[AUXILIARY] + [NOT] → [CONTRACTION]
do + not → don't
does + not → doesn't
will + not → won't
cannot → can't
```

**Structure 3: Informal Contractions**
```
[GOING TO] → [GONNA]
going to → gonna

[WANT TO] → [WANNA]
want to → wanna

[HAVE TO] → [HAFTA]
have to → hafta

[GOT TO] → [GOTTA]
got to → gotta
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Common Contractions:**

| Full Form | Contraction | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| I am | I'm | **I'm** happy. |
| You are | You're | **You're** right. |
| He is | He's | **He's** here. |
| She is | She's | **She's** coming. |
| It is | It's | **It's** raining. |
| We are | We're | **We're** going. |
| They are | They're | **They're** here. |
| I have | I've | **I've** seen it. |
| You have | You've | **You've** been. |
| We have | We've | **We've** finished. |
| They have | They've | **They've** left. |
| do not | don't | **Don't** go. |
| does not | doesn't | **Doesn't** she know? |
| did not | didn't | **Didn't** you call? |
| will not | won't | **Won't** you stay? |
| cannot | can't | **Can't** you see? |
| could not | couldn't | **Couldn't** you help? |
| should not | shouldn't | **Shouldn't** you go? |
| would not | wouldn't | **Wouldn't** you agree? |

**Informal Contractions:**

| Full Form | Contraction | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| going to | gonna | I'm **gonna** go. |
| want to | wanna | I **wanna** go. |
| have to | hafta | I **hafta** go. |
| got to | gotta | I **gotta** go. |
| kind of | kinda | It's **kinda** cold. |
| sort of | sorta | I'm **sorta** tired. |
| let me | lemme | **Lemme** see. |
| give me | gimme | **Gimme** a break. |
| don't know | dunno | I **dunno**. |
| out of | outta | Get **outta** here. |

**Contractions in Questions:**
- "**Don't** you like it?"
- "**Doesn't** she know?"
- "**Didn't** you call?"
- "**Can't** you see?"
- "**Won't** you stay?"

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Its raining. | **It's** raining. |
| Your right. | **You're** right. |
| Their coming. | **They're** coming. |
| I should of gone. | I **should've** gone. |
| Could of done. | **Could've** done. |

## 2. Short Answers

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Short answers** are quick, natural responses to yes/no questions. They use an auxiliary verb instead of repeating the full sentence.

**Key Points:**
- Quick responses to questions
- Use auxiliary verb
- Avoid repetition
- Sound natural and fluent

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Short Answer (Positive)**
```
[YES] + [,] + [SUBJECT] + [AUXILIARY]
Yes, + I + do.
Yes, + she + is.
Yes, + we + have.
```

**Structure 2: Short Answer (Negative)**
```
[NO] + [,] + [SUBJECT] + [AUXILIARY] + [NOT]
No, + I + don't.
No, + she + isn't.
No, + we + haven't.
```

**Structure 3: Short Answer with Modal**
```
[YES/NO] + [,] + [SUBJECT] + [MODAL]
Yes, + I + can.
No, + I + can't.
Yes, + she + would.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Short Answers with "Do":**
- Question: "**Do** you like coffee?"
- Answer: "Yes, I **do**." / "No, I **don't**."
- Question: "**Does** she work here?"
- Answer: "Yes, she **does**." / "No, she **doesn't**."
- Question: "**Did** you call her?"
- Answer: "Yes, I **did**." / "No, I **didn't**."

**Short Answers with "Be":**
- Question: "**Are** you ready?"
- Answer: "Yes, I **am**." / "No, I'm **not**."
- Question: "**Is** she coming?"
- Answer: "Yes, she **is**." / "No, she **isn't**."
- Question: "**Were** they there?"
- Answer: "Yes, they **were**." / "No, they **weren't**."

**Short Answers with "Have":**
- Question: "**Have** you finished?"
- Answer: "Yes, I **have**." / "No, I **haven't**."
- Question: "**Has** she called?"
- Answer: "Yes, she **has**." / "No, she **hasn't**."
- Question: "**Had** they arrived?"
- Answer: "Yes, they **had**." / "No, they **hadn't**."

**Short Answers with Modals:**
- Question: "**Can** you swim?"
- Answer: "Yes, I **can**." / "No, I **can't**."
- Question: "**Will** you come?"
- Answer: "Yes, I **will**." / "No, I **won't**."
- Question: "**Should** I go?"
- Answer: "Yes, you **should**." / "No, you **shouldn't**."
- Question: "**Could** you help?"
- Answer: "Yes, I **could**." / "No, I **couldn't**."

**Short Answers with "So" and "Neither":**
- "I like coffee." "**So do I**."
- "I don't like tea." "**Neither do I**."
- "She can swim." "**So can I**."
- "I haven't been there." "**Neither have I**."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Yes, I like. | Yes, I **do**. |
| No, she not. | No, she **doesn't**. |
| Yes, I am. (✓) | Yes, I **am**. |
| No, I don't. (✓) | No, I **don't**. |

## 3. Fillers

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Fillers** are words or sounds used to fill pauses in speech while thinking. They make speech sound more natural and give the speaker time to think.

**Key Points:**
- Fill pauses in speech
- Give time to think
- Sound natural and conversational
- Common in all languages

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Fillers for Pauses**
```
[FILLER] + [MAIN CLAUSE]
Um, + I think she's coming.
Well, + I don't know.
You know, + it's really hard.
```

**Structure 2: Fillers for Emphasis**
```
[FILLER] + [MAIN CLAUSE]
Like, + it's really good.
I mean, + that's what I think.
Actually, + I disagree.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Common Fillers:**

| Filler | Function | Example |
|--------|----------|---------|
| **Um/Uh** | Thinking pause | **Um**, I think so. |
| **Well** | Hesitation | **Well**, I'm not sure. |
| **You know** | Seeking agreement | It's hard, **you know**? |
| **Like** | Emphasis/approximation | It's, **like**, really good. |
| **I mean** | Clarification | **I mean**, that's what I think. |
| **Actually** | Correction | **Actually**, I disagree. |
| **Basically** | Summarizing | **Basically**, it's simple. |
| **Honestly** | Truthfulness | **Honestly**, I don't care. |
| **To be honest** | Truthfulness | **To be honest**, I don't like it. |
| **Anyway** | Returning to topic | **Anyway**, let's move on. |
| **So** | Transition | **So**, what do you think? |
| **Right** | Confirmation | **Right**, I see what you mean. |

**Fillers in Conversations:**
- "**Um**, I'm not sure about that."
- "**Well**, let me think about it."
- "It's really good, **you know**?"
- "It's, **like**, the best thing ever."
- "**I mean**, that's what I would do."
- "**Actually**, I think you're right."
- "**Basically**, it comes down to this."
- "**Honestly**, I don't know."
- "**To be honest**, I don't like it."
- "**Anyway**, we should get going."
- "**So**, what do you think?"
- "**Right**, I see what you mean."

**Fillers in Questions:**
- "**So**, what do you think?"
- "**Like**, do you know what I mean?"
- "**You know**, is that right?"
- "**Well**, what do you want to do?"

**Fillers in Responses:**
- "**Um**, I don't know."
- "**Well**, I'm not sure."
- "**Actually**, I think so."
- "**Basically**, yes."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Overusing fillers | Use them naturally |
| Using "like" too much | Vary with other fillers |
| Not using fillers | Speech sounds robotic |
| Using fillers in formal writing | Avoid in formal writing |

## 4. Natural Sentence Patterns

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Natural sentence patterns** are the common, everyday structures that native speakers use in conversation. They often differ from textbook grammar.

**Key Points:**
- Common in spoken English
- Often informal
- Shorter and more direct
- More natural sounding

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Gotta (Got to = Have to)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [GOTTA] + [VERB]
I + gotta + go.
She + gotta + leave.
```

**Structure 2: Wanna (Want to)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [WANNA] + [VERB]
I + wanna + go.
She + wanna + come.
```

**Structure 3: Gonna (Going to)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [GONNA] + [VERB]
I + gonna + go.
She + gonna + come.
```

**Structure 4: Hafta (Have to)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [HAFTA] + [VERB]
I + hafta + go.
She + hafta + leave.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Gotta (Got to = Have to):**
- "**I gotta go**." (I have to go)
- "**She gotta leave**." (She has to leave)
- "**We gotta study**." (We have to study)
- "**You gotta try this**." (You have to try this)
- "**I gotta get up early**." (I have to get up early)

**Wanna (Want to):**
- "**I wanna go**." (I want to go)
- "**She wanna come**." (She wants to come)
- "**We wanna stay**." (We want to stay)
- "**You wanna try it**?" (Do you want to try it?)
- "**I wanna see you**." (I want to see you)

**Gonna (Going to):**
- "**I'm gonna go**." (I am going to go)
- "**She's gonna come**." (She is going to come)
- "**We're gonna study**." (We are going to study)
- "**It's gonna rain**." (It is going to rain)
- "**I'm gonna be late**." (I am going to be late)

**Hafta (Have to):**
- "**I hafta go**." (I have to go)
- "**She hafta leave**." (She has to leave)
- "**We hafta study**." (We have to study)
- "**You hafta try this**." (You have to try this)

**Other Natural Patterns:**

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **I'm gonna** | I am going to | **I'm gonna** leave. |
| **I gotta** | I have got to | **I gotta** go. |
| **I wanna** | I want to | **I wanna** stay. |
| **I hafta** | I have to | **I hafta** work. |
| **Lemme** | Let me | **Lemme** see. |
| **Gimme** | Give me | **Gimme** that. |
| **Dunno** | Don't know | I **dunno**. |
| **Kinda** | Kind of | It's **kinda** cold. |
| **Sorta** | Sort of | I'm **sorta** tired. |
| **Outta** | Out of | Get **outta** here. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I gonna go. | **I'm** gonna go. |
| She wanna go. | She **wants** to go. |
| We gotta go. (✓) | We **gotta** go. |
| I hafta go. (✓) | I **hafta** go. |

## 5. Everyday Grammar

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Everyday grammar** refers to the grammatical structures used in daily life — at home, at work, in shops, and in social situations.

**Key Points:**
- Used in daily life
- Practical and functional
- Often informal
- Essential for everyday communication

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Making Requests**
```
[CAN/COULD/WOULD] + [YOU] + [VERB] + [REST]
Can + you + help + me?
Could + you + pass + the salt?
Would + you + open + the door?
```

**Structure 2: Making Offers**
```
[CAN/COULD/WOULD] + [I] + [VERB] + [REST]
Can + I + help + you?
Could + I + get + you something?
Would + you + like + some coffee?
```

**Structure 3: Asking for Information**
```
[WH WORD] + [DO/DOES/DID] + [YOU] + [VERB] + [REST]
What + do + you + want?
Where + does + she + live?
When + did + you + arrive?
```

**Structure 4: Expressing Opinions**
```
[I THINK/BELIEVE/FEEL] + [THAT] + [CLAUSE]
I think + that + she is right.
I believe + that + he is honest.
I feel + that + we should go.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Making Requests:**
- "**Can you** help me?"
- "**Could you** pass the salt?"
- "**Would you** open the door?"
- "**Can I** borrow your pen?"
- "**Could I** have some water?"

**Making Offers:**
- "**Can I** help you?"
- "**Could I** get you something?"
- "**Would you like** some coffee?"
- "**Do you want** some help?"
- "**Shall I** open the window?"

**Asking for Information:**
- "**What time** is it?"
- "**Where** is the station?"
- "**How much** does it cost?"
- "**How long** will it take?"
- "**Why** are you late?"

**Expressing Opinions:**
- "**I think** she is right."
- "**I believe** he is honest."
- "**I feel** we should go."
- "**In my opinion**, it's a good idea."
- "**To me**, it doesn't matter."

**Daily Life Phrases:**

| Situation | Phrase |
|-----------|--------|
| **Greeting** | How are you? / How's it going? |
| **Leaving** | I gotta go. / See you later. |
| **Thanking** | Thanks! / Thanks a lot! |
| **Apologizing** | Sorry! / I'm so sorry. |
| **Agreeing** | Yeah. / Right. / That's true. |
| **Disagreeing** | I don't think so. / Not really. |
| **Asking for help** | Can you help me? / I need a hand. |
| **Making plans** | What do you wanna do? / Let's go. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| What time it is? | **What time** is it? |
| Where she lives? | **Where does** she live? |
| Why you are late? | **Why are** you late? |
| I think she is right. (✓) | **I think** she is right. |

## 6. Conversational Grammar

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Conversational grammar** refers to the grammatical rules and patterns used in informal, spoken English. It includes many features that are different from formal written English.

**Key Points:**
- Informal spoken English
- Often breaks formal rules
- Includes slang and colloquialisms
- Varies by region and culture

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: "Ain't" (Am not / Are not / Is not / Has not / Have not)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [AIN'T] + [REST]
I + ain't + going.
She + ain't + here.
They + ain't + ready.
```

**Structure 2: Double Negatives (Informal)**
```
[SUBJECT] + [AIN'T] + [NO/NOTHING/NOBODY]
I + ain't + got no money.
She + didn't + see nobody.
```

**Structure 3: Dropped "That"**
```
[VERB] + [(THAT)] + [CLAUSE]
I think + (that) + she's right.
I know + (that) + he's coming.
```

**Structure 4: Dropped Prepositions**
```
[WH WORD] + [PREPOSITION]
Where are you + (at)?
What are you looking + (at)?
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**"Ain't" (Informal):**
- "**I ain't** going." (I'm not going)
- "**She ain't** here." (She isn't here)
- "**They ain't** ready." (They aren't ready)
- "**I ain't** got no money." (I haven't got any money)
- "**That ain't** fair." (That isn't fair)

**Dropped "That" (Informal):**
- "I think **she's right**." (I think that she's right)
- "I know **he's coming**." (I know that he's coming)
- "She said **she would**." (She said that she would)
- "I believe **it's true**." (I believe that it's true)

**Dropped Prepositions (Informal):**
- "**Where** are you **at**?" (Where are you?)
- "**What** are you looking **at**?" (What are you looking at?)
- "**Who** did you go **with**?" (Who did you go with?)
- "**Where** did you go **to**?" (Where did you go?)

**Slang and Colloquialisms:**
- "**Gonna**" (going to): "I'm gonna go."
- "**Wanna**" (want to): "I wanna go."
- "**Gotta**" (got to): "I gotta go."
- "**Hafta**" (have to): "I hafta go."
- "**Kinda**" (kind of): "It's kinda cold."
- "**Sorta**" (sort of): "I'm sorta tired."
- "**Lemme**" (let me): "Lemme see."
- "**Gimme**" (give me): "Gimme that."
- "**Dunno**" (don't know): "I dunno."
- "**Outta**" (out of): "Get outta here."

**Regional Variations (Informal):**

| Region | Example |
|--------|---------|
| **American** | "How's it going?" / "What's up?" |
| **British** | "Alright?" / "How do you do?" |
| **Australian** | "G'day mate!" / "How ya goin'?" |
| **American** | "I could care less." (meaning: I couldn't care less) |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Using slang in formal writing | Use formal language |
| Using ain't in formal speech | Use formal contractions |
| Overusing double negatives | Use single negatives |
| Dropping words in formal writing | Write full forms |

- B: "**Sure**. **What time**?"

- A: "**Okay**. **Lemme know**."
