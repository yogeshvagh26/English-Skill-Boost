## 1. Full Stop (.)

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** The **full stop** (period in American English) is used to end declarative sentences, imperative sentences, and abbreviations.

**Key Points:**
- Ends declarative sentences (statements)
- Ends imperative sentences (commands)
- Used in abbreviations
- Indicates a complete thought

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Full Stop at End of Statement**
```
[STATEMENT] + [.]
I am happy.
She works hard.
```

**Structure 2: Full Stop at End of Command**
```
[COMMAND] + [.]
Sit down.
Close the door.
```

**Structure 3: Full Stop in Abbreviations**
```
[ABBREVIATION] + [.]
Dr. (Doctor), etc. (et cetera), e.g. (for example)
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Ending Statements:**
- "I am happy."
- "She works hard."
- "The sun rises in the east."
- "I will call you tomorrow."
- "This is the best book I have ever read."

**Ending Commands:**
- "Sit down."
- "Close the door."
- "Please pass the salt."
- "Wait for me."
- "Take care of yourself."

**Abbreviations:**
- "Dr. Smith is here." (Doctor)
- "I need milk, eggs, etc." (et cetera)
- "For example, e.g., apples and oranges."
- "I will arrive at 5 p.m." (post meridiem)

**Full Stop in Numbers:**
- "1,000,000" (one million)
- "3.14" (pi)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I am happy (no period) | I am **happy.** |
| She works hard? (wrong mark) | She works **hard.** |
| Dr Smith (missing period) | **Dr.** Smith |
| I like apples, oranges. | I like apples and **oranges.** |

## 2. Comma (,)

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** The **comma** is used to separate elements within a sentence. It indicates a pause and helps clarify meaning.

**Key Points:**
- Separates items in a list
- Separates independent clauses with conjunctions
- Sets off introductory elements
- Sets off non-essential information

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Comma in a List**
```
[ITEM 1] + [,] + [ITEM 2] + [,] + [AND/OR] + [ITEM 3]
I like coffee, tea, and juice.
```

**Structure 2: Comma with Conjunctions**
```
[CLAUSE 1] + [,] + [CONJUNCTION] + [CLAUSE 2]
I like coffee, and she likes tea.
```

**Structure 3: Comma after Introductory Element**
```
[INTRODUCTORY ELEMENT] + [,] + [MAIN CLAUSE]
However, I will go.
Before I leave, I need to call you.
```

**Structure 4: Comma with Non-Essential Information**
```
[MAIN CLAUSE] + [,] + [NON-ESSENTIAL] + [,] + [REST]
My brother, who lives in London, is a doctor.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Comma in a List (Oxford Comma):**
- "I like **coffee, tea, and juice**." (with Oxford comma)
- "I like **coffee, tea and juice**." (without Oxford comma)
- "She bought **milk, bread, and eggs**."
- "We need **pens, paper, and scissors**."

**Comma with Conjunctions:**
- "I like coffee, **and** she likes tea."
- "I wanted to go, **but** I was tired."
- "She was tired, **so** she went to bed."
- "He studied hard, **and** he passed the exam."

**Comma after Introductory Elements:**
- "**However**, I will go."
- "**Before I leave**, I need to call you."
- "**After the meeting**, we can talk."
- "**To be honest**, I don't like it."
- "**In the morning**, I drink coffee."

**Comma with Non-Essential Information:**
- "My brother, **who lives in London**, is a doctor."
- "The book, **which I read**, is great."
- "My mother, **who is a teacher**, loves her job."
- "This car, **which I bought yesterday**, is red."

**Comma with Direct Address:**
- "**Sarah**, please come here."
- "Please come here, **Sarah**."
- "**John**, can you help me?"

**Comma with Dates and Addresses:**
- "July 4, 2026"
- "I was born on **June 5, 1990**."
- "She lives at **123 Main Street, London, England**."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I like coffee tea and juice. | I like coffee, tea, and juice. |
| I like coffee, and tea. (no clause) | I like coffee and tea. |
| Before I leave I need to call you. | Before I leave, I need to call you. |
| My brother who lives in London is a doctor. | My brother, who lives in London, is a doctor. |
| I went to the store, and I bought milk. (✓) | I went to the store, and I bought milk. |

## 3. Semicolon (;)

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** The **semicolon** is used to join two closely related independent clauses without a conjunction. It is stronger than a comma but weaker than a period.

**Key Points:**
- Joins related independent clauses
- Separates items in a complex list
- Indicates a close relationship between ideas

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Semicolon Joining Clauses**
```
[CLAUSE 1] + [;] + [CLAUSE 2]
I like coffee; she likes tea.
```

**Structure 2: Semicolon with Conjunctive Adverb**
```
[CLAUSE 1] + [;] + [CONJUNCTIVE ADVERB] + [,] + [CLAUSE 2]
I like coffee; however, she likes tea.
```

**Structure 3: Semicolon in Complex Lists**
```
[ITEM 1] + [;] + [ITEM 2] + [;] + [ITEM 3]
I visited London, England; Paris, France; and Rome, Italy.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Semicolon Joining Clauses:**
- "I like coffee; she likes tea."
- "I wanted to go; I was too tired."
- "She studied hard; she passed the exam."
- "The sun was setting; the birds were singing."

**Semicolon with Conjunctive Adverbs:**
- "I like coffee; **however**, she likes tea."
- "I wanted to go; **however**, I was too tired."
- "She studied hard; **therefore**, she passed the exam."
- "He was late; **as a result**, he missed the meeting."
- "I don't like it; **nevertheless**, I will do it."

**Semicolon in Complex Lists:**
- "I visited London, England; Paris, France; and Rome, Italy."
- "The conference will be held in New York, NY; Los Angeles, CA; and Chicago, IL."
- "We need chairs from the office; tables from the storage; and food from the kitchen."

**Semicolon vs. Comma:**
- "I like coffee, tea, and juice." (comma in simple list)
- "I visited London, England; Paris, France; and Rome, Italy." (semicolon in complex list)

**Semicolon vs. Period:**
- "I like coffee. She likes tea." (period = separate sentences)
- "I like coffee; she likes tea." (semicolon = related clauses)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I like coffee; and she likes tea. | I like coffee; she likes tea. |
| I like coffee, she likes tea. (comma splice) | I like coffee; she likes tea. |
| I like coffee; I like tea. (✓) | I like coffee; I like tea. |
| I like coffee; however, she likes tea. (✓) | I like coffee; however, she likes tea. |

## 4. Colon (:)

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** The **colon** is used to introduce a list, explanation, or quotation. It signals that what follows is directly related to what precedes it.

**Key Points:**
- Introduces a list
- Introduces an explanation
- Introduces a quotation
- Follows a complete sentence

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Colon Introducing a List**
```
[COMPLETE SENTENCE] + [:] + [LIST]
I need: milk, bread, and eggs.
She bought: apples, oranges, and bananas.
```

**Structure 2: Colon Introducing an Explanation**
```
[COMPLETE SENTENCE] + [:] + [EXPLANATION]
I have one goal: to become a doctor.
She had one wish: to travel the world.
```

**Structure 3: Colon Introducing a Quotation**
```
[COMPLETE SENTENCE] + [:] + [QUOTATION]
He said: "I will come."
She said: "I am happy."
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Colon Introducing a List:**
- "I need **: milk, bread, and eggs**."
- "She bought **: apples, oranges, and bananas**."
- "The recipe calls for **: flour, sugar, and eggs**."
- "We visited **: London, Paris, and Rome**."

**Colon Introducing an Explanation:**
- "I have one goal **: to become a doctor**."
- "She had one wish **: to travel the world**."
- "The truth is clear **: we need to work harder**."
- "There is one thing you need **: patience**."

**Colon Introducing a Quotation:**
- "He said **: 'I will come.'** "
- "She said **: 'I am happy.'** "
- "The teacher said **: 'Study hard.'** "

**Colon vs. Semicolon:**
- "I have one goal: to become a doctor." (colon = explanation)
- "I want to become a doctor; I have studied hard." (semicolon = related clauses)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| I need: milk, bread and eggs. (✓) | I need: milk, bread, and eggs. |
| I need milk, bread, and eggs. (no colon) | I need: milk, bread, and eggs. |
| I have one goal: to become a doctor. (✓) | I have one goal: to become a doctor. |

## 5. Apostrophe (')

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** The **apostrophe** is used to show possession and to form contractions. It indicates that letters have been omitted.

**Key Points:**
- Shows possession (Sarah's book)
- Forms contractions (don't, can't)
- Indicates omission of letters

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Apostrophe for Possession (Singular)**
```
[SINGULAR NOUN] + ['S] + [OBJECT]
Sarah + 's + book
The dog + 's + tail
```

**Structure 2: Apostrophe for Possession (Plural)**
```
[PLURAL NOUN ENDING IN S] + ['] + [OBJECT]
The students + ' + books
The dogs + ' + tails
```

**Structure 3: Apostrophe for Possession (Irregular Plural)**
```
[IRREGULAR PLURAL] + ['S] + [OBJECT]
The children + 's + toys
The men + 's + club
```

**Structure 4: Apostrophe for Contractions**
```
[AUXILIARY] + [NOT] → [CONTRACTION]
do + not → don't
can + not → can't
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Possession (Singular):**
- "**Sarah's** book is on the table."
- "**The dog's** tail is wagging."
- "**My mother's** house is beautiful."
- "**James's** car is new." (or James')

**Possession (Plural):**
- "**The students'** books are on the desk."
- "**The dogs'** tails are wagging."
- "**The girls'** bags are in the locker."
- "**The cats'** toys are everywhere."

**Possession (Irregular Plural):**
- "**The children's** playground is outside."
- "**The men's** club is on the next street."
- "**The women's** team won the game."
- "**The people's** choice was clear."

**Contractions:**
- "**Don't** go." (do not)
- "**Can't** you see?" (cannot)
- "I **won't** go." (will not)
- "She **doesn't** like it." (does not)
- "**It's** raining." (it is)
- "I **haven't** finished." (have not)

**Possession vs. Contraction:**
- "**Its** tail is long." (possession)
- "**It's** raining." (it is)
- "**Whose** book is this?" (possession)
- "**Who's** coming?" (who is)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| The students books are on the desk. | The **students'** books are on the desk. |
| The student's are here. (contraction) | The students **are** here. |
| Its raining. | **It's** raining. |
| The dog wagged it's tail. | The dog wagged **its** tail. |
| I dont like it. | I **don't** like it. |

## 6. Quotation Marks (" ")

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Quotation marks** are used to indicate direct speech, quotations, and titles of short works.

**Key Points:**
- Indicate direct speech
- Indicate quotations
- Indicate titles of short works

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Direct Speech**
```
[REPORTING VERB] + [,] + ["] + [QUOTATION] + ["]
She said, "I am happy."
He said, "I will come."
```

**Structure 2: Quotation Inside Quotation**
```
[REPORTING VERB] + [,] + ["] + [QUOTATION] + ["]
She said, "He said, 'I am happy.'"
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Direct Speech:**
- "She said, **'I am happy.'** "
- "He said, **'I will come.'** "
- "She asked, **'Are you coming?'** "
- "He shouted, **'Help!'** "

**Quotations:**
- "She said, **'The best time to plant a tree was 20 years ago.'** "
- "He quoted Shakespeare: **'To be or not to be.'** "
- "The teacher said, **'Study hard.'** "

**Titles:**
- "I read **'The Raven'** by Edgar Allan Poe." (short story/poem)
- "She sang **'Imagine'** by John Lennon." (song)
- "He wrote **'The Road Not Taken'**." (poem)

**Quotation Marks vs. Italics:**
- Short works (poems, songs, chapters) → Quotation marks
- Long works (books, movies, albums) → Italics

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She said, I am happy. | She said, **"I am happy."** |
| She asked, Are you coming? | She asked, **"Are you coming?"** |
| She said, "I am happy. (missing closing) | She said, **"I am happy."** |

## 7. Dash (—)

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** The **dash** is used to indicate an interruption, emphasis, or an abrupt change in thought. It is stronger than a comma.

**Key Points:**
- Indicates interruption
- Adds emphasis
- Shows an abrupt change

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Dash for Emphasis**
```
[MAIN CLAUSE] + [—] + [EMPHASIS] + [—] + [REST]
She — my sister — is here.
I saw her — she was happy.
```

**Structure 2: Dash for Interruption**
```
[MAIN CLAUSE START] + [—] + [INTERRUPTION] + [—] + [REST]
I was going — but I forgot.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Dash for Emphasis:**
- "She — **my sister** — is here."
- "I saw her — **she was happy**."
- "He is the best — **the very best** — at what he does."
- "I need one thing — **your help**."

**Dash for Interruption:**
- "I was going to call you — **but I forgot**."
- "She said — **I can't believe it** — that she was leaving."
- "He walked in — **without knocking** — and sat down."

**Dash vs. Comma:**
- "My brother, who lives in London, is a doctor." (comma = non-essential)
- "My brother — who lives in London — is a doctor." (dash = emphasis)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She my sister is here. | She — my sister — is here. |
| I was going but I forgot. | I was going — but I forgot. |
| I need one thing, your help. | I need one thing — your help. |

## 8. Hyphen (-)

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** The **hyphen** is used to join compound words and to connect numbers. It is shorter than a dash.

**Key Points:**
- Joins compound words
- Connects numbers
- Used in some prefixes

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Hyphen in Compound Words**
```
[WORD 1] + [-] + [WORD 2]
well + - + known = well-known
mother + - + in + - + law = mother-in-law
```

**Structure 2: Hyphen in Numbers**
```
[NUMBER] + [-] + [NUMBER]
twenty + - + one = twenty-one
thirty + - + five = thirty-five
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Compound Words:**
- "She is a **well-known** author."
- "My **mother-in-law** is visiting."
- "He is a **good-looking** man."
- "I need a **part-time** job."
- "This is a **world-famous** restaurant."

**Numbers:**
- "twenty-one"
- "thirty-five"
- "forty-seven"
- "ninety-nine"

**Prefixes with Hyphens:**
- "self-esteem"
- "ex-husband"
- "pre-arrange"
- "re-elect"

**Hyphen vs. Dash:**
- "well-known" (hyphen = joins words)
- "She — my sister — is here." (dash = emphasis)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She is a well known author. | She is a **well-known** author. |
| My mother in law is visiting. | My **mother-in-law** is visiting. |
| twenty one | **twenty-one** |
| self esteem | **self-esteem** |

## 9. Parentheses ( )

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Parentheses** are used to add extra information or clarification that is not essential to the sentence.

**Key Points:**
- Add extra information
- Clarify meaning
- Non-essential information

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Parentheses for Extra Information**
```
[MAIN CLAUSE] + [(] + [EXTRA INFORMATION] + [)] + [REST]
She (my sister) is here.
The book (which is on the table) is mine.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Parentheses for Extra Information:**
- "She **(my sister)** is here."
- "The book **(which is on the table)** is mine."
- "I need your help **(please)** ."
- "He was born **(in 1990)** in London."

**Parentheses for Clarification:**
- "I need to see the doctor **(Dr. Smith)** ."
- "She loves classical music **(especially Mozart)** ."
- "The project **(which took six months)** was a success."

**Parentheses vs. Commas:**
- "My brother, who lives in London, is a doctor." (commas = non-essential)
- "My brother (who lives in London) is a doctor." (parentheses = extra info)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She my sister is here. | She **(my sister)** is here. |
| The book which is on the table is mine. | The book **(which is on the table)** is mine. |

## 10. Brackets [ ]

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Brackets** are used to add clarifying information inside quotations or to indicate editorial changes.

**Key Points:**
- Clarify within quotations
- Indicate editorial changes
- Add missing information

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Brackets in Quotations**
```
["] + [QUOTATION] + [ [CLARIFICATION] ] + [QUOTATION] + ["]
He said, "I [John] will go."
She said, "I [she] was happy."
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Brackets in Quotations:**
- "He said, **'I [John] will go.'** "
- "She said, **'I [she] was happy.'** "
- "The document says, **'All citizens must [comply with] the law.'** "

**Brackets for Editorial Changes:**
- "He said, **'I [will] go.'** " (changed tense)
- "She said, **'It [the book] was good.'** "
- "The teacher said, **'You [students] must study.'** "

**Brackets vs. Parentheses:**
- "She (my sister) is here." (parentheses = extra info)
- "He said, 'I [John] will go.'" (brackets = clarification in quote)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| He said, "I will go." (if clarifying John) | He said, "I **[John]** will go." |
| She said, "I was happy." (if clarifying she) | She said, "I **[she]** was happy." |

3. I like coffee she likes tea. (missing semicolon)
4. I need milk, bread and eggs. (missing colon)
5. Sarahs book is on the table. (missing apostrophe)

## 📚 Quick Reference Card

| Mark | Name | Function | Example |
|------|------|----------|---------|
| . | Full Stop | Ends a sentence | I am happy. |
| , | Comma | Separates elements | I like coffee, tea, and juice. |
| ; | Semicolon | Joins related clauses | I like coffee; she likes tea. |
| : | Colon | Introduces list/explanation | I need: milk and bread. |
| ' | Apostrophe | Possession/contraction | Sarah's book, don't |
| " " | Quotation Marks | Direct speech/titles | She said, "Hello." |
| — | Dash | Emphasis/interruption | I saw her — she was happy. |
| - | Hyphen | Joins compound words | well-known |
| ( ) | Parentheses | Extra information | She (my sister) is here. |
| [ ] | Brackets | Clarification in quotes | He said, "I [John] will go." |
