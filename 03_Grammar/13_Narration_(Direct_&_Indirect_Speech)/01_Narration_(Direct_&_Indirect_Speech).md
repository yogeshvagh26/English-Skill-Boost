# Phase 13: Narration (Direct & Indirect Speech)

Welcome to Phase 13! You've completed the core grammar roadmap (Phases 1-12). Now it's time to explore **Narration** — the art of reporting what someone else has said. Mastering direct and indirect speech is essential for storytelling, reporting conversations, and academic writing.

Think of it this way: *There are two ways to report what someone says. You can quote their exact words (Direct Speech) or you can paraphrase their words (Indirect/Reported Speech). "She said, 'I am tired'" (Direct) vs. "She said that she was tired" (Indirect). Knowing when and how to use each form is a key skill in English.*

## 1. Direct Speech

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Direct speech** repeats the exact words someone said, using quotation marks. It is often used in conversations, stories, and interviews.

**Key Points:**
- Uses quotation marks (" ")
- Reports the exact words
- The reporting verb comes before or after the quote
- Punctuation goes inside the quotation marks

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Reporting Verb Before Quotation**
```
[REPORTING VERB] + [,] + ["QUOTATION"]
She + said, + "I am tired."
He + said, + "I will come."
```

**Structure 2: Quotation Before Reporting Verb**
```
["QUOTATION"] + [,] + [REPORTING VERB]
"I am tired," + she + said.
"I will come," + he + said.
```

**Structure 3: Reporting Verb in the Middle**
```
["QUOTATION PART 1"] + [,] + [REPORTING VERB] + [,] + ["QUOTATION PART 2"]
"I am tired," + she + said, + "but I will continue."
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Reporting Verb Before:**
- "She said, **'I am happy.'** "
- "He said, **'I will call you.'** "
- "They said, **'We are coming.'** "
- "She asked, **'Where is the station?'** "

**Quotation Before:**
- "**'I am happy,'** she said."
- "**'I will call you,'** he said."
- "**'We are coming,'** they said."
- "**'Where is the station?'** she asked."

**Reporting Verb in Middle:**
- "**'I am happy,'** she said, **'but I am also tired.'** "
- "**'I will call you,'** he said, **'if I have time.'** "
- "**'We are coming,'** they said, **'but we will be late.'** "

**Punctuation Rules for Direct Speech:**

| Situation | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| Statement | She said, **"I am happy."** |
| Question | She asked, **"Are you happy?"** |
| Exclamation | She shouted, **"I am happy!"** |
| Quotation after verb | She said, **"I am happy."** |
| Quotation before verb | **"I am happy,"** she said. |
| Question before verb | **"Are you happy?"** she asked. |
| Exclamation before verb | **"I am happy!"** she shouted. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She said "I am happy". | She said, **"I am happy."** |
| She said "I am happy"? | She said, **"I am happy."** |
| She asked "Are you happy?" (✓) | She asked, **"Are you happy?"** |
| "I am happy", she said. | **"I am happy,"** she said. |

## 2. Indirect Speech (Reported Speech)

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Indirect speech** reports what someone said without using their exact words. It paraphrases the meaning and often changes the tense, pronouns, and time expressions.

**Key Points:**
- No quotation marks
- The tense often changes (backshift)
- Pronouns change
- Time and place expressions change

### 📐 Study: The Golden Rules of Indirect Speech

**Rule 1: Remove Quotation Marks**
- Direct: She said, **"I am happy."**
- Indirect: She said **that she was happy.**

**Rule 2: Backshift the Tense (Usually)**
- Present → Past
- Past → Past Perfect
- Present Perfect → Past Perfect
- Future → Conditional

**Rule 3: Change Pronouns**
- I → He/She
- You → I/He/She/They
- We → They

**Rule 4: Change Time and Place Expressions**
- Now → Then
- Today → That day
- Tomorrow → The next day
- Yesterday → The day before
- Here → There
- This → That

### 👀 See: Examples

**Direct to Indirect Transformation:**

| Direct | Indirect |
|--------|----------|
| She said, "I am happy." | She said **that she was happy.** |
| He said, "I will come." | He said **that he would come.** |
| They said, "We are coming." | They said **that they were coming.** |
| She said, "I have finished." | She said **that she had finished.** |

**Tense Changes (Backshift):**

| Direct Tense | Indirect Tense | Example |
|--------------|----------------|---------|
| Simple Present → | Simple Past | "I am happy" → She said she **was** happy. |
| Present Continuous → | Past Continuous | "I am working" → She said she **was working**. |
| Present Perfect → | Past Perfect | "I have finished" → She said she **had finished**. |
| Simple Past → | Past Perfect | "I went home" → She said she **had gone** home. |
| Past Continuous → | Past Perfect Continuous | "I was working" → She said she **had been working**. |
| Simple Future → | Conditional | "I will go" → She said she **would go**. |
| Future Perfect → | Conditional Perfect | "I will have gone" → She said she **would have gone**. |

**Modal Verb Changes:**

| Direct Modal | Indirect Modal | Example |
|--------------|----------------|---------|
| can → | could | "I can swim" → She said she **could** swim. |
| may → | might | "I may come" → She said she **might** come. |
| must → | had to | "I must go" → She said she **had to** go. |
| will → | would | "I will go" → She said she **would** go. |
| shall → | should/would | "I shall go" → She said she **would** go. |
| could → | could | (No change) |
| should → | should | (No change) |
| would → | would | (No change) |
| might → | might | (No change) |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She said that she is happy. | She said that she **was** happy. |
| He said that he will come. | He said that he **would** come. |
| She said that she has finished. | She said that she **had** finished. |
| He said that he can swim. | He said that he **could** swim. |

## 3. Reporting Statements

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** When reporting statements (declarative sentences), use "that" to introduce the reported clause. The tense and pronouns change according to the rules.

**Key Points:**
- Use "that" (optional)
- Backshift the tense
- Change pronouns
- Change time and place expressions

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure: Reporting Statements**
```
[REPORTING VERB] + [(THAT)] + [REPORTED CLAUSE]
She + said + (that) + she was happy.
He + told + me + (that) + he was tired.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Reporting Statements:**

| Direct | Indirect |
|--------|----------|
| She said, "I am happy." | She said **that she was happy.** |
| He said, "I work hard." | He said **that he worked hard.** |
| They said, "We are coming." | They said **that they were coming.** |
| She said, "I have finished." | She said **that she had finished.** |
| He said, "I will call you." | He said **that he would call me.** |
| She said, "I can swim." | She said **that she could swim.** |
| He said, "I must go." | He said **that he had to go.** |

**With Time Changes:**

| Direct | Indirect |
|--------|----------|
| She said, "I am happy now." | She said **that she was happy then.** |
| He said, "I will come today." | He said **that he would come that day.** |
| She said, "I went yesterday." | She said **that she had gone the day before.** |
| He said, "I will go tomorrow." | He said **that he would go the next day.** |
| They said, "We are here." | They said **that they were there.** |
| She said, "I like this." | She said **that she liked that.** |

**Say vs. Tell:**

| Verb | Structure | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **say** | say + (that) + clause | She **said** (that) she was happy. |
| **say** | say + to + person | She **said to me** (that) she was happy. |
| **tell** | tell + person + (that) + clause | She **told me** (that) she was happy. |

**Examples:**
- "She **said** she was happy." (no object)
- "She **said to me** she was happy." (with object)
- "She **told me** she was happy." (with object, no "to")

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She said me that she was happy. | She **told me** that she was happy. |
| She told that she was happy. | She **said** that she was happy. |
| He said to me that he was tired. (✓) | He **told me** that he was tired. |
| She said, "I am happy." | She said **that she was happy.** |

## 4. Reporting Questions

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** When reporting questions, the word order changes from question to statement (subject + verb). The tense, pronouns, and time expressions also change.

**Key Points:**
- Change question word order to statement order
- Use "if" or "whether" for yes/no questions
- No question mark in indirect speech
- Backshift the tense

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Wh- Questions**
```
[REPORTING VERB] + [WH- WORD] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB]
She asked + where + I + was.
He asked + what + time + it + was.
```

**Structure 2: Yes/No Questions**
```
[REPORTING VERB] + [IF/WHETHER] + [SUBJECT] + [VERB]
She asked + if + I + was happy.
He asked + whether + I + was coming.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Wh- Questions:**

| Direct | Indirect |
|--------|----------|
| She asked, "Where are you?" | She asked **where I was.** |
| He asked, "What time is it?" | He asked **what time it was.** |
| She asked, "Why are you late?" | She asked **why I was late.** |
| He asked, "How are you?" | He asked **how I was.** |
| She asked, "Who is that?" | She asked **who that was.** |

**Yes/No Questions:**

| Direct | Indirect |
|--------|----------|
| She asked, "Are you happy?" | She asked **if I was happy.** |
| He asked, "Do you like coffee?" | He asked **whether I liked coffee.** |
| She asked, "Have you finished?" | She asked **if I had finished.** |
| He asked, "Will you come?" | He asked **whether I would come.** |

**Question Word Order:**
- Direct: "Where **are** you?" (verb + subject)
- Indirect: She asked where I **was**. (subject + verb)

**If vs. Whether:**
- "She asked **if** I was happy." (informal)
- "She asked **whether** I was happy." (formal)
- "She asked **whether or not** I was happy." (emphasizes the choice)

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She asked where was I. | She asked **where I was.** |
| She asked if was I happy. | She asked **if I was happy.** |
| He asked what time it was? | He asked **what time it was.** |
| She asked me if I am happy. | She asked me **if I was happy.** |

## 5. Reporting Commands

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** When reporting commands (imperatives), we use "tell" + object + infinitive. The imperative changes to an infinitive.

**Key Points:**
- Use "tell" (or "ask," "order," "request")
- Change the imperative to infinitive
- Positive: tell + object + to + verb
- Negative: tell + object + not to + verb

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Positive Command**
```
[TELL] + [OBJECT] + [TO] + [VERB]
She + told + me + to + sit down.
He + told + us + to + be quiet.
```

**Structure 2: Negative Command**
```
[TELL] + [OBJECT] + [NOT] + [TO] + [VERB]
She + told + me + not + to + go.
He + told + us + not + to + worry.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Positive Commands:**

| Direct | Indirect |
|--------|----------|
| She said, "Sit down." | She **told me to sit down.** |
| He said, "Be quiet." | He **told us to be quiet.** |
| She said, "Open the door." | She **told me to open the door.** |
| He said, "Wait for me." | He **told me to wait for him.** |
| She said, "Call me." | She **told me to call her.** |

**Negative Commands:**

| Direct | Indirect |
|--------|----------|
| She said, "Don't go." | She **told me not to go.** |
| He said, "Don't worry." | He **told us not to worry.** |
| She said, "Don't be late." | She **told me not to be late.** |
| He said, "Don't forget." | He **told me not to forget.** |
| She said, "Don't touch that." | She **told me not to touch that.** |

**Other Reporting Verbs for Commands:**

| Verb | Example |
|------|---------|
| **ask** | She **asked** me to help her. |
| **order** | The officer **ordered** us to stop. |
| **request** | He **requested** us to be quiet. |
| **command** | The general **commanded** the soldiers to attack. |
| **warn** | She **warned** me not to go. |
| **advise** | He **advised** me to study harder. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She said me to sit down. | She **told me** to sit down. |
| He told me sit down. | He told me **to** sit down. |
| She told me to not go. | She told me **not to** go. |
| He told me don't go. | He **told me not to go.** |

## 6. Reporting Requests

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** When reporting requests, we use "ask" + object + infinitive. Requests are similar to commands but usually more polite.

**Key Points:**
- Use "ask" (or "request")
- Change to infinitive
- Positive: ask + object + to + verb
- Negative: ask + object + not to + verb

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Positive Request**
```
[ASK] + [OBJECT] + [TO] + [VERB]
She + asked + me + to + help her.
He + asked + us + to + be quiet.
```

**Structure 2: Negative Request**
```
[ASK] + [OBJECT] + [NOT] + [TO] + [VERB]
She + asked + me + not + to + go.
He + asked + us + not + to + worry.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Positive Requests:**

| Direct | Indirect |
|--------|----------|
| She said, "Please help me." | She **asked me to help her.** |
| He said, "Could you please wait?" | He **asked me to wait.** |
| She said, "Will you open the door?" | She **asked me to open the door.** |
| He said, "Please be quiet." | He **asked us to be quiet.** |
| She said, "Can you call me?" | She **asked me to call her.** |

**Negative Requests:**

| Direct | Indirect |
|--------|----------|
| She said, "Please don't go." | She **asked me not to go.** |
| He said, "Please don't worry." | He **asked me not to worry.** |
| She said, "Please don't forget." | She **asked me not to forget.** |
| He said, "Please don't be late." | He **asked me not to be late.** |

**Request vs. Command:**
- Request: "Please help me." → She **asked** me to help her.
- Command: "Help me!" → She **told** me to help her.

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She asked me help her. | She asked me **to** help her. |
| He asked me to not go. | He asked me **not to** go. |
| She said me to help her. | She **asked me** to help her. |
| He asked me please to help. | He **asked me to help.** |

## 7. Reporting Exclamations

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** When reporting exclamations, we use verbs like "exclaim," "shout," "say," "cry," etc. The exclamation becomes a statement in indirect speech.

**Key Points:**
- Use exclamation verbs (exclaim, shout, cry, etc.)
- Change to statement form
- Often use "that" + what/how

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Reporting Exclamations**
```
[REPORTING VERB] + [THAT] + [CLAUSE]
She + exclaimed + that + it was beautiful.
He + shouted + that + he was happy.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

| Direct | Indirect |
|--------|----------|
| She said, "What a beautiful day!" | She **exclaimed that it was a beautiful day.** |
| He said, "How wonderful!" | He **exclaimed how wonderful it was.** |
| She said, "What a surprise!" | She **exclaimed that it was a surprise.** |
| He said, "I'm so happy!" | He **shouted that he was very happy.** |
| She said, "Oh no!" | She **cried out that something was wrong.** |
| He said, "Wow!" | He **exclaimed in surprise.** |

**Exclamation Words:**
- What a + noun!
- How + adjective!
- Such + noun!
- So + adjective!

**Reporting Exclamations:**
- "What a beautiful day!" → She exclaimed **that it was a beautiful day.**
- "How wonderful!" → She exclaimed **how wonderful it was.**
- "Such a surprise!" → She exclaimed **that it was such a surprise.**
- "So beautiful!" → She exclaimed **that it was so beautiful.**

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She exclaimed what a beautiful day. | She exclaimed **that it was a beautiful day.** |
| He shouted how wonderful. | He shouted **how wonderful it was.** |
| She said that it was a beautiful day. (✓) | She said **that it was a beautiful day.** |

## 8. Reporting Verbs

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Reporting verbs** are used to introduce direct and indirect speech. Different verbs convey different meanings and tones.

**Key Points:**
- Use different verbs for different meanings
- Some verbs require "that"
- Some verbs require an object
- Some verbs are followed by infinitive

### 📐 Study: Reporting Verbs Chart

| Verb | Structure | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **say** | say + (that) + clause | She **said** (that) she was happy. |
| **tell** | tell + object + (that) + clause | She **told me** (that) she was happy. |
| **ask** | ask + object + wh-/if + clause | She **asked** where I was. |
| **ask** | ask + object + to + infinitive | She **asked me to** help. |
| **tell** | tell + object + to + infinitive | She **told me to** sit down. |
| **order** | order + object + to + infinitive | She **ordered me to** stop. |
| **request** | request + object + to + infinitive | She **requested me to** help. |
| **advise** | advise + object + to + infinitive | She **advised me to** study. |
| **warn** | warn + object + to/not to + infinitive | She **warned me not to** go. |
| **promise** | promise + to + infinitive | She **promised to** come. |
| **offer** | offer + to + infinitive | She **offered to** help. |
| **agree** | agree + to + infinitive | She **agreed to** come. |
| **refuse** | refuse + to + infinitive | She **refused to** go. |
| **explain** | explain + (that) + clause | She **explained** why she was late. |
| **suggest** | suggest + gerund / (that) + clause | She **suggested going** / **suggested that we go**. |

### 👀 See: Examples

**Verbs with "That" Clause:**
- "She **said** that she was tired."
- "He **told me** that he was tired."
- "She **explained** that she was late."
- "He **admitted** that he had made a mistake."
- "She **agreed** that it was a good idea."
- "He **believed** that she was right."

**Verbs with Infinitive:**
- "She **offered** to help."
- "He **promised** to come."
- "She **refused** to go."
- "He **agreed** to wait."
- "She **advised** me to study harder."
- "He **warned** me not to go."

**Verbs with Gerund:**
- "She **suggested** going to the park."
- "He **recommended** taking a break."
- "She **admitted** making a mistake."
- "He **denied** stealing the money."
- "She **apologized for** being late."

**Verbs with Object + Infinitive:**
- "She **asked** me to help."
- "He **told** me to wait."
- "She **advised** me to study."
- "He **warned** me not to go."
- "She **invited** me to come."
- "He **reminded** me to call."

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She said me that she was happy. | She **told me** that she was happy. |
| He told that he was tired. | He **said** that he was tired. |
| She suggested to go. | She suggested **going**. |
| He advised me studying. | He advised me **to study**. |

## 9. Time and Place Changes

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** When converting direct speech to indirect speech, time and place expressions often change to reflect the new perspective.

**Key Points:**
- Change time expressions
- Change place expressions
- Change demonstratives

### 📐 Study: Time and Place Changes Chart

| Direct | Indirect |
|--------|----------|
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| tonight | that night |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| yesterday | the day before / the previous day |
| this week | that week |
| last week | the week before / the previous week |
| next week | the following week / the next week |
| ago | before / earlier |
| here | there |
| this | that |
| these | those |
| come | go |

### 👀 See: Examples

**Time Changes:**

| Direct | Indirect |
|--------|----------|
| She said, "I am happy **now**." | She said she was happy **then**. |
| He said, "I will come **today**." | He said he would come **that day**. |
| She said, "I went **yesterday**." | She said she had gone **the day before**. |
| He said, "I will go **tomorrow**." | He said he would go **the next day**. |
| She said, "I saw him **last week**." | She said she had seen him **the week before**. |
| He said, "I will see you **next week**." | He said he would see me **the following week**. |

**Place Changes:**

| Direct | Indirect |
|--------|----------|
| She said, "I am **here**." | She said she was **there**. |
| He said, "I will wait **here**." | He said he would wait **there**. |
| She said, "I like **this**." | She said she liked **that**. |
| He said, "I want **these**." | He said he wanted **those**. |

**Change of "Come" to "Go":**
- Direct: She said, "I will **come**."
- Indirect: She said she would **go**.

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| She said she was happy now. | She said she was happy **then**. |
| He said he would come today. | He said he would come **that day**. |
| She said she was here. | She said she was **there**. |
| He said he liked this. | He said he liked **that**. |

## 10. Summary: Direct to Indirect Transformation

### 📚 Complete Reference Chart

| Direct Element | Indirect Change | Example |
|----------------|-----------------|---------|
| **Tense** | Backshift one step | "I am happy" → She said she **was** happy. |
| **Pronouns** | Change perspective | "I" → She said **she** was happy. |
| **Time** | Change to perspective | "now" → **then** |
| **Place** | Change to perspective | "here" → **there** |
| **Demonstratives** | Change to perspective | "this" → **that** |
| **Question Word Order** | Change to statement order | "Where are you?" → She asked **where I was**. |
| **Yes/No Questions** | Add if/whether | "Are you happy?" → She asked **if** I was happy. |
| **Commands** | Change to infinitive | "Sit down" → She told me **to sit down**. |

## 💬 Practice: Conversations

### Dialogue 1: Reporting a Conversation

**Original Conversation:**
- A: "I am tired."
- B: "I will make you some coffee."
- A: "Thank you. That would be great."

**Reported:**
- A **said** that he was tired.
- B **told him** that he would make him some coffee.
- A **thanked** B and said that that would be great.

### Dialogue 2: Reporting a Question

**Original Conversation:**
- A: "Where are you going?"
- B: "I am going to the store."
- A: "Can you buy some bread?"
- B: "Sure. I will buy some bread."

**Reported:**
- A **asked** B where he was going.
- B **said** that he was going to the store.
- A **asked** if B could buy some bread.
- B **said** that he would buy some bread.

### Dialogue 3: Reporting Commands and Requests

**Original Conversation:**
- Boss: "Please finish the report today."
- Employee: "I will finish it by 5 PM."
- Boss: "Make sure it's perfect."
- Employee: "I will do my best."

**Reported:**
- The boss **asked** the employee to finish the report that day.
- The employee **said** that he would finish it by 5 PM.
- The boss **told** the employee to make sure it was perfect.
- The employee **promised** to do his best.
