# Topic: Managing a CSR / ESG Reporting and Stakeholder Engagement Process

**Scenario:** You are the Director of Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) at a multinational consumer goods company. The company publishes an annual ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) report, which is closely scrutinized by investors, customers, NGOs, and regulators. This year’s report shows strong progress on carbon reduction but highlights a gap in supply chain diversity and employee well-being metrics. You are now presenting the draft report to the executive committee (CEO, CFO, COO, CHRO, and Head of Legal) to secure their approval and alignment on the narrative, and to prepare for engagement with key external stakeholders. Your goal is to present the data transparently, address the gaps with a clear action plan, manage internal concerns about reputational risk, and commit to a robust stakeholder dialogue process.

---

## Dialogue

| **Speaker** | **Dialogue / Text** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **You** | *(opening the presentation)* Thank you for the time. I'm here to present our draft ESG report for the past fiscal year. This is not just a compliance document—it's a strategic communication that shapes how our investors, customers, and communities perceive our values and performance. I'll cover our achievements, our gaps, and our commitments for the coming year. |
| **CEO** | Give us the headline. |
| **You** | The headline is: we've made significant progress on environmental metrics—carbon emissions down 12% year‑over‑year—but we have gaps in supply chain diversity and employee well‑being. We also need to strengthen our governance disclosures. |
| **CFO** | What are the financial implications of the gaps? |
| **You** | Investors are increasingly tying ESG performance to investment decisions. A weak score could affect our cost of capital. We've included a forward‑looking plan to address these gaps, which we estimate will cost $5M over two years—but will mitigate long‑term risks and enhance brand equity. |
| **COO** | What about the supply chain diversity gap? |
| **You** | We've set a target to increase spend with diverse suppliers from 8% to 15% by 2028. We've already identified 20 potential suppliers and are piloting a mentorship program to help them scale. |
| **CHRO** | And employee well‑being? |
| **You** | Our engagement survey shows a dip in work‑life balance. We're launching a flexible work policy pilot and introducing mental health support programs. We'll track these metrics quarterly. |
| **Head of Legal** | Any regulatory risks? |
| **You** | We're aligned with the upcoming EU sustainability reporting standards (CSRD). We've already begun the necessary data collection and verification processes. |
| **You** | *(sharing the engagement plan)* For external stakeholders, we'll hold a webcast for investors, a town hall for employees, and a consultation with key NGOs. We'll also publish a summary on our website. We need to be prepared for tough questions on the gaps. |
| **CEO** | How do we address the gaps without undermining confidence? |
| **You** | We'll frame them as opportunities for improvement. We'll say: "We are proud of our progress, and we are committed to going further. Here is our roadmap." |
| **CFO** | I'm comfortable with the report and the engagement plan. Let's proceed. |
| **You** | Thank you. I'll finalize the report with the feedback from this meeting, and we'll schedule the external launch for next month. |
| *(3 months later – follow-up)* | |
| **You** | *(to the executive committee)* The ESG report is out. Investor feedback has been positive, and we've received recognition from a leading ESG rating agency. The supply chain diversity pilot is already showing early results. |
| **CEO** | This is exactly the reputation boost we needed. Thank you for leading this. |
| **You** | It's a team effort. Now we'll continue to execute on our commitments and report transparently next year. |

## Vocabulary

| **Word / Phrase** | **Part of Speech** | **Meaning** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CSR (Corporate Social Responsibility)** | Noun | A company's commitment to ethical and sustainable practices. |
| **ESG (Environmental, Social, Governance)** | Noun | A framework for evaluating corporate sustainability and ethics. |
| **Sustainability reporting** | Noun Phrase | Disclosing environmental and social performance. |
| **Supply chain diversity** | Noun Phrase | Sourcing from suppliers owned by underrepresented groups. |
| **Employee well‑being** | Noun Phrase | The physical and mental health of employees. |
| **Cost of capital** | Noun Phrase | The cost a company pays to finance its operations. |
| **Brand equity** | Noun Phrase | The value of a brand based on consumer perception. |
| **Mentorship program** | Noun Phrase | A system where experienced people guide others. |
| **CSRD** | Noun | Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (EU). |
| **Verification** | Noun | The process of checking accuracy and compliance. |
| **Stakeholder consultation** | Noun Phrase | Gathering input from affected parties. |
| **NGOs (Non‑Governmental Organizations)** | Noun | Non‑profit groups that advocate for social or environmental causes. |
| **Webcast** | Noun | A live online broadcast. |
| **Town hall** | Noun | A company‑wide meeting for communication. |
| **Roadmap** | Noun | A strategic plan with milestones. |
| **Rating agency** | Noun | An organization that evaluates corporate ESG performance. |
| **Disclosure** | Noun | The public release of information. |
| **Benchmark** | Noun | A standard for comparison. |
| **Commitment** | Noun | A promise to take specific actions. |
| **Governance** | Noun | The system of rules and practices for leadership. |
