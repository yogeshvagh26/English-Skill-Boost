## 1. Phrasal Verbs

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** A **phrasal verb** is a verb combined with a particle (preposition or adverb) that creates a meaning different from the original verb. They are very common in spoken English.

**Key Points:**
- Verb + particle = new meaning
- Very common in spoken English
- Can be transitive or intransitive
- Can be separable or inseparable

### 📐 Study: Types of Phrasal Verbs

**Type 1: Intransitive (No Object)**

| Phrasal Verb | Meaning | Example |
|--------------|---------|---------|
| wake up | stop sleeping | I **woke up** at 7 AM. |
| grow up | become an adult | She **grew up** in London. |
| give up | stop trying | Don't **give up**! |
| show up | arrive | He **showed up** late. |
| break down | stop working | My car **broke down**. |

**Type 2: Transitive Separable (Object Can Go Between)**

| Phrasal Verb | Meaning | Example |
|--------------|---------|---------|
| turn on | start (a device) | **Turn on** the TV. / **Turn** the TV **on**. |
| turn off | stop (a device) | **Turn off** the lights. / **Turn** the lights **off**. |
| put on | wear | **Put on** your coat. / **Put** your coat **on**. |
| take off | remove | **Take off** your shoes. / **Take** your shoes **off**. |
| pick up | collect | **Pick up** the book. / **Pick** the book **up**. |
| throw away | discard | **Throw away** the trash. / **Throw** the trash **away**. |

**Type 3: Transitive Inseparable (Object Must Come After)**

| Phrasal Verb | Meaning | Example |
|--------------|---------|---------|
| look after | take care of | She **looks after** her children. |
| run into | meet unexpectedly | I **ran into** an old friend. |
| get over | recover from | She **got over** her illness. |
| look for | search for | I'm **looking for** my keys. |
| go through | experience | He **went through** a difficult time. |

## 2. Fixed Expressions

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Fixed expressions** are set phrases that are used in specific situations. They are not usually changed or modified.

**Key Points:**
- Set phrases for specific situations
- Cannot be changed
- Used in everyday conversation
- Make speech sound natural

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Greetings**
```
[HOW ARE YOU?] + [I'M FINE, THANKS]
How are you? + I'm fine, thanks.
```

**Structure 2: Introductions**
```
[NICE TO MEET YOU] + [NICE TO MEET YOU TOO]
Nice to meet you. + Nice to meet you too.
```

**Structure 3: Farewells**
```
[SEE YOU LATER] + [SEE YOU LATER]
See you later! + See you later!
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Fixed Expressions by Situation:**

**Greetings:**
- "**How are you?** "
- "**How's it going?** "
- "**What's up?** "
- "**How have you been?** "
- "**Good to see you!** "

**Responses:**
- "**I'm fine, thanks.** "
- "**I'm doing well.** "
- "**Not bad, thanks.** "
- "**Pretty good, you?** "
- "**Same as always.** "

**Introductions:**
- "**Nice to meet you.** "
- "**Pleased to meet you.** "
- "**How do you do?** " (formal)
- "**My name is...** "
- "**I'd like to introduce...** "

**Farewells:**
- "**See you later.** "
- "**Take care.** "
- "**Have a good day.** "
- "**Goodbye.** "

**Agreeing:**
- "**I agree.** "
- "**You're right.** "
- "**That's true.** "
- "**Absolutely.** "
- "**Definitely.** "

**Disagreeing:**
- "**I don't think so.** "
- "**I disagree.** "
- "**That's not true.** "
- "**I'm not sure about that.** "
- "**I see your point, but...** "

**Asking for Help:**
- "**Can you help me?** "
- "**Could you please...?** "
- "**Would you mind...?** "
- "**I need a hand.** "
- "**Could you do me a favor?** "

**Thanking:**
- "**Thank you.** "

**Apologizing:**

**Accepting Apologies:**

## 3. Collocations

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Collocations** are words that naturally go together. They sound "right" to native speakers because they are commonly used together.

**Key Points:**
- Words that naturally go together
- Sound natural to native speakers
- Cannot be changed without sounding strange
- Important for fluency

### 📐 Study: Types of Collocations

**Type 1: Verb + Noun**

| Collocation | Example |
|-------------|---------|
| make a decision | I need to **make a decision**. |
| take a break | Let's **take a break**. |
| have a conversation | We **had a conversation**. |
| do business | They **do business** together. |
| give a presentation | She **gave a presentation**. |

**Type 2: Adjective + Noun**

| Collocation | Example |
|-------------|---------|
| heavy rain | There was **heavy rain**. |
| strong coffee | I like **strong coffee**. |
| fast food | I don't eat **fast food**. |
| big mistake | That was a **big mistake**. |
| close friend | She is a **close friend**. |

**Type 3: Adverb + Adjective**

| Collocation | Example |
|-------------|---------|
| highly recommended | This book is **highly recommended**. |
| deeply sorry | I am **deeply sorry**. |
| extremely important | It's **extremely important**. |
| completely different | It's **completely different**. |
| absolutely certain | I'm **absolutely certain**. |

**Type 4: Noun + Noun**

| Collocation | Example |
|-------------|---------|
| business meeting | We have a **business meeting**. |
| job interview | I have a **job interview**. |
| health care | **Health care** is important. |
| traffic jam | I was stuck in a **traffic jam**. |
| computer program | She wrote a **computer program**. |

## 4. Binomials

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Binomials** are pairs of words joined by "and" or "or" that are used together in a fixed order. They are very common in English.

**Key Points:**
- Two words joined by "and" or "or"
- Fixed order (cannot be reversed)
- Very common in spoken English
- Sound natural to native speakers

### 📐 Study: Binomial Structures

**Structure 1: Binomials with "and"**
```
[WORD A] + [AND] + [WORD B]
black + and + white
ups + and + downs
```

**Structure 2: Binomials with "or"**
```
[WORD A] + [OR] + [WORD B]
now + or + never
make + or + break
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Common Binomials with "and":**

| Binomial | Meaning | Example |
|----------|---------|---------|
| black and white | two colors / clear | It's not **black and white**. |
| up and down | in all directions | He walked **up and down**. |
| ups and downs | good and bad times | Life has **ups and downs**. |
| back and forth | moving both ways | I walked **back and forth**. |
| right and left | both sides | Look **right and left**. |
| here and there | in various places | I looked **here and there**. |
| now and then | occasionally | I visit **now and then**. |
| sooner or later | eventually | **Sooner or later**, you'll understand. |
| more or less | approximately | It's **more or less** the same. |
| all and all | considering everything | **All and all**, it was a good day. |
| pros and cons | advantages and disadvantages | Consider the **pros and cons**. |
| give and take | compromise | Marriage requires **give and take**. |
| rain or shine | regardless of weather | We'll go **rain or shine**. |
| shot and shell | various weapons | The soldier faced **shot and shell**. |
| first and foremost | most importantly | **First and foremost**, be kind. |
| far and wide | over a large area | People came from **far and wide**. |
| by and large | generally | **By and large**, it was a success. |
| sick and tired | very tired/annoyed | I'm **sick and tired** of this. |
| nice and easy | very easy | Take it **nice and easy**. |
| hard and fast | strict | There are **hard and fast** rules. |

**Common Binomials with "or":**

| Binomial | Meaning | Example |
|----------|---------|---------|
| now or never | at this moment | It's **now or never**. |
| make or break | success or failure | This is a **make or break** moment. |
| all or nothing | total or nothing | It's **all or nothing**. |
| do or die | succeed or fail | It's a **do or die** situation. |
| take it or leave it | accept or reject | That's my offer - **take it or leave it**. |
| more or less | approximately | It's **more or less** done. |
| sooner or later | eventually | **Sooner or later**, you'll see. |
| believe it or not | surprisingly | **Believe it or not**, I saw a ghost. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| White and black | **Black and white** |
| Down and up | **Up and down** |
| Now and then (✓) | **Now and then** |
| More or less (✓) | **More or less** |

## 5. Common Idioms

### 📚 Learn: The Concept

**Definition:** **Idioms** are figurative expressions whose meaning is not literal. They are deeply ingrained in English and used frequently by native speakers.

**Key Points:**
- Figurative meaning (not literal)
- Cannot be understood from individual words
- Very common in spoken English
- Important for fluency

### 📐 Study: Sentence Structures

**Structure 1: Idiom in a Sentence**
```
[SUBJECT] + [IDIOM] + [REST]
It's + raining cats and dogs + outside.
I + let the cat out of the bag + by accident.
```

### 👀 See: Examples

**Common Idioms by Category:**

**Weather Idioms:**
- "It's **raining cats and dogs**." (raining heavily)
- "I feel **under the weather**." (feeling sick)
- "Every cloud has a **silver lining**." (every bad situation has a positive side)
- "**Break the ice**." (start a conversation)
- "**Chase rainbows**." (pursue impossible dreams)

**Body Idioms:**
- "She **keeps an eye on** her children." (watches carefully)
- "He **costs an arm and a leg**." (very expensive)
- "I **got cold feet**." (became nervous)
- "She **has a heart of gold**." (very kind)
- "He's **all ears**." (listening carefully)
- "I **put my foot in my mouth**." (said something embarrassing)
- "She **keeps her chin up**." (remains optimistic)
- "He **stuck his neck out**." (took a risk)
- "I **hit the nail on the head**." (said exactly the right thing)
- "She **turned a blind eye**." (ignored something)

**Food Idioms:**
- "It's a **piece of cake**." (very easy)
- "**Spill the beans**." (tell a secret)
- "He's **full of beans**." (energetic)
- "She's **in hot water**." (in trouble)
- "**Take it with a grain of salt**." (don't believe it completely)
- "It's **not my cup of tea**." (not something I like)
- "He **brought home the bacon**." (earned the money)
- "She's **nuts**." (crazy)
- "**Butter someone up**." (flatter someone)
- "**Couch potato**." (lazy person who watches TV)

**Animal Idioms:**
- "I **let the cat out of the bag**." (revealed a secret)
- "She's **a night owl**." (stays up late)
- "He's **an early bird**." (wakes up early)
- "**Kill two birds with one stone**." (achieve two things at once)
- "**Hold your horses**." (be patient)
- "**The elephant in the room**." (an obvious problem no one discusses)
- "**When pigs fly**." (something that will never happen)
- "**A fish out of water**." (someone in an unfamiliar situation)
- "**The lion's share**." (the largest part)
- "**Monkey business**." (silly behavior)

**Time Idioms:**
- "**Time flies**." (time passes quickly)
- "**Better late than never**." (late is better than not at all)
- "**It's high time**." (time is up)
- "**Save time**." (do something more efficiently)
- "**Waste time**." (do something unnecessary)
- "**In the nick of time**." (at the last moment)
- "**Once in a blue moon**." (very rarely)
- "**Since time immemorial**." (for a very long time)

**Common Idioms in Daily Use:**

| Idiom | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| break the ice | start a conversation | I told a joke to **break the ice**. |
| piece of cake | very easy | The exam was a **piece of cake**. |
| spill the beans | reveal a secret | She **spilled the beans** about the party. |
| under the weather | feeling sick | I'm feeling **under the weather**. |
| cost an arm and a leg | very expensive | The car **cost an arm and a leg**. |
| hit the nail on the head | say exactly the right thing | You **hit the nail on the head**. |
| let the cat out of the bag | reveal a secret | I **let the cat out of the bag** by accident. |
| hold your horses | be patient | **Hold your horses**! We'll leave soon. |
| in hot water | in trouble | He's **in hot water** with his boss. |
| not my cup of tea | not what I like | Soccer is **not my cup of tea**. |
| raining cats and dogs | raining heavily | It's **raining cats and dogs** outside. |
| time flies | time passes quickly | **Time flies** when you're having fun. |
| better late than never | late is better than not at all | I finally called her - **better late than never**. |
| kill two birds with one stone | achieve two things at once | I'll **kill two birds with one stone** by walking to work. |

### ⚠️ Learn: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| It's raining dogs and cats. | It's **raining cats and dogs**. |
| It's a cake piece. | It's a **piece of cake**. |
| She spilled the beans. (✓) | She **spilled the beans**. |
| I'm under the weather. (✓) | I'm **under the weather**. |
